General Instructions:
  • This is an open-book test. Focus on logical reasoning and application of concepts rather than direct lifting of text.
  • The paper is divided into three sections: A, B, and C.
  • Section A: Contains 4 Objective type questions (1 mark each). Includes MCQs and Assertion-Reasoning.
  • Section B: Contains 3 Short Answer questions (2 marks each) and 2 Short Answer questions (3 marks each) testing higher-order thinking.
  • Section C: Contains 1 Case-based question (4 marks) with sub-parts.
  • Write balanced chemical equations wherever necessary.
SECTION A (4 Marks)(Objective Type Questions - 1 Mark Each)
1.
A metal 'M' does not liberate hydrogen gas from dilute hydrochloric acid but reacts with oxygen to form a black compound. Which of the following best describes the reaction of metal 'M' with silver nitrate solution?
(a) It displaces silver and the solution turns colourless.
(b) It displaces silver and the solution turns blue.
(c) No reaction takes place as 'M' is less reactive than silver.
(d) It forms a white precipitate of metal nitrate.
2.
An element 'X' reacts with oxygen to give a compound with a high melting point. This compound is also highly soluble in water. The element 'X' is likely to be:
(a) Calcium
(b) Carbon
(c) Silicon
(d) Iron
For questions 3 and 4, two statements are given—one labelled Assertion (A) and the other labelled Reason (R). Select the correct answer to these questions from the codes (a), (b), (c), and (d) as given below.
(a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true, but R is false.
(d) A is false, but R is true.
3.
Assertion (A): When zinc is added to an aqueous solution of iron (II) sulphate, the green colour of the solution gradually fades.
Reason (R): Zinc is less reactive than iron and cannot displace it from its salt solution.
4.
Assertion (A): Hydrogen gas is generally not evolved when a metal reacts with dilute nitric acid (\(HNO_3\)).
Reason (R): Nitric acid is a strong oxidising agent; it oxidises the \(H_2\) produced to water and itself gets reduced to a nitrogen oxide.
SECTION B (12 Marks)(Short Answer Type - Reasoning & Application)
5.
A lustrous, divalent element 'M' reacts vigorously with cold water to form a basic solution and liberates a gas that catches fire easily.
(a) Identify the gas liberated and give the reason why it catches fire. (1 Mark)
(b) Identify the element 'M' (from the reactivity series) and write the balanced chemical equation for its reaction with water. (1 Mark)
6.
You are given three metals: Sodium, Magnesium, and Copper. Suggest an experimental method using only water (in different physical states) to arrange these metals in the decreasing order of their reactivity. State your expected observations. (2 Marks)
7.
A non-metal 'X' exists in two different physical forms 'Y' and 'Z'. 'Y' is the hardest natural substance known, while 'Z' is a good conductor of electricity. Identify 'X', 'Y', and 'Z'. Explain the fundamental structural difference that causes 'Z' to conduct electricity while 'Y' does not, even though both are made of the same element. (2 Marks)
8.
An element 'A' burns with a golden flame in air. It reacts with another element 'B' (atomic number 17) to give a product 'C'.
(a) Identify A, B, and C. (1.5 Marks)
(b) Predict the physical state of compound 'C' at room temperature. Does it conduct electricity in the solid state? Justify your answer based on the nature of bonding. (1.5 Marks)
9.
Carbon can reduce copper oxide to copper, but it cannot reduce calcium oxide to calcium. Explain why this happens with reference to the affinity of metals for oxygen. Give the chemical equation for the reduction of copper oxide by carbon. (3 Marks)
SECTION C (4 Marks)(Case Study / Data Based Question)
10.
Read the following passage and analyse the table to answer the questions that follow:

A student performed a displacement reaction experiment. Samples of four metals P, Q, R, and S were taken and added to test tubes containing different salt solutions one by one. The observations made are tabulated below:
Metal Iron (II) sulphate Copper (II) sulphate Zinc sulphate Silver nitrate
P No reaction Displacement No reaction Displacement
Q Displacement Displacement No reaction Displacement
R No reaction No reaction No reaction Displacement
S No reaction No reaction No reaction No reaction
(a) Identify the most reactive and the least reactive metal among P, Q, R, and S. Give a reason for your choice. (1.5 Marks)
(b) What would you observe if metal 'Q' is added to a solution of Copper (II) sulphate? What type of reaction is this? (1 Mark)
(c) Arrange the metals P, Q, R, and S in the order of increasing reactivity. (1 Mark)
(d) Which among the given metals (P, Q, R, or S) is most likely to be Silver (Ag)? (0.5 Mark)
