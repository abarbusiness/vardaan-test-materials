SECTION A (2 Marks Each)
Q1. Find the HCF and LCM of 144 and 198 using the prime factorization method.
[2 Marks]
Solution:

First, we find the prime factorization of both numbers:

$$ 144 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 = 2^4 \times 3^2 $$ $$ 198 = 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 11 = 2^1 \times 3^2 \times 11^1 $$

HCF (Highest Common Factor): Product of the lowest powers of common prime factors.

$$ \text{HCF} = 2^1 \times 3^2 = 2 \times 9 = 18 $$

LCM (Least Common Multiple): Product of the highest powers of all prime factors involved.

$$ \text{LCM} = 2^4 \times 3^2 \times 11^1 = 16 \times 9 \times 11 = 1584 $$
Q2. The HCF of two numbers is 18 and their product is 12960. Find the LCM of the given numbers.
[2 Marks]
Solution:

We know the relationship between HCF, LCM, and the product of two numbers:

$$ \text{HCF} \times \text{LCM} = \text{Product of the two numbers} $$

Substituting the given values:

\begin{align*} 18 \times \text{LCM} &= 12960 \\\\ \text{LCM} &= \frac{12960}{18} \\\\ \text{LCM} &= 720 \end{align*}
Q3. Evaluate the following expression: $2^0 + 3^0 - 4^0 + (1/2)^{-1}$
[2 Marks]
Solution:

Using the laws of exponents, $a^0 = 1$ (for $a \neq 0$) and $(a/b)^{-n} = (b/a)^n$.

\begin{align*} &= 1 + 1 - 1 + \left(\frac{2}{1}\right)^1 \\\\ &= 2 - 1 + 2 \\\\ &= 1 + 2 \\\\ &= 3 \end{align*}
Q4. Simplify: $(81 / 16)^{-3/4}$
[2 Marks]
Solution:

First, write the bases as powers:

\begin{align*} \left(\frac{81}{16}\right)^{-3/4} &= \left(\frac{3^4}{2^4}\right)^{-3/4} \\\\ &= \left( \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^4 \right)^{-3/4} \\\\ \end{align*}

Multiply the powers $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$:

\begin{align*} &= \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{4 \times \frac{-3}{4}} \\\\ &= \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{-3} \\\\ &= \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^3 \\\\ &= \frac{8}{27} \end{align*}
Q5. Find the value of $x$ if $3^{2x + 1} \div 9 = 27$.
[2 Marks]
Solution:

Express all bases as powers of 3:

\begin{align*} 3^{2x + 1} \div 3^2 &= 3^3 \\\\ \end{align*}

Using the law of exponents for division $a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n}$:

\begin{align*} 3^{(2x + 1) - 2} &= 3^3 \\\\ 3^{2x - 1} &= 3^3 \end{align*}

Since bases are equal, equate the powers:

\begin{align*} 2x - 1 &= 3 \\\\ 2x &= 4 \\\\ x &= 2 \end{align*}
SECTION B (3 Marks Each)
Q6. Three bells toll at intervals of 12, 15, and 18 minutes respectively. If they start tolling together at 8:00 AM, at what time will they next toll together?
[3 Marks]
Solution:

The time they will toll together again is given by the LCM of their individual intervals (12, 15, and 18 minutes).

Finding the LCM using prime factorization:

\begin{align*} 12 &= 2^2 \times 3 \\\\ 15 &= 3 \times 5 \\\\ 18 &= 2 \times 3^2 \end{align*}

LCM = Product of highest powers of all prime factors.

$$ \text{LCM} = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 5 = 4 \times 9 \times 5 = 180 \text{ minutes} $$

Convert minutes to hours:

$$ 180 \text{ minutes} = \frac{180}{60} \text{ hours} = 3 \text{ hours} $$

They started at 8:00 AM. They will toll together after 3 hours.

Next toll time: 8:00 AM + 3 hours = 11:00 AM

Q7. Find the greatest number of 4 digits which is exactly divisible by 12, 16, and 24.
[3 Marks]
Solution:

First, find the LCM of 12, 16, and 24 to find their smallest common divisible number.

\begin{align*} 12 &= 2^2 \times 3 \\\\ 16 &= 2^4 \\\\ 24 &= 2^3 \times 3 \\\\ \text{LCM} &= 2^4 \times 3 = 16 \times 3 = 48 \end{align*}

The greatest 4-digit number is 9999. Now, divide 9999 by the LCM (48) to find the remainder.

$$ 9999 \div 48 = 208 \text{ with a remainder of } 15 $$

To find the number exactly divisible by 48, subtract the remainder from 9999.

$$ \text{Required Number} = 9999 - 15 = 9984 $$
Q8. Simplify the following expression: $\frac{3^{n+1} - 3^n}{3^{n+2} + 3^n}$
[3 Marks]
Solution:

Factor out the common term $3^n$ from both the numerator and the denominator.

\begin{align*} \frac{3^{n+1} - 3^n}{3^{n+2} + 3^n} &= \frac{3^n \cdot 3^1 - 3^n \cdot 1}{3^n \cdot 3^2 + 3^n \cdot 1} \\\\ \end{align*}

Taking $3^n$ as common:

\begin{align*} &= \frac{3^n(3^1 - 1)}{3^n(3^2 + 1)} \\\\ \end{align*}

Cancel out $3^n$ from numerator and denominator:

\begin{align*} &= \frac{3 - 1}{9 + 1} \\\\ &= \frac{2}{10} \\\\ &= \frac{1}{5} \end{align*}
Q9. Prove that: $\left(\frac{x^a}{x^b}\right)^{a+b} \times \left(\frac{x^b}{x^c}\right)^{b+c} \times \left(\frac{x^c}{x^a}\right)^{c+a} = 1$
[3 Marks]
Solution:

Consider the Left Hand Side (LHS):

Using the law of exponents $\frac{x^m}{x^n} = x^{m-n}$ inside the brackets:

\begin{align*} \text{LHS} &= (x^{a-b})^{a+b} \times (x^{b-c})^{b+c} \times (x^{c-a})^{c+a} \\\\ \end{align*}

Using the power of a power rule $(x^m)^n = x^{mn}$:

\begin{align*} &= x^{(a-b)(a+b)} \times x^{(b-c)(b+c)} \times x^{(c-a)(c+a)} \\\\ \end{align*}

Applying the algebraic identity $(A-B)(A+B) = A^2 - B^2$:

\begin{align*} &= x^{a^2 - b^2} \times x^{b^2 - c^2} \times x^{c^2 - a^2} \\\\ \end{align*}

When bases are same, powers are added $x^m \times x^n = x^{m+n}$:

\begin{align*} &= x^{(a^2 - b^2) + (b^2 - c^2) + (c^2 - a^2)} \\\\ &= x^{a^2 - a^2 - b^2 + b^2 - c^2 + c^2} \\\\ &= x^0 \\\\ &= 1 = \text{RHS} \end{align*}

Hence proved.

Q10. Find the value of $x$ if: $2^{x - 5} \times 5^{x - 4} = 5$
[3 Marks]
Solution:

We can rewrite the equation to group similar terms. Break down $5^{x-4}$ to match the exponent $x-5$.

\begin{align*} 2^{x - 5} \times 5^{(x - 5) + 1} &= 5 \\\\ 2^{x - 5} \times 5^{x - 5} \times 5^1 &= 5 \\\\ \end{align*}

Combine the terms with the same exponent using $a^m \times b^m = (ab)^m$:

\begin{align*} (2 \times 5)^{x - 5} \times 5 &= 5 \\\\ 10^{x - 5} &= \frac{5}{5} \\\\ 10^{x - 5} &= 1 \end{align*}

We know that any non-zero number to the power of 0 is 1. Thus, $1 = 10^0$.

\begin{align*} 10^{x - 5} &= 10^0 \\\\ \end{align*}

Equating the powers:

\begin{align*} x - 5 &= 0 \\\\ x &= 5 \end{align*}
SECTION C (5 Marks Each)
Q11. In a morning walk, three persons step off together. Their steps measure 80 cm, 85 cm, and 90 cm respectively. What is the minimum distance each should walk so that all can cover the same distance in complete steps? Express your final answer in meters.
[5 Marks]
Solution:

The minimum distance required so that each person covers the same distance in complete steps will be the LCM of their individual step lengths (80 cm, 85 cm, and 90 cm).

Step 1: Find prime factors.

\begin{align*} 80 &= 2^4 \times 5 \\\\ 85 &= 5 \times 17 \\\\ 90 &= 2 \times 3^2 \times 5 \end{align*}

Step 2: Calculate LCM.

The LCM is the product of the highest powers of all prime factors occurring in the numbers.

\begin{align*} \text{LCM} &= 2^4 \times 3^2 \times 5 \times 17 \\\\ &= 16 \times 9 \times 5 \times 17 \\\\ &= 144 \times 85 \\\\ &= 12240 \text{ cm} \end{align*}

Step 3: Convert into meters.

\begin{align*} \text{Distance in meters} &= \frac{12240}{100} \text{ m} \\\\ &= 122.4 \text{ meters} \end{align*}

The minimum distance each should walk is 122.4 meters.

Q12. Simplify the following expression completely using the laws of exponents:

$\left(\frac{25}{9}\right)^{-5/2} \times \left(\frac{27}{8}\right)^{2/3} \div \left(\frac{8}{27}\right)^{-1/3}$
[5 Marks]
Solution:

Let's simplify each term individually before combining them.

Term 1:

\begin{align*} \left(\frac{25}{9}\right)^{-5/2} &= \left( \left(\frac{5}{3}\right)^2 \right)^{-5/2} = \left(\frac{5}{3}\right)^{-5} = \left(\frac{3}{5}\right)^5 = \frac{3^5}{5^5} = \frac{243}{3125} \end{align*}

Term 2:

\begin{align*} \left(\frac{27}{8}\right)^{2/3} &= \left( \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^3 \right)^{2/3} = \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{3^2}{2^2} = \frac{9}{4} \end{align*}

Term 3:

\begin{align*} \left(\frac{8}{27}\right)^{-1/3} &= \left( \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^3 \right)^{-1/3} = \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{-1} = \frac{3}{2} \end{align*}

Now substitute these back into the original expression:

\begin{align*} &= \left(\frac{243}{3125}\right) \times \left(\frac{9}{4}\right) \div \left(\frac{3}{2}\right) \\\\ \end{align*}

Convert the division into multiplication by taking the reciprocal:

\begin{align*} &= \left(\frac{243}{3125}\right) \times \left(\frac{9}{4}\right) \times \left(\frac{2}{3}\right) \\\\ \end{align*}

Simplify by canceling out common factors:

\begin{align*} &= \frac{243}{3125} \times \frac{9 \times 2}{4 \times 3} \\\\ &= \frac{243}{3125} \times \frac{3}{2} \\\\ &= \frac{729}{6250} \end{align*}
Q13. If $x = 3 + 2\sqrt{2}$, determine the value of $x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2}$.
[5 Marks]
Solution:

Step 1: Find the value of $\frac{1}{x}$ by rationalizing the denominator.

\begin{align*} \frac{1}{x} &= \frac{1}{3 + 2\sqrt{2}} \\\\ &= \frac{1}{3 + 2\sqrt{2}} \times \frac{3 - 2\sqrt{2}}{3 - 2\sqrt{2}} \\\\ &= \frac{3 - 2\sqrt{2}}{(3)^2 - (2\sqrt{2})^2} \\\\ &= \frac{3 - 2\sqrt{2}}{9 - 8} \\\\ &= 3 - 2\sqrt{2} \end{align*}

Step 2: Find the value of $\left(x + \frac{1}{x}\right)$.

\begin{align*} x + \frac{1}{x} &= (3 + 2\sqrt{2}) + (3 - 2\sqrt{2}) \\\\ x + \frac{1}{x} &= 6 \end{align*}

Step 3: Square both sides to find $x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2}$.

\begin{align*} \left(x + \frac{1}{x}\right)^2 &= 6^2 \\\\ \end{align*}

Using the identity $(a+b)^2 = a^2 + b^2 + 2ab$:

\begin{align*} x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2} + 2\left(x\right)\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) &= 36 \\\\ x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2} + 2 &= 36 \\\\ x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2} &= 36 - 2 \\\\ x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2} &= 34 \end{align*}
