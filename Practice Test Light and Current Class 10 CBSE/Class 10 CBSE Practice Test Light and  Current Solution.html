SECTION A (2 Marks Each)
Q1.
Equivalent resistance across diameter of closed circle wire of resistance R.
[2 Marks]
Solution:

When the wire of resistance $R$ is bent into a circle, the two semicircles across any diameter form two separate parallel paths.

The resistance of each semicircle is $\frac{R}{2}$.

Since they are in parallel: $\frac{1}{R_{eq}} = \frac{1}{R/2} + \frac{1}{R/2} = \frac{2}{R} + \frac{2}{R} = \frac{4}{R}$.

Final Answer: $R_{eq} = \frac{R}{4}$

Q2.
New angle of reflection if mirror rotated by $10^\circ$.
[2 Marks]
Solution:

Initial angle of incidence $i = 45^\circ$. If the mirror is rotated by an angle $\theta$, the normal rotates by $\theta$. Thus, the angle of incidence changes by $\theta$.

New angle of incidence $i' = 45^\circ \pm 10^\circ$. (Depending on direction of rotation, it becomes $55^\circ$ or $35^\circ$).

According to laws of reflection, Angle of Reflection = Angle of Incidence.

Final Answer: $55^\circ$ or $35^\circ$

Q3.
Power consumed by $1100\text{W}, 220\text{V}$ iron operated on $110\text{V}$.
[2 Marks]
Solution:

First, find the resistance of the iron using $P = \frac{V^2}{R}$.

$R = \frac{V^2}{P} = \frac{220 \times 220}{1100} = 44 \Omega$.

Now, calculate new power with $V' = 110\text{V}$:

$P' = \frac{(V')^2}{R} = \frac{110 \times 110}{44} = \frac{12100}{44} = 275 \text{ W}$.

(Shortcut: Voltage is halved, so Power becomes $1/4$th. $1100 / 4 = 275\text{W}$).

Final Answer: $275 \text{ W}$

Q4.
Maximum bending of light among Media A, B, C.
[2 Marks]
Solution:

Bending of light depends on the difference in refractive index between two adjacent media.

Optical density is highest in Medium C ($n=2.42$).

Maximum bending occurs when light travels between the media with the greatest difference in refractive index. Here, light emerging from Medium C ($2.42$) to Air ($1.00$) experiences the greatest change in optical density.

Final Answer: Between Medium C and Air.

Q5.
Which is thicker: Copper or Manganin wire of equal length and resistance?
[2 Marks]
Solution:

We know $R = \rho \frac{l}{A}$. Since $R$ and $l$ are constant for both wires, Area ($A$) is directly proportional to resistivity ($\rho$).

Given $\rho_{\text{manganin}} \gt \rho_{\text{copper}}$, it follows that $A_{\text{manganin}} \gt A_{\text{copper}}$.

Final Answer: Manganin wire is thicker.

SECTION B (3 Marks Each)
Q6.
Wire length for $10\Omega$ and resistance change on diameter doubling.
[3 Marks]
Solution:

Given: $d = 0.5 \text{ mm} = 5 \times 10^{-4} \text{ m}$, $\rho = 1.6 \times 10^{-8} \Omega\cdot\text{m}$, $R = 10 \Omega$.

Area $A = \frac{\pi d^2}{4} = \frac{3.14 \times (5 \times 10^{-4})^2}{4} = \frac{3.14 \times 25 \times 10^{-8}}{4} = 19.625 \times 10^{-8} \text{ m}^2$.

Length $l = \frac{R \cdot A}{\rho} = \frac{10 \times 19.625 \times 10^{-8}}{1.6 \times 10^{-8}} = \frac{196.25}{1.6} = 122.6 \text{ m}$.

If diameter is doubled, Area becomes 4 times ($A \propto d^2$). Since $R \propto 1/A$, the new resistance becomes $\frac{1}{4}$ of original.

Final Answer: Length = $122.6 \text{ m}$. New Resistance = $2.5 \Omega$.

Q7.
Virtual image by concave mirror. Find focal length and position.
[3 Marks]
Solution:

Given: $h_o = 3 \text{ cm}$, $u = -8 \text{ cm}$, $h_i = +4.5 \text{ cm}$ (virtual implies erect).

Magnification $m = \frac{h_i}{h_o} = \frac{4.5}{3} = +1.5$.

Also $m = -\frac{v}{u} \implies 1.5 = -\frac{v}{-8} \implies v = 1.5 \times 8 = +12 \text{ cm}$ (Image is behind the mirror).

Mirror formula: $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{12} + \frac{1}{-8} = \frac{2 - 3}{24} = \frac{-1}{24}$.

Final Answer: Focal length $f = -24 \text{ cm}$. Image position $v = 12 \text{ cm}$ behind mirror.

Q8.
Number of electrons per second and work done moving $5\text{C}$ across $15\text{V}$.
[3 Marks]
Solution:

Current $I = \frac{Q}{t} \implies Q = I \times t$. For $1\text{A}$ in $1\text{s}$, $Q = 1 \text{ C}$.

Number of electrons $n = \frac{Q}{e} = \frac{1}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}} = 6.25 \times 10^{18}$ electrons.

Work done $W = V \times Q = 15 \text{ V} \times 5 \text{ C} = 75 \text{ J}$.

Final Answer: $n = 6.25 \times 10^{18}$. $W = 75 \text{ J}$.

Q9.
Verify Snell's Law observation for glass slab.
[3 Marks]
Solution:

According to Snell's Law, refractive index $n = \frac{\sin i}{\sin r}$.

Case 1: $n = \frac{\sin 60^\circ}{\sin 40^\circ} = \frac{0.866}{0.642} \approx 1.34$.

Case 2: $n = \frac{\sin 40^\circ}{\sin 28^\circ} = \frac{0.642}{0.469} \approx 1.36$.

The standard refractive index of typical crown glass is approximately $1.5$. Neither observation is perfectly accurate for standard glass, but Case 2 is slightly closer to a consistent ratio in practical low-index glass/water scenarios. However, the student's readings show a lack of precision, likely yielding a refractive index around $1.35$ rather than $1.5$.

Conclusion: Both observations imply a medium less dense than typical glass (closer to water's $1.33$). Based purely on consistency, Case 2 ($1.36$) is a more likely empirical reading.

Q10.
Ratio of heat produced in series vs parallel combinations.
[3 Marks]
Solution:

Let the resistance of each wire be $R$.

Series resistance $R_s = R + R = 2R$. Heat $H_s = \frac{V^2}{R_s} \times t = \frac{V^2}{2R} \times t$.

Parallel resistance $R_p = \frac{R}{2}$. Heat $H_p = \frac{V^2}{R_p} \times t = \frac{V^2}{(R/2)} \times t = \frac{2V^2}{R} \times t$.

Ratio $\frac{H_s}{H_p} = \frac{V^2/2R}{2V^2/R} = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{4}$.

Final Answer: $1:4$

Q11.
Concave lens position and magnification.
[3 Marks]
Solution:

For a concave lens, $f$ is negative. Image is always virtual, erect, and on the same side, so $v$ is negative.

$f = -15 \text{ cm}$, $v = -10 \text{ cm}$.

Lens Formula: $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} \implies \frac{1}{-15} = \frac{1}{-10} - \frac{1}{u}$

$\frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{-10} + \frac{1}{15} = \frac{-3 + 2}{30} = \frac{-1}{30} \implies u = -30 \text{ cm}$.

Magnification $m = \frac{v}{u} = \frac{-10}{-30} = \frac{1}{3} = +0.33$.

Final Answer: Object distance $30 \text{ cm}$. Magnification $+0.33$.

Q12.
Percentage change in resistance on stretching by 20%.
[3 Marks]
Solution:

Original length $l$, Original Area $A$. $R = \rho\frac{l}{A}$.

New length $l' = l + 0.20l = 1.2l$.

Since volume is constant, $A \cdot l = A' \cdot l' \implies A' = \frac{A \cdot l}{1.2l} = \frac{A}{1.2}$.

New Resistance $R' = \rho \frac{1.2l}{A/1.2} = 1.44 \rho\frac{l}{A} = 1.44 R$.

Percentage increase $= \frac{1.44R - R}{R} \times 100\% = 44\%$.

Resistivity depends only on material and temperature, so it remains unchanged.

Final Answer: Resistance increases by $44\%$. Resistivity remains unchanged.

Q13.
Convex mirror rear-view image calculations.
[3 Marks]
Solution:

Given: $R = +3.00 \text{ m} \implies f = +1.50 \text{ m}$. $u = -5.00 \text{ m}$.

Mirror Formula: $\frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f} \implies \frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{1.50} - \frac{1}{-5.00} = \frac{1}{1.50} + \frac{1}{5.00}$.

$\frac{1}{v} = \frac{10}{15} + \frac{3}{15} = \frac{13}{15} \implies v = \frac{15}{13} \approx +1.15 \text{ m}$.

Magnification $m = -\frac{v}{u} = -\frac{1.15}{-5.00} = +0.23$.

Final Answer: Image is virtual, erect, and diminished (0.23 times). Formed $1.15 \text{ m}$ behind mirror.

Q14.
Circuit diagram calculations (Equivalent R, Current, PD).
[4 Marks]
Solution:

(a) The $8 \Omega$ and $12 \Omega$ resistors are in parallel.

$\frac{1}{R_p} = \frac{1}{8} + \frac{1}{12} = \frac{3 + 2}{24} = \frac{5}{24} \implies R_p = 4.8 \Omega$.

Q15.
Concave mirror forming magnified real and virtual images.
[4 Marks]
Solution:

(a) Real image magnified 3 times $\implies m = -3$. $u = -20 \text{ cm}$.

$m = -\frac{v}{u} \implies -3 = -\frac{v}{-20} \implies v = -60 \text{ cm}$.

$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{-60} + \frac{1}{-20} = \frac{-1 - 3}{60} = \frac{-4}{60} = \frac{-1}{15} \implies f = -15 \text{ cm}$.

(b) Virtual image magnified 3 times $\implies m = +3$.

$m = -\frac{v}{u'} \implies 3 = -\frac{v}{u'} \implies v = -3u'$.

$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u'} \implies \frac{1}{-15} = \frac{1}{-3u'} + \frac{1}{u'} = \frac{-1 + 3}{3u'} = \frac{2}{3u'}$.

$3u' = -30 \implies u' = -10 \text{ cm}$.

Final Answer: (a) $f = -15 \text{ cm}$. (b) Object should be placed at $10 \text{ cm}$.

Q17.
Concave mirror shifting magnification from -1/2 to -1/5.

SECTION D (6 Marks Each)
Q18.
Ohm's Law, Parallel Derivation, and Meter placement.
[6 Marks]
Solution:

(a) Ohm's Law: The current flowing through a metallic conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference applied across its ends, provided temperature and physical conditions remain constant. $V \propto I \implies V = IR$.

(b) Parallel Derivation: In parallel, potential difference $V$ across each resistor is same. Total current $I = I_1 + I_2 + I_3$.

Using Ohm's Law ($I = V/R$): $\frac{V}{R_p} = \frac{V}{R_1} + \frac{V}{R_2} + \frac{V}{R_3}$. Canceling $V$ gives $\frac{1}{R_p} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3}$.

(c) Meters: Ammeter has very low resistance. It is placed in series to measure total current without altering circuit resistance. Voltmeter has infinite/very high resistance. It is placed in parallel to draw negligible current while measuring PD. If an ammeter is connected in parallel to a battery, its low resistance causes a massive short-circuit current to flow, which will instantly burn out the ammeter.

Q20.
Number of parallel resistors and custom resistor combinations.
[6 Marks]
Solution:

(a) Total resistance required $R_{eq} = \frac{V}{I} = \frac{220}{5} = 44 \Omega$.

If $n$ resistors of $176 \Omega$ are in parallel, $R_{eq} = \frac{176}{n}$.

$44 = \frac{176}{n} \implies n = \frac{176}{44} = 4$. Thus, 4 resistors are required.

(b) Given three $6 \Omega$ resistors.

(i) To get $9 \Omega$: Connect two $6 \Omega$ resistors in parallel, and one in series with this combination.

Parallel part: $\frac{1}{R_p} = \frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{2}{6} \implies R_p = 3 \Omega$. Total $R = 3 + 6 = 9 \Omega$.

(ii) To get $4 \Omega$: Connect two $6 \Omega$ resistors in series, and one in parallel with this combination.

Series part: $R_s = 6 + 6 = 12 \Omega$. Total $R_p$: $\frac{1}{R_{total}} = \frac{1}{12} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1+2}{12} = \frac{3}{12} \implies R_{total} = 4 \Omega$.

Q21.
Absolute refractive index, speed of light, and relative RI.
[6 Marks]
Solution:

(a) Absolute Refractive Index is the ratio of the speed of light in a vacuum ($c$) to the speed of light in the given medium ($v$). $n = \frac{c}{v}$.

(b) $n_g = 1.50$, $c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$. Speed in glass $v = \frac{c}{n_g} = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{1.50} = 2 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$.

(c) Refractive index of diamond w.r.t water $= \frac{n_d}{n_w} = \frac{2.42}{1.33} \approx 1.82$.

Because the relative refractive index is greater than 1, it indicates that water is optically rarer than diamond, meaning light travels faster in water than in diamond.

Q22.
Lamps in parallel and series: Power, Current, and Brightness.
[6 Marks]
Solution:

(a) Since they are in parallel across $220\text{V}$, they operate at rated power.

Total Power $P_{total} = 100\text{W} + 60\text{W} = 160\text{W}$.

Total Current $I = \frac{P_{total}}{V} = \frac{160}{220} = 0.727 \text{ A}$.

(b) Using $R = \frac{V^2}{P}$:

$R_{100} = \frac{220^2}{100} = 484 \Omega$. $R_{60} = \frac{220^2}{60} \approx 806.67 \Omega$. Thus, the $60\text{W}$ lamp has higher resistance.

(c) If connected in series, the same current $I$ flows through both. The power dissipated as heat/light is given by $P = I^2R$.

Since $R_{60} \gt R_{100}$, the power dissipated by the $60\text{W}$ lamp ($I^2 \times R_{60}$) is greater than that of the $100\text{W}$ lamp ($I^2 \times R_{100}$). Therefore, in a series connection, the $60\text{W}$ lamp will glow brighter.
