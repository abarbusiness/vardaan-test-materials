SECTION A

Q1. Factorise by taking out the highest common factor: \( 12x^2y^3 - 21x^3y^2 \)
[2 Marks]
Step 1: Find the Highest Common Factor (HCF) of the numerical coefficients \(12\) and \(21\), which is \(3\).
Step 2: Find the HCF of the literal coefficients. The lowest power of \(x\) is \(x^2\), and for \(y\) is \(y^2\). So, common variable factor is \(x^2y^2\).
Taking out the overall common factor \( 3x^2y^2 \):
$$ = 3x^2y^2 \left( \frac{12x^2y^3}{3x^2y^2} - \frac{21x^3y^2}{3x^2y^2} \right) $$ $$ = 3x^2y^2(4y - 7x) $$
Final Answer: \( 3x^2y^2(4y - 7x) \) (2 Marks for correct factorization)
Q2. Factorise the given expression completely: \( a(x + y) + b(x + y) \)
[2 Marks]
Step 1: Observe that the binomial \( (x + y) \) is common to both terms.
Taking out \( (x + y) \) as the common factor:
$$ = (x + y)(a + b) $$
Final Answer: \( (x + y)(a + b) \) (2 Marks)
Q3. Factorise using the identity for the difference of two squares: \( x^2 - 81 \)
[2 Marks]
Step 1: Express both terms as perfect squares.
$$ x^2 - 81 = (x)^2 - (9)^2 $$
(1 Mark for rewriting as squares)
Step 2: Use the identity \( a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b) \).
$$ = (x - 9)(x + 9) $$
Final Answer: \( (x - 9)(x + 9) \) (1 Mark)
Q4. Factorise the quadratic trinomial: \( p^2 - p - 42 \)
[2 Marks]
Step 1: We need to split the middle term (\(-1p\)) into two terms whose sum is \(-1\) and whose product is \(-42\).
The numbers are \(-7\) and \(+6\).
$$ = p^2 - 7p + 6p - 42 $$
(1 Mark for correct splitting)
Step 2: Group the terms and factorise.
$$ = p(p - 7) + 6(p - 7) $$ $$ = (p - 7)(p + 6) $$
Final Answer: \( (p - 7)(p + 6) \) (1 Mark)
Q5. Evaluate using factorization: \( (9.8)^2 - (0.2)^2 \)
[2 Marks]
Step 1: Apply the difference of squares identity: \( a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b) \).
$$ = (9.8 - 0.2)(9.8 + 0.2) $$
(1 Mark for applying identity)
Step 2: Simplify the brackets.
$$ = (9.6) \times (10.0) $$ $$ = 96 $$
Final Answer: 96 (1 Mark)

SECTION B

Q6. Factorise by grouping the terms appropriately: \( ax + bx - ay - by \)
[3 Marks]
Step 1: Group the first two terms together and the last two terms together.
$$ = (ax + bx) - (ay + by) $$
(1 Mark for correct grouping with sign consideration)
Step 2: Take out common factors from each group.
$$ = x(a + b) - y(a + b) $$
(1 Mark for extracting common terms)
Step 3: Take out the common binomial factor \( (a + b) \).
$$ = (a + b)(x - y) $$
Final Answer: \( (a + b)(x - y) \) (1 Mark)
Q7. Factorise completely: \( 16a^4 - 81b^4 \)
[3 Marks]
Step 1: Express as a difference of squares.
$$ = (4a^2)^2 - (9b^2)^2 $$ $$ = (4a^2 - 9b^2)(4a^2 + 9b^2) $$
(1.5 Marks for first factorization)
Step 2: Notice that the first bracket is again a difference of squares: \( (2a)^2 - (3b)^2 \). The second bracket cannot be factorised further with real numbers.
$$ = ((2a)^2 - (3b)^2)(4a^2 + 9b^2) $$ $$ = (2a - 3b)(2a + 3b)(4a^2 + 9b^2) $$
Final Answer: \( (2a - 3b)(2a + 3b)(4a^2 + 9b^2) \) (1.5 Marks for complete factorization)
Q8. Factorise by splitting the middle term: \( 2x^2 + 7x + 6 \)
[3 Marks]
Step 1: Find two numbers whose product is \( 2 \times 6 = 12 \) and whose sum is \( 7 \).
The numbers are \( 4 \) and \( 3 \).
(1 Mark for identifying correct numbers)
Step 2: Split the middle term and factorise by grouping.
$$ = 2x^2 + 4x + 3x + 6 $$ $$ = 2x(x + 2) + 3(x + 2) $$
(1 Mark for grouping correctly)
$$ = (x + 2)(2x + 3) $$
Final Answer: \( (x + 2)(2x + 3) \) (1 Mark)
Q9. Factorise the expression: \( (x - y)^2 - 4(x - y) - 21 \)
[3 Marks]
Step 1: Let \( (x - y) = a \). The expression becomes:
$$ a^2 - 4a - 21 $$
(1 Mark for correct substitution)
Step 2: Factorise by splitting the middle term. Find two numbers that multiply to \(-21\) and add to \(-4\). These are \(-7\) and \(+3\).
$$ = a^2 - 7a + 3a - 21 $$ $$ = a(a - 7) + 3(a - 7) $$ $$ = (a - 7)(a + 3) $$
(1 Mark for factorizing substituted equation)
Step 3: Substitute back \( (x - y) \) for \( a \).
$$ = (x - y - 7)(x - y + 3) $$
Final Answer: \( (x - y - 7)(x - y + 3) \) (1 Mark)
Q10. Factorise using standard identities: \( 25(a - b)^2 - 16(a + b)^2 \)
[3 Marks]
Step 1: Express as a difference of two squares.
$$ = [5(a - b)]^2 - [4(a + b)]^2 $$
(1 Mark for setting up identity form)
Step 2: Apply \( X^2 - Y^2 = (X - Y)(X + Y) \).
$$ = [5(a - b) - 4(a + b)][5(a - b) + 4(a + b)] $$
(1 Mark for applying identity)
Step 3: Expand the inner brackets and simplify.
$$ = [5a - 5b - 4a - 4b][5a - 5b + 4a + 4b] $$ $$ = (a - 9b)(9a - b) $$
Final Answer: \( (a - 9b)(9a - b) \) (1 Mark)

SECTION C

Q11(a). Factorise completely: \( x^4 - y^4 \)
[2.5 Marks]
Express as a difference of squares:
$$ = (x^2)^2 - (y^2)^2 $$ $$ = (x^2 - y^2)(x^2 + y^2) $$
(1.5 Marks)
Factorise the first bracket further:
$$ = (x - y)(x + y)(x^2 + y^2) $$
Final Answer: \( (x - y)(x + y)(x^2 + y^2) \) (1 Mark)
Q11(b). Factorise: \( x^2 - 1 - 2a - a^2 \)
[2.5 Marks]
Group the last three terms and take negative sign common:
$$ = x^2 - (1 + 2a + a^2) $$ $$ = x^2 - (a^2 + 2a + 1) $$
(1 Mark for identifying perfect square trinomial)
Recognise the perfect square: \( (a + 1)^2 = a^2 + 2a + 1 \)
$$ = x^2 - (a + 1)^2 $$
Apply difference of squares identity:
$$ = [x - (a + 1)][x + (a + 1)] $$ $$ = (x - a - 1)(x + a + 1) $$
Final Answer: \( (x - a - 1)(x + a + 1) \) (1.5 Marks)
Q12(a). Factorise the quadratic expression: \( 3x^2 - 14x + 8 \)
[2.5 Marks]
Find two numbers whose product is \( 3 \times 8 = 24 \) and whose sum is \(-14\). The numbers are \(-12\) and \(-2\).
(1 Mark for splitting middle term correctly)
$$ = 3x^2 - 12x - 2x + 8 $$ $$ = 3x(x - 4) - 2(x - 4) $$
(0.5 Marks for correct grouping)
$$ = (x - 4)(3x - 2) $$
Final Answer: \( (x - 4)(3x - 2) \) (1 Mark)
Q12(b). Area of rectangle is \( 2x^2 + 9x - 5 \). Find possible expressions for length and breadth.
[2.5 Marks]
Since Area = Length \( \times \) Breadth, we need to factorise the area polynomial.
We need two numbers that multiply to \( 2 \times -5 = -10 \) and add up to \( 9 \). The numbers are \( 10 \) and \( -1 \).
(1 Mark for logic and splitting)
$$ \text{Area} = 2x^2 + 10x - x - 5 $$ $$ = 2x(x + 5) - 1(x + 5) $$ $$ = (x + 5)(2x - 1) $$
(1 Mark for complete factorization)
Therefore, the dimensions represent the length and breadth.
Final Answer: Length and breadth are \( (x + 5) \) and \( (2x - 1) \) or vice-versa. (0.5 Mark)
Q13(a). Factorise by rearranging and grouping the terms: \( xy(z^2 + 1) + z(x^2 + y^2) \)
[2.5 Marks]
First, expand the brackets to remove current grouping:
$$ = xyz^2 + xy + zx^2 + zy^2 $$
(0.5 Marks for expansion)
Rearrange terms to form new groups. Let's group the 1st with the 3rd, and the 2nd with the 4th.
$$ = (zx^2 + xyz^2) + (zy^2 + xy) $$
(1 Mark for new valid grouping)
Take out common factors from each new group:
$$ = zx(x + yz) + y(yz + x) $$
Note that \( (x + yz) \) is the same as \( (yz + x) \). Take it out as a common factor.
$$ = (x + yz)(zx + y) $$
Final Answer: \( (x + yz)(zx + y) \) (1 Mark)
Q13(b). Evaluate using the algebraic formula for the difference of two squares: \( 105 \times 95 \)
[2.5 Marks]
Express both numbers in terms of a common base (100).
$$ = (100 + 5)(100 - 5) $$
(1 Mark for proper setup)
Apply the identity: \( (a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2 \) where \( a = 100 \) and \( b = 5 \).
$$ = (100)^2 - (5)^2 $$
(0.5 Marks)
Calculate the squares and subtract:
$$ = 10000 - 25 $$ $$ = 9975 $$
Final Answer: 9975 (1 Mark for final calculation)
