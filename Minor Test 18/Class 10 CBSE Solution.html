SECTION A

Q1. Name two metals that form amphoteric oxides. Write the balanced chemical equation for the reaction of one of these oxides with sodium hydroxide.
[2 Marks]
1. Metals forming amphoteric oxides: Aluminium (Al) and Zinc (Zn). (1 Mark)
2. Chemical Equation: Reaction of Aluminium oxide with Sodium hydroxide:
$$ Al_2O_3(s) + 2NaOH(aq) \rightarrow 2NaAlO_2(aq) + H_2O(l) $$
(Sodium aluminate and water are formed) (1 Mark)
Alternative (Zinc): \( ZnO + 2NaOH \rightarrow Na_2ZnO_2 + H_2O \) (Sodium zincate)
Q2. When calcium is dropped in water, it starts floating, whereas potassium catches fire immediately. Give scientific reasons to explain these observations.
[2 Marks]
Calcium floating: The reaction of calcium with water is less violent. The heat evolved is not sufficient for the hydrogen gas to catch fire. Instead, the bubbles of hydrogen gas formed stick to the surface of the calcium metal, making it buoyant and causing it to float. (1 Mark)
Potassium catching fire: The reaction of potassium with water is highly exothermic and violent. The huge amount of heat generated instantly ignites the hydrogen gas evolved, causing it to catch fire immediately. (1 Mark)
Q3. Give reasons: (i) Iodine is a non-metal, yet it is lustrous. (ii) Sodium metal is always kept immersed in kerosene oil.
[2 Marks]
(i) Iodine: It is an exception among non-metals. Because of its large atomic size and molecular structure, its electrons interact with light in a way that gives it a metallic shine (lustre). (1 Mark)
(ii) Sodium in Kerosene: Sodium is highly reactive. If kept in the open, it reacts vigorously with atmospheric oxygen and moisture, and can catch fire. To prevent accidental fires and protect it from air/moisture, it is stored immersed in kerosene oil. (1 Mark)
Q4. What is anodising? State one major advantage of this process in everyday life.
[2 Marks]
Definition: Anodising is the process of forming a thick oxide layer of aluminium. When aluminium is exposed to air, it naturally develops a thin oxide layer. In anodising, an aluminium article is made the anode and electrolysed with dilute sulphuric acid to artificially thicken this protective layer. (1 Mark)
Advantage: This thick aluminium oxide layer greatly enhances the resistance of the metal against corrosion. It can also be dyed easily to give attractive finishes (e.g., in pressure cookers or window frames). (1 Mark)
Q5. A jeweller dipped old and dull gold ornaments in a specific fuming liquid mixture which completely dissolved the gold. Name the liquid mixture and state its chemical composition.
[2 Marks]
Name of liquid: Aqua Regia (Royal Water). (1 Mark)
Chemical Composition: It is a freshly prepared mixture of concentrated hydrochloric acid (HCl) and concentrated nitric acid (\(HNO_3\)) in the ratio of 3:1 respectively. (1 Mark)

SECTION B

Q6. An element 'X' displaces element 'Y' from its salt solution but cannot displace element 'Z'. (a) Arrange X, Y, and Z in increasing order of their reactivity. (b) What specific type of chemical reaction is this? (c) Give one practical application.
[3 Marks]
(a) Order of reactivity: Since X displaces Y, X is more reactive than Y (X > Y). Since X cannot displace Z, Z is more reactive than X (Z > X).
Therefore, increasing order is: Y < X < Z (1 Mark)
(b) Type of reaction: Single Displacement Reaction (or simply Displacement reaction). (1 Mark)
(c) Practical Application: Thermit welding. A more reactive metal like Aluminium displaces Iron from iron oxide, releasing immense heat to melt the iron, which is used to join cracked railway tracks. (1 Mark)
Q7. Explain the formation of magnesium chloride (\(MgCl_2\)) by the transfer of electrons. Also, explain why ionic compounds generally possess high melting points.
[3 Marks]
Formation of \(MgCl_2\):
Electronic configuration: Mg (12) = 2, 8, 2 ; Cl (17) = 2, 8, 7.
Magnesium needs to lose 2 electrons to achieve an octet, becoming \(Mg^{2+}\). Each Chlorine atom needs 1 electron to complete its octet, becoming \(Cl^{-}\).
Mg \(\rightarrow\) \(Mg^{2+}\) + \(2e^-\)
2Cl + \(2e^-\) \(\rightarrow\) 2\(Cl^-\)
Therefore, one Mg atom transfers its 2 valence electrons to two Cl atoms (one to each).
$$ \text{Electron dot transfer: } \cdot Mg \cdot \xrightarrow{\text{gives 1 } e^- \text{ each}} 2 \left[:\ddot{Cl}\cdot\right] \rightarrow [Mg]^{2+} \left[:\ddot{Cl}:\right]^{-}_2 $$
(2 Marks for correct explanation and transfer representation)
Reason for high melting point: Ionic compounds have strong electrostatic forces of attraction between oppositely charged ions. A significant amount of heat energy is required to break these strong inter-ionic bonds. (1 Mark)
Q8. Copper does not react with dilute \(HCl\) but reacts with dilute \(HNO_3\). Explain why. Also, name two metals that react with very dilute \(HNO_3\) to evolve hydrogen gas.
[3 Marks]
Why Copper does not react with HCl: Copper is placed below hydrogen in the reactivity series. Therefore, it is less reactive than hydrogen and cannot displace hydrogen gas from dilute hydrochloric acid. (1 Mark)
Why Copper reacts with \(HNO_3\): Nitric acid (\(HNO_3\)) is a strong oxidising agent. Even though copper cannot displace hydrogen, \(HNO_3\) oxidises the copper to copper nitrate and gets itself reduced to nitrogen oxides (like \(NO_2\) or NO), rather than evolving \(H_2\) gas. (1 Mark)
Metals evolving \(H_2\) with very dilute \(HNO_3\): Magnesium (Mg) and Manganese (Mn) are the only two common metals that react with very dilute (~5%) nitric acid to evolve hydrogen gas. (1 Mark)

Q10. What is a thermit reaction? Write its balanced chemical equation. State one crucial engineering application where this reaction is heavily utilised.
[3 Marks]
Definition: The reduction of a metal oxide to form metal by using aluminium powder as a reducing agent is highly exothermic. When Iron(III) oxide (\(Fe_2O_3\)) is reduced by aluminium powder, massive heat is evolved, producing molten iron. This specific reaction is known as the thermit reaction. (1 Mark)
Chemical Equation:
$$ Fe_2O_3(s) + 2Al(s) \rightarrow 2Fe(l) + Al_2O_3(s) + \text{Heat} $$
(1 Mark - state symbols like (l) for Fe are important)
Application: Because the iron is produced in a molten state due to extreme heat, this reaction is heavily utilised to join cracked railway tracks or broken machine parts. (1 Mark)

SECTION C

Q11. (a) Zinc in copper sulphate. (b) Silver nitrate in copper vessel. (c) Aluminium for cooking utensils.
[5 Marks]
(a) Zinc in Copper Sulphate:
Zinc is more reactive than copper. It displaces copper from copper sulphate solution.
Observation: The blue colour of the copper sulphate solution gradually fades and becomes colourless (formation of zinc sulphate). A reddish-brown deposit of copper forms on the zinc granules.
$$ Zn(s) + CuSO_4(aq) \rightarrow ZnSO_4(aq) + Cu(s) $$
(2 Marks)
(b) Silver Nitrate in Copper Vessel:
Copper is placed above silver in the reactivity series (Cu > Ag). If silver nitrate (\(AgNO_3\)) is kept in a copper vessel, copper will slowly displace silver from the solution, forming copper nitrate and corroding the vessel. Hence, it spoils both the solution and the container.
$$ Cu(s) + 2AgNO_3(aq) \rightarrow Cu(NO_3)_2(aq) + 2Ag(s) $$
(1.5 Marks)
(c) Aluminium for Utensils:
Though aluminium is highly reactive, it reacts with atmospheric oxygen to form a thin, tough, continuous, and highly protective layer of aluminium oxide (\(Al_2O_3\)) on its surface. This layer prevents further corrosion (rusting) of the metal underneath. Furthermore, Aluminium is a good conductor of heat. (1.5 Marks)
Q12. Electrolytic refining of Copper.
[5 Marks]
(a) Process of Electrolytic Refining:
Electrolytic refining is based on the principle of electrolysis to purify impure metals.
  • Anode (+): A thick block of the impure copper metal.
  • Cathode (-): A thin strip of pure copper metal.
  • Electrolyte: An aqueous solution of the metal salt, i.e., acidified copper sulphate solution (\(CuSO_4\) + dil. \(H_2SO_4\)).
Working: When electric current is passed through the electrolyte, pure copper from the impure anode dissolves into the electrolyte. An equivalent amount of pure copper from the electrolyte gets deposited on the pure cathode. The cathode gets thicker while the anode gets thinner.
At Anode: \( Cu(s) \rightarrow Cu^{2+}(aq) + 2e^- \)
At Cathode: \( Cu^{2+}(aq) + 2e^- \rightarrow Cu(s) \)
(3 Marks)
(b) Insoluble Impurities:
The soluble impurities go into the solution, whereas the insoluble impurities (like gold, silver) settle down at the bottom of the tank directly below the anode. This mud-like collection is called anode mud. (1 Mark)
(c) Other metals purified:
Gold (Au), Silver (Ag), Zinc (Zn), Nickel (Ni). (Any two) (1 Mark)
Q13. Element 'A' (Z=11) and 'B' (Z=8).
[5 Marks]
Identify Elements: A (Z=11) is Sodium (Na) [2, 8, 1]. B (Z=8) is Oxygen (O) [2, 6].
(a) Electron dot structures:
A (Na) has 1 valence electron: \( Na\cdot \)
B (O) has 6 valence electrons: \( :\ddot{O}\cdot \)
Compound Formation (\(Na_2O\)): Two sodium atoms will each transfer one electron to one oxygen atom to complete its octet.
$$ 2 \times Na\cdot + \cdot\ddot{O}\cdot \rightarrow [Na^+]_2 \left[:\ddot{O}:\right]^{2-} $$
(2 Marks)
(b) Nature of bond:
Ionic bond (or Electrovalent bond) because it is formed by the complete transfer of electrons from a metal to a non-metal. The compound is an ionic compound. (1 Mark)
(c) Electricity Conduction:
No. In the solid state, ionic compounds do not conduct electricity because the ions (\(Na^+\) and \(O^{2-}\)) are held together by strong electrostatic forces in a rigid lattice structure. Since the ions are not free to move, they cannot conduct current. (1 Mark)
(d) Behaviour in water:
Being an ionic compound, it is generally soluble in water. Specifically, Sodium oxide reacts with water to form an alkali (Sodium hydroxide solution) which turns red litmus blue. (\(Na_2O + H_2O \rightarrow 2NaOH\)). (1 Mark)
