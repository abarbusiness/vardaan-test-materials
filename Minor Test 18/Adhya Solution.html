Minor Test 18 | Class 6 ICSE | Subject: Chemistry (Water) | Max Marks: 40

SECTION A - DETAILED SOLUTIONS (10 Marks)

Q1. Define: (a) Potable water (b) Transpiration
[2 Marks]
Solution & Explanation:

(a) Potable Water: Water that is clean, free from disease-causing germs and harmful toxic substances, and completely fit/safe for drinking is called potable water.

(b) Transpiration: The process by which green plants release excess water from their aerial parts (leaves) into the atmosphere in the form of water vapour is called transpiration.

Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for defining potable water accurately; 1 Mark for defining transpiration correctly.
Q2. Name gases causing acid rain and its effect on monuments.
[2 Marks]
Solution & Explanation:

Gases Responsible: Sulphur dioxide (\(\text{SO}_2\)) and Nitrogen dioxide (\(\text{NO}_2\)).

Reaction & Formation: These gases react with atmospheric oxygen and water vapour to form sulfuric acid (\(\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4\)) and nitric acid (\(\text{HNO}_3\)), which fall along with rain as acid rain.

Harmful Effect on Monuments: Acid rain corrodes and discolours historical monuments made of marble (\(\text{CaCO}_3\)) and limestone. For example, it is causing the white marble of the Taj Mahal to turn yellow ("marble cancer").

Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for naming sulphur dioxide and nitrogen dioxide; 1 Mark for mentioning the corrosion/yellowing effect on marble/monuments.
Q3. Define Solute and Solvent. Identify both in a sugar solution.
[2 Marks]
Solution & Explanation:

Definitions:

  • Solute: The substance that dissolves in a liquid to form a solution (present in a smaller quantity).
  • Solvent: The liquid or medium in which the solute dissolves (present in a larger quantity).

In a Sugar-Water Solution:

  • Sugar is the Solute.
  • Water is the Solvent.
Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for definitions (0.5 mark each); 1 Mark for correctly identifying sugar as solute and water as solvent.

Aquifer: An aquifer is a huge underground layer of permeable rock, gravel, or sand that holds groundwater trapped between layers of hard rocks.

Devices to Extract Groundwater:

  2. Tubewells / Open Wells
Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for defining aquifer; 1 Mark for listing two valid extraction devices.

SECTION B - DETAILED SOLUTIONS (15 Marks)

Q6. (a) Water as universal solvent (b) Saturated solution and heating effect.
[3 Marks]
Solution & Explanation:

(a) Universal Solvent: Water can dissolve a larger variety and quantity of substances (solids, liquids, and gases) than any other known liquid solvent. Hence, it is called a universal solvent.

(b) Saturated Solution: A solution in which no more solute can dissolve at a specific given temperature is called a saturated solution.

Effect of Heating: When a saturated solution is heated, the solubility of the solute increases. As a result, more solute can be dissolved in the heated solution.

Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for explaining universal solvent; 1 Mark for defining saturated solution; 1 Mark for stating that heating increases solubility allowing more solute to dissolve.
Q7. Three states of water with natural examples.
[3 Marks]
Solution & Explanation:

Water exists naturally in three states:

  1. Solid State: Found as ice and snow on high mountains, glaciers, and polar ice caps.
  2. Liquid State: Found in water bodies such as oceans, seas, rivers, lakes, ponds, and groundwater.
  3. Gaseous State: Found as water vapour (humidity) in the surrounding atmosphere/air.
Marking Scheme: 1 Mark per state along with its correct natural example (3 × 1 = 3 Marks).
Q8. Explain Algal Bloom and oxygen starvation in aquatic animals.
[3 Marks]
Solution & Explanation:

Algal Bloom: The rapid and excessive growth of green aquatic plants called algae on the surface of a water body, forming a dense green layer, is known as an algal bloom.

Mechanism of Oxygen Starvation:

  1. Chemical fertilisers (containing nutrients like nitrogen and phosphorus) washed away from fields enter nearby ponds/lakes.
  2. These nutrients cause algae to multiply excessively, covering the water surface and blocking sunlight from reaching submerged aquatic plants.
  3. When these algae and aquatic plants die, oxygen-consuming bacteria decompose them.
  4. During decomposition, bacteria consume a large portion of the dissolved oxygen present in water, drastically reducing oxygen levels.
  5. As a consequence, fish and other aquatic creatures die due to oxygen starvation.
Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for defining Algal Bloom; 2 Marks for explaining the step-by-step reduction of dissolved oxygen leading to aquatic death.
Q9. Compare Drought and Flood: Causes and Adverse Effects.
[3 Marks]
Solution & Explanation:

1. Drought:

  • Cause: An extended period of months or years with abnormally low or no rainfall in a region.
  • Adverse Effect: Drying up of soil and water sources, loss of crops, severe shortage of food and fodder, malnutrition, and death of livestock.

2. Flood:

  • Cause: Continuous heavy rainfall that causes water levels in rivers, lakes, and ponds to overflow onto surrounding land.
  • Adverse Effect: Submergence and destruction of standing crops, damage to buildings/property, soil erosion/landslides, and spread of waterborne diseases (e.g., cholera, gastroenteritis).
Marking Scheme: 1.5 Marks for Drought (cause + effect); 1.5 Marks for Flood (cause + effect).
Q10. (a) Rainwater Harvesting (b) Two daily habits to conserve water.
[3 Marks]
Solution & Explanation:

(a) Rainwater Harvesting: The technique or process of collecting, filtering, and storing rainwater from rooftops or ground surfaces for future direct use or for recharging underground aquifers.

(b) Household Water Conservation Practices:

  • Turn off tap water tightly while brushing teeth or washing hands instead of letting it run continuously.
  • Use a bucket and mug for bathing instead of taking long showers.
  • Reuse water used for washing fruits and vegetables to water garden plants.
  • Fix leaking pipes and running toilets immediately.
Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for definition of Rainwater Harvesting; 2 Marks for listing two practical water conservation habits.

SECTION C - DETAILED SOLUTIONS (15 Marks)

Q11. Municipal water supply tanks and household purification.
[5 Marks]
Solution & Explanation:

(a) Roles of Tanks in Town Water Treatment:

  1. Loading Tank: Alum (potash alum) is added to the water. Alum attaches to fine, light suspended clay particles, making them heavier and larger so that they settle rapidly during sedimentation.
  2. Filtration Tank: Water passes through thick layers of fine sand, coarse gravel, and charcoal. This removes fine suspended impurities and remaining solid particles.
  3. Chlorination Tank: Chlorine gas or chlorine tablets are added/bubbled through water. Chlorine acts as a disinfectant, killing harmful disease-causing germs and bacteria.

(b) Two Chemical/Household Methods for Safe Drinking Water:

  • Boiling: Boiling water vigorously for 10-15 minutes kills all disease-causing pathogens and germs.
  • Use of RO / Water Filters / Chlorination: Using candle filters or adding chlorine/bleaching powder tablets to purify water.
Marking Scheme: 3 Marks for explaining the three municipal tanks (1 mark each); 2 Marks for stating two home purification methods (1 mark each).
Q12. The Water Cycle in nature and its importance.
[5 Marks]
Solution & Explanation:

(a) Step-by-Step Explanation of the Water Cycle:

  1. Evaporation & Transpiration: Solar heat causes water from oceans, rivers, and lakes to evaporate into water vapour. Plants also release water vapour into the air through transpiration.
  2. Cooling & Condensation: As water vapour rises high into the atmosphere where temperatures are low, it cools down and condenses into tiny liquid water droplets, forming clouds.
  3. Precipitation: When these tiny droplets combine to form bigger, heavier drops, they fall back to the Earth's surface as rain, hail, or snow.
  4. Infiltration & Collection: Rainwater fills rivers, lakes, and oceans. A portion seeps into the soil (infiltration) to replenish groundwater, completing the continuous cycle.

(b) Importance of Water Cycle:

  • It continuously purifies and recycles freshwater naturally, making it available to all living organisms.
  • It plays a vital role in regulating global weather patterns and climate conditions.
Marking Scheme: 3 Marks for step-by-step description of water cycle stages; 2 Marks for stating two valid reasons for its environmental importance.
Q13. Water Pollution: Definition, Causes, and Prevention.
[5 Marks]
Solution & Explanation:

(a) Definitions:

  • Water Pollution: The contamination of natural water bodies (lakes, rivers, oceans, groundwater) by harmful domestic, industrial, or agricultural wastes, making it unfit for use.
  • Water Pollutants: The unwanted, toxic, or harmful substances (like sewage, toxic chemicals, heavy metals, fertilisers) that degrade water quality.

(b) Three Major Sources of Water Pollution:

  1. Untreated Domestic Sewage: Release of untreated household waste and soapy water containing bacteria and harmful germs directly into rivers.
  2. Toxic Industrial Effluents: Factories (paper, leather, battery manufacturing) discharging untreated toxic chemicals like lead, mercury, and arsenic into river waters.
  3. Agricultural Runoff: Excessive use of chemical fertilisers, pesticides, and insecticides being washed into water bodies by rain.

(c) Preventive Measure by Factories:

Factories must set up Effluent Treatment Plants (ETPs) to treat and neutralize poisonous chemicals and remove hazardous toxins from waste water before discharging it into natural water bodies.

Marking Scheme: 1.5 Marks for definitions; 2.5 Marks for listing 3 pollution sources; 1 Mark for suggesting pre-treatment of industrial effluents.
