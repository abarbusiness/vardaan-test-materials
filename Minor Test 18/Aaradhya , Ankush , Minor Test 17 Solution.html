SECTION A

Q1. Find two rational numbers between \(\frac{1}{4}\) and \(\frac{1}{2}\).
[2 Marks]
Step 1: Make the denominators same.
LCM of 4 and 2 is 4. However, to find multiple numbers, let's use a larger equivalent denominator, say 16.
\[ \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1 \times 4}{4 \times 4} = \frac{4}{16} \] \[ \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1 \times 8}{2 \times 8} = \frac{8}{16} \]
Step 2: Pick rational numbers between the numerators.
Rational numbers between \(\frac{4}{16}\) and \(\frac{8}{16}\) are \(\frac{5}{16}, \frac{6}{16}, \frac{7}{16}\).
Answer: \(\frac{5}{16}\) and \(\frac{3}{8}\) (or any other correct equivalents)
Q2. Solve the linear equation for \(x\): \(3x - 5 = 2x + 7\)
[2 Marks]
Transposing variables to one side and constants to the other:
\begin{aligned} 3x - 2x &= 7 + 5 \\ x &= 12 \end{aligned}
Answer: \(x = 12\)
Q3. The angles of a quadrilateral are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4 : 6. Find the measure of the largest angle.
[2 Marks]
Let the angles be \(2x\), \(3x\), \(4x\), and \(6x\).
By angle sum property of a quadrilateral, the sum of all angles is \(360^\circ\).
\begin{aligned} 2x + 3x + 4x + 6x &= 360^\circ \\ 15x &= 360^\circ \\ x &= \frac{360^\circ}{15} = 24^\circ \end{aligned}
The largest angle corresponds to the largest ratio part (6).
Largest angle = \(6 \times 24^\circ = 144^\circ\).
Answer: \(144^\circ\)
Q4. Evaluate using laws of exponents: \((2^{-1} \times 4^{-1}) \div 2^{-2}\)
[2 Marks]
Using the law \(a^{-m} = \frac{1}{a^m}\):
\begin{aligned} \left(\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{4}\right) \div \frac{1}{2^2} &= \left(\frac{1}{8}\right) \div \frac{1}{4} \\ &= \frac{1}{8} \times 4 \\ &= \frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2} \end{aligned}
Answer: \(\frac{1}{2}\)
Q5. Find the product of the algebraic expressions: \((3x + 2y)\) and \((3x - 2y)\).
[2 Marks]
Using the algebraic identity \((a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2\), where \(a = 3x\) and \(b = 2y\):
\begin{aligned} (3x + 2y)(3x - 2y) &= (3x)^2 - (2y)^2 \\ &= 9x^2 - 4y^2 \end{aligned}
Answer: \(9x^2 - 4y^2\)

SECTION B

Q6. A rectangular piece of paper \(11\text{ cm} \times 4\text{ cm}\) is folded without overlapping to make a cylinder of height \(4\text{ cm}\). Find the volume.
[3 Marks]
When folded along its length to form a cylinder of height \(4\text{ cm}\), the length of the paper (\(11\text{ cm}\)) becomes the circumference of the cylinder's base.

Let \(r\) be the radius of the base.
\begin{aligned} 2\pi r &= 11 \\ 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times r &= 11 \\ r &= \frac{11 \times 7}{2 \times 22} = \frac{7}{4}\text{ cm} \end{aligned}
Now, calculate the Volume (\(V\)) = \(\pi r^2 h\):
\begin{aligned} V &= \frac{22}{7} \times \left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^2 \times 4 \\ &= \frac{22}{7} \times \frac{49}{16} \times 4 \\ &= \frac{22 \times 7 \times 4}{16} = \frac{616}{16} = 38.5\text{ cm}^3 \end{aligned}
Answer: \(38.5\text{ cm}^3\)
Q7. The sum of three consecutive multiples of \(8\) is \(888\). Find the multiples.
[3 Marks]
Let the three consecutive multiples of 8 be \(8x\), \(8(x+1)\), and \(8(x+2)\).
According to the question:
\begin{aligned} 8x + 8(x+1) + 8(x+2) &= 888 \\ 8 (x + x + 1 + x + 2) &= 888 \\ 3x + 3 &= \frac{888}{8} = 111 \\ 3x &= 111 - 3 = 108 \\ x &= \frac{108}{3} = 36 \end{aligned}
The multiples are:
  • First: \(8 \times 36 = 288\)
  • Second: \(8 \times 37 = 296\)
  • Third: \(8 \times 38 = 304\)
Answer: \(288\), \(296\), and \(304\)
Q8. Factorise the given expression completely: \(a^4 - b^4\)
[3 Marks]
We use the identity \(x^2 - y^2 = (x - y)(x + y)\).
Rewrite the expression: \(a^4 - b^4 = (a^2)^2 - (b^2)^2\)
\begin{aligned} &= (a^2 - b^2)(a^2 + b^2) \end{aligned}
The term \((a^2 - b^2)\) can be further factorised using the same identity:
\begin{aligned} &= (a - b)(a + b)(a^2 + b^2) \end{aligned}
Answer: \((a - b)(a + b)(a^2 + b^2)\)
Q9. Find the smallest whole number by which \(252\) must be multiplied to get a perfect square. Also, find the square root of the perfect square so obtained.
[3 Marks]
First, find the prime factorization of 252:
\(252 = 2 \times 126 = 2 \times 2 \times 63 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 21 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 7\)
\(252 = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 7\)
Here, the prime factor 7 does not have a pair. Therefore, 252 must be multiplied by 7 to make it a perfect square.

New perfect square = \(252 \times 7 = 1764\).
Square root of 1764 = \(\sqrt{2^2 \times 3^2 \times 7^2} = 2 \times 3 \times 7 = 42\).
Answer: Multiply by 7; Square Root is 42
Q10. Simplify: \(\frac{3^{-5} \times 10^{-5} \times 125}{5^{-7} \times 6^{-5}}\)
[3 Marks]
First, express all composite numbers as prime factors: \(10 = 2 \times 5\), \(125 = 5^3\), \(6 = 2 \times 3\).
\begin{aligned} \text{Expression} &= \frac{3^{-5} \times (2 \times 5)^{-5} \times 5^3}{5^{-7} \times (2 \times 3)^{-5}} \\ &= \frac{3^{-5} \times 2^{-5} \times 5^{-5} \times 5^3}{5^{-7} \times 2^{-5} \times 3^{-5}} \end{aligned}
Now, apply the laws of exponents (cancel common terms and combine powers of same bases):
\begin{aligned} &= \frac{2^{-5}}{2^{-5}} \times \frac{3^{-5}}{3^{-5}} \times \frac{5^{-5} \times 5^3}{5^{-7}} \\ &= 1 \times 1 \times \frac{5^{-2}}{5^{-7}} \\ &= 5^{-2 - (-7)} \\ &= 5^{-2 + 7} = 5^5 \end{aligned}
Answer: \(5^5\) (or \(3125\))

SECTION C

Q11. The parallel sides of a trapezium are \(25\text{ cm}\) and \(13\text{ cm}\). Its non-parallel sides are equal, each being \(10\text{ cm}\). Find the area of the trapezium.
[5 Marks]
Let the trapezium be ABCD with AB || DC. AB = 13 cm and DC = 25 cm. AD = BC = 10 cm (isosceles trapezium).
Draw perpendiculars AM and BN from A and B to DC.
Since ABCD is an isosceles trapezium, the triangles ADM and BCN are congruent.
Thus, \(DM = NC\). Let \(DM = NC = x\).
\begin{aligned} DC &= DM + MN + NC \\ 25 &= x + 13 + x \\ 2x &= 25 - 13 = 12 \\ x &= 6\text{ cm} \end{aligned}
In right-angled \(\Delta ADM\), apply Pythagoras theorem to find height (h = AM):
\begin{aligned} AM^2 + DM^2 &= AD^2 \\ h^2 + 6^2 &= 10^2 \\ h^2 &= 100 - 36 = 64 \\ h &= \sqrt{64} = 8\text{ cm} \end{aligned}
Area of Trapezium = \(\frac{1}{2} \times (\text{Sum of parallel sides}) \times \text{height}\)
\begin{aligned} \text{Area} &= \frac{1}{2} \times (25 + 13) \times 8 \\ &= \frac{1}{2} \times 38 \times 8 = 38 \times 4 = 152\text{ cm}^2 \end{aligned}
Answer: \(152\text{ cm}^2\)
Q12. (i) Prove \((a+b)^2 - (a-b)^2 = 4ab\). (ii) Evaluate \(102^2 - 98^2\). (iii) Simplify \((2x+5)^2 - (2x-5)^2\).
[5 Marks]
Part (i): Proof
\begin{aligned} \text{LHS} &= (a^2 + b^2 + 2ab) - (a^2 + b^2 - 2ab) \\ &= a^2 + b^2 + 2ab - a^2 - b^2 + 2ab \\ &= 4ab = \text{RHS} \text{ (Proved)} \end{aligned}
Part (ii): Evaluation
Using the proven identity, substitute \(a = 100\) and \(b = 2\) (Or directly compare \(102 = a+b\) and \(98 = a-b\) which means \(a=100, b=2\)):
\begin{aligned} 102^2 - 98^2 &= (100+2)^2 - (100-2)^2 \\ &= 4 \times 100 \times 2 = 800 \end{aligned}
Part (iii): Simplification
Using the same identity where \(a = 2x\) and \(b = 5\):
\begin{aligned} (2x+5)^2 - (2x-5)^2 &= 4(2x)(5) \\ &= 40x \end{aligned}
Answer: (i) Proved (ii) 800 (iii) \(40x\)
Q13. A shopkeeper bought a VCR and a TV for ₹8,000 each. Loss of 4% on VCR and profit of 8% on TV. Find total gain or loss %.
[5 Marks]
For the VCR:
Cost Price (CP) = ₹8,000
Loss % = 4%
\begin{aligned} \text{Loss} &= \frac{4}{100} \times 8000 = \text{₹}320 \\ \text{Selling Price (SP) of VCR} &= \text{CP} - \text{Loss} = 8000 - 320 = \text{₹}7680 \end{aligned}
For the TV:
Cost Price (CP) = ₹8,000
Profit % = 8%
\begin{aligned} \text{Profit} &= \frac{8}{100} \times 8000 = \text{₹}640 \\ \text{Selling Price (SP) of TV} &= \text{CP} + \text{Profit} = 8000 + 640 = \text{₹}8640 \end{aligned}
Total Transaction:
\begin{aligned} \text{Total CP} &= 8000 + 8000 = \text{₹}16,000 \\ \text{Total SP} &= 7680 + 8640 = \text{₹}16,320 \end{aligned}
Since Total SP > Total CP, there is a Gain (Profit).
\begin{aligned} \text{Total Profit} &= 16320 - 16000 = \text{₹}320 \\ \text{Profit \%} &= \left(\frac{\text{Total Profit}}{\text{Total CP}} \times 100\right)\% \\ &= \left(\frac{320}{16000} \times 100\right)\% = 2\% \end{aligned}
Answer: 2% Overall Profit (Gain)
