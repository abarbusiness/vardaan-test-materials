SECTION A - DETAILED SOLUTIONS (10 Marks)

Q1. (a) Definition of Heat. (b) SI and CGS units of Heat Energy.
[2 Marks]
Solution & Explanation:

(a) Definition of Heat:

Heat is a form of energy that flows from a body at a higher temperature to a body at a lower temperature due to the temperature difference between them.

(b) Units of Heat Energy:

  • SI Unit: Joule (\(\text{J}\))
  • CGS Unit: Calorie (\(\text{cal}\))
Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for correct definition of heat; 0.5 Mark each for SI unit (\(\text{Joule}\)) and CGS unit (\(\text{Calorie}\)).
Q2. (a) Modes of heat transfer. (b) Mode requiring no material medium.
[2 Marks]
Solution & Explanation:

(a) Three modes of heat transfer:

  1. Conduction
  2. Convection
  3. Radiation

(b) Mode requiring no medium:

Radiation does not require any material medium to transfer heat (it can propagate through a vacuum, e.g., solar radiation reaching Earth).

Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for naming all 3 modes correctly; 1 Mark for identifying Radiation as requiring no medium.

SECTION B - DETAILED SOLUTIONS (15 Marks)

Q6. Differentiation between Conduction and Convection.
[3 Marks]
Solution & Explanation:
Property Conduction Convection
Material Medium Requires a solid/material medium. Requires a fluid (liquid or gas) medium.
Particle Movement Particles vibrate about their fixed positions without actual movement. Heat is transferred by the actual movement of heated particles (convection currents).
Primary State of Matter Occurs mainly in solids. Occurs in liquids and gases.
Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for each valid point of difference (Total 3 Marks).
Q7. Scientific reasoning for thermal expansion & insulation applications.
[3 Marks]
Solution & Explanation:

(a) Gaps in Railway Tracks:

Iron expands on heating during hot summer weather. The small gap provides necessary space for thermal expansion of the iron rails. If no gap is left, the expanding rails would bend or buckle, leading to train derailments and severe accidents.

(b) Wooden or Ebonite Handles:

Wood and ebonite are bad conductors of heat (insulators). When utensils get very hot during cooking, these handles do not allow heat to conduct to our hands, allowing us to hold utensil safely.

Marking Scheme: 1.5 Marks for explanation of railway track expansion gap; 1.5 Marks for explanation of insulating handles.
Q8. Reasons why Air is a Mixture and not a Compound.
[3 Marks]
Solution & Explanation:

Air is considered a mixture because of the following reasons:

  1. Variable Composition: The ratio of constituent gases in air (e.g., water vapour, carbon dioxide) varies from place to place and time to time.
  2. Retains Individual Properties: The component gases retain their unique properties (e.g., oxygen supports burning, carbon dioxide turns lime water milky).
  3. No Chemical Formula or Energy Change: Air cannot be represented by a single chemical formula, and no heat or light is evolved or absorbed during its formation.
  4. Physical Separation: Components of air can be separated by physical methods like fractional distillation of liquid air.
Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for each valid reason provided (Any 3 points = 3 Marks).
Q9. Greenhouse Effect, gases, and Global Warming.
[3 Marks]
Solution & Explanation:

(a) Greenhouse Effect:

It is the natural process in which certain gases in Earth's atmosphere trap heat radiated from Earth's surface, preventing it from escaping into space and keeping the planet warm enough to sustain life.

(b) Greenhouse Gases:

Carbon dioxide (\(\text{CO}_2\)) and Methane (\(\text{CH}_4\)) [or Water Vapour / Nitrous Oxide].

(c) Harmful Consequence of Global Warming:

Melting of polar ice caps and glaciers, leading to a rise in sea levels and coastal flooding.

Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for Greenhouse Effect definition; 1 Mark for naming two gases; 1 Mark for stating a consequence of Global Warming.
Q10. (a) Mechanism of Land Breeze. (b) Smog definition & health hazard.
[3 Marks]
Solution & Explanation:

(a) Land Breeze:

At night, land cools down faster than sea water because specific heat capacity of water is higher. The air over the warmer sea expands, becomes lighter, and rises up. Cool, denser air from the land blows towards the sea to take its place, forming a land breeze.

(b) Smog and its Health Hazard:

Smog is a combination of Smoke and Fog (\(\text{Smog} = \text{Smoke} + \text{Fog}\)).

Health Hazard: It causes severe respiratory problems such as asthma, bronchitis, coughing, and irritation in eyes.

Marking Scheme: 1.5 Marks for explanation of Land Breeze; 1.5 Marks for definition of Smog and health hazard.

SECTION C - DETAILED SOLUTIONS (15 Marks)

Q11. (a) Working of Thermos Flask. (b) Temperature conversion (\(37^\circ\text{C}\) to \(^\circ\text{F}\)).
[5 Marks]
Solution & Explanation:

(a) Minimization of Heat Transfer in a Thermos Flask:

  • Prevention of Conduction & Convection: The double-walled glass vessel has a vacuum between its walls. Since conduction and convection both require a material medium, heat cannot enter or escape by these two modes.
  • Prevention of Radiation: The inner surfaces of glass walls are silvered (highly polished). The inner silvered wall reflects radiation back into liquid, and outer silvered wall reflects external radiation away.
  • Cork Stopper: Made of plastic/cork (insulators) to prevent heat loss from the top opening via conduction and convection.

(b) Temperature Scale Conversion Calculation:

Given: \(C = 37^\circ\text{C}\). Formula: \(\dfrac{C}{5} = \dfrac{F - 32}{9}\)

Substituting \(C = 37\):

\[ \dfrac{37}{5} = \dfrac{F - 32}{9} \]
\[ 7.4 = \dfrac{F - 32}{9} \]
\[ F - 32 = 7.4 \times 9 \]
\[ F - 32 = 66.6 \]
\[ F = 66.6 + 32 = 98.6^\circ\text{F} \]

Answer: \(37^\circ\text{C}\) is equal to \(98.6^\circ\text{F}\).

Marking Scheme: (a) 3 Marks for explaining prevention of conduction, convection, and radiation in Thermos Flask (1 mark each). (b) 2 Marks for step-by-step mathematical conversion to \(98.6^\circ\text{F}\).
Q12. (a) Experiment showing air contains Oxygen for burning. (b) Acid Rain & its impacts.
[5 Marks]
Solution & Explanation:

(a) Candle & Bell Jar Experiment:

  • Procedure: Fix a small lighted candle on a trough filled with colored water. Cover the burning candle with an inverted glass jar or glass beaker.
  • Observation: The candle continues to burn for a few seconds and then gets extinguished. Simultaneously, water level inside the inverted glass jar rises up to approximately \(\dfrac{1}{5}\text{th}\) (\(21\%\)) of the total volume of the jar.
  • Conclusion: The oxygen present inside the jar was consumed during combustion. Once oxygen was completely used up, burning stopped, and water rose to fill the space previously occupied by oxygen gas.

(b) Acid Rain and its Harmful Impacts:

Acid Rain: Rainwater that contains dissolved acidic gases like Sulphur dioxide (\(\text{SO}_2\)) and Nitrogen dioxide (\(\text{NO}_2\)), forming Sulphuric acid (\(\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4\)) and Nitric acid (\(\text{HNO}_3\)).

Harmful Impacts:

  1. Corrodes marble monuments (e.g., Taj Mahal) forming marble cancer: \[ \text{CaCO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightarrow \text{CaSO}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2 \]
  2. Increases soil acidity, damaging crops and aquatic organisms in rivers and lakes.
Marking Scheme: (a) 3 Marks for procedure, observation, and conclusion of candle experiment. (b) 2 Marks for definition of acid rain and 2 harmful impacts.
Q13. (a) Thermal expansion comparison. (b) Ozone layer importance & depletion.
[5 Marks]
Solution & Explanation:

(a) Thermal Expansion in States of Matter:

Thermal Expansion: The increase in dimensions (size, volume, length) of a substance when heated is called thermal expansion.

Increasing order of expansion:

\[ \text{Solids} < \text{Liquids} < \text{Gases} \]

Reason: In solids, intermolecular forces of attraction are very strong, keeping molecules tightly bound. In gases, intermolecular forces are extremely weak, allowing molecules to expand freely upon gaining kinetic energy from heat.

(b) Ozone Layer Importance & Depletion:

  • Ozone Layer (\(\text{O}_3\)): A protective layer of ozone gas present in the stratosphere region of the Earth's atmosphere.
  • Importance: It shields Earth by absorbing harmful Ultraviolet (\(\text{UV}\)) radiation coming from the Sun, preventing skin cancer, cataracts, and crop damage.
  • Pollutant Responsible for Depletion: Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) used in refrigerators, air-conditioners, and aerosol sprays.
Marking Scheme: (a) 3 Marks for thermal expansion definition, correct order, and molecular force reason. (b) 2 Marks for Ozone layer definition, importance, and naming CFCs.
