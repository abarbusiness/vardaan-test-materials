Minor Test 18 - Metals and Non-metals
Class: 10 CBSE Subject: Science Time: 1 Hour Max. Marks: 40
General Instructions:
  • All questions are compulsory.
  • The paper is divided into three sections: A, B, and C.
  • Section A comprises 5 questions of 2 marks each.
  • Section B comprises 5 questions of 3 marks each.
  • Section C comprises 3 questions of 5 marks each.
  • Write your answers neatly and legibly. Use chemical equations where necessary.
SECTION A (10 Marks)(Attempt all questions - 2 Marks Each)
1.
Name two metals that form amphoteric oxides. Write the balanced chemical equation for the reaction of one of these oxides with sodium hydroxide.
2.
When calcium is dropped in water, it starts floating, whereas potassium catches fire immediately. Give scientific reasons to explain these observations.
3.
Give reasons for the following:
(i) Iodine is a non-metal, yet it is lustrous.
(ii) Sodium metal is always kept immersed in kerosene oil.
4.
What is anodising? State one major advantage of this process in everyday life.
5.
A jeweller dipped old and dull gold ornaments in a specific fuming liquid mixture which completely dissolved the gold. Name the liquid mixture and state its chemical composition.
SECTION B (15 Marks)(Short Answer Type - 3 Marks Each)
6.
An element 'X' displaces element 'Y' from its salt solution but cannot displace element 'Z'.
(a) Arrange X, Y, and Z in increasing order of their reactivity.
(b) What specific type of chemical reaction is this?
(c) Give one practical industrial application of highly reactive metals displacing less reactive metals.
7.
Explain the formation of magnesium chloride (\(MgCl_2\)) by the transfer of electrons. Also, explain why ionic compounds generally possess high melting points. (Atomic numbers: Mg = 12, Cl = 17)
8.
Copper does not react with dilute \(HCl\) but reacts with dilute \(HNO_3\). Explain why. Also, name two metals that react with very dilute \(HNO_3\) to evolve hydrogen gas.
9.
Differentiate between the processes of 'Roasting' and 'Calcination' used in metallurgy, supporting your answer with one suitable balanced chemical equation for each.
10.
What is a thermit reaction? Write its balanced chemical equation. State one crucial engineering application where this reaction is heavily utilised.
SECTION C (15 Marks)(Long Answer Type - 5 Marks Each)
11.
Account for the following:
(a) What happens when zinc granules are added to a blue-coloured copper sulphate solution? Write the chemical equation and state the physical observation. (2 marks)
(b) Why is it strictly advised not to store silver nitrate solution in a copper vessel? (1.5 marks)
(c) State the reason why aluminium, despite being a highly reactive metal, is extensively used for making cooking utensils. (1.5 marks)
12.
During the extraction of metals, electrolytic refining is used to obtain metals of high purity.
(a) Explain the process of electrolytic refining of Copper. Clearly identify the materials used as the Anode, the Cathode, and the Electrolyte. (3 marks)
(b) Where do the insoluble impurities collect in this setup, and what is this collection technically called? (1 mark)
(c) Name two other metals (besides copper) that are commonly purified using this method. (1 mark)
13.
An element 'A' (Atomic number 11) reacts with an element 'B' (Atomic number 8) to form a stable compound.
(a) Write the electron dot structures of element A, element B, and the compound formed. (2 marks)
(b) State the exact nature of the chemical bond formed between them. (1 mark)
(c) Will this compound conduct electricity in its solid state? Justify your answer with a valid scientific reason. (1 mark)
(d) How does this compound behave when dissolved in water? (1 mark)
