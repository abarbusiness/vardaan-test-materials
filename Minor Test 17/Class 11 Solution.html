Q1. Which of the following molecules has a non-zero dipole moment despite having polar bonds?
[1 Mark]
Correct Option: (C) $\text{NH}_3$
Detailed Explanation: $\text{BF}_3$ (Trigonal Planar), $\text{CCl}_4$ (Tetrahedral), and $\text{CO}_2$ (Linear) possess completely symmetrical geometries. Their individual bond dipoles cancel each other out vectorially, yielding a net dipole moment $\mu = 0$.
In $\text{NH}_3$, the trigonal pyramidal geometry with one lone pair leads to a resultant bond dipole that reinforces the lone pair orbital dipole, giving a net dipole moment $\mu = 1.47\text{ D} \neq 0$.
Q2. According to Molecular Orbital Theory, what is the bond order of the peroxide ion ($\text{O}_2^{2-}$)?
[1 Mark]
Correct Option: (A) $1.0$
Detailed Explanation: Total electrons in $\text{O}_2^{2-} = 16 + 2 = 18\text{ e}^-$.
MO Configuration: $\sigma 1s^2, \sigma^* 1s^2, \sigma 2s^2, \sigma^* 2s^2, \sigma 2p_z^2, (\pi 2p_x^2 = \pi 2p_y^2), (\pi^* 2p_x^2 = \pi^* 2p_y^2)$.
Number of bonding electrons ($N_b$) = 10, antibonding electrons ($N_a$) = 8.
$$\text{Bond Order} = \frac{N_b - N_a}{2} = \frac{10 - 8}{2} = 1.0$$
Q3. What is the shape of $\text{XeF}_4$ molecule according to VSEPR theory?
[1 Mark]
Correct Option: (B) Square Planar
Detailed Explanation: Central atom $\text{Xe}$ has 8 valence electrons + 4 fluorine bond pairs = 12 electrons (6 electron pairs).
Steric Number = 6 ($sp^3d^2$ hybridisation). Geometry = Octahedral.
With 4 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs ($AB_4E_2$), the two lone pairs occupy axial positions to minimize repulsions ($180^\circ$ apart), resulting in a Square Planar shape.
Q4. According to Fajan's rules, which halide possesses the highest covalent character?
[1 Mark]
Correct Option: (D) $\text{LiI}$
Detailed Explanation: According to Fajan's Rules, covalent character increases with a smaller cation size and a larger anion size.
For lithium halides ($\text{LiF, LiCl, LiBr, LiI}$), the cation $\text{Li}^+$ is identical. Among the anions ($\text{F}^-, \text{Cl}^-, \text{Br}^-, \text{I}^-$), the iodide ion ($\text{I}^-$) has the largest size, making it most easily polarized by $\text{Li}^+$. Hence, $\text{LiI}$ has the maximum covalent character.
Q5. The hybridisation of the central atom in gaseous phosphorus pentachloride ($\text{PCl}_5$) is:
[1 Mark]
Correct Option: (B) $sp^3d$
Detailed Explanation: $\text{P}$ (Group 15) has 5 valence electrons, forming 5 single bonds with chlorine atoms.
Total electron pairs = 5 bond pairs + 0 lone pairs = 5 $\to sp^3d$ hybridisation (Trigonal bipyramidal geometry).
Q6. Calculate the formal charge on the central oxygen atom in Ozone ($\text{O}_3$).
[1 Mark]
Detailed Solution:
Structure of Ozone: $\text{O}_{(1)} = \text{O}_{(2)}^+ - \text{O}_{(3)}^-$, where Central atom is atom (2).
Formula: $\text{Formal Charge} = V - L - \frac{1}{2}S$
Where $V = 6$ (valence $\text{e}^-$), $L = 2$ (lone pair $\text{e}^-$), $S = 6$ (shared bonding $\text{e}^-$).
$$\text{Formal Charge on Central O} = 6 - 2 - \frac{1}{2}(6) = +1$$
Q7. State the type of atomic orbital overlap involved in forming a $\pi$ (pi) bond.
[1 Mark]
Detailed Solution:
A $\pi$ bond is formed by the lateral (sideways) overlap of atomic orbitals ($p-p$ or $d-p$) whose axes are parallel to each other and perpendicular to the internuclear axis.
Q8. Why is the molecule $\text{He}_2$ predicted to be non-existent using molecular orbital theory?
[1 Mark]
Detailed Solution:
Electronic configuration of $\text{He}_2 (4\text{e}^-) = \sigma 1s^2, \sigma^* 1s^2$.
$N_b = 2$, $N_a = 2$.
$$\text{Bond Order} = \frac{N_b - N_a}{2} = \frac{2 - 2}{2} = 0$$ A bond order of zero means no net attractive force holds the atoms together; hence $\text{He}_2$ does not exist.

Detailed Solution:
According to MOT, the valence shell electron configuration of $\text{O}_2$ ends in $(\pi^* 2p_x^1 = \pi^* 2p_y^1)$. The presence of these two unpaired electrons in antibonding molecular orbitals causes $\text{O}_2$ to be paramagnetic.
Q10. Why does water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) have a much higher boiling point than $\text{H}_2\text{S}$?
[1 Mark]
Detailed Solution:
Oxygen is highly electronegative, enabling $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ molecules to form extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonds. Hydrogen sulphide ($\text{H}_2\text{S}$) experiences only weak van der Waals forces because sulfur is less electronegative and larger in size. Significant thermal energy is required to break H-bonds in water, leading to its higher boiling point.
SECTION B — DETAILED SOLUTIONS (10 Marks)
Q11. In $\text{PCl}_5$, why are axial bonds longer and weaker than equatorial bonds?
[2 Marks]
Detailed Solution:
$\text{PCl}_5$ has a trigonal bipyramidal structure with $sp^3d$ hybridisation.
• The three equatorial $\text{P-Cl}$ bonds lie in one plane at bond angles of $120^\circ$.
• The two axial $\text{P-Cl}$ bonds lie perpendicular to the equatorial plane at an angle of $90^\circ$ to the equatorial bonds.
• Axial bond pairs experience greater repulsion from three equatorial bond pairs at $90^\circ$, whereas equatorial bond pairs experience repulsion from only two axial pairs at $90^\circ$.
To minimize repulsion, axial bonds stretch, making them longer ($240\text{ pm}$) and weaker than equatorial bonds ($202\text{ pm}$).
Q12. Steam volatility of $o$-nitrophenol vs $p$-nitrophenol and type of H-bonding.
[2 Marks]
Detailed Solution:
• $o$-Nitrophenol is steam volatile due to intramolecular hydrogen bonding (chelation within the same molecule between $-\text{OH}$ and $-\text{NO}_2$ groups). This reduces intermolecular attraction, lowering its boiling point.
• $p$-Nitrophenol forms intermolecular hydrogen bonding (between adjacent molecules), causing molecular association. It requires higher energy to break these bonds, making it less volatile.
Marking Breakdown: Correct choice + Intramolecular H-bonding reason (1 Mark); Explanation of Intermolecular H-bonding in para-isomer (1 Mark).
Q13. Differences between $\sigma$ (sigma) and $\pi$ (pi) bonds.
[2 Marks]
Detailed Solution:
Property $\sigma$ (Sigma) Bond $\pi$ (Pi) Bond
(i) Mode of Overlap Formed by axial / end-to-end (head-on) overlap along internuclear axis. Formed by lateral / sideways overlap perpendicular to internuclear axis.
(ii) Strength & Rotation Strong bond due to large orbital overlap; free rotation around bond axis is possible. Weaker bond due to smaller extent of overlap; free rotation is restricted.
Q14. Resonance structures of carbonate ion ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$) and average formal charge on oxygen.
[2 Marks]
Detailed Solution:
Carbonate ion exhibits three equivalent resonance structures:
1. $[\text{O}=\text{C}(-\text{O}^-)_2]$     2. $[^-\text{O}-\text{C}(=\text{O})-\text{O}^-]$     3. $[(^-\text{O})_2\text{C}=\text{O}]$

Formal Charge Calculation:
Total negative charge = $-2$ units spread over $3$ oxygen atoms.
$$\text{Average Formal Charge on each Oxygen atom} = \frac{\text{Total Charge}}{\text{Number of Oxygen Atoms}} = \frac{-2}{3} = -0.67$$
Q15. VSEPR explanation for geometry of $\text{BeH}_2$ vs $\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
[2 Marks]
Detailed Solution:
• In $\text{BeH}_2$: Central $\text{Be}$ atom has 2 bond pairs and 0 lone pairs. Repulsion between bond pairs is minimized at $180^\circ$, giving it a Linear geometry.
• In $\text{H}_2\text{O}$: Central $\text{O}$ atom has 2 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs (Total 4 electron pairs, $sp^3$). According to VSEPR theory, lone pair-lone pair ($lp-lp$) repulsion > lone pair-bond pair ($lp-bp$) repulsion > bond pair-bond pair ($bp-bp$) repulsion.
The two lone pairs compress the $\text{H-O-H}$ bond angle from regular tetrahedral $109.5^\circ$ down to $104.5^\circ$, resulting in a Bent / V-shaped geometry.

Q17. Explain dipole moment comparison between $\text{NH}_3$ and $\text{NF}_3$ with vector diagrams.
[3 Marks]
Detailed Solution:
Both molecules have trigonal pyramidal shape with one lone pair on Nitrogen.
• In $\text{NH}_3$: Nitrogen is more electronegative than Hydrogen. Bond dipoles of the three $\text{N-H}$ bonds act in the direction towards Nitrogen. The orbital dipole due to the lone pair on Nitrogen acts in the same upward direction. The resultants add up, producing a large net dipole moment ($\mu = 1.47\text{ D}$).
$$\vec{\mu}_{\text{net}} = \vec{\mu}_{\text{lone pair}} + \vec{\mu}_{\text{bonds}}$$ • In $\text{NF}_3$: Fluorine is more electronegative than Nitrogen. Bond dipoles of the three $\text{N-F}$ bonds act downwards (towards Fluorine). The lone pair orbital dipole acts upwards. Thus, the bond dipoles oppose the lone pair dipole, partially canceling each other out, resulting in a very low net dipole moment ($\mu = 0.24\text{ D}$).
Q18. MOT configuration, Bond order, Magnetic property, and stability of $\text{N}_2$ vs $\text{N}_2^+$.
[3 Marks]
Detailed Solution:
$\text{N}_2$ has 14 electrons ($\le 14\text{e}^-$ configuration energy level sequence):
$$\text{MO Config: } \sigma 1s^2, \sigma^* 1s^2, \sigma 2s^2, \sigma^* 2s^2, (\pi 2p_x^2 = \pi 2p_y^2), \sigma 2p_z^2$$
(i) Bond Order calculation: $N_b = 10$, $N_a = 4$. $$\text{BO} = \frac{10 - 4}{2} = 3.0$$ (ii) Magnetic Behaviour: Diamagnetic (all electrons are paired).
(iii) Stability comparison with $\text{N}_2^+$:
$\text{N}_2^+$ has 13 electrons $\to N_b = 9, N_a = 4 \implies \text{BO} = \frac{9-4}{2} = 2.5$.
Higher bond order corresponds to higher bond dissociation energy and greater stability. Therefore, $\text{N}_2$ is more stable than $\text{N}_2^+$.
Q19. Fajan's Rules and covalent character ordering.
[3 Marks]
Detailed Solution:
Fajan's Rules State:
1. Smaller cation size & higher positive charge $\to$ greater polarizing power.
2. Larger anion size & higher negative charge $\to$ higher polarisability.
3. Pseudo-noble gas configuration ($18\text{ e}^-$ valence shell) cations impart greater covalent character.

Ordering Justification:
(a) $\text{NaCl} < \text{MgCl}_2 < \text{AlCl}_3$: Cation charge increases ($\text{Na}^+ < \text{Mg}^{2+} < \text{Al}^{3+}$) and cation size decreases ($\text{Na}^+ > \text{Mg}^{2+} > \text{Al}^{3+}$). Higher charge density increases polarising power $\to$ Covalent character increases.
(b) $\text{LiF} < \text{LiCl} < \text{LiBr} < \text{LiI}$: Anion size increases ($\text{F}^- < \text{Cl}^- < \text{Br}^- < \text{I}^-$). Larger anions are more easily distorted by $\text{Li}^+$ $\to$ Polarisability and covalent character increase.
Q20. $sp^3d^2$ hybridisation in Sulfur Hexafluoride ($\text{SF}_6$).
[3 Marks]
Detailed Solution:
• In ground state, $\text{S ([Ne]} 3s^2 3p^4)$ has 2 unpaired electrons. In 2nd excited state, one $3s$ and one $3p$ electron are promoted to $3d$ orbitals giving 6 unpaired electrons ($\text{[Ne]} 3s^1 3p_x^1 3p_y^1 3p_z^1 3d_{z^2}^1 3d_{x^2-y^2}^1$).
• Intermixing of one $3s$, three $3p$, and two $3d$ orbitals yields 6 equivalent $sp^3d^2$ hybrid orbitals directed towards corners of an octahedron.
• Geometry & Shape: Regular Octahedral.
• Bond Angles: All $\text{F-S-F}$ bond angles are $90^\circ$ (and $180^\circ$ for trans pairs).
• Equivalence: All 6 $\text{S-F}$ bonds are identical because all corners of a regular octahedron are geometrically equivalent.

Q22. Hybridisation definition, features, and detailed structure of $\text{XeF}_4$.
[5 Marks]
Detailed Solution:
(a) Hybridisation & Salient Features (2 Marks):
Definition: The process of intermixing of atomic orbitals of slightly different energies of the same atom to redistribute their energies, forming a new set of equivalent orbitals of identical shape and energy.
Features:
1. Number of hybrid orbitals produced equals the number of atomic orbitals hybridized.
2. Hybrid orbitals form stronger bonds than pure atomic orbitals.
3. They orient in space in specific directions to minimize electron pair repulsion.

(b) Hybridisation and Structure of $\text{XeF}_4$ (3 Marks):
• Central Xenon atom valence configuration: $5s^2 5p^6$. In 2nd excited state, 2 electrons shift to $5d \to 5s^2 5p_x^2 5p_y^1 5p_z^1 5d_1^1 5d_2^1$.
• Intermixing of one $5s$, three $5p$, and two $5d$ orbitals gives six $sp^3d^2$ hybrid orbitals.
• Steric count: 4 bond pairs + 2 lone pairs.
• Geometry: Octahedral base. The 2 lone pairs occupy axial positions at $180^\circ$ to each other to eliminate $lp-lp$ repulsion at $90^\circ$.
• Resulting molecular shape is Square Planar with $\text{F-Xe-F}$ bond angles of $90^\circ$.
Q23. Hydrogen Bonding: Definition, types, and density anomaly of ice.
[5 Marks]
Detailed Solution:
(a) Definition & Conditions (1.5 Marks):
The electrostatic attractive force binding the Hydrogen atom of one molecule (covalently attached to a highly electronegative atom) to an electronegative atom ($\text{F, O, N}$) of the same or another molecule.
Conditions: (i) High electronegativity of attached atom. (ii) Small atomic size of electronegative element.

(b) Intermolecular vs Intramolecular H-Bonding (2 Marks):
• Intermolecular H-Bonding: Formed between two different molecules of the same or different compounds.
Example: Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}\cdots\text{H-O-H}\cdots\text{H}_2\text{O}$) or Hydrogen Fluoride ($\text{H-F}\cdots\text{H-F}$).
• Intramolecular H-Bonding: Formed within the same single molecule between Hydrogen and an electronegative atom.
Example: $o$-Nitrophenol or Salicylaldehyde.

(c) Anomaly of Ice Floating on Water (1.5 Marks):
In ice structure, each water molecule is tetrahedrally surrounded by four other water molecules through intermolecular hydrogen bonds, forming a 3D open cage-like crystalline lattice with substantial empty interstitial space.
When ice melts into liquid water at $0^\circ\text{C}$, some H-bonds break, causing the cage structure to collapse. Water molecules pack closer together, making liquid water denser than ice. Hence, ice has lower density and floats on water.
