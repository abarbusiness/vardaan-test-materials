MINOR TEST 17: CURRENT ELECTRICITY
Class: 10 CBSE Subject: Science (Physics) Time: 1.5 Hours Max. Marks: 60
General Instructions:
  • The question paper consists of 25 questions divided into 5 sections: A, B, C, D, and E.
  • Section A: Q1 to Q10 are objective type questions carrying 1 mark each.
  • Section B: Q11 to Q15 are short answer type questions carrying 2 marks each.
  • Section C: Q16 to Q20 are short answer type questions carrying 3 marks each.
  • Section D: Q21 to Q23 are long answer type questions carrying 5 marks each.
  • Section E: Q24 & Q25 are case-based/competency questions carrying 5 marks each.
  • Use of calculators is not permitted. Write proper SI units in numericals.
SECTION A (10 Marks)(Objective Type Questions - 1 Mark Each)
1.
The rate of flow of electric charges is called:
(a) Electric potential
(b) Electrical resistance
(c) Electric current
(d) Electric power
2.
A piece of wire of resistance \( R \) is cut into five equal parts. These parts are then connected in parallel. If the equivalent resistance of this combination is \( R' \), then the ratio \( R/R' \) is:
(a) \( 1/25 \)
(b) \( 1/5 \)
(c) \( 5 \)
(d) \( 25 \)
3.
Which of the following terms does not represent electrical power in a circuit?
(a) \( I^2R \)
(b) \( IR^2 \)
(c) \( VI \)
(d) \( V^2/R \)
4.
An electric bulb is rated 220 V and 100 W. When it is operated on 110 V, the power consumed will be:
(a) 100 W
(b) 75 W
(c) 50 W
(d) 25 W
5.
How much work is done in moving a charge of 2 C across two points having a potential difference of 12 V?
(a) 6 J
(b) 24 J
(c) 14 J
(d) 10 J
6.
The resistivity of a given metallic wire depends upon:
(a) its length
(b) its thickness
(c) its shape
(d) nature of material
7.
Ammeter is always connected in ________ in a circuit.
(a) series
(b) parallel
(c) mixed
(d) anywhere
8.
The commercial unit of electrical energy is kilowatt-hour (kWh). 1 kWh is equal to:
(a) \( 3.6 \times 10^5 \text{ J} \)
(b) \( 3.6 \times 10^6 \text{ J} \)
(c) \( 36 \times 10^6 \text{ J} \)
(d) \( 3.6 \times 10^4 \text{ J} \)
Directions for Q9 & Q10: In the following questions, a statement of Assertion (A) is followed by a statement of Reason (R). Choose the correct option.
(a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true but R is false.
(d) A is false but R is true.
9.
Assertion (A): The connecting wires are generally made of copper.
Reason (R): Copper has a very high electrical resistivity.
10.
Assertion (A): Series arrangement is not used for domestic circuits.
Reason (R): In a series circuit, if one component fails, the circuit is broken and none of the components works.
SECTION B (10 Marks)(Short Answer Type - 2 Marks Each)
11.
Define 1 Ohm. Calculate the current flowing through a resistor of \( 5\ \Omega \) when a potential difference of 10 V is applied across its ends.
12.
(a) Why is tungsten used almost exclusively for the filament of electric lamps?
(b) Why are the conductors of electric heating devices, such as bread-toasters and electric irons, made of an alloy rather than a pure metal?
13.
A wire of a given material having length \( l \) and area of cross-section \( A \) has a resistance of \( 4\ \Omega \). What would be the resistance of another wire of the same material having length \( l/2 \) and area of cross-section \( 2A \)?
14.
Draw a schematic diagram of a circuit consisting of a battery of three cells of 2 V each, a \( 5\ \Omega \) resistor, an \( 8\ \Omega \) resistor, and a \( 12\ \Omega \) resistor, and a plug key, all connected in series.
15.
How much current will an electric heater coil draw from a 220 V source, if the resistance of the heater coil is \( 100\ \Omega \)? What will be the current if the same heater is connected to a 110 V source?
SECTION C (15 Marks)(Short Answer Type - 3 Marks Each)
16.
State Ohm's law. Draw a labelled circuit diagram to verify this law in the laboratory, clearly indicating the positive and negative terminals of the battery and the respective meters.
17.
Compute the heat generated while transferring 96,000 coulombs of charge in one hour through a potential difference of 50 V.
18.
In the given circuit diagram, \( R_1 = 10\ \Omega, R_2 = 40\ \Omega, R_3 = 30\ \Omega, R_4 = 20\ \Omega, \) and \( R_5 = 60\ \Omega \). A 12 V battery is connected to the arrangement. Calculate:
(a) The total resistance in the circuit.
(b) The total current flowing in the circuit.
19.
Give reason for the following:
(a) Why is an ammeter always connected in series in a circuit?
(b) How does the resistance of a wire vary with its area of cross-section?
(c) Why is a voltmeter connected in parallel with the component across which potential difference is to be measured?
20.
An electric iron of resistance \( 20\ \Omega \) takes a current of 5 A. Calculate the heat developed in 30 s. Also, calculate the electric power consumed by the iron.
SECTION D (15 Marks)(Long Answer Type - 5 Marks Each)
21.
(a) Deduce the expression for the equivalent resistance of the parallel combination of three resistors \( R_1, R_2 \) and \( R_3 \).
(b) Several electric bulbs designed to be used on a 220 V electric supply line, are rated 10 W. How many lamps can be connected in parallel with each other across the two wires of 220 V line if the maximum allowable current is 5 A?
22.
(a) State Joule's law of heating and express it mathematically.
(b) Two conducting wires of the same material and of equal lengths and equal diameters are first connected in series and then parallel in a circuit across the same potential difference. Find the ratio of heat produced in series and parallel combinations.
23.
(a) What is electrical resistivity? Write its SI unit.
(b) A copper wire has a diameter of 0.5 mm and a resistivity of \( 1.6 \times 10^{-8}\ \Omega\text{ m} \). What will be the length of this wire to make its resistance \( 10\ \Omega \)? (Take \( \pi = 3.14 \))
(c) How much does the resistance change if the diameter is doubled? Explain.
SECTION E (10 Marks)(Case Based/Competency Questions - 5 Marks Each)
24.
Case Study 1: V-I Characteristics
A student sets up a circuit to study the relationship between the potential difference (V) across a nichrome wire and the current (I) flowing through it. He records multiple readings and plots a graph between V and I, which comes out to be a straight line passing through the origin.

(i) What fundamental law of electricity is verified by this straight-line graph? (1 Mark)
(ii) If the resistance of the nichrome wire used is \( 10\ \Omega \), calculate the current flowing through it when the potential difference across it is 5 V. (2 Marks)
(iii) If the experiment is performed for a long time and the wire gets heated significantly, will the V-I graph still remain a straight line? What happens to the resistance of the wire upon heating and why? (2 Marks)
25.
Case Study 2: Domestic Electric Circuits
In our homes, we receive supply of electric power through a main supply. Household circuits are always connected in parallel combinations to ensure that appliances operate correctly and independently.

(i) Give one primary advantage of connecting electrical appliances in parallel rather than in series. (1 Mark)
(ii) A television is rated 200 W and operates on a 220 V supply. Calculate the current drawn by it. (2 Marks)
(iii) In a household, three tube lights of 40 W each and two fans of 60 W each are used for 5 hours a day. Calculate the electrical energy consumed in 30 days and the total cost if the rate of electricity is Rs. 5.00 per kWh. (2 Marks)
