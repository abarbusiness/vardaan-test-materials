DETAILED SOLUTIONS: Minor Test 17 (Class 7 CBSE - Soil)
Class: 7 CBSE Subject: Science Total Marks: 40
Q1. Define soil based on its natural occurrence and composition.
[2 Marks]
Answer:
Soil can be defined as the naturally occurring, loose covering of broken rock particles and decaying organic matter (humus) on the surface of the Earth that is capable of supporting plant life.
Q2. State what term is used for the intermediate particles of soil that are neither very rough nor too smooth. Give one main characteristic feature of this soil type.
[2 Marks]
Answer:
- Term used: Silt.
- Characteristic feature: Silt particles have intermediate size (between sand and clay) and moderate water retention capacity.
Q3. Define the term 'Cohesion' in relation to soil properties.
[2 Marks]
Answer:
Cohesion is defined as the tendency of similar particles to stick to one another due to internal attractive forces present within the soil matrix.
Q4. Why is earthworm known as 'nature's ploughman'?
[2 Marks]
Answer:
Earthworms burrow through the soil, which helps in aerating the soil roots, mixing organic waste, and turning over the soil naturally. Hence, they are referred to as 'nature's ploughman'.
Q5. Name any two main causes of soil pollution.
[2 Marks]
Answer:
1. Excessive use of chemical fertilizers and pesticides.
2. Dumping of non-biodegradable wastes like plastics and industrial effluents.
Q6. State the meaning of the term 'soil'. Explain why top soil is considered so valuable and delicate.
[3 Marks]
Answer:
Soil is the loose surface layer of Earth supporting plant life, made of rock particles and humus.
- Why valuable and delicate: It is the most productive layer containing essential nutrients and humus, but it takes roughly 500 years to produce just less than an inch of topsoil, meaning it cannot be replaced within a human lifespan if eroded.
Q7. List the four main agents of physical weathering responsible for soil formation.
[3 Marks]
Answer:
The four main physical agents of weathering are:
1. Wind
2. Water
3. Temperature fluctuations
4. Organic acids (or action of plant roots)
Q8. Give one significant difference between each of the following: (a) Sand and Silt (b) Infiltration rate and Percolation rate
[3 Marks]
Answer:
(a) Sand vs Silt: Sand particles are coarse, large, and gritty with high drainage, whereas silt particles are finer, intermediate in size, and smoother with better water holding capacity.
(b) Infiltration vs Percolation: Infiltration refers to the initial absorption/soaking of water into the top surface of soil, whereas percolation refers to the downward movement of water through deeper layers of soil.
Q9. Explain why coconut trees thrive in coastal regions where the soil present is mainly sandy.
[3 Marks]
Answer:
Sandy soil has large air spaces, excellent drainage, and allows roots to breathe easily and spread rapidly. Coconut trees are well-adapted to well-drained sandy conditions and tolerate the saline coastal environment effectively.
Q10. What is soil erosion? Mention any two ways by which soil erosion can be avoided.
[3 Marks]
Answer:
- Soil erosion: The removal or washing away of the fertile top layer of soil by agents like wind and flowing water.
- Ways to avoid:
1. Afforestation (planting trees to bind soil roots).
2. Preventing overgrazing by cattle and practicing terrace farming on slopes.
Q11. Classify and explain the categories of soil on the basis of 'texture of the soil'. Name the soil type that can retain the highest amount of water.
[5 Marks]
Answer:
Based on texture, soil is primarily classified into three main types:
1. Sandy Soil: Contains a high proportion of large, coarse particles; has high aeration and rapid drainage, but poor nutrient and water retention.
2. Clayey Soil: Contains a high proportion of fine particles tightly packed together; has very low percolation, high water retention, and becomes sticky when wet.
3. Loamy Soil: A balanced mixture of sand, silt, and clay along with humus; considered the best agricultural soil due to ideal water retention and aeration.
- Highest water retention: Clayey soil retains the highest amount of water because of its extremely fine pores and high capillary action.
Q12. Discuss the overall composition of soil in detail. Explain what components make up a healthy soil mix.
[5 Marks]
Answer:
A healthy soil is a complex heterogeneous mixture composed of several key components:
1. Mineral Particles (~45%): Weathered rock fragments classified as sand, silt, and clay that provide structural framework and inorganic nutrients.
2. Organic Matter / Humus (~5%): Decayed remains of plants and animals that enrich fertility, provide nutrients, and improve texture.
3. Soil Water (~25%): Moisture held within soil pores, acting as a medium for dissolving nutrients absorbed by plant roots.
4. Soil Air (~25%): Gases present in pore spaces crucial for root respiration and survival of soil organisms like earthworms and beneficial bacteria.
Q13. (a) Explain why the type of crop grown in a particular field depends on the type of soil with examples. (3 Marks)
(b) Why is it said that 'plasticity' and 'cohesion' play an important role in the workability of a soil? (2 Marks)
[5 Marks]
Answer:
(a) Crop dependence on soil type: Different crops have specific water and nutrient requirements. For example, paddy (rice) requires clayey soil which holds water efficiently. Wheat and gram grow well in loamy soil which provides balanced drainage and aeration. Cotton thrives best in black/clay-loam soils.
(b) Plasticity and workability: Plasticity allows soil to be molded easily when wet without crumbling, while cohesion gives particles the ability to stick together. Together, these properties determine how easily soil can be tilled, plowed, and shaped by farmers.
