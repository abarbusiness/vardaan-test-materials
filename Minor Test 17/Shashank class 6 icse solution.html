DETAILED SOLUTIONS: Minor Test 17 (Class 6 - Light & Force)
SECTION A (10 Marks)
1. What is meant by the rectilinear propagation of light? Give one natural phenomenon that occurs due to this property.
[2 Marks]
Solution:

Rectilinear propagation of light is the property of light traveling in a straight line in a uniform medium. (1 Mark)

Natural phenomenon: Formation of shadows OR occurrence of eclipses (Solar/Lunar eclipse). (1 Mark)

2. Define force. State its S.I. unit.
[2 Marks]
Solution:

Force is a push or a pull applied on an object that tends to change its state of rest or of motion, or its shape and size. (1 Mark)

The S.I. unit of force is Newton (N). (1 Mark)

3. Differentiate between transparent and translucent materials. Give one example of each.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
  • Transparent materials: Allow light to pass through them completely, so objects on the other side can be seen clearly. Example: Clear glass, pure water. (1 Mark)
  • Translucent materials: Allow only a partial amount of light to pass through them, so objects on the other side appear blurred or fuzzy. Example: Butter paper, frosted glass. (1 Mark)
4. What are non-contact forces? Name any two non-contact forces.
[2 Marks]
Solution:

Non-contact forces are forces that act on an object without any physical contact between the interacting bodies. (1 Mark)

Examples: Gravitational force, Magnetic force, Electrostatic force (Any two = 1 Mark).

5. State any two effects that a force can have on a stationary or moving object.
[2 Marks]
Solution:

A force can (Any two points - 1 Mark each):

  • Move a stationary object.
  • Stop a moving object.
  • Change the speed or direction of a moving object.
SECTION B (15 Marks)
6. Why do we slip easily on a smooth wet floor? Which specific contact force is responsible for helping us walk normally? Explain briefly.
[3 Marks]
Solution:

We slip on a smooth wet floor because the water acts as a lubricant, which heavily reduces the friction between our feet and the floor. (1.5 Marks)

The contact force responsible for helping us walk normally is Frictional force (or Friction). Friction provides the necessary grip that prevents us from slipping backward when we push the ground to move forward. (1.5 Marks)

7. How is a shadow formed? State the three essential things required for the formation of a shadow.
[3 Marks]
Solution:

A shadow is formed when an opaque object comes in the path of light, blocking the light from passing through. Because light travels in a straight line, a dark patch is created behind the object. (1.5 Marks)

Three essential requirements for a shadow are: (1.5 Marks - 0.5 each)

  1. A source of light.
  2. An opaque object to block the light.
  3. A screen (or surface) on which the shadow can form.
8. Identify the type of force acting in the following cases:
(a) A fruit falling from a branch towards the ground.
(b) A bullock pulling a heavy cart.
(c) Tiny bits of paper getting attracted to a plastic ruler rubbed with dry hair.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
  • (a) Gravitational Force (Non-contact force) (1 Mark)
  • (b) Muscular Force (Contact force) (1 Mark)
  • (c) Electrostatic Force (Non-contact force) (1 Mark)
9. Explain the basic principle of a pinhole camera. State the two characteristics of the image formed by it.
[3 Marks]
Solution:

Principle: The pinhole camera works on the principle of rectilinear propagation of light (light travels in a straight line). Light rays from the top and bottom of the object cross each other at the pinhole and strike the screen to form an image. (1.5 Marks)

Characteristics of the image: (1.5 Marks - 0.75 each)

  1. The image is inverted (upside down).
  2. The image is real (can be obtained on a screen).
10. A child is pushing a heavy table, but the table does not move. Has the child applied force? Why is the table not moving despite the applied force?
[3 Marks]
Solution:

Yes, the child has applied force (muscular force). (1 Mark)

The table does not move because the muscular force applied by the child is being opposed and completely balanced by the force of friction acting between the bottom of the table and the floor. Since the applied force is not greater than the maximum static friction, the state of rest does not change. (2 Marks)

SECTION C (15 Marks)
11. (a) With the help of a neat and labeled diagram, explain the formation of a Solar Eclipse.
(b) Why are we advised not to look at a solar eclipse directly with naked eyes?
[5 Marks]
Solution:

(a) Formation of Solar Eclipse:
A solar eclipse occurs when the Moon comes exactly between the Sun and the Earth, blocking the sunlight from reaching the Earth. The Moon casts its shadow on the Earth. People in the shadow region cannot see the Sun partially or completely.
Diagram requirement: Students must draw the Sun, Moon, and Earth in a straight line (in that order), showing rays from the Sun being blocked by the Moon, forming the Umbra (dark inner shadow) and Penumbra (lighter outer shadow) on Earth. (Explanation + Diagram = 3 Marks)

(b) Safety:
Looking directly at a solar eclipse can cause permanent eye damage or blindness because the Sun emits harmful ultraviolet (UV) and infrared rays. Even during an eclipse, the exposed parts of the sun are intensely bright and can severely burn the retina of the eye. (2 Marks)

12. Force cannot be seen, but it can be judged by its effects. Explain any five effects of force on an object. Provide one clear example for each effect.
[5 Marks]
Solution:

Five effects of force are: (1 Mark for each point with example)

  1. Force can make a stationary object move: Pushing a stationary toy car makes it move.
  2. Force can stop a moving object: A goalkeeper catching a football stops its motion.
  3. Force can change the speed of a moving object: Pedaling a bicycle faster increases its speed.
  4. Force can change the direction of motion: A batsman hitting a bowled cricket ball changes its direction.
  5. Force can change the shape and size of an object: Squeezing a lump of dough or stretching a rubber band changes its shape and size.
13. (a) What is meant by the reflection of light? How does a mirror form an image?
(b) Differentiate between luminous and non-luminous objects, giving one example for each.
[5 Marks]
Solution:

(a) Reflection and Mirrors:
The bouncing back of light rays when they hit a smooth, shiny, and polished surface is called the reflection of light. (1.5 Marks)
A mirror forms an image because it has a highly polished and silvered surface. When light from an object strikes the mirror, it reflects the light systematically into our eyes, allowing us to see an upright, virtual image of the object. (1.5 Marks)

(b) Luminous vs Non-luminous: (2 Marks)

  • Luminous objects: Objects that emit their own light. Example: The Sun, a burning candle, a glowing electric bulb.
  • Non-luminous objects: Objects that do not emit their own light but are visible only when light from a luminous source falls on them and reflects to our eyes. Example: The Moon, a book, a wooden table.
