DETAILED SOLUTIONS: CURRENT ELECTRICITY (MINOR TEST 17)

SECTION A

1. The rate of flow of electric charges is called:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (c) Electric current
Explanation: By definition (NCERT Pg 199), electric current is expressed by the amount of charge flowing through a particular area in unit time. Mathematically, \( I = \frac{Q}{t} \).
2. A piece of wire of resistance \( R \) is cut into five equal parts. These parts are then connected in parallel. If the equivalent resistance of this combination is \( R' \), then the ratio \( R/R' \) is:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (d) 25
Explanation: Resistance is directly proportional to length. When cut into 5 equal parts, the resistance of each part becomes \( R_1 = R/5 \).
When connected in parallel, the equivalent resistance \( R' \) is given by:
\[ \frac{1}{R'} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_1} = \frac{5}{R_1} \]
Substituting \( R_1 = R/5 \), we get \( \frac{1}{R'} = \frac{5}{R/5} = \frac{25}{R} \). Therefore, \( \frac{R}{R'} = 25 \). (NCERT Exercise Q1)
3. Which of the following terms does not represent electrical power in a circuit?
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (b) \( IR^2 \)
Explanation: Electrical power is given by \( P = VI \). Applying Ohm's law (\( V = IR \)), we get \( P = (IR)I = I^2R \). Alternatively, substituting \( I = V/R \), we get \( P = V(V/R) = V^2/R \). Therefore, \( IR^2 \) is not a formula for power. (NCERT Exercise Q2)
4. An electric bulb is rated 220 V and 100 W. When it is operated on 110 V, the power consumed will be:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (d) 25 W
Explanation: The resistance of the bulb is constant.
From \( P = \frac{V^2}{R} \), we have \( R = \frac{V^2}{P} = \frac{(220)^2}{100} = 484\ \Omega \).
When operated at 110 V, the new power is \( P' = \frac{(V')^2}{R} = \frac{(110)^2}{484} = \frac{12100}{484} = 25\ \text{W} \).
5. How much work is done in moving a charge of 2 C across two points having a potential difference of 12 V?
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (b) 24 J
Explanation: Potential difference \( V = \frac{W}{Q} \).
Therefore, Work done \( W = V \times Q = 12\ \text{V} \times 2\ \text{C} = 24\ \text{J} \).
6. The resistivity of a given metallic wire depends upon:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (d) nature of material
Explanation: Resistivity (\( \rho \)) is an intrinsic property of the material. It depends only on the nature of the material and its temperature, not on its shape or size (length/thickness).
7. Ammeter is always connected in ________ in a circuit.
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (a) series
Explanation: An ammeter measures current, which remains constant in a series circuit. It has very low resistance so that it does not alter the current flowing through the circuit.
8. The commercial unit of electrical energy is kilowatt-hour (kWh). 1 kWh is equal to:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (b) \( 3.6 \times 10^6 \text{ J} \)
Explanation: \( 1\ \text{kWh} = 1000\ \text{W} \times 3600\ \text{s} = 3,600,000\ \text{J} = 3.6 \times 10^6\ \text{J} \).
9. Assertion (A): The connecting wires are generally made of copper.
Reason (R): Copper has a very high electrical resistivity.
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (c) A is true but R is false.
Explanation: Connecting wires are made of copper because it is a very good conductor of electricity, meaning it has a very low electrical resistivity, not high.
10. Assertion (A): Series arrangement is not used for domestic circuits.
Reason (R): In a series circuit, if one component fails, the circuit is broken and none of the components works.
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
Explanation: In a series circuit, there is only one path for the current to flow. If one appliance stops working (e.g., bulb fuses), the circuit breaks and all other appliances stop working. Hence, parallel arrangement is preferred in domestic circuits.

SECTION B

11. Define 1 Ohm. Calculate the current flowing through a resistor of \( 5\ \Omega \) when a potential difference of 10 V is applied across its ends.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
Definition (1 Mark): 1 Ohm is the resistance of a conductor such that when a potential difference of 1 Volt is applied to its ends, a current of 1 Ampere flows through it.
Calculation (1 Mark):
Given, Resistance \( R = 5\ \Omega \), Potential Difference \( V = 10\ \text{V} \).
From Ohm's law, \( I = \frac{V}{R} \)
\( I = \frac{10}{5} = 2\ \text{A} \).
The current flowing is 2 A.
12. (a) Why is tungsten used almost exclusively for the filament of electric lamps?
(b) Why are the conductors of electric heating devices made of an alloy rather than a pure metal?
[2 Marks]
Solution:
(a) (1 Mark): Tungsten is used because it has a very high melting point (3380 °C) and a high resistivity. It can become very hot and emit light without melting.
(b) (1 Mark): Alloys are used because (i) their resistivity is generally higher than that of their constituent pure metals, and (ii) they do not oxidize (burn) readily at high temperatures.
13. A wire of a given material having length \( l \) and area of cross-section \( A \) has a resistance of \( 4\ \Omega \). What would be the resistance of another wire of the same material having length \( l/2 \) and area of cross-section \( 2A \)?
[2 Marks]
Solution:
For the first wire: \( R_1 = \rho \frac{l}{A} = 4\ \Omega \) (Given) ... [½ Mark]
For the second wire, length \( l' = l/2 \) and Area \( A' = 2A \).
Resistance \( R_2 = \rho \frac{l'}{A'} \) ... [½ Mark]
\[ R_2 = \rho \frac{(l/2)}{(2A)} = \frac{1}{4} \left( \rho \frac{l}{A} \right) \] ... [½ Mark]
Substituting the value of \( \rho \frac{l}{A} \):
\( R_2 = \frac{1}{4} \times 4\ \Omega = 1\ \Omega \). ... [½ Mark]
The resistance of the new wire is \( 1\ \Omega \).

15. How much current will an electric heater coil draw from a 220 V source, if the resistance of the heater coil is \( 100\ \Omega \)? What will be the current if the same heater is connected to a 110 V source?
[2 Marks]
Solution:
Case 1 (1 Mark): Given \( V = 220\ \text{V}, R = 100\ \Omega \).
By Ohm's law, \( I = \frac{V}{R} = \frac{220}{100} = 2.2\ \text{A} \).
Case 2 (1 Mark): The resistance remains same (\( 100\ \Omega \)). New Voltage \( V' = 110\ \text{V} \).
New Current \( I' = \frac{V'}{R} = \frac{110}{100} = 1.1\ \text{A} \).

16. State Ohm's law. Draw a labelled circuit diagram to verify this law in the laboratory.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Statement (1 Mark): Ohm's law states that the potential difference (\( V \)) across the ends of a given metallic wire in an electric circuit is directly proportional to the current (\( I \)) flowing through it, provided its temperature remains the same. (\( V \propto I \) or \( V = IR \)).
Circuit Diagram (2 Marks): Needs battery (or multiple cells), key, ammeter in series, voltmeter in parallel with the resistor, and a variable resistor (rheostat) to change the current. Proper polarities (+/-) must be marked.
17. Compute the heat generated while transferring 96,000 coulombs of charge in one hour through a potential difference of 50 V.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Given: Charge (\( Q \)) = 96,000 C; Potential difference (\( V \)) = 50 V; Time (\( t \)) = 1 hour (not required for direct formula). ... [1 Mark]
We know that work done (\( W \)) in moving a charge \( Q \) through a potential difference \( V \) is converted into heat energy (\( H \)).
Formula: \( H = W = V \times Q \) ... [1 Mark]
\[ H = 50\ \text{V} \times 96000\ \text{C} \] \[ H = 4,800,000\ \text{J} = 4.8 \times 10^6\ \text{J} \]
... [1 Mark]
(Alternative method using \( I = Q/t \) and \( H = VIt \) will yield the exact same correct result.)
18. In the given circuit diagram, find total resistance and total current.

\( V = 12\ \text{V}, R = 18\ \Omega \).

19. Give reasons for the following setup/properties.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
(a) (1 Mark): An ammeter is connected in series because current remains constant in a series circuit, allowing it to measure the entire current flowing through the component. It has very low resistance so it doesn't affect the circuit's current.
(b) (1 Mark): Resistance of a wire is inversely proportional to its area of cross-section (\( R \propto \frac{1}{A} \)). A thicker wire has more area, hence less resistance, because there is more space for electrons to flow, reducing collision frequency.
(c) (1 Mark): A voltmeter is connected in parallel because potential difference is constant across parallel branches. It has very high resistance so that it draws negligible current from the circuit, ensuring an accurate reading.
20. An electric iron of resistance \( 20\ \Omega \) takes a current of 5 A. Calculate the heat developed in 30 s. Also, calculate the electric power consumed.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Given: \( R = 20\ \Omega \), \( I = 5\ \text{A} \), \( t = 30\ \text{s} \).
Heat Developed (2 Marks):
By Joule's law of heating, \( H = I^2Rt \)
\[ H = (5)^2 \times 20 \times 30 = 25 \times 600 = \mathbf{15,000\ \text{J}} \text{ (or 15 kJ)} \]
Power Consumed (1 Mark):
\( P = I^2R = (5)^2 \times 20 = 25 \times 20 = \mathbf{500\ \text{W}} \).

SECTION D

21. (a) Derive parallel equivalent resistance. (b) Number of 10W bulbs in 220V, 5A line.
[5 Marks]
Solution:
(a) Derivation (3 Marks):
Let three resistors \( R_1, R_2, R_3 \) be connected in parallel across a potential difference \( V \).
In a parallel circuit, the voltage \( V \) across each resistor is the same, but the total current \( I \) is the sum of currents through each resistor:
\[ I = I_1 + I_2 + I_3 \]
According to Ohm's law, \( I_1 = \frac{V}{R_1} \), \( I_2 = \frac{V}{R_2} \), and \( I_3 = \frac{V}{R_3} \).
If \( R_p \) is the equivalent resistance of the combination, then \( I = \frac{V}{R_p} \).
Substituting these values into the first equation:
\[ \frac{V}{R_p} = \frac{V}{R_1} + \frac{V}{R_2} + \frac{V}{R_3} \]
Dividing both sides by \( V \):
\[ \frac{1}{R_p} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3} \]
(b) Numerical (2 Marks):
Power of each bulb (\( P \)) = 10 W, Supply voltage (\( V \)) = 220 V.
Maximum allowable current (\( I \)) = 5 A.
Total maximum power the circuit can handle = \( P_{total} = VI = 220 \times 5 = 1100\ \text{W} \).
Let \( n \) be the number of bulbs. Total power = \( n \times 10\ \text{W} \).
\[ n \times 10 = 1100 \implies n = \frac{1100}{10} = \mathbf{110\ \text{bulbs}} \]
22. (a) Joule's law of heating. (b) Ratio of heat in series vs parallel.
[5 Marks]
Solution:
(a) Joule's Law (2 Marks):
It states that the heat produced in a resistor is (i) directly proportional to the square of current for a given resistance, (ii) directly proportional to resistance for a given current, and (iii) directly proportional to the time for which current flows. Mathematically: \( H = I^2Rt \).

(b) Numerical (3 Marks):
Let resistance of each wire be \( R \). Both are identical. Voltage is \( V \).
Series combination: Equivalent resistance \( R_s = R + R = 2R \).
Heat produced in time \( t \) is \( H_s = \frac{V^2}{R_s} t = \frac{V^2}{2R} t \). ... [1 Mark]
Parallel combination: Equivalent resistance \( R_p \) is given by \( \frac{1}{R_p} = \frac{1}{R} + \frac{1}{R} \implies R_p = \frac{R}{2} \).
Heat produced in time \( t \) is \( H_p = \frac{V^2}{R_p} t = \frac{V^2}{R/2} t = \frac{2V^2}{R} t \). ... [1 Mark]
Ratio:
\[ \frac{H_s}{H_p} = \frac{\frac{V^2}{2R} t}{\frac{2V^2}{R} t} = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{4} \]
The ratio is 1:4. ... [1 Mark]
23. (a) Electrical resistivity. (b) Calculate length of wire. (c) Change in resistance if diameter doubled.
[5 Marks]
Solution:
(a) (1 Mark): Electrical resistivity is a fundamental property of a material that quantifies how strongly it resists electric current. It is numerically equal to the resistance of a conductor of unit length and unit cross-sectional area. SI Unit: Ohm-meter (\( \Omega\text{ m} \)).

(b) (2.5 Marks):
Given: \( d = 0.5\ \text{mm} = 0.5 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{m} \implies \text{radius } r = 0.25 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{m} \).
\( \rho = 1.6 \times 10^{-8}\ \Omega\text{ m} \), \( R = 10\ \Omega \).
Area \( A = \pi r^2 = 3.14 \times (0.25 \times 10^{-3})^2 = 3.14 \times 0.0625 \times 10^{-6} = 0.19625 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{m}^2 \).
From \( R = \rho \frac{l}{A} \), we have \( l = \frac{RA}{\rho} \).
\[ l = \frac{10 \times 0.19625 \times 10^{-6}}{1.6 \times 10^{-8}} = \frac{1.9625 \times 10^{-6}}{1.6 \times 10^{-8}} = \frac{196.25}{1.6} \approx \mathbf{122.65\ \text{m}} \]
(c) (1.5 Marks):
Resistance \( R = \rho \frac{l}{\pi (d/2)^2} \implies R \propto \frac{1}{d^2} \).
If the diameter is doubled (\( d' = 2d \)), the new area becomes 4 times the original area.
Therefore, new resistance \( R' = R/4 = 10/4 = \mathbf{2.5\ \Omega} \).
The resistance becomes one-fourth of its original value.

SECTION E (Case Studies)

24. Case Study 1: V-I Characteristics
[5 Marks]
Solution:
(i) (1 Mark): The straight line passing through the origin verifies Ohm's Law, which states that potential difference is directly proportional to current (\( V \propto I \)).

(ii) (2 Marks):
Given \( R = 10\ \Omega \) and \( V = 5\ \text{V} \).
According to Ohm's Law, \( I = \frac{V}{R} \).
\( I = \frac{5}{10} = \mathbf{0.5\ \text{A}} \).

(iii) (2 Marks):
If the wire is heated significantly, the V-I graph will no longer remain a straight line (it will curve towards the voltage axis).
Reason: The resistance of a metallic wire increases with an increase in temperature. This happens because thermal energy increases the kinetic energy and vibration of atoms/ions in the metal, leading to more frequent collisions with the flowing electrons, thereby impeding their flow (increasing resistance).
25. Case Study 2: Domestic Electric Circuits
[5 Marks]
Solution:
(i) (1 Mark): In a parallel connection, if one appliance stops working (or is switched off), all other appliances continue to work normally because each has its own separate path for current. Also, each appliance gets the full source voltage (220 V). (Any one valid point is sufficient).

(ii) (2 Marks):
Power of TV (\( P \)) = 200 W, Voltage (\( V \)) = 220 V.
Using the formula \( P = VI \), the current \( I = \frac{P}{V} \).
\( I = \frac{200}{220} = \frac{20}{22} = \frac{10}{11}\ \text{A} \approx \mathbf{0.91\ \text{A}} \).

(iii) (2 Marks):
Power of 3 tube lights = \( 3 \times 40\ \text{W} = 120\ \text{W} \).
Power of 2 fans = \( 2 \times 60\ \text{W} = 120\ \text{W} \).
Total power consumed = \( 120 + 120 = 240\ \text{W} = 0.24\ \text{kW} \).
Time per day = 5 hours.
Energy consumed per day = Power \(\times\) Time = \( 0.24\ \text{kW} \times 5\ \text{h} = 1.2\ \text{kWh} \).
Energy consumed in 30 days = \( 1.2 \times 30 = \mathbf{36\ \text{kWh}} \text{ (or 36 units)} \).
Total Cost = Energy \(\times\) Rate = \( 36 \times 5 = \mathbf{Rs.\ 180} \).
