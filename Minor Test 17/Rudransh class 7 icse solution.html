(Class 7 ICSE - Triangles & Pythagoras Theorem)
SECTION A (10 Marks)
1. State the 'Angle Sum Property' and the 'Exterior Angle Property' of a triangle.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
Angle Sum Property: The sum of the measures of the three interior angles of a triangle is always equal to \(180^\circ\). (1 Mark)
Exterior Angle Property: The measure of any exterior angle of a triangle is equal to the sum of the measures of its two interior opposite angles. (1 Mark)
2. Is it possible to construct a triangle with side lengths 5 cm, 8 cm, and 14 cm? Give a mathematical reason to support your answer.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
Property: According to the Triangle Inequality Theorem, the sum of the lengths of any two sides of a triangle must be strictly greater than the length of the third side. (1 Mark)
Verification: Adding the two smaller sides:
\( 5 \text{ cm} + 8 \text{ cm} = 13 \text{ cm} \)
Since \(13 \text{ cm} < 14 \text{ cm}\) (the sum of the two smaller sides is less than the third side), it is not possible to construct this triangle. (1 Mark)
3. In \(\triangle ABC\), \(\angle A = 50^\circ\) and \(\angle B = 60^\circ\). Find the measure of the exterior angle formed by producing side \(BC\).
[2 Marks]
Solution:
Let the exterior angle formed by producing side \(BC\) at vertex \(C\) be \(\angle ACD\).
By the Exterior Angle Property:
Exterior \(\angle ACD = \angle A + \angle B\) (1 Mark)
\(\angle ACD = 50^\circ + 60^\circ\)
\(\angle ACD = 110^\circ\)
The measure of the exterior angle is \(110^\circ\). (1 Mark)
4. State the Pythagoras Theorem. For a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse \(c\) and legs \(a\) and \(b\), write the mathematical equation representing the theorem.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
Pythagoras Theorem: In a right-angled triangle, the square of the length of the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the squares of the lengths of the other two sides. (1 Mark)
Equation: If \(c\) is the hypotenuse, and \(a\), \(b\) are the legs, then:
\(c^2 = a^2 + b^2\)
(1 Mark)
5. Determine if a triangle with side lengths 9 cm, 12 cm, and 15 cm is a right-angled triangle. Show your working.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
We check the converse of Pythagoras Theorem, where the longest side (15 cm) is tested as the hypotenuse.
Square of the longest side:
\(15^2 = 225\)
(0.5 Mark)
Sum of the squares of the other two sides:
\(9^2 + 12^2 = 81 + 144 = 225\)
(1 Mark)
Since \(15^2 = 9^2 + 12^2\), the given side lengths satisfy the Pythagoras theorem. Therefore, yes, it is a right-angled triangle. (0.5 Mark)
SECTION B (15 Marks)
6. In \(\triangle PQR\), the exterior angle at \(R\) is \(130^\circ\). If the interior opposite angles \(\angle P\) and \(\angle Q\) are in the ratio \(2:3\), find the measure of all three interior angles of the triangle.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Let \(\angle P = 2x\) and \(\angle Q = 3x\). (0.5 Mark)
By the Exterior Angle Property:
\(\text{Exterior } \angle R = \angle P + \angle Q\)
\(130^\circ = 2x + 3x\)
\(130^\circ = 5x\)
\(x = \frac{130^\circ}{5} = 26^\circ\)
(1.5 Marks)
Calculating interior angles:
\(\angle P = 2 \times 26^\circ = 52^\circ\)
\(\angle Q = 3 \times 26^\circ = 78^\circ\)
Interior \(\angle R = 180^\circ - 130^\circ = 50^\circ\) (linear pair with exterior angle) (1 Mark)

Final Answer: The interior angles are \(52^\circ\), \(78^\circ\), and \(50^\circ\).

7. The lengths of two sides of a triangle are 12 cm and 18 cm. Between what two distinct positive values must the length of the third side fall? Explain the property you used to find the range.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Property Used: Triangle Inequality Theorem, which states that the third side must be less than the sum of the other two sides and greater than their difference. (1 Mark)
Let the third side be \(x\).
Upper limit: \(x < 12 + 18 \implies x < 30 \text{ cm}\) (1 Mark)
Lower limit: \(x > 18 - 12 \implies x > 6 \text{ cm}\) (1 Mark)

Final Answer: The third side must fall between 6 cm and 30 cm (\(6 < x < 30\)).

8. A ladder 13 m long is placed against a wall such that its foot is at a distance of 5 m from the base of the wall. Find the height at which the ladder reaches the wall.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Let the height reached by the ladder be \(h\) meters. The wall, ground, and ladder form a right-angled triangle.
Here, hypotenuse \(c = 13\text{ m}\), base \(b = 5\text{ m}\). (0.5 Mark)
Applying Pythagoras Theorem:
\(h^2 + b^2 = c^2\)
\(h^2 + 5^2 = 13^2\) (1 Mark)
\(h^2 + 25 = 169\)
\(h^2 = 169 - 25 = 144\)
\(h = \sqrt{144} = 12 \text{ m}\)
(1.5 Marks)

Final Answer: The ladder reaches a height of 12 m.

9. In \(\triangle LMN\), \(LM = LN\) (isosceles triangle). If the exterior angle formed at vertex \(N\) is \(110^\circ\), find the measures of all the interior angles of \(\triangle LMN\).
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Since \(LM = LN\), angles opposite to them are equal: \(\angle M = \angle N\) (interior). (0.5 Mark)
Interior \(\angle N = 180^\circ - 110^\circ = 70^\circ\) (linear pair). (1 Mark)
Therefore, \(\angle M = 70^\circ\). (0.5 Mark)
Using Angle Sum Property: \(\angle L + \angle M + \angle N = 180^\circ\)
\(\angle L + 70^\circ + 70^\circ = 180^\circ \implies \angle L + 140^\circ = 180^\circ \implies \angle L = 40^\circ\)
(1 Mark)

Final Answer: \(\angle L = 40^\circ\), \(\angle M = 70^\circ\), \(\angle N = 70^\circ\).

10. A tree breaks due to a storm and the broken part bends so that the top of the tree touches the ground at a distance of 8 m from the base of the tree. If the point where it broke is 6 m above the ground, what was the total original height of the tree before it broke?
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Let the remaining upright trunk be \(p = 6\text{ m}\), base distance from base to top on ground be \(b = 8\text{ m}\), and the broken part be hypotenuse \(h\). (0.5 Mark)
Using Pythagoras Theorem:
\(h^2 = 6^2 + 8^2 = 36 + 64 = 100 \implies h = 10 \text{ m}\)
(1.5 Marks)
Total original height = upright trunk + broken part = \(6\text{ m} + 10\text{ m} = 16\text{ m}\). (1 Mark)

Final Answer: The original height was 16 m.

SECTION C (15 Marks)
11. Find the value of \(x\) and \(y\) in the given figure. Give geometrical reasons for each step.
[5 Marks]
Solution:
Finding \(y\):
Angles \(y\) and \(120^\circ\) form a linear pair on straight line \(BD\).
\(y + 120^\circ = 180^\circ \implies y = 180^\circ - 120^\circ = 60^\circ\)
(2 Marks)
Finding \(x\):
Using the Exterior Angle Property on \(\triangle ABC\):
Exterior angle at \(C\) (\(120^\circ\)) is equal to the sum of its two interior opposite angles (\(\angle A + \angle B\)).
\(x + 55^\circ = 120^\circ \implies x = 120^\circ - 55^\circ = 65^\circ\)
(3 Marks)

Final Answer: \(x = 65^\circ\) and \(y = 60^\circ\).

12. Rohan walks 15 km towards North, then turns West and walks 8 km.
a) Draw a neat labeled sketch.
b) Calculate his shortest distance from the starting point.
c) State the theorem used.
[5 Marks]
Solution:
a) Labeled Sketch: (1.5 Marks)
b) Calculating Shortest Distance: (2.5 Marks)
Shortest distance is hypotenuse \(AC\):
\(AC^2 = 15^2 + 8^2 = 225 + 64 = 289 \implies AC = \sqrt{289} = 17 \text{ km}\)
c) Theorem Used: (1 Mark) Pythagoras Theorem.
13. In a rhombus \(ABCD\), diagonals \(AC\) and \(BD\) are 24 cm and 10 cm respectively.
a) Draw a rough sketch.
b) Find the length of one side.
c) Calculate the perimeter.
[5 Marks]
Solution:
a) Rough Sketch: (1 Mark)
b) Finding length of one side: (3 Marks)
Diagonals bisect at \(90^\circ\). Half diagonals are \(AO = \frac{24}{2} = 12\text{ cm}\) and \(BO = \frac{10}{2} = 5\text{ cm}\).
In right \(\triangle AOB\):
\(AB^2 = 12^2 + 5^2 = 144 + 25 = 169 \implies AB = 13 \text{ cm}\)
c) Calculating perimeter: (1 Mark)
\(\text{Perimeter} = 4 \times \text{side} = 4 \times 13 = 52 \text{ cm}\).
