DETAILED SOLUTIONS: History Minor Test (Turkish Invasions & Delhi Sultanate)
Class 7 ICSE | Max Marks: 40
SECTION A (10 Marks)
Q1. Name the two major rulers belonging to the Ghaznavid and Ghurid dynasties who invaded India from the Northwest.
[2 Marks]
Mahmud of Ghazni and Muhammad Ghori.
Mahmud of Ghazni belonged to the Ghaznavid dynasty and led 17 raids into India.
Muhammad Ghori belonged to the Ghurid dynasty and laid the actual foundation of Turkish rule in India.
Q2. State any two main objectives behind Mahmud of Ghazni’s invasions of India.
[2 Marks]
Plundering wealth and propagating Islam / consolidating power in Central Asia.
1. To plunder the immense wealth (gold, silver, and temple treasures) of India to finance his wars and empire-building in Central Asia.
2. To spread Islam or establish his political supremacy, though wealth acquisition remained his primary driving motive.
Q3. Who was the founder of the Slave Dynasty (the first dynasty of the Delhi Sultanate)? In which year was the Delhi Sultanate established?
[2 Marks]
Qutb-ud-din Aibak | Established in 1206 CE.
Founder: Qutb-ud-din Aibak, a trusted general and former slave of Muhammad Ghori.
Year: The Delhi Sultanate formally began in 1206 CE following Ghori's death.
Q4. Mention the famous historical book written by Al-Biruni that provides valuable information about India during the 11th century.
[2 Marks]
Kitab-ul-Hind (or Tarikh-ul-Hind).
Al-Biruni was a scholar who accompanied Mahmud of Ghazni to India. His book Kitab-ul-Hind gives a comprehensive account of Indian science, philosophy, religion, and social life during the 11th century.
Q5. Name the two historical battles fought between Prithviraj Chauhan and Muhammad Ghori, along with their respective outcomes.
[2 Marks]
First Battle of Tarain (1191) & Second Battle of Tarain (1192).
First Battle of Tarain (1191 CE): Won by Prithviraj Chauhan; Muhammad Ghori was defeated and forced to retreat.
Second Battle of Tarain (1192 CE): Won by Muhammad Ghori; Prithviraj Chauhan was defeated, marking a turning point in Indian history.
SECTION B (15 Marks)
Q6. Give three reasons for the defeat of the Indian Rajput rulers against the invading Turkish forces.
[3 Marks]
Lack of unity, outdated military tactics, and rigid caste structure.
1. Lack of Political Unity: Rajput kingdoms were fragmented and constantly fought amongst themselves, failing to form a united front against foreign invaders.
2. Outdated Warfare: Rajput armies heavily relied on slow-moving elephants, whereas the Turks used swift cavalry and superior archery (mounted archers).
3. Social Divisions: The rigid caste system and feudal structure weakened societal cohesion and limited military recruitment.
Q7. Write a short note on Razia Sultana. Why was her accession to the throne significant in the history of the Delhi Sultanate?
[3 Marks]
First and only Muslim woman ruler of Delhi; broke gender barriers.
Who she was: Razia Sultana was the capable daughter of Sultan Iltutmish. She ruled Delhi from 1236 to 1240 CE.
Significance: Her accession was groundbreaking because Iltutmish believed his sons were unworthy and preferred her over them, proving that ability mattered more than gender. However, she faced severe opposition from Turkish nobles (Chahalgani) who resented being ruled by a woman.
Q8. Explain the term 'Iqta' system introduced during the Delhi Sultanate. Who were called Iqtadars?
[3 Marks]
Land revenue assignment system; military commanders/governors called Iqtadars.
Iqta System: It was a unique land-distribution and administrative system where the Sultan divided the empire into tracts of land called Iqtas.
Iqtadars (Muqtis): The holders or assignees of these tracts were called Iqtadars. Their job was to collect land revenue, maintain law and order, and supply troops to the Sultan when required.
Q9. Briefly describe any three major economic or administrative reforms introduced by Ala-ud-din Khilji.
[3 Marks]
Market control, strict land revenue, and branding of horses (Daag/Chehra).
1. Market Regulation: Fixed strict prices for essential commodities like food grains, cloth, and horses to maintain a large army on low salaries.
2. Land Revenue Assessment: Measured land accurately and increased land tax to 50% of the produce, paid directly to the royal treasury.
3. Military Reforms: Introduced the practice of Daag (branding of horses) and Chehra (descriptive roll of soldiers) to prevent corruption and bogus reviews.
Q10. Mention three architectural or cultural contributions made by the rulers of the Delhi Sultanate.
[3 Marks]
Qutb Minar, Indo-Islamic architecture style, development of Urdu/Hindi languages.
1. Monuments: Construction of iconic structures like the Qutb Minar, Alai Darwaza, and various fortified cities (like Siri).
2. Art & Architecture: Introduction of the Indo-Islamic style, featuring arches, domes, geometrical designs, and calligraphic art.
3. Language & Culture: The interaction between Persian/Arabic and local languages gave birth to new cultural synthesis and linguistic developments.
SECTION C (15 Marks)
Q11. Compare the motives and impacts of Mahmud of Ghazni and Muhammad Ghori on India. How did Muhammad Ghori's campaigns permanently alter the political landscape of Northern India?
[5 Marks]
Mahmud looted and left; Ghori conquered and established permanent rule.
Mahmud of Ghazni: His motive was purely imperialistic and financial—plundering India's riches to build Ghazni. He did not intend to establish an empire in India. His raids left North-Western India politically shattered and economically drained.
Muhammad Ghori: His motive was distinct—he wanted to establish a permanent Islamic empire in India. Following his victory in the Second Battle of Tarain (1192 CE) and Jayachandra's defeat at Chandawar (1194 CE), he left his general Qutb-ud-din Aibak in charge.
Political Impact: Ghori's campaigns ended centuries of Rajput supremacy and permanently anchored Turkish/Muslim political authority in Delhi, setting the stage for the Delhi Sultanate.
Q12. Discuss the reign and policies of Muhammad-bin-Tughlaq. Explain any two of his ambitious experiments that failed miserably due to poor execution.
[5 Marks]
Brilliant scholar but failed experiments: Token Currency & Capital Transfer.
Overview: Muhammad-bin-Tughlaq was one of the most learned and visionary rulers of the Delhi Sultanate, but his radical ideas failed due to poor planning and lack of practical execution.
1. Transfer of Capital (1327 CE): He shifted his capital from Delhi to Daulatabad (in the Deccan) to centrally govern the empire. However, forcing the entire population to march hundreds of miles caused immense suffering, and realizing its impracticality, he ordered a return to Delhi.
2. Introduction of Token Currency (1329-1330 CE): Due to a shortage of silver, he introduced bronze/copper coins with the same face value as silver coins. Since the government failed to prevent the unauthorized minting of coins at home, counterfeit coins flooded the market, wrecking trade and the royal treasury.
Q13. Write short notes on the following dynasties of the Delhi Sultanate:
(a) The Khilji Dynasty (Expansion and control)
(b) The Lodi Dynasty (Significance as the final Afghan ruling house)
[5 Marks]
Khiljis expanded south; Lodis were Afghans defeated by Babur in 1526.
(a) The Khilji Dynasty (1290–1320 CE): Founded by Jalal-ud-din Khilji, it reached its zenith under Ala-ud-din Khilji. Ala-ud-din transformed the Sultanate into an aggressive imperialist power by conquering Malwa, Gujarat, and deep into the Deccan (aided by Malik Kafur). They introduced rigid market controls and centralized military administration.
(b) The Lodi Dynasty (1451–1526 CE): Founded by Bahlul Lodi, this was the first Afghan (non-Turkish) ruling dynasty of the Delhi Sultanate. Sikandar Lodi was its most notable ruler, who founded Agra and made it his secondary capital. The dynasty ended with Ibrahim Lodi, who was defeated by Babur in the First Battle of Panipat in 1526 CE, paving the way for the Mughal Empire.
