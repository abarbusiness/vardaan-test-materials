SECTION A (10 Marks) — Short Answer / Conceptual Questions
Q1. Find the 15th term of the Arithmetic Progression (A.P.): $-10, -6, -2, 2, \dots$
[2 Marks]
Final Answer: $T_{15} = 46$
Given A.P.: $-10, -6, -2, 2, \dots$
First term ($a$) = $-10$
Common difference ($d$) = $T_2 - T_1 = -6 - (-10) = -6 + 10 = 4$
Formula for the $n^{\text{th}}$ term of an A.P. is:
$$T_n = a + (n - 1)d$$
Substituting $n = 15$, $a = -10$, and $d = 4$:
$$T_{15} = -10 + (15 - 1) \times 4$$
$$T_{15} = -10 + 14 \times 4$$
$$T_{15} = -10 + 56 = 46$$
ICSE Board Tip: Always double check the sign when calculating $d = T_2 - T_1$. A common error is writing $d = -6 - 10 = -16$.
Q2. If the $4^{\text{th}}$ and $9^{\text{th}}$ terms of a G.P. are $54$ and $13122$ respectively, find the common ratio ($r$).
[2 Marks]
Final Answer: Common ratio $r = 3$
Let the first term of the G.P. be $a$ and common ratio be $r$.
Formula for $n^{\text{th}}$ term of G.P. is $T_n = a \cdot r^{n-1}$.
Given:
$$T_4 = a \cdot r^3 = 54 \quad \text{--- (Equation 1)}$$
$$T_9 = a \cdot r^8 = 13122 \quad \text{--- (Equation 2)}$$
Dividing Equation (2) by Equation (1):
$$\frac{a \cdot r^8}{a \cdot r^3} = \frac{13122}{54}$$
$$r^5 = 243$$
$$r^5 = 3^5 \implies r = 3$$
Q3. Which term of the Arithmetic Progression $5, 8, 11, 14, \dots$ is equal to $68$?
[2 Marks]
Final Answer: $22^{\text{nd}}$ term
Given A.P.: $5, 8, 11, 14, \dots$
First term ($a$) = $5$, Common difference ($d$) = $8 - 5 = 3$.
Let $T_n = 68$.
$$T_n = a + (n - 1)d = 68$$
$$5 + (n - 1) \times 3 = 68$$
$$3(n - 1) = 68 - 5 = 63$$
$$n - 1 = \frac{63}{3} = 21 \implies n = 22$$
Hence, the $22^{\text{nd}}$ term of the given A.P. is $68$.
Q4. Find the $5^{\text{th}}$ term from the end of the Geometric Progression: $3, 6, 12, \dots, 1536$.
[2 Marks]
Final Answer: $96$
Given G.P.: $3, 6, 12, \dots, 1536$
Last term ($l$) = $1536$, Common ratio ($r$) = $\frac{6}{3} = 2$.
Formula for $n^{\text{th}}$ term from the end of a G.P. is:
$$\text{Term from end} = \frac{l}{r^{n-1}}$$
For $n = 5$:
$$T_{5\text{ (end)}} = \frac{1536}{2^{5-1}} = \frac{1536}{2^4} = \frac{1536}{16} = 96$$
Q5. Find the sum of the first $20$ terms of an A.P. whose $n^{\text{th}}$ term is given by $T_n = 3n + 5$.
[2 Marks]
Final Answer: $S_{20} = 730$
Given $T_n = 3n + 5$.
First term ($a$) = $T_1 = 3(1) + 5 = 8$.
$20^{\text{th}}$ term ($l$) = $T_{20} = 3(20) + 5 = 65$.
Sum formula using first and last terms:
$$S_n = \frac{n}{2}(a + l)$$
$$S_{20} = \frac{20}{2}(8 + 65) = 10 \times 73 = 730$$
SECTION B (15 Marks) — Short Answer Type Questions
Q6. The sum of three consecutive terms in an Arithmetic Progression is $24$ and their product is $440$. Find the three numbers.
[3 Marks]
Final Answer: The numbers are $5, 8, 11$ (or $11, 8, 5$)
Step 1: Assume standard terms
Let three consecutive terms in A.P. be $(a - d)$, $a$, and $(a + d)$.
Step 2: Use Sum Condition
$$(a - d) + a + (a + d) = 24$$
$$3a = 24 \implies a = 8$$
Step 3: Use Product Condition
$$(a - d) \times a \times (a + d) = 440$$
$$a(a^2 - d^2) = 440$$
Substitute $a = 8$:
$$8(8^2 - d^2) = 440$$
$$64 - d^2 = \frac{440}{8} = 55$$
$$d^2 = 64 - 55 = 9 \implies d = \pm 3$$
Step 4: Find the numbers
• If $d = 3$: Numbers are $(8-3), 8, (8+3) \implies \mathbf{5, 8, 11}$.
• If $d = -3$: Numbers are $(8+3), 8, (8-3) \implies \mathbf{11, 8, 5}$.
Q7. Determine the sum of the first $10$ terms of the Geometric Progression: $2, 6, 18, 54, \dots$
[3 Marks]
Final Answer: $S_{10} = 59048$
Given G.P.: $2, 6, 18, 54, \dots$
First term ($a$) = $2$, Common ratio ($r$) = $\frac{6}{2} = 3 > 1$.
Formula for sum of $n$ terms of a G.P. when $r > 1$:
$$S_n = \frac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1}$$
For $n = 10$:
$$S_{10} = \frac{2(3^{10} - 1)}{3 - 1} = \frac{2(3^{10} - 1)}{2} = 3^{10} - 1$$
Since $3^{10} = 59049$:
$$S_{10} = 59049 - 1 = 59048$$
Q8. How many terms of the A.P. $24, 21, 18, \dots$ must be taken so that their sum is $99$? Explain the reason for getting a double answer.
[3 Marks]
Final Answer: $n = 6$ or $n = 11$
Given A.P.: $24, 21, 18, \dots$
$a = 24$, $d = 21 - 24 = -3$, $S_n = 99$.
Formula: $S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n - 1)d]$
$$99 = \frac{n}{2}[2(24) + (n - 1)(-3)]$$
$$198 = n[48 - 3n + 3]$$
$$198 = n[51 - 3n]$$
$$3n^2 - 51n + 198 = 0$$
Dividing by 3:
$$n^2 - 17n + 66 = 0$$
$$(n - 6)(n - 11) = 0 \implies n = 6 \quad \text{or} \quad n = 11$$
Explanation of Double Answer (1 Mark in Board Exam):
The common difference is negative ($d = -3$). The terms from the $7^{\text{th}}$ to the $11^{\text{th}}$ term are $9, 6, 3, 0, -3$. Their sum is $9 + 6 + 3 + 0 + (-3) + (-6) + (-9) = 0$. Hence, adding these 5 terms does not increase or decrease the sum of the first 6 terms.
Q9. If $(x + 1)$, $3x$, and $(4x + 12)$ are three consecutive terms of a Geometric Progression, determine the possible real values of $x$.
[3 Marks]
Final Answer: $x = 4$ or $x = -\frac{3}{5}$
For three numbers $A, B, C$ to be in G.P., $B^2 = A \cdot C$.
Here $A = (x + 1)$, $B = 3x$, $C = (4x + 12)$.
$$(3x)^2 = (x + 1)(4x + 12)$$
$$9x^2 = 4x^2 + 12x + 4x + 12$$
$$9x^2 = 4x^2 + 16x + 12$$
$$5x^2 - 16x - 12 = 0$$
Factorizing the quadratic equation ($5 \times -12 = -60 \implies -20 \times 3$):
$$5x^2 - 20x + 3x - 12 = 0$$
$$5x(x - 4) + 3(x - 4) = 0$$
$$(5x + 3)(x - 4) = 0$$
Thus, $x = 4$ or $x = -\frac{3}{5}$.
Q10. Find three numbers in Geometric Progression such that their sum is $19$ and their product is $216$.
[3 Marks]
Final Answer: The numbers are $4, 6, 9$
Let three terms in G.P. be $\frac{a}{r}, a, ar$.
Product Condition:
$$\left(\frac{a}{r}\right) \times a \times (ar) = 216 \implies a^3 = 216 \implies a = 6$$
Sum Condition:
$$\frac{6}{r} + 6 + 6r = 19 \implies 6\left(\frac{1}{r} + r\right) = 13$$
$$\frac{6(1 + r^2)}{r} = 13 \implies 6r^2 - 13r + 6 = 0$$
Solving quadratic equation ($6 \times 6 = 36 \implies -9 \times -4$):
$$6r^2 - 9r - 4r + 6 = 0 \implies 3r(2r - 3) - 2(2r - 3) = 0$$
$$(3r - 2)(2r - 3) = 0 \implies r = \frac{3}{2} \quad \text{or} \quad r = \frac{2}{3}$$
For $r = \frac{3}{2}$, numbers are $\frac{6}{3/2}, 6, 6\left(\frac{3}{2}\right) \implies \mathbf{4, 6, 9}$.

Q11. (a) $4^{\text{th}}$ term is 3 times first term; $7^{\text{th}}$ term exceeds twice $3^{\text{rd}}$ term by 1.
(b) Loan repayment problem.
[5 Marks]
Final Answers: (a) $a = 3, d = 2$ | (b) $n = 20$ months
Part (a) [2.5 Marks]:
Given $T_4 = 3a \implies a + 3d = 3a \implies 2a = 3d \implies a = \frac{3}{2}d \quad \text{--- (1)}$
Also, $T_7 = 2 T_3 + 1$:
$$a + 6d = 2(a + 2d) + 1$$
$$a + 6d = 2a + 4d + 1 \implies a - 2d = -1 \quad \text{--- (2)}$$
Substituting (1) into (2):
$$\frac{3}{2}d - 2d = -1 \implies -\frac{1}{2}d = -1 \implies d = 2$$
$$a = \frac{3}{2}(2) = 3$$
Part (b) [2.5 Marks]:
Total loan amount ($S_n$) = ₹32,500
First installment ($a$) = ₹200, Monthly increase ($d$) = ₹150.
$$S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2(200) + (n - 1)150] = 32500$$
$$n[400 + 150n - 150] = 65000$$
$$150n^2 + 250n - 65000 = 0$$
Divide by 50:
$$3n^2 + 5n - 1300 = 0$$
$$3n^2 + 65n - 60n - 1300 = 0$$
$$n(3n + 65) - 20(3n + 65) = 0 \implies (n - 20)(3n + 65) = 0$$
Since $n$ cannot be negative, $n = 20$ months.
Q12. (a) Sum of $7 + 77 + 777 + \dots n$ terms.
(b) Proof for $x^2, b^2, y^2$ in A.P.
[5 Marks]
Final Answer: (a) $S_n = \frac{70}{81}(10^n - 1) - \frac{7n}{9}$ | (b) Proof Complete
Part (a) [3 Marks]:
$$S_n = 7 + 77 + 777 + \dots \text{ to } n \text{ terms}$$
$$S_n = 7(1 + 11 + 111 + \dots \text{ to } n \text{ terms})$$
$$S_n = \frac{7}{9}(9 + 99 + 999 + \dots \text{ to } n \text{ terms})$$
$$S_n = \frac{7}{9}[(10 - 1) + (10^2 - 1) + (10^3 - 1) + \dots + (10^n - 1)]$$
$$S_n = \frac{7}{9}\left[(10 + 10^2 + \dots + 10^n) - (1 + 1 + \dots n \text{ times})\right]$$
$$S_n = \frac{7}{9}\left[\frac{10(10^n - 1)}{10 - 1} - n\right] = \frac{70}{81}(10^n - 1) - \frac{7n}{9}$$
Part (b) [2 Marks]:
• $a, b, c$ in A.P. $\implies 2b = a + c \quad \text{--- (1)}$
• $a, x, b$ in G.P. $\implies x^2 = ab \quad \text{--- (2)}$
• $b, y, c$ in G.P. $\implies y^2 = bc \quad \text{--- (3)}$
To prove $x^2, b^2, y^2$ are in A.P., we must show $x^2 + y^2 = 2b^2$.
$$\text{L.H.S.} = x^2 + y^2 = ab + bc = b(a + c)$$
From (1), substitute $(a + c) = 2b$:
$$\text{L.H.S.} = b(2b) = 2b^2 = \text{R.H.S.}$$
Hence, $x^2, b^2, y^2$ are in Arithmetic Progression. (Proved)
Q13. (a) Given $S_n = 2n^2 + 5n$, find $T_n$ and $T_{25}$.
(b) G.P. with $T_3 = 12, T_6 = 96$, find $S_8$.
[5 Marks]
Final Answers: (a) $T_n = 4n + 3, T_{25} = 103$ | (b) $S_8 = 765$
Part (a) [2 Marks]:
We know that $T_n = S_n - S_{n-1}$.
$$S_n = 2n^2 + 5n$$
$$S_{n-1} = 2(n-1)^2 + 5(n-1) = 2(n^2 - 2n + 1) + 5n - 5 = 2n^2 + n - 3$$
Subtracting $S_{n-1}$ from $S_n$:
$$T_n = (2n^2 + 5n) - (2n^2 + n - 3) = 4n + 3$$
For $25^{\text{th}}$ term:
$$T_{25} = 4(25) + 3 = 103$$
Part (b) [3 Marks]:
Given $T_3 = a \cdot r^2 = 12$ and $T_6 = a \cdot r^5 = 96$.
$$\frac{T_6}{T_3} = \frac{a \cdot r^5}{a \cdot r^2} = \frac{96}{12} \implies r^3 = 8 \implies r = 2$$
Substitute $r = 2$ into $T_3$:
$$a(2^2) = 12 \implies 4a = 12 \implies a = 3$$
Calculate sum of first 8 terms ($S_8$):
$$S_8 = \frac{a(r^8 - 1)}{r - 1} = \frac{3(2^8 - 1)}{2 - 1} = 3(256 - 1) = 3 \times 255 = 765$$
