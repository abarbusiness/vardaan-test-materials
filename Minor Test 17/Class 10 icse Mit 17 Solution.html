SECTION A (10 Marks)
Q1. Define the electromotive force (e.m.f.) of a cell. Write its SI unit.
[2 Marks]
Electromotive Force (e.m.f.): It is defined as the work done in moving a unit positive charge once around the complete circuit (both inside and outside the cell). Alternatively, it is the potential difference between the terminals of a cell when no current is drawn from it (open circuit).   (1 Mark)
SI Unit: The SI unit of e.m.f. is Volt (V) or Joules per Coulomb ($\text{J C}^{-1}$).   (1 Mark)
Q2. A metallic wire of resistance $R$ is stretched to double its original length. What will be its new resistance? Justify your answer.
[2 Marks]
Let original length be $l$ and original cross-sectional area be $A$.
Original Resistance $R = \rho \frac{l}{A}$.
When the wire is stretched, its volume remains constant. Therefore, if length is doubled ($l' = 2l$), the cross-sectional area becomes half ($A' = \frac{A}{2}$).   (1 Mark)
New Resistance $R' = \rho \frac{l'}{A'} = \rho \frac{2l}{\frac{A}{2}} = 4 \left( \rho \frac{l}{A} \right) = 4R$.
Final Answer: The new resistance will be 4 times the original resistance.   (1 Mark)
Q3. State Ohm's Law. Name one material or component that is a non-ohmic conductor.
[2 Marks]
Ohm's Law: It states that the electric current flowing through a metallic conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference applied across its ends, provided physical conditions (like temperature, mechanical strain, etc.) remain constant.   (1 Mark)
Non-Ohmic Conductor: Light Emitting Diode (LED), Junction Diode, Transistor, or Filament of an electric bulb. (Any one)   (1 Mark)
Q4. Calculate the equivalent resistance when two resistors of $3 \Omega$ and $6 \Omega$ are connected in parallel.
[2 Marks]
For resistors in parallel, the equivalent resistance ($R_p$) is given by:
$\frac{1}{R_p} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2}$   (1 Mark)
$\frac{1}{R_p} = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{2 + 1}{6} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$
Therefore, $R_p = 2 \Omega$.   (1 Mark)
Q5. What are the factors on which the internal resistance of a cell depends? (State any two).
[2 Marks]
The internal resistance of a cell depends on (any two of the following):
  • The surface area of the electrodes (larger area $\rightarrow$ lesser resistance).
  • The distance between the electrodes (larger distance $\rightarrow$ greater resistance).
  • The nature and concentration of the electrolyte.
  • The temperature of the electrolyte (higher temperature $\rightarrow$ lesser resistance).
(1 Mark for each correct point)

Q7. A steady current of $2 \text{ A}$ flows through a circuit for $10 \text{ minutes}$. Calculate the amount of electric charge that flows through the circuit. Also, find the total number of electrons transferred during this time. (Given $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$)
[3 Marks]
Given: Current ($I$) $= 2 \text{ A}$, Time ($t$) $= 10 \text{ minutes} = 10 \times 60 = 600 \text{ s}$.
Charge ($Q$):
$Q = I \times t = 2 \times 600 = 1200 \text{ Coulombs (C)}$.   (1.5 Marks)
Number of electrons ($n$):
Using quantization of charge, $Q = n \times e$
$n = \frac{Q}{e} = \frac{1200}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}}$
$n = \frac{12000}{16} \times 10^{19} = 750 \times 10^{19} = 7.5 \times 10^{21}$ electrons.   (1.5 Marks)
Q8. Define specific resistance (resistivity). Write its SI unit. Does the specific resistance of a conductor depend on its length or cross-sectional area?
[3 Marks]
Definition: Specific resistance (or resistivity) of a material is the resistance offered by a conductor of that material having unit length and unit cross-sectional area.   (1 Mark)
SI Unit: Ohm-meter ($\Omega \cdot \text{m}$).   (1 Mark)
Dependency: No, specific resistance does not depend on the length or cross-sectional area of the conductor. It is a characteristic property of the material and only depends on the nature of the material and its temperature.   (1 Mark)
Q9. Calculate the total electrical energy consumed (in kWh) by a $100 \text{ W}$ bulb operating for $5 \text{ hours}$ and a $400 \text{ W}$ refrigerator operating for $2 \text{ hours}$ in a single day.
[3 Marks]
Energy consumed by the bulb ($E_1$):
$E_1 = \text{Power} \times \text{Time} = 100 \text{ W} \times 5 \text{ h} = 500 \text{ Wh}$.   (1 Mark)
Energy consumed by the refrigerator ($E_2$):
$E_2 = \text{Power} \times \text{Time} = 400 \text{ W} \times 2 \text{ h} = 800 \text{ Wh}$.   (1 Mark)
Total Energy ($E$) $= E_1 + E_2 = 500 + 800 = 1300 \text{ Wh}$.
Converting to kWh: $\frac{1300}{1000} = 1.3 \text{ kWh}$.   (1 Mark)
Q10. Two resistors of $4 \Omega$ and $6 \Omega$ are connected in series to a $5 \text{ V}$ battery. Calculate (i) total current, (ii) potential difference across $4 \Omega$ resistor.
[3 Marks]
(i) Total Current:
In a series circuit, Equivalent Resistance ($R_s$) $= 4 \Omega + 6 \Omega = 10 \Omega$.   (0.5 Mark)
Using Ohm's law, $I = \frac{V}{R_s} = \frac{5}{10} = 0.5 \text{ A}$.   (1 Mark)
(ii) Potential difference across $4 \Omega$ resistor ($V_1$):
In a series circuit, current remains the same. So, $I = 0.5 \text{ A}$.
$V_1 = I \times R_1 = 0.5 \times 4 = 2.0 \text{ V}$.   (1.5 Marks)
SECTION C (15 Marks)
Q11. (a) Derive an expression for equivalent resistance ($R_p$) of three resistors in parallel. (b) Why is parallel combination preferred in domestic wiring? (Two reasons)
[5 Marks]
(a) Derivation (3 Marks):
Let three resistors $R_1$, $R_2$, and $R_3$ be connected in parallel across a battery of potential difference $V$.
In a parallel combination, the potential difference across each resistor is the same ($V$), but the total current $I$ divides into $I_1$, $I_2$, and $I_3$ through the respective resistors.
So, $I = I_1 + I_2 + I_3$ --- (Eq 1)   (1 Mark)
According to Ohm's Law: $I_1 = \frac{V}{R_1}$, $I_2 = \frac{V}{R_2}$, and $I_3 = \frac{V}{R_3}$.
If $R_p$ is the equivalent resistance of the entire parallel combination, then total current $I = \frac{V}{R_p}$.
Substituting these values in Eq 1:
$\frac{V}{R_p} = \frac{V}{R_1} + \frac{V}{R_2} + \frac{V}{R_3}$   (1 Mark)
Canceling $V$ from both sides:
$\frac{1}{R_p} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3}$   (1 Mark)
(b) Advantages of Parallel Circuit (2 Marks):
1. Independent Operation: Each appliance has its own switch. If one appliance stops working (or is switched off), the others continue to work normally.   (1 Mark)
2. Full Voltage: Every appliance gets the same supply voltage (e.g., 220V) as that of the main line, ensuring they run efficiently at their rated power. (Alternatively, overall equivalent resistance is reduced, drawing sufficient current).   (1 Mark)
Q12. Cell $E=2 \text{ V}, r=1.2 \Omega$, Ammeter $R=0.8 \Omega$, and parallel resistors $4.5 \Omega$ and $9 \Omega$. Calculate: (a) External equivalent resistance (b) Ammeter reading (c) P.d. across parallel combination.
[5 Marks]
(a) Equivalent resistance of the external circuit:
First, find the equivalent resistance of the parallel combination ($R_p$) of $4.5 \Omega$ and $9 \Omega$.
$\frac{1}{R_p} = \frac{1}{4.5} + \frac{1}{9} = \frac{2}{9} + \frac{1}{9} = \frac{3}{9} = \frac{1}{3}$
$R_p = 3 \Omega$.   (1 Mark)
This parallel combination is in series with the ammeter.
Total external resistance ($R_{ext}$) $= R_p + \text{Resistance of Ammeter}$
$R_{ext} = 3 \Omega + 0.8 \Omega = 3.8 \Omega$.   (1 Mark)
(b) Ammeter reading (Total Current $I$):
Total resistance of the entire circuit $= R_{ext} + \text{internal resistance } (r)$.
$R_{total} = 3.8 + 1.2 = 5.0 \Omega$.   (0.5 Mark)
Using $I = \frac{E}{R_{total}}$
$I = \frac{2}{5} = 0.4 \text{ A}$.   (1 Mark)
(c) Potential difference across the parallel combination ($V_p$):
The total current $I = 0.4 \text{ A}$ flows into the parallel block.
$V_p = I \times R_p = 0.4 \text{ A} \times 3 \Omega = 1.2 \text{ V}$.   (1.5 Marks)
Q13. (a) V-I graph for Ohmic conductor and its slope. (b) Heater rated $220 \text{ V}, 2 \text{ kW}$. Calculate (i) safe current, (ii) resistance, (iii) cost of running 2 hours/day for 30 days @ ₹5/kWh.
[5 Marks]
(a) V-I Graph:
The V-I graph for an Ohmic conductor is a straight line passing through the origin.   (1 Mark for stating or conceptualizing a straight line)
Slope: If Voltage ($V$) is plotted on the Y-axis and Current ($I$) on the X-axis, the slope of the graph ($\frac{\Delta V}{\Delta I}$) represents the Resistance ($R$) of the conductor.   (1 Mark)
(b) Numericals for the Heater:
Given: Power ($P$) $= 2 \text{ kW} = 2000 \text{ W}$, Voltage ($V$) $= 220 \text{ V}$.
(i) Safe Current ($I$):
$P = V \times I \implies I = \frac{P}{V} = \frac{2000}{220} = \frac{100}{11} \approx 9.09 \text{ A}$.   (1 Mark)
(ii) Resistance ($R$):
$R = \frac{V^2}{P} = \frac{220 \times 220}{2000} = \frac{48400}{2000} = 24.2 \Omega$.   (1 Mark)
(iii) Cost of usage:
Energy consumed in 1 day $= \text{Power (kW)} \times \text{Time (h)} = 2 \text{ kW} \times 2 \text{ h} = 4 \text{ kWh}$.
Total Energy in 30 days $= 4 \times 30 = 120 \text{ kWh}$ (or units).
Total Cost $= \text{Total Energy} \times \text{Rate} = 120 \times 5 = \text{₹ } 600$.   (1 Mark)
