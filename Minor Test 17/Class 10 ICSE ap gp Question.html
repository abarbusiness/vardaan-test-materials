SECTION A (10 Marks) (Attempt all questions — 5 Questions $\times$ 2 Marks Each)
Q1.
[2 Marks] Find the 15th term of the Arithmetic Progression (A.P.): $-10, -6, -2, 2, \dots$
Q2.
[2 Marks] If the $4^{\text{th}}$ and $9^{\text{th}}$ terms of a Geometric Progression (G.P.) are $54$ and $13122$ respectively, find the common ratio ($r$) of the progression.
Q3.
[2 Marks] Which term of the Arithmetic Progression $5, 8, 11, 14, \dots$ is equal to $68$?
Q4.
[2 Marks] Find the $5^{\text{th}}$ term from the end of the Geometric Progression: $3, 6, 12, \dots, 1536$.
Q5.
[2 Marks] Find the sum of the first $20$ terms of an A.P. whose $n^{\text{th}}$ term is given by $T_n = 3n + 5$.
SECTION B (15 Marks) (Short Answer Type — 5 Questions $\times$ 3 Marks Each)
Q6.
[3 Marks] The sum of three consecutive terms in an Arithmetic Progression is $24$ and their product is $440$. Find the three numbers.
Q7.
[3 Marks] Determine the sum of the first $10$ terms of the Geometric Progression: $2, 6, 18, 54, \dots$
Q8.
[3 Marks] How many terms of the A.P. $24, 21, 18, \dots$ must be taken so that their sum is $99$? Explain the reason for getting a double answer.
Q9.
[3 Marks] If $(x + 1)$, $3x$, and $(4x + 12)$ are three consecutive terms of a Geometric Progression, determine the possible real values of $x$.
Q10.
[3 Marks] Find three numbers in Geometric Progression such that their sum is $19$ and their product is $216$.
SECTION C (15 Marks) (Long Answer Type — 3 Questions $\times$ 5 Marks Each)
Q11.
[5 Marks]
(a) The $4^{\text{th}}$ term of an A.P. is equal to $3$ times its first term, and its $7^{\text{th}}$ term exceeds twice its $3^{\text{rd}}$ term by $1$. Find the first term ($a$) and the common difference ($d$). [2.5 Marks]
(b) A person repays a loan of ₹32,500 by paying ₹200 in the first month and then increasing the payment by ₹150 every month. How many months will it take him to completely clear the loan? [2.5 Marks]
Q12.
[5 Marks]
(a) Find the sum of $n$ terms of the geometric sequence: $7 + 77 + 777 + \dots \text{to } n \text{ terms}$. [3 Marks]
(b) If $a, b, c$ are in Arithmetic Progression, and $a, x, b$ as well as $b, y, c$ are in Geometric Progression, prove that $x^2, b^2, y^2$ are in Arithmetic Progression. [2 Marks]
Q13.
[5 Marks]
(a) The sum of the first $n$ terms of an A.P. is given by $S_n = 2n^2 + 5n$. Find the general formula for its $n^{\text{th}}$ term ($T_n$) and hence calculate its $25^{\text{th}}$ term. [2 Marks]
(b) The $3^{\text{rd}}$ term of a Geometric Progression is $12$ and its $6^{\text{th}}$ term is $96$. Calculate the sum of the first $8$ terms of this progression. [3 Marks]
