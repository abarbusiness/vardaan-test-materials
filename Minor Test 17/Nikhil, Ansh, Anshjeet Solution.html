MINOR TEST 17
Class: 8 ICSE Subject: Science Time: 1 Hour Max. Marks: 40
General Instructions:
  • All questions are compulsory.
  • The paper is divided into three sections: A, B, and C.
  • Write your answers neatly and legibly. Show clear steps for numerical problems.
  • Diagrams should be drawn with a pencil where necessary.
SECTION A (10 Marks)(Attempt all questions - 2 Marks Each)
1.
Define atomic number and mass number of an element. How are they related to each other?
2.
Define the term 'work' in Physics. State the SI unit of work and define it.
3.
Write any two fundamental postulates of Dalton's Atomic Theory.
4.
State the difference between potential energy and kinetic energy. Give one common example of a body possessing kinetic energy.
5.
An atom has \(11\) protons and \(12\) neutrons in its nucleus. Calculate its atomic mass and write down its electronic configuration.
SECTION B (15 Marks)(Short Answer Type - 3 Marks Each)
6.
Dalton's atomic theory was a milestone in Chemistry, but it had its limitations. Mention any three major drawbacks of Dalton's atomic theory based on modern atomic structure discoveries.
7.
A boy of mass \(40 \text{ kg}\) climbs up a flight of \(20\) steps, each \(20 \text{ cm}\) high, in \(10\) seconds. Calculate:
(i) the total work done by the boy.
(ii) the power developed by him.
(Take \(g = 10 \text{ m/s}^2\))
8.
Explain the \(2n^2\) rule (Bohr-Bury scheme) for the distribution of electrons in different shells. Using this rule, find the maximum number of electrons that can be accommodated in the M shell.
9.
Define power. How is power mathematically related to work and time? Name the commercial unit of electrical energy and state its relationship with Joules.
10.
State any three main observations made by Ernest Rutherford during his alpha-particle scattering experiment on gold foil.
SECTION C (15 Marks)(Long Answer Type - 5 Marks Each)
11.
(a) Describe Rutherford's nuclear model of the atom based on the conclusions derived from his scattering experiment. Mention three key features of this model. (3 Marks)
(b) Draw a comparative table to distinguish between a proton, a neutron, and an electron with respect to their relative mass, relative electrical charge, and specific location within the atom. (2 Marks)
12.
(a) State the Law of Conservation of Energy. Give one practical example where this law is demonstrated. (2 Marks)
(b) A car weighing \(1000 \text{ kg}\) is moving with a velocity of \(20 \text{ m/s}\). On applying brakes, it comes to rest in \(10\) seconds. Calculate the initial kinetic energy of the car and the power exerted by the brakes to stop the car. (3 Marks)
13.
(a) Consider an element having atomic number \(17\) and mass number \(35\). Determine the number of protons, neutrons, and electrons. Draw a neat diagram showing its atomic structure (distribution of electrons in shells and contents of the nucleus). (3 Marks)
(b) A force of \(50 \text{ N}\) acts on a heavy box and displaces it by \(4 \text{ m}\) in the direction of the force. Calculate the work done. Under what condition will the same force do exactly zero work, even if the body undergoes a displacement of \(4 \text{ m}\)? (2 Marks)
