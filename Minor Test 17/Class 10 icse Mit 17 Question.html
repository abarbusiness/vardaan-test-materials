MINOR TEST 17
Class: 10 ICSE Subject: Physics (Current Electricity) Time: 1 Hour Max. Marks: 40
General Instructions:
  • All questions are compulsory. Keep your calculations neat and clean.
  • The paper is divided into three sections: A, B, and C.
  • Write your answers step-by-step. Include correct SI units in all numericals.
  • Marks for each question are indicated in the respective sections.
SECTION A (10 Marks)(Attempt all questions - 2 Marks Each)
1.
Define the electromotive force (e.m.f.) of a cell. Write its SI unit.
2.
A metallic wire of resistance $R$ is stretched to double its original length. What will be its new resistance? Justify your answer.
3.
State Ohm's Law. Name one material or component that is a non-ohmic conductor.
4.
Calculate the equivalent resistance when two resistors of $3 \Omega$ and $6 \Omega$ are connected in parallel.
5.
What are the factors on which the internal resistance of a cell depends? (State any two).
SECTION B (15 Marks)(Short Answer Type - 3 Marks Each)
6.
Differentiate between electromotive force (e.m.f.) and terminal voltage of a cell. (Write any two points). State the mathematical relation between them when current is drawn from the cell.
7.
A steady current of $2 \text{ A}$ flows through a circuit for $10 \text{ minutes}$. Calculate the amount of electric charge that flows through the circuit. Also, find the total number of electrons transferred during this time. (Given charge on an electron, $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$)
8.
Define specific resistance (resistivity). Write its SI unit. Does the specific resistance of a conductor depend on its length or cross-sectional area?
9.
Calculate the total electrical energy consumed (in kWh) by a $100 \text{ W}$ bulb operating for $5 \text{ hours}$ and a $400 \text{ W}$ refrigerator operating for $2 \text{ hours}$ in a single day.
10.
Two resistors of $4 \Omega$ and $6 \Omega$ are connected in series to a $5 \text{ V}$ battery. Calculate:
(i) The total current flowing in the circuit.
(ii) The potential difference across the $4 \Omega$ resistor.
SECTION C (15 Marks)(Long Answer Type - 5 Marks Each)
11.
Answer the following based on household circuits:
(a) Three resistors $R_1$, $R_2$, and $R_3$ are connected in parallel. Derive an expression for the equivalent resistance ($R_p$) of the combination. (3 Marks)
(b) Why is a parallel combination of electrical appliances preferred over a series combination in domestic wiring? (State two reasons). (2 Marks)
12.
A cell of e.m.f. $2 \text{ V}$ and internal resistance $1.2 \Omega$ is connected to an ammeter of resistance $0.8 \Omega$ and two external resistors of $4.5 \Omega$ and $9 \Omega$ which are connected in parallel to each other. Calculate:
(a) The equivalent resistance of the external circuit. (2 Marks)
(b) The reading of the ammeter (total current drawn from the cell). (1.5 Marks)
(c) The potential difference across the parallel combination of the $4.5 \Omega$ and $9 \Omega$ resistors. (1.5 Marks)
13.
Answer the following questions based on practical electricity:
(a) Draw a neat and labeled V-I graph for an Ohmic conductor. What physical quantity does the slope of this graph represent (if V is on the Y-axis and I is on the X-axis)? (2 Marks)
(b) An electric heater is rated $220 \text{ V}, 2 \text{ kW}$. Calculate:
    (i) The safe current required for the heater.
    (ii) Its electrical resistance.
    (iii) The cost of using it for $2 \text{ hours}$ daily for $30 \text{ days}$, if electrical energy costs ₹ $5.00$ per kWh. (3 Marks)
