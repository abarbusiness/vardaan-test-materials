DETAILED SOLUTIONS: Minor Test 17 (Science - Class 8)
Topics: Force, Elements, Compounds & Mixtures
SECTION A
Q1. Define force. State whether opening a table drawer is a push or a pull.
[2 Marks]
Definition: A push or a pull exerted on an object is called force. (1 Mark)
Action: Opening a table drawer is a pull. (1 Mark)
Q2. What is an element? Give any two examples of elements.
[2 Marks]
Definition: An element is a pure substance made up of only one kind of atom. It cannot be broken down into simpler substances. (1 Mark)
Examples: Oxygen, Gold, Iron, Hydrogen (Any two valid examples). (1 Mark - 0.5 each)
Q3. What is a contact force? Give one simple example from your daily life.
[2 Marks]
Definition: A force that acts only when two objects are in physical contact with each other is called a contact force. (1 Mark)
Example: Pushing a heavy box, kicking a ball, or friction between shoes and the ground. (1 Mark)
Q4. Define a mixture. Give one example of a mixture.
[2 Marks]
Definition: A substance made by simply mixing two or more different substances together physically (in any ratio) without any chemical reaction is called a mixture. (1 Mark)
Example: Lemonade, Air, Sand mixed with water. (1 Mark)
Q5. Give two examples from daily life where applying force changes the shape of an object.
[2 Marks]
Examples:
  • Squeezing a toothpaste tube. (1 Mark)
  • Stretching a rubber band OR kneading dough to make chapatis. (1 Mark)
SECTION B
Q6. (a) What is muscular force? (b) Why is muscular force called a contact force?
[3 Marks]
(a) Muscular Force: The force resulting from the action of muscles in our body is called muscular force. (1.5 Marks)
(b) Reason: It is called a contact force because muscular force can only be applied when our body is in direct physical contact with the object (e.g., holding a bucket). (1.5 Marks)
Q7. Classify into elements, compounds, and mixtures: (i) Gold (ii) Sugar (iii) Air
[3 Marks]
  • (i) Gold: Element (1 Mark)
  • (ii) Sugar: Compound (1 Mark)
  • (iii) Air: Mixture (1 Mark)
Q8. What is frictional force? In which direction does friction act on a moving object?
[3 Marks]
Frictional Force: The force that opposes the relative motion between two surfaces in contact with each other is called friction. (1.5 Marks)
Direction: Friction always acts in the direction opposite to the direction of motion of the object. (1.5 Marks)
Q9. Write three simple differences between an element and a compound.
[3 Marks]
Element Compound
1. Made of only one type of atom. 1. Made of two or more types of atoms.
2. Cannot be broken down further chemically. 2. Can be broken down into simpler elements chemically.
3. E.g., Oxygen, Iron. 3. E.g., Water, Salt.
(1 mark for each valid difference)
Q10. What is gravitational force? Give one example to show the effect of gravity.
[3 Marks]
Gravitational Force: It is the attractive force exerted by the earth on all objects, pulling them towards its center. (1.5 Marks)
Example: An apple falling from a tree to the ground, or a coin dropping from our hand. (1.5 Marks)
SECTION C
Q11. (a) What is a non-contact force? (b) Name two non-contact forces and explain them briefly with an example.
[5 Marks]
(a) Non-Contact Force: A force that can act on an object from a distance without touching it is called a non-contact force. (1 Mark)
(b) Two Non-Contact Forces:
1. Magnetic Force: The force exerted by a magnet is called magnetic force. (1 Mark)
Example: A magnet pulling iron pins or paperclips towards it from a distance. (1 Mark)
2. Electrostatic Force: The force exerted by a charged body on another charged or uncharged body. (1 Mark)
Example: A plastic comb rubbed in dry hair attracts tiny pieces of paper without touching them. (1 Mark)
Q12. (a) What is a compound? (b) Is water considered an element, a mixture, or a compound? Give two simple reasons.
[5 Marks]
(a) Compound: A pure substance formed when two or more elements combine chemically in a fixed proportion. (2 Marks)
(b) Water: Water is considered a Compound. (1 Mark)
Reasons: (2 Marks - 1 each)
  • It is made up of Hydrogen and Oxygen combined in a fixed ratio, not just randomly mixed.
  • Water has completely different properties from its elements. For example, water puts out fire, but hydrogen gas easily catches fire.
Q13. (a) Can force change the state of motion? Explain. (b) Can force change the direction of a moving object? Explain.
[5 Marks]
(a) State of Motion: Yes, a force can change the state of motion of an object (make a stationary object move or stop a moving object). (1 Mark)
Example: When we kick a stationary football, it starts moving. Conversely, a goalkeeper applies force to stop a moving football. (1.5 Marks)
(b) Change in Direction: Yes, a force can change the direction of a moving object. (1 Mark)
Example: A cricket batsman hitting a fast-moving ball with his bat changes the direction of the ball by applying force. (1.5 Marks)
