Q1. Define matter. Give two examples of things that are considered matter and two that are not matter.
[2 Marks]
Answer:
Anything that has mass, occupies space, and can be perceived by our senses is called matter.
- Examples of matter: Water, Pencil, Air, Iron block (Any two).
- Examples of non-matter (forms of energy/feelings): Light, Heat, Sound, Love, or Happiness (Any two).
Q2. State the two main characteristics of particles of matter with respect to space between them and their movement.
[2 Marks]
Answer:
1. Inter-particle spaces: There are vacant spaces (inter-molecular spaces) between the particles of matter.
2. Continuous random motion: The particles of matter are in continuous, random, haphazard motion possessing kinetic energy.
Q3. Define inter-molecular spaces and inter-molecular forces of attraction.
[2 Marks]
Answer:
- Inter-molecular spaces: The empty or vacant spaces present between the constituent particles (atoms or molecules) of matter are called inter-molecular spaces.
- Inter-molecular forces of attraction: The attractive force acting between neighbouring particles (molecules) of matter that holds them together is called inter-molecular force.
Q4. Why are solids rigid and incompressible, whereas gases can be easily compressed?
[2 Marks]
Answer:
In solids, inter-molecular spaces are extremely small and inter-molecular forces of attraction are very strong, locking particles in fixed positions (making them rigid and incompressible). In contrast, gases have vast inter-molecular spaces and negligible forces of attraction, allowing gas molecules to be squeezed closer together easily under pressure (high compressibility).
Q5. What is meant by the term 'change of state'? Give an example of a substance that changes directly from solid to gas on heating.
[2 Marks]
Answer:
The process of conversion of matter from one physical state (solid, liquid, or gas) to another by absorption or release of heat is called change of state.
- Example of substance undergoing direct solid-to-gas conversion (sublimation): Camphor, Ammonium chloride, or Dry ice (Solid \(\text{CO}_2$).
Q6. Differentiate between solids, liquids, and gases based on: (a) Shape and Volume (b) Fluidity (c) Compressibility
[3 Marks]
Answer:
(a) Shape and Volume: Solids have a definite shape and definite volume. Liquids have a definite volume but no fixed shape (take the shape of the container). Gases have neither definite shape nor definite volume.
(b) Fluidity: Solids cannot flow (not fluids). Both liquids and gases can flow easily and are called fluids.
(c) Compressibility: Solids are virtually incompressible. Liquids are negligibly compressible. Gases are highly compressible.
Q7. Explain the phenomenon of Diffusion with a suitable daily life example. Why does diffusion happen faster in gases than in liquids?
[3 Marks]
Answer:
Diffusion is the intermingling of particles of two different substances on their own due to their random motion.
- Daily life example: Spreading of the fragrance of an incense stick (agarbatti) lit in one corner of a room, or mixing of a drop of ink in a glass of water.
- Reason for faster rate in gases: Gas particles possess much higher kinetic energy, move at very high speeds, and have large inter-molecular spaces compared to liquids.
Q8. Explain the effect of heat on the kinetic energy of particles of matter. How does heating a solid lead to its conversion into a liquid (melting)?
[3 Marks]
Answer:
When a substance is heated, the thermal energy supplied is absorbed by its particles, which increases their kinetic energy and makes them vibrate or move faster.
When a solid is heated, particles vibrate more vigorously until they overcome the strong inter-molecular forces holding them in rigid positions. At the melting point, particles break free from fixed lattice sites and begin to flow past one another, turning the solid into a liquid.
Q9. Define the following terms clearly: (a) Melting point (b) Boiling point (c) Condensation
[3 Marks]
Answer:
(a) Melting point: The constant temperature at which a solid changes into its liquid state at normal atmospheric pressure.
(b) Boiling point: The constant temperature at which a liquid rapidly changes into its gaseous state throughout its bulk at normal atmospheric pressure.
(c) Condensation: The process in which a gas or vapour changes into its liquid state upon cooling.
Q10. Give scientific reasons for the following: (a) A gas fills completely the vessel in which it is kept. (b) Wooden blocks cannot flow like water.
[3 Marks]
Answer:
(a) Gas molecules have negligible inter-molecular forces of attraction and extremely high kinetic energy, causing them to move randomly in all directions at high speeds to occupy any available volume.
(b) Wooden blocks are solids with strong inter-molecular forces and fixed rigid positions of particles, whereas water is a liquid with weak enough forces to allow particles to slide over one another (fluidity).
Q11. Describe the kinetic theory of matter in detail. List any four key postulates of this theory and explain how it helps us understand the three states of matter.
[5 Marks]
Answer:
The kinetic theory of matter explains the behavior and physical states of matter based on the motion of its particles.
Four Key Postulates:
1. All matter is made up of extremely small particles called atoms or molecules.
2. These particles are in continuous, random motion, possessing kinetic energy.
3. There are vacant spaces between particles known as inter-molecular spaces.
4. Particles attract each other with forces known as inter-molecular forces.
Explanation of States:
- Solids: Forces of attraction are maximum; spaces are minimum; motion is restricted to vibration.
- Liquids: Forces are moderate; spaces are greater than solids; particles can slide past each other.
- Gases: Forces are negligible; spaces are maximum; particles move freely at high speeds.
Q12. Explain the complete cycle of changes of state with the help of a descriptive flow diagram or text description covering melting, vaporization, condensation, freezing, and sublimation.
[5 Marks]
Answer:
The transitions between states occur due to addition or removal of heat:
1. Melting (Fusion): Solid \(\rightarrow\) Liquid (on heating). Example: Ice turning to water.
2. Vaporization (Evaporation/Boiling): Liquid \(\rightarrow\) Gas (on heating). Example: Water turning to steam.
3. Condensation: Gas \(\rightarrow\) Liquid (on cooling). Example: Steam turning to water droplets.
4. Freezing (Solidification): Liquid \(\rightarrow\) Solid (on cooling). Example: Water freezing into ice.
5. Sublimation: Solid \(\rightarrow\) Gas directly without forming liquid (on heating). Example: Camphor or Iodine.
Q13. (a) State three activities or observations from daily life that prove matter is made up of tiny particles with spaces between them. (b) Why does a solid expand very slightly on heating, while a gas expands to a large extent?
[5 Marks]
Answer:
(a) Three observations (3 Marks):
1. Dissolving sugar in water: When sugar is dissolved in water, the volume of water does not increase significantly because sugar molecules occupy the vacant inter-molecular spaces between water molecules.
2. Spread of perfume: Fragrance travels across a room because gas particles diffuse into air spaces.
3. Compression of air in a syringe: Air inside a closed syringe can be compressed because of large gaps between gas particles.

(b) Expansion on heating (2 Marks):
Solids have tightly bound particles with strong attractive forces, so heating only slightly increases their vibrational amplitude. In contrast, gases have large vacant spaces and negligible forces, so thermal energy causes a massive increase in inter-particle distances and volume.
