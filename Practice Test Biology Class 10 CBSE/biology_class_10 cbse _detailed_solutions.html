Chapter: Life Processes Q1. Write the balanced chemical equation representing Photosynthesis, main events, and stomatal regulation.

1. Balanced Chemical Equation for Photosynthesis:

$$6\text{CO}_2 + 12\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow[\text{Chlorophyll}]{\text{Sunlight}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$$

2. Major Events in Chloroplast during Photosynthesis:

  • Absorption of light energy by chlorophyll molecules.
  • Conversion of light energy into chemical energy and Splitting (Photolysis) of water molecules into hydrogen and oxygen.
  • Reduction of carbon dioxide into carbohydrates (Glucose).

3. Regulation of Gaseous Exchange by Stomata:

Stomatal pores are flanked by a pair of Guard Cells. When water flows into the guard cells, they swell and become turgid, causing the pore to open. When guard cells lose water, they shrink and become flaccid, causing the pore to close.

Chapter: Life Processes Q2. Explain Double Circulation in humans and the pathway of blood flow.
Definition: In human double circulation, blood passes through the heart twice during one complete cycle through the body.

Two Pathways involved:

1. Pulmonary Circulation: Deoxygenated blood from right ventricle $\rightarrow$ Pulmonary Artery $\rightarrow$ Lungs $\rightarrow$ Oxygenated blood $\rightarrow$ Pulmonary Veins $\rightarrow$ Left Atrium.

2. Systemic Circulation: Oxygenated blood from left ventricle $\rightarrow$ Aorta $\rightarrow$ Body Tissues $\rightarrow$ Deoxygenated blood $\rightarrow$ Vena Cava $\rightarrow$ Right Atrium.

Significance of Separation:

Mammals and birds have a 4-chambered heart that completely separates oxygenated and deoxygenated blood. This prevents mixing and provides a highly efficient supply of oxygen needed to maintain constant body temperature (warm-blooded/endothermic metabolic needs).

Chapter: Control & Coordination Q3. Explain Reflex Arc, impulse pathway, and the role of the spinal cord.

Reflex Arc: The anatomical pathway traversed by sensory and motor nerve impulses during an involuntary, rapid response (reflex action) to a stimulus.

Pathway of Nerve Impulse on touching a hot object:

Stimulus (Heat) $\rightarrow$ Receptor (Heat Receptors in Skin) $\rightarrow$ Sensory Neuron $\rightarrow$ Spinal Cord (Relay Neuron) $\rightarrow$ Motor Neuron $\rightarrow$ Effector (Hand Muscles) $\rightarrow$ Response (Withdrawal of Hand).

Why Spinal Cord Governs Reflexes:

Thinking process in the brain takes time. Since direct damage can occur rapidly, reflex pathways are processed immediately in the spinal cord for a quicker response, although the signal is simultaneously sent to the brain for information.

Chapter: Control & Coordination Q4. Explain Phototropism mechanism in plants with reference to Auxin.

Phototropism: The directional growth movement of a plant part in response to light.

Mechanism of Auxin Action:

  • Auxin is synthesized at the shoot tips.
  • When directional light falls on one side of the shoot, auxin diffuses away from light toward the shaded side of the shoot.
  • The higher concentration of auxin on the shaded side stimulates cells there to elongate longer and faster compared to the illuminated side.
  • As a result, the shoot bends toward the light source (Positive Phototropism).
Chapter: How do Organisms Reproduce? Q5. Compare binary fission in Amoeba vs Leishmania and explain the importance of variation.

Difference in Binary Fission:

  • In Amoeba: Splitting/division of the cell into two equal halves can take place in any plane because it lacks a fixed body shape.
  • In Leishmania: Has a whip-like flagellum at one end, so binary fission occurs strictly in a fixed orientation/longitudinal plane relative to the flagellum.

Importance of Variation to Species:

DNA copying during reproduction is not 100% precise, leading to variations. If an ecosystem undergoes drastic environmental changes (e.g., sudden temperature rise), organisms without variation might die out completely. Individuals possessing resistant variations survive and reproduce, ensuring the survival and continuity of the species.

Chapter: How do Organisms Reproduce? Q6. Location of testes in scrotum and functions of Testosterone.

Reason for Scrotal Location:

Sperm formation (Spermatogenesis) requires a temperature about $2^\circ\text{C}$ to $2.5^\circ\text{C}$ lower than normal human body temperature ($37^\circ\text{C}$). The scrotal sac is positioned outside the abdominal cavity to maintain this cooler environment.

Two Key Functions of Testosterone:

  1. Regulates the production of sperms.
  2. Brings about secondary sexual characteristics in human males during puberty (e.g., growth of facial hair, deepening of voice, broader shoulders).
Chapter: Life Processes Q7. Pathways of Glucose Breakdown in living organisms.

Step 1: Glycolysis (Common Initial Step):

Glucose (6-carbon molecule) is converted into Pyruvate (3-carbon molecule) in the Cytoplasm of the cell.

Step 2: Three Distinct Pathways of Pyruvate Breakdown:

  • 1. Presence of Oxygen (Aerobic Respiration in Mitochondria):
    Pyruvate $\rightarrow$ Carbon Dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) + Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) + Energy (38 ATP).
  • 2. Lack of Oxygen (In Human Muscle Cells during rigorous exercise):
    Pyruvate $\rightarrow$ Lactic Acid (3-carbon molecule) + Energy (causes muscle cramps).
  • 3. Absence of Oxygen (Anaerobic Respiration / Fermentation in Yeast):
    Pyruvate $\rightarrow$ Ethanol ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$) + Carbon Dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) + Energy (2 ATP).
Chapter: Control & Coordination Q8. Structure of Neuron and Synaptic Transmission mechanism.

Structure of a Neuron:

Composed of three main parts: (1) Dendrites (receive signals), (2) Cyton / Cell Body (contains nucleus and cytoplasm), and (3) Axon (long nerve fiber carrying impulse away from cell body).

Mechanism of Signal Conduction & Synapse Transmission:

  1. Information is acquired at the tip of the dendrites, setting off a chemical reaction that creates an electrical impulse.
  2. The electrical impulse travels from the dendrite to the cell body, and then along the axon to its end terminal.
  3. At the Synapse (microscopic gap between axon terminal of one neuron and dendrite of next neuron), the electrical impulse triggers the release of chemical substances called Neurotransmitters.
  4. These chemicals cross the synapse gap and start a similar electrical impulse in the dendrite of the next neuron.
Chapter: How do Organisms Reproduce? Q9. Explain Double Fertilization and post-fertilization changes in plants.

Double Fertilization Process:

Pollen tube releases two male gametes into the embryo sac:

  • Syngamy: One male gamete fuses with the egg cell nucleus to form a diploid Zygote.
  • Triple Fusion: The second male gamete fuses with the two polar nuclei to form the triploid Primary Endosperm Nucleus (PEN).

Post-Fertilization Transformations:

  • (a) Ovule: Develops a tough coat and gradually turns into a Seed (containing the embryo and food reserves).
  • (b) Ovary: Grows rapidly and ripens to form the Fruit (ovary wall becomes fruit wall / pericarp).
Chapter: Life Processes Q10. Structural unit of Kidney and Nephron urine formation process.

Unit of Kidney:

The structural and functional filtration unit of the human kidney is the Nephron.

Mechanism of Urine Formation in a Nephron:

  1. Glomerular Ultrafiltration: High pressure blood enters the Glomerulus (capillary cluster inside Bowman's capsule). Nitrogenous wastes, glucose, amino acids, salts, and water pass into Bowman's capsule to form initial filtrate.
  2. Selective Reabsorption: As the filtrate flows along the coiled tubular part of the nephron, useful substances like glucose, amino acids, salts, and major amount of water are selectively reabsorbed back into surrounding blood capillaries.
  3. Tubular Secretion: Excess water and nitrogenous wastes (Urea, Uric Acid) remain in the tubule to form concentrated Urine, which flows into collecting ducts leading to the ureter.
