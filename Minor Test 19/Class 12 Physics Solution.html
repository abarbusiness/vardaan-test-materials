DETAILED SOLUTIONS: Class 12 – Physics MCQ Test

Topic: Electrostatics & Magnetism

Q1. Two point charges $+q$ and $-q$ are separated by a distance $r$ in vacuum. The force between them is $F$. If a dielectric material of dielectric constant $K$ is completely filled between them, the new force will be:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (B) $F / K$
Explanation: By Coulomb's Law, the force in vacuum is: $$F = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{q^2}{r^2}$$ When a medium of dielectric constant $K$ is introduced, the permittivity becomes $\epsilon = K\epsilon_0$. The new force is: $$F' = \frac{1}{4\pi(K\epsilon_0)} \frac{q^2}{r^2} = \frac{F}{K}$$ Thus, the force decreases by a factor of $K$.
Q2. The electric flux through a closed Gaussian surface enclosing an electric dipole is:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (C) Zero
Explanation: An electric dipole consists of two equal and opposite charges ($+q$ and $-q$). According to Gauss's Law, the total electric flux through a closed surface is: $$\phi_E = \frac{q_{net}}{\epsilon_0}$$ Since the net charge enclosed $q_{net} = (+q) + (-q) = 0$, the total electric flux is zero.
Q3. A hollow conducting sphere of radius $R$ carries a charge $Q$. The electric potential at its center is:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (B) $\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{Q}{R}$
Explanation: The electric field inside a hollow conducting sphere is zero. Because $E = -\frac{dV}{dr} = 0$, the potential $V$ must be constant everywhere inside the sphere and equal to its value on the surface. Therefore, the potential at the center is the same as on the surface: $V = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{Q}{R}$.
Q4. Three identical capacitors, each of capacitance $C$, are connected in series. Their equivalent capacitance is:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (C) $C / 3$
Explanation: For capacitors in series, the equivalent capacitance $C_{eq}$ is given by the reciprocal sum: $$\frac{1}{C_{eq}} = \frac{1}{C_1} + \frac{1}{C_2} + \frac{1}{C_3}$$ Here, $C_1 = C_2 = C_3 = C$. So, $\frac{1}{C_{eq}} = \frac{3}{C} \implies C_{eq} = \frac{C}{3}$.
Q5. What is the work done in moving a test charge $q_0$ along an equipotential surface?
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (C) Zero
Explanation: An equipotential surface is one where the potential $V$ is constant everywhere. The work done in moving a charge is $W = q_0 \Delta V$. Since the potential difference $\Delta V$ between any two points on an equipotential surface is zero, the work done $W = 0$.
Q6. The energy density of the electric field $E$ in free space is given by:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (A) $\frac{1}{2} \epsilon_0 E^2$
Explanation: Energy density (energy per unit volume) in an electric field in vacuum is a standard derived formula. It is directly proportional to the square of the electric field intensity and is given as $u_e = \frac{1}{2} \epsilon_0 E^2$.
Q7. An electric dipole placed in a uniform electric field experiences:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (B) Only a torque
Explanation: In a uniform electric field, the force on $+q$ is $qE$ and on $-q$ is $-qE$. The net force $F_{net} = qE - qE = 0$. However, these forces act at different points, forming a couple, which exerts a net torque given by $\vec{\tau} = \vec{p} \times \vec{E}$.
Q8. A charge $q$ is placed at the center of a cube of side $l$. What is the electric flux passing through one face of the cube?
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (D) $\frac{q}{6\epsilon_0}$
Explanation: According to Gauss's law, the total flux through the entire cube is $\Phi_{total} = \frac{q}{\epsilon_0}$. Because the charge is exactly at the center, the flux is symmetrically distributed among all $6$ identical faces. Thus, the flux through one face is: $$\Phi_{face} = \frac{q}{6\epsilon_0}$$
Q9. The electric field intensity due to an infinite uniformly charged straight wire at a perpendicular distance $r$ is proportional to:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (C) $1/r$
Explanation: Using Gauss's Law, the electric field due to an infinitely long straight wire with linear charge density $\lambda$ is given by: $$E = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0 r}$$ Therefore, $E \propto \frac{1}{r}$.
Q10. A parallel plate capacitor is charged and the charging battery is disconnected. If a dielectric slab is now inserted between the plates, the potential difference across the plates will:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (B) Decrease
Explanation: When the battery is disconnected, the charge $Q$ on the plates remains constant. Introducing a dielectric of constant $K$ increases the capacitance to $C' = K \cdot C$. Since $V = \frac{Q}{C'}$, and $C'$ increases, the potential difference $V$ must decrease ($V' = V/K$).
Q11. According to Biot-Savart's law, the magnetic field $d\vec{B}$ due to a current element $Id\vec{l}$ at a distance $\vec{r}$ is:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (B) $\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{I(d\vec{l} \times \vec{r})}{r^3}$
Explanation: The Biot-Savart law in vector form is written as: $$d\vec{B} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi} \frac{d\vec{l} \times \hat{r}}{r^2}$$ Since unit vector $\hat{r} = \frac{\vec{r}}{r}$, substituting this gives: $$d\vec{B} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi} \frac{d\vec{l} \times \vec{r}}{r^3}$$
Q12. A charged particle of charge $q$ enters a uniform magnetic field $\vec{B}$ with velocity $\vec{v}$ perpendicular to the field. The path of the particle will be:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (C) Circular
Explanation: The magnetic Lorentz force $\vec{F} = q(\vec{v} \times \vec{B})$ acts perpendicular to both velocity and the magnetic field. This perpendicular force provides the centripetal force required for circular motion, causing the particle to trace a circular path.
Q13. Two long parallel wires carrying steady currents in the same direction will:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (A) Attract each other
Explanation: Wire 1 produces a magnetic field at the location of Wire 2. According to Fleming's Left-Hand Rule, the force on Wire 2 due to this magnetic field points toward Wire 1. Hence, currents in the same direction attract each other.
Q14. To convert a moving coil galvanometer into an ammeter, we must connect:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (D) Low resistance in parallel
Explanation: An ammeter is placed in series in a circuit and must have a very low resistance so it doesn't alter the main current. To achieve this, a very low resistance called a "shunt" is connected in parallel with the galvanometer.
Q15. The magnetic field $B$ inside a long straight solenoid carrying current $I$ and having $n$ turns per unit length is:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (A) $\mu_0 n I$
Explanation: Applying Ampere's Circuital Law to a rectangular loop inside an ideal long solenoid yields $\oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0 I_{enclosed}$. This simplifies to $B \cdot L = \mu_0 (nL) I$, giving the standard result $B = \mu_0 n I$.
Q16. The angle of dip at the Earth's magnetic poles is:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (C) $90^\circ$
Explanation: At the Earth's magnetic poles, the magnetic field lines enter or leave the Earth vertically. Therefore, the horizontal component is zero, and the angle of dip (the angle the magnetic field makes with the horizontal) is exactly $90^\circ$.
Q17. The radius of the circular path of a charged particle moving perpendicularly in a uniform magnetic field is directly proportional to its:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (B) Momentum
Explanation: The radius of a charged particle in a magnetic field is given by $r = \frac{mv}{qB} = \frac{p}{qB}$, where $p$ is momentum. Since $q$ and $B$ are in the denominator, the radius is directly proportional to the momentum $p$.
Q18. The magnetic dipole moment $M$ of a circular coil of radius $r$ carrying current $I$ and having $N$ turns is:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (A) $N I \pi r^2$
Explanation: The magnetic dipole moment of a current-carrying loop is $M = N I A$. For a circular coil of radius $r$, the area $A = \pi r^2$. Substituting this gives $M = N I (\pi r^2)$.
Q19. The magnetic susceptibility ($\chi$) of a diamagnetic material is:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (C) Negative and small
Explanation: Diamagnetic materials develop an induced magnetic dipole moment in a direction opposite to the applied magnetic field. Because they are weakly repelled by magnetic fields, their magnetic susceptibility $\chi$ is negative and very small in magnitude.
Q20. At a certain place, the horizontal component of Earth's magnetic field is equal to the vertical component. The angle of dip at that place is:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (C) $45^\circ$
Explanation: The angle of dip $\delta$ is related to the horizontal ($B_H$) and vertical ($B_V$) components by the equation $\tan \delta = \frac{B_V}{B_H}$. Since $B_V = B_H$, we have $\tan \delta = 1$, which means $\delta = 45^\circ$.
Q21. The electric field inside a uniformly charged non-conducting solid sphere of radius $R$ at a distance $r$ ($r < R$) from its center is proportional to:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (C) $r$
Explanation: Using Gauss's Law for a point inside a uniformly charged solid sphere, the electric field is given by $E = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{Q \cdot r}{R^3}$. Therefore, $E \propto r$.
Q22. The dimensional formula of permittivity of free space $\epsilon_0$ is:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (A) $[M^{-1} L^{-3} T^4 A^2]$
Explanation: From Coulomb's Law, $\epsilon_0 = \frac{q_1 q_2}{4\pi F r^2}$. Putting dimensions: $\frac{[A T] \cdot [A T]}{[M L T^{-2}] [L^2]} = [M^{-1} L^{-3} T^4 A^2]$.
Q23. A soap bubble is given a negative charge. Its radius will:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (A) Increase
Explanation: When a soap bubble is charged, the excess charges (being of the same sign) mutually repel each other. This repulsive force pushes the surface outward, increasing its radius.
Q24. Two capacitors of capacitance $2 \mu\text{F}$ and $3 \mu\text{F}$ are connected in parallel. Their equivalent capacitance is:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (B) $5 \mu\text{F}$
Explanation: For capacitors connected in parallel, the equivalent capacitance is simply the algebraic sum of the individual capacitances. $C_{eq} = C_1 + C_2 = 2 \mu\text{F} + 3 \mu\text{F} = 5 \mu\text{F}$.
Q25. The dielectric constant of metals is:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (D) Infinity
Explanation: The electric field inside a conductor is zero. Since $E = \frac{E_0}{K}$ and $E = 0$ inside a metal, the dielectric constant $K$ must be infinity.
Q26. The unit of electric flux is:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (B) V-m
Explanation: Electric flux $\Phi_E = E \cdot A$. The unit of Electric field $E$ is Volt/meter (V/m). Multiplying by area ($m^2$) gives $(V/m) \cdot m^2 = V \cdot m$.
Q27. If the distance between two equal point charges is doubled, the electrostatic force between them becomes:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (C) One-fourth
Explanation: According to Coulomb's Law, the force is inversely proportional to the square of the distance between charges ($F \propto 1/r^2$). If $r$ becomes $2r$, the new force becomes $F' \propto 1/(2r)^2 = 1/4r^2$, which is one-fourth of the original force.
Q28. Work done in moving a charge of $2\text{ C}$ across two points having a potential difference of $5\text{ V}$ is:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (B) 10 J
Explanation: Work done is calculated as $W = q \Delta V$. Here, $q = 2\text{ C}$ and $\Delta V = 5\text{ V}$. Hence, $W = 2 \times 5 = 10\text{ J}$.
Q29. The electrostatic potential energy of a dipole of moment $\vec{p}$ in a uniform electric field $\vec{E}$ is given by:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (B) $-\vec{p} \cdot \vec{E}$
Explanation: The potential energy of a dipole is calculated as the work done to rotate it in the field, derived as $U = -pE \cos\theta$, which in vector form is written as $-\vec{p} \cdot \vec{E}$.
Q30. An uncharged insulated conductor $A$ is brought near a charged insulated conductor $B$. What happens to charge and potential of $B$?
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (A) Charge remains same, potential decreases
Explanation: Bringing conductor A near B causes induction, reducing the net potential of B. However, since the systems are insulated and no charge is transferred, the net charge on B remains the same.
Q31. Which of the following is NOT a valid unit for electric field intensity?
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (C) J/C
Explanation: The standard units of electric field are Newton/Coulomb (N/C) and Volt/meter (V/m). Since $1\text{ V} = 1\text{ J/C}$, $V/m$ is equivalent to $J/(C\cdot m)$. However, Joules/Coulomb (J/C) is strictly the unit of Electric Potential, not Electric Field.
Q32. The electric field lines provide information about:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (D) All of these
Explanation: Electric field lines give the direction (tangent to the line), field strength (density of lines), and nature of charge (originating from positive, terminating at negative).
Q33. If a conducting slab of thickness $t$ ($t < d$) is introduced between the plates of a parallel plate capacitor of plate separation $d$, its capacitance:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (A) Increases
Explanation: The capacitance with a conducting slab is $C' = \frac{\epsilon_0 A}{d - t}$. Since the denominator decreases ($d-t < d$), the capacitance $C'$ increases.
Q34. Equipotential surfaces associated with an isolated point charge are:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (A) Concentric spheres
Explanation: The potential due to a point charge is $V = k \frac{q}{r}$. For $V$ to be constant, $r$ must be constant. The locus of points with constant $r$ in 3D space is a sphere centered at the charge.
Q35. Gauss's law will be invalid if:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (B) Inverse square law is not exactly true
Explanation: Gauss's law depends fundamentally on the inverse square law of the electric field ($1/r^2$). If the field obeyed a different distance dependency, the solid angle derivation for Gauss's law would fail.
Q36. The magnetic field at the center of a circular current carrying loop of radius $r$ and $N$ turns is:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (B) $\frac{\mu_0 N I}{2 r}$
Explanation: By Biot-Savart Law integrated over a full circle, $B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2r}$ for one turn. For $N$ turns, it is scaled to $B = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2r}$.
Q37. The frequency of a charged particle circulating in a cyclotron is independent of:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (D) Velocity of the particle
Explanation: The cyclotron frequency is given by $f = \frac{qB}{2\pi m}$. It depends on the charge $q$, mass $m$, and magnetic field $B$, but is completely independent of velocity (and radius).
Q38. The Lorentz force on a particle of charge $q$ moving with velocity $\vec{v}$ in combined electric field $\vec{E}$ and magnetic field $\vec{B}$ is:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (B) $q(\vec{E} + \vec{v} \times \vec{B})$
Explanation: The total force is the sum of the electric force $\vec{F_e} = q\vec{E}$ and the magnetic force $\vec{F_m} = q(\vec{v} \times \vec{B})$. Thus, $\vec{F} = q(\vec{E} + \vec{v} \times \vec{B})$.
Q39. Which of the following material has a negative magnetic susceptibility?
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (C) Diamagnetic
Explanation: Diamagnetic materials develop a weak induced magnetization in a direction opposite to the applied magnetic field, leading to a small, negative magnetic susceptibility ($\chi < 0$).
Q40. The temperature above which a ferromagnetic substance becomes paramagnetic is called:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (B) Curie temperature
Explanation: The Curie temperature ($T_c$) is the critical temperature where the thermal agitation completely destroys the magnetic domain structure, turning a ferromagnetic material into a paramagnetic one.
Q41. To convert a galvanometer into a voltmeter, we must connect a:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (C) High resistance in series
Explanation: A voltmeter needs a very high resistance so it draws negligible current from the circuit when placed in parallel. This is achieved by connecting a high resistance in series with the galvanometer coil.
Q42. The SI unit of magnetic field ($\vec{B}$) is:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (B) Tesla
Explanation: The SI unit of Magnetic field $B$ is Tesla (T), which is also equivalent to Weber per square meter ($Wb/m^2$). Gauss is the CGS unit.
Q43. Two long parallel wires carrying steady currents in opposite directions will:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (B) Repel each other
Explanation: When currents are antiparallel (opposite directions), the magnetic field produced by one wire causes an outward Lorentz force on the electrons in the second wire, resulting in a repulsive force.
Q44. The work done by a magnetic field on a moving charge is always:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (C) Zero
Explanation: The magnetic force $\vec{F} = q(\vec{v} \times \vec{B})$ is always perpendicular to velocity $\vec{v}$. Because force and displacement are perpendicular, work done $W = \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{s} = 0$.
Q45. The magnetic dipole moment of a revolving electron in the $n^{th}$ orbit is given by:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (A) $\frac{n e \hbar}{2m_e}$
Explanation: From Bohr's quantization, $L = n\frac{h}{2\pi} = n\hbar$. The magnetic moment is related to angular momentum by $M = \frac{e}{2m_e} L$. Substituting $L$ gives $M = \frac{n e \hbar}{2m_e}$.
Q46. The total magnetic flux passing through a closed surface is:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (C) Zero
Explanation: According to Gauss's Law for Magnetism, $\oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{A} = 0$. This indicates that isolated magnetic poles (monopoles) do not exist, and magnetic field lines form continuous closed loops.
Q47. The magnetic field produced at the center of a current carrying toroid of mean radius $r$ having $N$ total turns and current $I$ is:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (A) $\frac{\mu_0 N I}{2\pi r}$
Explanation: Inside a toroid, $B = \mu_0 n I$, where $n$ is turns per unit length. Since the total length is the circumference $2\pi r$, $n = \frac{N}{2\pi r}$. Substituting this gives $B = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2\pi r}$.
Q48. If a charged particle is moving perfectly parallel to a uniform magnetic field, the magnetic force acting on it is:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (C) Zero
Explanation: The magnetic force is $F = qvB \sin\theta$. If the particle moves parallel to the field, $\theta = 0^\circ$, and $\sin 0^\circ = 0$. Thus, $F = 0$.
Q49. The area of a hysteresis loop of a magnetic material is a measure of:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (B) Energy dissipated per cycle per unit volume
Explanation: When a magnetic material undergoes a complete cycle of magnetization and demagnetization, the area enclosed by the B-H curve (hysteresis loop) equals the thermal energy lost per unit volume.
Q50. According to Ampere's circuital law, the line integral of magnetic field $\vec{B}$ around any closed path in vacuum is equal to:
[4 Marks]
Correct Option: (A) $\mu_0$ times the total current threading the closed path
Explanation: Ampere's circuital law states that $\oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0 I_{enc}$, meaning the integral around a closed loop is directly proportional to the total net current enclosed by that loop.
