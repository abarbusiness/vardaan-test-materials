DETAILED SOLUTIONS: ELECTROMAGNETIC WAVES
1. The basic source of electromagnetic waves is:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (c) An oscillating charge
According to Maxwell's theory, an oscillating electric charge produces a time-varying electric field, which in turn produces a time-varying magnetic field. These mutually regenerating fields propagate through space as electromagnetic waves. A charge at rest produces only an electrostatic field, and a charge in uniform motion produces steady electric and magnetic fields.
2. Displacement current arises due to:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (b) Changing electric field
Maxwell introduced the concept of displacement current (\(I_d\)) to remove the inconsistency in Ampere's circuital law. It exists purely due to a time-varying electric field (or electric flux) in a region, given by \(I_d = \epsilon_0 \frac{d\Phi_E}{dt}\).
3. In an electromagnetic wave in vacuum, the ratio of the amplitudes of the electric and magnetic fields (\(E_0/B_0\)) is equal to:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (a) Speed of light in vacuum
The amplitudes of the electric and magnetic fields in an EM wave are related by the formula \(c = \frac{E_0}{B_0}\), where \(c\) is the speed of light in vacuum (\(3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}\)).
4. The phase difference between the electric and magnetic fields in an electromagnetic wave is:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (c) 0
In an electromagnetic wave, both the electric field vector (\(\vec{E}\)) and the magnetic field vector (\(\vec{B}\)) oscillate in phase with each other. They reach their maxima and minima at the same time and at the same point in space.
5. The direction of propagation of an electromagnetic wave is given by the direction of:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (c) \(\vec{E} \times \vec{B}\)
Electromagnetic waves are transverse in nature. The electric field (\(\vec{E}\)) and magnetic field (\(\vec{B}\)) are mutually perpendicular and both are perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation. The cross product \(\vec{E} \times \vec{B}\) gives the Poynting vector, which defines the direction of propagation.
6. Which of the following EM waves has the maximum frequency?
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (b) Gamma rays
In the electromagnetic spectrum, as we move from Radio waves to Gamma rays, the wavelength decreases and the frequency increases. Gamma rays have the shortest wavelength and hence the highest frequency.
7. Momentum transferred by an EM wave of total energy \(U\) to a completely absorbing surface is:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (a) \(U/c\)
EM waves carry momentum. If an EM wave of total energy \(U\) is incident on a completely absorbing surface, the momentum transferred is given by \(p = \frac{U}{c}\).
8. Electromagnetic waves used in radar systems for aircraft navigation are:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (c) Microwaves
Microwaves have short wavelengths (in the range of millimeters to centimeters), which makes them suitable for radar systems. Because of their short wavelengths, they do not diffract (bend) much around objects and can form sharp beams required for precise aircraft navigation.
9. Welder's glasses are used to protect the eyes primarily from:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (c) Ultraviolet rays
Welding arcs produce highly intense ultraviolet (UV) radiations which are harmful to the eyes and can cause permanent damage (photokeratitis). Welder's glasses are specially designed to absorb these UV rays.
10. The speed of electromagnetic waves in a medium of permittivity \(\epsilon\) and permeability \(\mu\) is:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (b) \(1/\sqrt{\mu \epsilon}\)
The velocity of an EM wave in any medium is determined by the electrical and magnetic properties of that medium. It is given by the formula \(v = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu \epsilon}}\), where \(\mu\) is the permeability and \(\epsilon\) is the permittivity of the medium.
11. Which of the following is NOT an electromagnetic wave?
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (c) \(\beta\)-rays
\(\beta\)-rays (beta rays) are streams of high-energy, high-speed electrons or positrons emitted by certain types of radioactive nuclei. They are particulate matter with mass and charge, unlike EM waves which are composed of oscillating electric and magnetic fields (photons) with no rest mass.
12. The mathematical expression for displacement current \(I_d\) is:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (b) \(\epsilon_0 \frac{d\Phi_E}{dt}\)
Maxwell defined displacement current as \(I_d = \epsilon_0 \frac{d\Phi_E}{dt}\), where \(\Phi_E\) is the electric flux. It accounts for the changing electric field acting as a source of the magnetic field.
13. The ozone layer in the earth's atmosphere absorbs:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (c) Ultraviolet radiations
The stratospheric ozone layer effectively absorbs the vast majority of harmful high-frequency Ultraviolet (UV-B and UV-C) radiations emitted by the sun, protecting life on Earth from radiation damage.
14. TV remote controls use:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (b) Infrared waves
Infrared (IR) waves are commonly used in domestic electronic applications such as TV remote controls, burglar alarms, and night vision devices. The remote has a small LED that flashes IR light to transmit signals to the TV.
15. In an EM wave, the average energy density of the electric field (\(u_E\)) and magnetic field (\(u_B\)) are related as:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (c) \(u_E = u_B\)
In an electromagnetic wave, the energy is equally distributed between the electric and magnetic fields. The average electric energy density (\(\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_0 E^2\)) is equal to the average magnetic energy density (\(\frac{1}{2\mu_0} B^2\)).
16. Electromagnetic waves are deflected by:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (d) Neither electric nor magnetic fields
Electromagnetic waves consist of uncharged particles (photons) or oscillating fields. Since they carry no net electric charge, they are not deflected by external electric or magnetic fields.
17. Which of the following relates wavelength (\(\lambda\)), frequency (\(\nu\)), and speed of an EM wave (\(c\))?
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (b) \(c = \nu \lambda\)
For any wave, the wave speed is the product of its frequency and wavelength. For electromagnetic waves propagating in vacuum, this universal wave equation is expressed as \(c = \nu \lambda\).
18. The wavelength range of the visible spectrum is approximately:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (b) 400 nm to 700 nm
The visible spectrum, which is the portion of the EM spectrum that human eyes can detect, lies roughly between 400 nm (violet) and 700 nm (red) according to standard NCERT data.
19. The Greenhouse effect is mainly due to:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (c) Infrared rays
Infrared rays, also known as heat waves, are absorbed by greenhouse gases (like \(CO_2, CH_4\)) in the atmosphere. The earth absorbs short-wavelength radiation from the sun and re-radiates it as longer-wavelength infrared radiation, which gets trapped, warming the earth.
20. Which waves are produced by the radioactive decay of the nucleus?
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (c) Gamma rays
Gamma rays (\(\gamma\)-rays) are high-energy photons emitted during the radioactive decay of atomic nuclei when an excited nucleus transitions to a lower energy state.
21. The generalized Ampere's circuital law (Ampere-Maxwell law) is given by:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (b) \(\oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0 (I_c + \epsilon_0 \frac{d\Phi_E}{dt})\)
Maxwell modified Ampere's law by adding the displacement current term (\(I_d\)) to the conduction current (\(I_c\)) to ensure logical consistency when fields are time-dependent. Thus, \(\oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0(I_c + I_d)\).
22. If an EM wave is propagating along the +z-axis and the electric field is along the +x-axis, then the magnetic field is along the:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (a) +y-axis
The direction of propagation is given by \(\vec{E} \times \vec{B}\). Here, direction is \(\hat{k}\) (z-axis) and \(\vec{E}\) is \(\hat{i}\) (x-axis). Let \(\vec{B}\) be \(\hat{j}\) (y-axis). Then, \(\hat{i} \times \hat{j} = \hat{k}\), which satisfies the condition.
23. The quantity \(\sqrt{\mu_0 / \epsilon_0}\) has the unit of:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (b) Resistance (Ohm)
The quantity \(\sqrt{\frac{\mu_0}{\epsilon_0}}\) is called the characteristic impedance of free space. Its value is approximately \(377 \Omega\), and its unit is Ohms (\(\Omega\)).
24. Cellular phones transmit voice communications using:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (b) Ultra High Frequency (UHF) radio waves
Cellular phones utilize ultra-high frequency (UHF) radio waves (and microwaves) to transmit information. This band provides the necessary bandwidth for carrying audio and data signals over the cellular network.
25. Which waves are heavily used in the study of crystal structures?
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (a) X-rays
The wavelength of X-rays is of the same order of magnitude as the interatomic spacing in crystals (\(\sim 1 \text{ \AA}\)). Due to this, X-rays exhibit diffraction when passed through crystals (Bragg's Law), revealing their structural arrangement.
26. Water purifiers commonly use which of the following to kill bacteria?
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (c) UV rays
Ultraviolet (UV) rays have sufficient energy to destroy the DNA of microorganisms, bacteria, and viruses, thereby rendering them inactive. This makes UV radiation highly effective for water sterilization.
27. The speed of an electromagnetic wave in vacuum is independent of its:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (d) All of these
The speed of an EM wave in vacuum is an absolute constant (\(c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}\)). It does not depend on the wave's frequency, wavelength, or intensity.
28. While a capacitor is charging, the conduction current \(I_c\) and displacement current \(I_d\) are related as:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (c) \(I_c = I_d\)
To maintain the continuity of current in a circuit, the conduction current \(I_c\) flowing through the connecting wires is exactly equal in magnitude to the displacement current \(I_d\) flowing between the plates of the capacitor.
29. The pressure exerted by an EM wave of intensity \(I\) on a perfectly reflecting surface is:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (b) \(2I/c\)
For a perfectly reflecting surface, the change in momentum is double that of an absorbing surface (since the wave bounces back). Therefore, the radiation pressure is \(P = \frac{2I}{c}\).
30. LASIK eye surgery utilizes highly focused:
[1 Mark]
Correct Answer: (c) Ultraviolet lasers
LASIK eye surgery employs Excimer lasers, which operate in the ultraviolet (UV) range. UV lasers are capable of breaking molecular bonds in the cornea with extreme precision without causing thermal damage to surrounding tissue.
