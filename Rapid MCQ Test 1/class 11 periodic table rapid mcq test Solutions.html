1.The modern periodic law states that the physical and chemical properties of elements are a periodic function of their:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) Atomic number
Proposed by Henry Moseley, it established that the fundamental property determining an element's characteristics is its atomic number (Z), not atomic mass.
2.The IUPAC name of the element with atomic number 119 is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: A) Ununennium
Using IUPAC roots for atomic numbers > 100: 1 = un, 1 = un, 9 = enn. Suffix is -ium. Hence, Un-un-enn-ium.
3.Who formulated the Law of Octaves?
[1 Mark]
Answer: C) Newlands
John Newlands arranged elements in increasing order of atomic weights and noted that every eighth element had properties similar to the first, akin to musical octaves.
4.Eka-Aluminium, predicted by Mendeleev, was later discovered as:
[1 Mark]
Answer: C) Gallium
Mendeleev left gaps for undiscovered elements. The element below Aluminium in group 13 was named Eka-Aluminium, which was later discovered and named Gallium (Ga).
5.An element with atomic number 114 belongs to which group?
[1 Mark]
Answer: A) Group 14
For elements with Z > 100, the last two digits generally correspond to the group number in the p-block. $Z=114$ has configuration $[Rn] 5f^{14} 6d^{10} 7s^2 7p^2$, placing it in Group $10+2+2 = 14$.
6.The general outer electronic configuration of d-block elements is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) (n-1)d¹⁻¹⁰ ns⁰⁻²
In d-block elements, the differentiating electron enters the $(n-1)d$ subshell. The $ns$ orbital can have 0 to 2 electrons (e.g., Pd has $5s^0$, Cr has $4s^1$).
7.Which block contains metals, non-metals, and metalloids?
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) p-block
The p-block (Groups 13-18) is the most diverse block. The s, d, and f blocks exclusively contain metals.
8.The element with the electronic configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁵ belongs to:
[1 Mark]
Answer: A) Period 3, Group 17
Principal quantum number $n = 3$ indicates Period 3. Outer configuration $3s^2 3p^5$ gives $2+5 = 7$ valence electrons. Group = $10 + 7 = 17$ (Halogens). The element is Chlorine.
9.Elements in which the 4f orbitals are progressively filled are called:
[1 Mark]
Answer: C) Lanthanoids
Lanthanoids involve the filling of $4f$ orbitals (Z=58 to 71). Actinoids involve the filling of $5f$ orbitals.
10.The elements of Group 1 and Group 2 are collectively known as:
[1 Mark]
Answer: C) s-block elements
The last electron in Group 1 ($ns^1$) and Group 2 ($ns^2$) enters the s-orbital.
11.Which of the following has the largest atomic radius?
[1 Mark]
Answer: A) Li
Lithium, Beryllium, Boron, and Carbon belong to Period 2. Atomic size decreases across a period from left to right due to increased effective nuclear charge. Hence, Li is the largest.
12.As we move from left to right in a period, the atomic radius generally:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) Decreases
Across a period, electrons are added to the same principal shell, but nuclear charge increases, pulling the electron cloud closer to the nucleus.
13.Among the following, the species with the smallest size is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: C) Al³⁺
$\text{Na}^+, \text{Mg}^{2+}, \text{Al}^{3+}$ are isoelectronic (10 electrons each).
Size is inversely proportional to nuclear charge (Z). Al (Z=13) has highest charge, pulling the 10 electrons most strongly, resulting in smallest size.
14.An isoelectronic series contains species with the same:
[1 Mark]
Answer: C) Number of electrons
"Iso" means equal. Isoelectronic species are atoms/ions having identical number of electrons but different nuclear charges (protons).
15.Which is the correct order of increasing size?
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) Cl⁺ < Cl < Cl⁻
For a given element, a cation is always smaller due to greater effective nuclear charge, and an anion is always larger due to increased electron-electron repulsion. Size order: Cation < Neutral Atom < Anion.
16.The cation is always smaller than its parent atom due to:
[1 Mark]
Answer: A) Increased effective nuclear charge
When an electron is lost, electron-electron repulsion decreases while the number of protons remains the same. The nucleus pulls the remaining electrons more tightly inward.
17.Identify the wrong statement regarding isoelectronic species:
[1 Mark]
Answer: D) All species have the same effective nuclear charge.
Isoelectronic species have the same number of electrons but different number of protons (Z). Therefore, their effective nuclear charge ($Z_{eff}$) varies.
18.Which of the following pairs is not isoelectronic?
[1 Mark]
Answer: C) Fe²⁺, Mn²⁺
$\text{Fe}^{2+}$ has $26-2=24$ electrons. $\text{Mn}^{2+}$ has $25-2=23$ electrons. They do not have the same number of electrons.
19.Down a group, the atomic radius increases primarily due to:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) Addition of new principal quantum shells
As you move down a group, new electron shells (higher principal quantum number, n) are added. This increase in size overrides the increase in nuclear charge due to shielding.
20.For noble gases, the measured radius is generally:
[1 Mark]
Answer: D) van der Waals radius
Noble gases do not form stable covalent bonds under normal conditions. Therefore, their size is measured via van der Waals radius (internuclear distance between non-bonded adjacent atoms).
21.The first ionization enthalpy is maximum for:
[1 Mark]
Answer: D) Noble gases
Noble gases possess a highly stable, completely filled octet ($ns^2 np^6$). Removing an electron disrupts this stable configuration, requiring a massive amount of energy.
22.Which of the following elements has the lowest first ionization enthalpy?
[1 Mark]
Answer: D) Rb
Ionization enthalpy decreases down a group due to increased size and shielding effect. Among Li, Na, K, Rb, Rubidium (Rb) is the lowest in the group and largest in size.
23.The correct order of first ionization enthalpy for Be, B, N, and O is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: A) B < Be < O < N
Normally IE increases across a period. Exceptions: Be ($2s^2$) > B ($2s^2 2p^1$) due to stable fully-filled $s$-orbital. N ($2p^3$) > O ($2p^4$) due to stable half-filled $p$-orbital in N.
24.Why is the first ionization enthalpy of Nitrogen greater than that of Oxygen?
[1 Mark]
Answer: C) Nitrogen has a stable half-filled 2p orbital.
Nitrogen has an exact half-filled configuration ($1s^2 2s^2 2p^3$), conferring extra stability based on Hund's Rule. Oxygen ($2p^4$) has interelectronic repulsion, making it easier to lose one electron.
25.The second ionization enthalpy is always:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) Greater than the first ionization enthalpy.
Removing an electron from a neutral atom requires energy ($IE_1$). Removing a second electron from a positive ion ($M^+$) requires significantly more energy because the effective nuclear charge pulling the remaining electrons is higher.
26.Which element shows an exceptionally high jump between 2nd and 3rd ionization enthalpies?
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) Mg
Magnesium has 2 valence electrons ($3s^2$). Removing them is relatively easy ($IE_1, IE_2$). The 3rd electron must be removed from the stable inner noble gas core ($2p^6$), resulting in a huge jump in $IE_3$.
27.Ionization enthalpy generally decreases down a group due to:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) Increase in shielding effect and atomic size.
Though nuclear charge increases down a group, the addition of inner shells increases the shielding effect and atomic radius, meaning valence electrons are held less tightly.
28.Which orbital is most penetrating towards the nucleus for a given principal quantum number?
[1 Mark]
Answer: A) s-orbital
Penetration effect order: $s > p > d > f$. An s-electron spends more time closer to the nucleus compared to electrons in other orbitals of the same principal shell.
29.The unit of Ionization Enthalpy is usually expressed in:
[1 Mark]
Answer: A) kJ/mol
Standard thermodynamic quantities like enthalpy are typically measured in kilojoules per mole (kJ mol⁻¹). Alternatively, it is expressed in electron-volts per atom (eV/atom).
30.For which species is the removal of an electron easiest?
[1 Mark]
Answer: D) S²⁻
Anions have excess electrons, causing high interelectronic repulsion and a lower effective nuclear charge. $S^{2-}$ has the highest negative charge, making it easiest to remove an electron (lowest ionization enthalpy).
31.The element with the highest negative electron gain enthalpy is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) Chlorine
While Fluorine is the most electronegative, Chlorine has the highest negative electron gain enthalpy due to its larger size, accommodating the incoming electron with less repulsion than the tiny F atom.
32.The electron gain enthalpy of noble gases is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: C) Positive
Noble gases have a stable $ns^2 np^6$ configuration. Adding an electron forces it into a higher principal energy level, requiring energy input. Hence, the enthalpy change is positive.
33.Why is the electron gain enthalpy of Fluorine less negative than that of Chlorine?
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) High interelectronic repulsion in the small 2p subshell of F.
Fluorine's atom is very small. The incoming electron experiences strong repulsion from the tightly packed 7 valence electrons in the small $2p$ orbitals.
34.Which group has the most negative electron gain enthalpy?
[1 Mark]
Answer: D) Group 17 (Halogens)
Halogens ($ns^2 np^5$) are one electron short of a stable noble gas configuration. They readily accept an electron and release a large amount of energy in the process.
35.As we move from left to right in a period, the electron gain enthalpy generally becomes:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) More negative
Across a period, effective nuclear charge increases and size decreases. The added electron is more strongly attracted by the nucleus, releasing more energy.
36.Which of the following statements is correct?
[1 Mark]
Answer: A) Second electron gain enthalpy is always positive.
Adding a second electron to a negatively charged ion (e.g., $O^- \rightarrow O^{2-}$) involves overcoming strong electrostatic repulsion between the anion and the incoming electron, requiring energy input.
37.Which of the following processes requires energy?
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) O⁻(g) + e⁻ → O²⁻(g)
This is the second electron gain enthalpy. As explained above, forcing an electron onto an already negatively charged $O^-$ ion requires energy to overcome electrostatic repulsion.
38.The correct order of electron gain enthalpy with a negative sign is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: A) O < S < F < Cl
Halogens (F, Cl) have more negative values than Chalcogens (O, S). Also, 3rd period elements (Cl, S) have more negative values than 2nd period counterparts (F, O) due to less inter-electronic repulsion.
39.Which factor does NOT heavily influence electron gain enthalpy?
[1 Mark]
Answer: D) Isotopic mass
Isotopic mass concerns the nucleus (neutrons) and has negligible effect on the chemical attraction of electrons. Atomic size, nuclear charge, and electron configuration govern periodic properties.
40.Nitrogen has a slightly positive/near-zero electron gain enthalpy because:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) It has a stable half-filled p-orbital.
Nitrogen's configuration is $1s^2 2s^2 2p^3$. Adding an electron disrupts the highly stable, symmetrical half-filled state, hence it strongly resists electron gain.
41.The most electronegative element in the periodic table is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: C) Fluorine
Fluorine has the highest tendency to attract shared electrons in a covalent bond due to its extremely small size and high effective nuclear charge. Assigned a value of 4.0 on the Pauling scale.
42.Electronegativity generally ________ down a group and ________ across a period.
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) Decreases, Increases
Down a group, size increases, and the nucleus's hold on shared electrons weakens. Across a period, size decreases and nuclear charge increases, strengthening the attraction.
43.Which scale is widely used to measure electronegativity?
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) Pauling Scale
Linus Pauling developed the most commonly used scale based on bond dissociation energies. Fluorine is assigned 4.0, Oxygen 3.5, etc.
44.The second most electronegative element is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) Oxygen
Oxygen is assigned an electronegativity of 3.5 on the Pauling scale, trailing only behind Fluorine (4.0).
45.Which of the following is considered a metalloid?
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) Silicon
Silicon sits on the border between metals and non-metals in the p-block and exhibits properties intermediate between both classes.
46.Which series of elements occupies the d-block?
[1 Mark]
Answer: A) Transition elements
The d-block elements (Groups 3-12) are termed transition elements because they represent a transition in properties from highly reactive s-block metals to less reactive p-block elements.
47.The effective nuclear charge (Z_eff) across a period:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) Increases
As electrons are added to the same principal shell, shielding ($S$) doesn't increase as rapidly as the nuclear charge ($Z$). Hence $Z_{eff} = Z - S$ increases.
48.Among the given ions, which one has the largest size?
[1 Mark]
Answer: A) N³⁻
$\text{N}^{3-}, \text{O}^{2-}, \text{F}^-, \text{Na}^+$ are isoelectronic (10 e⁻). Size is inversely proportional to atomic number. N (Z=7) has the lowest nuclear charge, exerting the weakest pull, making $\text{N}^{3-}$ the largest.
49.The minimum ionization energy is required for removing an electron from a:
[1 Mark]
Answer: D) 4s orbital
Among the choices given, the 4s orbital has the highest principal quantum number (n=4), meaning its electrons are furthest from the nucleus and experience the greatest shielding, hence requiring the least energy to remove.
50.Elements with atomic numbers greater than 92 are called:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) Transuranium elements
Elements beyond Uranium (Z=92) are synthetic, radioactive, and are collectively referred to as transuranium elements.
