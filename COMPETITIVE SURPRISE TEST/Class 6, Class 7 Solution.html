DETAILED SOLUTIONS: SCHOLARSHIP TEST PHASE I

Section A: Mathematics

1. Evaluate: \( 5 + 2 \times 3 - 8 \div 4 \)
[1 Mark]
b) 9
Using BODMAS rule: First Division: \( 8 \div 4 = 2 \). Next Multiplication: \( 2 \times 3 = 6 \). Next Addition/Subtraction: \( 5 + 6 - 2 = 11 - 2 = 9 \).
2. What is the sum of the first five prime numbers?
[1 Mark]
b) 28
The first five prime numbers are 2, 3, 5, 7, and 11. Their sum is \( 2 + 3 + 5 + 7 + 11 = 28 \). Remember, 1 is neither prime nor composite.
3. Find the ratio of 45 minutes to 1.5 hours in its simplest form.
[1 Mark]
a) 1:2
First, convert quantities to the same unit. 1.5 hours = \( 1.5 \times 60 \) minutes = 90 minutes. Ratio = \( \frac{45}{90} = \frac{1}{2} \) or 1:2.
4. If \( x - 15 = -2 \), what is the value of \( x \)?
[1 Mark]
c) 13
Transposing -15 to the right side changes its sign: \( x = -2 + 15 \). Therefore, \( x = 13 \).
5. A man walks 5 km North, turns right and walks 3 km, turns right again and walks 5 km. How far is he from his starting point?
[1 Mark]
b) 3 km
He walked North (up), turned right (East) for 3 km, then right again (South) for 5 km. He is exactly 3 km East of his starting point, forming three sides of a rectangle.
6. The perimeter of a square is 64 cm. What is its area?
[1 Mark]
b) 256 cm²
Perimeter of a square = 4 × side. So, \( 4 \times s = 64 \), meaning side \( s = 16 \) cm. Area = \( s \times s = 16 \times 16 = 256 \) cm².
7. Which of the following fractions is the largest?
[1 Mark]
b) \( \frac{7}{8} \)
Convert to decimals or find LCM. \( \frac{3}{4} = 0.75 \), \( \frac{7}{8} = 0.875 \), \( \frac{5}{6} \approx 0.833 \), \( \frac{2}{3} \approx 0.666 \). Thus, 7/8 is the largest.
8. Find the next number in the series: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, __
[1 Mark]
b) 42
The differences between consecutive terms are increasing even numbers: +4, +6, +8, +10. The next difference will be +12. So, \( 30 + 12 = 42 \).
9. If 15 pens cost ₹225, what is the cost of 8 pens?
[1 Mark]
a) ₹120
Unitary method: Cost of 1 pen = \( \frac{225}{15} = 15 \). Cost of 8 pens = \( 15 \times 8 = 120 \).
10. Two angles of a triangle are 45° and 65°. What is the third angle?
[1 Mark]
b) 70°
Angle sum property of a triangle states the sum is 180°. Third angle = \( 180° - (45° + 65°) = 180° - 110° = 70° \).
11. What is 25% of 400?
[1 Mark]
c) 100
\( 25\% \) is mathematically equivalent to \( \frac{1}{4} \). So, \( \frac{1}{4} \times 400 = 100 \).
12. Simplify: \( (-5) \times (-4) \times (-3) \)
[1 Mark]
b) -60
Multiplying two negative numbers gives a positive: \( (-5) \times (-4) = 20 \). Then, positive times negative gives negative: \( 20 \times (-3) = -60 \).
13. The LCM of 12 and 18 is:
[1 Mark]
c) 36
Multiples of 12: 12, 24, 36, 48... Multiples of 18: 18, 36, 54... The lowest common multiple is 36.
14. Express 0.045 as a fraction in simplest form.
[1 Mark]
a) \( \frac{9}{200} \)
\( 0.045 = \frac{45}{1000} \). Dividing both numerator and denominator by their HCF (5) gives \( \frac{9}{200} \).
15. A car travels at a speed of 60 km/h. How long will it take to cover 150 km?
[1 Mark]
b) 2.5 hours
Time = Distance / Speed. Time = \( \frac{150}{60} = \frac{15}{6} = 2.5 \) hours.
16. If \( a = 2 \) and \( b = 3 \), find the value of \( a^2 + b^2 \).
[1 Mark]
b) 13
Substitute the values: \( (2)^2 + (3)^2 = 4 + 9 = 13 \).
17. How many lines of symmetry does a regular hexagon have?
[1 Mark]
c) 6
A regular polygon with 'n' sides has 'n' lines of symmetry. A hexagon has 6 sides, hence 6 lines of symmetry (3 connecting opposite vertices, 3 connecting midpoints of opposite sides).

Section B: Science

18. Which part of the plant is primarily responsible for photosynthesis?
[1 Mark]
c) Leaf
Leaves contain chlorophyll, which traps sunlight to synthesize food from carbon dioxide and water, making them the "food factories" of the plant.
19. The standard unit of measuring force is:
[1 Mark]
b) Newton
The SI unit of force is the Newton (N), named after Sir Isaac Newton in recognition of his work on classical mechanics.
20. Which vitamin is produced in the human body by exposure to sunlight?
[1 Mark]
d) Vitamin D
Our body synthesizes Vitamin D when our skin is exposed to the ultraviolet (UV) rays present in sunlight.
21. The process of conversion of water vapor into liquid water is called:
[1 Mark]
b) Condensation
Condensation is the process where gas (vapor) cools and transforms back into a liquid state, commonly seen as dew or clouds.
22. Which of the following is a reversible change?
[1 Mark]
b) Melting of ice
Melting ice results in liquid water, which can easily be frozen back into ice. Baking, burning, and rusting alter the chemical composition permanently.
23. A device that breaks or completes a circuit is called a:
[1 Mark]
c) Switch
A switch provides control over an electric circuit, allowing you to easily open (break) or close (complete) the path for current flow.
24. Which acid is naturally found in lemons and oranges?
[1 Mark]
c) Citric acid
Citrus fruits like lemons, oranges, and grapefruits are natural sources of citric acid, giving them their characteristic sour taste.
25. Animals that eat both plants and other animals are called:
[1 Mark]
c) Omnivores
Omnivores (like humans, bears, and crows) have a diverse diet consisting of both plant matter and animal tissue.
26. Sound cannot travel through:
[1 Mark]
d) Vacuum
Sound is a mechanical wave that requires a material medium (solid, liquid, or gas) to propagate. It cannot pass through the empty space of a vacuum.
27. The innermost part of a flower, containing the female reproductive organs, is the:
[1 Mark]
c) Pistil
The pistil (or carpel) is centrally located in the flower and consists of the stigma, style, and ovary (which contains the ovules).
28. Which planet is known as the "Red Planet"?
[1 Mark]
b) Mars
Mars appears red due to the widespread presence of iron oxide (rust) dust on its surface and in its thin atmosphere.
29. The gas most abundant in Earth's atmosphere is:
[1 Mark]
c) Nitrogen
Nitrogen makes up approximately 78% of the Earth's atmosphere by volume, while oxygen makes up about 21%.
30. Which of these materials is a good conductor of electricity?
[1 Mark]
d) Copper
Metals like copper have free electrons that easily allow the flow of electric current. Wood, plastic, and rubber are insulators.
31. The joint in the human shoulder is an example of a:
[1 Mark]
c) Ball and socket joint
The rounded head of the arm bone fits into a cup-like cavity of the shoulder bone, allowing a wide range of motion in almost all directions.
32. Blue litmus paper turns red when introduced to a substance. The substance is:
[1 Mark]
a) Acidic
Litmus is a natural indicator. Acids turn blue litmus paper red, while bases turn red litmus paper blue.
33. What is the SI unit of electric current?
[1 Mark]
b) Ampere
The Ampere (often shortened to "Amp") is the standard unit of measurement for the rate of electron flow or current in an electrical conductor.
34. Which of the following is responsible for carrying oxygen in human blood?
[1 Mark]
d) Hemoglobin
Hemoglobin is the iron-containing protein pigment present in red blood cells that binds to oxygen and transports it throughout the body.

Section C: Social Studies

35. The Great Bath, an ancient structure, was found in which Indus Valley city?
[1 Mark]
c) Mohenjo-Daro
The Great Bath is one of the most famous structures of the Indus Valley Civilization, discovered in the ruins of Mohenjo-Daro (present-day Pakistan).
36. The network of intersecting lines of latitude and longitude is called a:
[1 Mark]
b) Grid
The geographic grid is the intersecting pattern formed by parallels of latitude and meridians of longitude used to pinpoint exact locations on Earth.
37. Which emperor is famous for adopting 'Dhamma' after the Kalinga war?
[1 Mark]
c) Ashoka
Emperor Ashoka of the Mauryan dynasty renounced violence and embraced Buddhism and the policy of 'Dhamma' (righteousness) after witnessing the massive bloodshed of the Kalinga War.
38. The local government at the village level in India is known as:
[1 Mark]
b) Gram Panchayat
The Gram Panchayat is the foundational institution of the Panchayati Raj system, responsible for local self-governance at the village level in India.
39. The imaginary line that divides the Earth into Northern and Southern hemispheres is:
[1 Mark]
c) Equator
The Equator is the 0° line of latitude running horizontally around the exact middle of the Earth.
40. Who wrote the book 'Arthashastra'?
[1 Mark]
b) Chanakya
The Arthashastra, an ancient Indian treatise on statecraft, economic policy, and military strategy, is traditionally attributed to Chanakya (also known as Kautilya).
41. A form of government where citizens elect their representatives is called a:
[1 Mark]
c) Democracy
In a representative democracy, sovereign power rests with the people, who choose their leaders through regular, free, and fair elections.
42. The lowest layer of Earth's atmosphere, where weather occurs, is the:
[1 Mark]
b) Troposphere
The troposphere begins at the Earth's surface. It contains almost all the water vapor and dust in the atmosphere, making it the zone where all weather events happen.
43. Which movement of the Earth causes changes in seasons?
[1 Mark]
b) Revolution
The Earth's revolution (orbit) around the Sun, combined with the tilt of its axis, results in varying amounts of sunlight reaching different hemispheres, causing seasons.
44. The historical period before written records were kept is known as:
[1 Mark]
b) Prehistory
Prehistory covers the vast span of human existence (like the Stone Age) before the invention of writing systems allowed for recorded history.
45. Indian Standard Time (IST) is calculated from the longitude of:
[1 Mark]
c) 82.5° E
The standard meridian of India is 82°30'E (82.5°E), which passes near Mirzapur in Uttar Pradesh. The time here is taken as standard for the whole country.
46. Which document acts as the supreme law of a democratic country like India?
[1 Mark]
b) The Constitution
A Constitution is the foundational legal document laying out the fundamental political code, structure, procedures, powers, and duties of government institutions and citizens' rights.
47. The manuscript texts of ancient India were usually written on:
[1 Mark]
b) Palm leaves
Before the widespread use of paper, ancient Indian scholars inscribed manuscripts heavily on prepared palm leaves or the specially prepared bark of birch trees.
48. A narrow zone of the earth where land, water, and air interact to support life is the:
[1 Mark]
d) Biosphere
The biosphere represents the sphere of life; it is the unique intersection of the lithosphere (land), hydrosphere (water), and atmosphere (air).
49. The founder of Buddhism was:
[1 Mark]
b) Siddhartha Gautama
Buddhism was founded in ancient India by Siddhartha Gautama, a prince who achieved enlightenment and became known as the Buddha ("The Awakened One").
50. If it is 12:00 Noon at the Prime Meridian (0°), what is the time at 15° East longitude?
[1 Mark]
c) 1:00 PM
The Earth rotates 360° in 24 hours, meaning 15° every 1 hour. Time advances by 1 hour for every 15° moving East. So, 12:00 Noon + 1 hour = 1:00 PM.
