Q1.
Find the greatest number of six digits exactly divisible by 18, 24, and 36.
[2 Marks]
Step 1: First, find the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of 18, 24, and 36.
Prime factorization:
\( 18 = 2 \times 3^2 \)
\( 24 = 2^3 \times 3 \)
\( 36 = 2^2 \times 3^2 \)
LCM(18, 24, 36) = \( 2^3 \times 3^2 = 8 \times 9 = 72 \).
Step 2: The greatest six-digit number is 999999.
Step 3: Divide 999999 by 72 to find the remainder.
\( 999999 \div 72 = 13888 \) with a remainder of \( 63 \).
Step 4: Subtract the remainder from the greatest six-digit number.
\[ 999999 - 63 = 999936 \]
Final Answer: The greatest six-digit number is 999936.
Q2.
Determine whether the points \( (a, a) \), \( (-a, -a) \) and \( (-a\sqrt{3}, a\sqrt{3}) \) form an equilateral triangle.
[2 Marks]
Step 1: Let the points be \( A(a, a) \), \( B(-a, -a) \) and \( C(-a\sqrt{3}, a\sqrt{3}) \). Use distance formula \( d = \sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2 + (y_2-y_1)^2} \).
Step 2: Find length AB:
\[ AB^2 = (-a - a)^2 + (-a - a)^2 = (-2a)^2 + (-2a)^2 = 4a^2 + 4a^2 = 8a^2 \]
Step 3: Find length BC:
\[ BC^2 = (-a\sqrt{3} - (-a))^2 + (a\sqrt{3} - (-a))^2 \] \[ = a^2(-\sqrt{3} + 1)^2 + a^2(\sqrt{3} + 1)^2 \] \[ = a^2(3 + 1 - 2\sqrt{3}) + a^2(3 + 1 + 2\sqrt{3}) \] \[ = a^2(4 - 2\sqrt{3} + 4 + 2\sqrt{3}) = 8a^2 \]
Step 4: Find length AC:
\[ AC^2 = (-a\sqrt{3} - a)^2 + (a\sqrt{3} - a)^2 \] \[ = a^2(-\sqrt{3} - 1)^2 + a^2(\sqrt{3} - 1)^2 \] \[ = a^2(3 + 1 + 2\sqrt{3}) + a^2(3 + 1 - 2\sqrt{3}) = 8a^2 \]
Step 5: Since \( AB^2 = BC^2 = AC^2 \), it implies \( AB = BC = AC \).
Final Answer: Yes, the points form an equilateral triangle because all three sides are equal.

Here, \( a = (k+4) \), \( b = (k+1) \), and \( c = 1 \).

Q5.
If \( p = ab^2 \) and \( q = a^3b \); \( a, b \) being primes, prove \( \text{LCM}(p, q) \times \text{HCF}(p, q) = p \times q \).
[2 Marks]
Step 1: Find the Highest Common Factor (HCF). Take the smallest power of each common prime factor.
\[ \text{HCF}(p, q) = \text{HCF}(ab^2, a^3b) = a^1 \times b^1 = ab \]
Step 2: Find the Least Common Multiple (LCM). Take the highest power of each prime factor involved.
\[ \text{LCM}(p, q) = \text{LCM}(ab^2, a^3b) = a^3 \times b^2 = a^3b^2 \]
Step 3: Calculate LHS: \( \text{LCM}(p, q) \times \text{HCF}(p, q) \).
\[ \text{LHS} = (a^3b^2) \times (ab) = a^{(3+1)} \times b^{(2+1)} = a^4b^3 \]
Step 4: Calculate RHS: Product of the numbers \( p \times q \).
\[ \text{RHS} = p \times q = (ab^2) \times (a^3b) = a^{(1+3)} \times b^{(2+1)} = a^4b^3 \]
Step 5: Compare LHS and RHS.
\[ \text{LHS} = \text{RHS} \]
Final Answer: Hence Proved.

Q8.
Prove that \( \sqrt{p} + \sqrt{q} \) is an irrational number, given that \( p \) and \( q \) are prime numbers.
[3 Marks]
Step 1: Let us assume, to the contrary, that \( \sqrt{p} + \sqrt{q} \) is a rational number, say \( a \).
\[ \sqrt{p} + \sqrt{q} = a \] \[ \sqrt{p} = a - \sqrt{q} \]
Step 2: Squaring both sides:
\[ p = (a - \sqrt{q})^2 \] \[ p = a^2 + q - 2a\sqrt{q} \]
Step 3: Rearranging the terms to isolate the square root:
\[ 2a\sqrt{q} = a^2 + q - p \] \[ \sqrt{q} = \frac{a^2 + q - p}{2a} \]
Step 4: Since \( p \) and \( q \) are prime integers and \( a \) is rational, \( \frac{a^2 + q - p}{2a} \) is a rational number.
This implies that \( \sqrt{q} \) is a rational number.
Step 5: However, we know that the square root of a prime number is irrational. Therefore, \( \sqrt{q} \) is an irrational number.
This contradicts our result in Step 4.
Step 6: The contradiction has arisen because of our incorrect assumption that \( \sqrt{p} + \sqrt{q} \) is rational.
Final Answer: Hence, \( \sqrt{p} + \sqrt{q} \) is an irrational number.
Q9.
Solve for \( x \): \( \frac{1}{a + b + x} = \frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} + \frac{1}{x} \)
[3 Marks]
Step 1: Transpose the term \( 1/x \) to the LHS.
\[ \frac{1}{a + b + x} - \frac{1}{x} = \frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} \]
Step 2: Take the LCM on both sides.
\[ \frac{x - (a + b + x)}{x(a + b + x)} = \frac{b + a}{ab} \] \[ \frac{x - a - b - x}{x(a + b + x)} = \frac{a + b}{ab} \] \[ \frac{-(a + b)}{x(a + b + x)} = \frac{a + b}{ab} \]
Step 3: Since \( a+b \neq 0 \), we can divide both sides by \( (a+b) \).
\[ \frac{-1}{x^2 + ax + bx} = \frac{1}{ab} \]
Step 4: Cross-multiply.
\[ -ab = x^2 + ax + bx \] \[ x^2 + ax + bx + ab = 0 \]
Step 5: Factorize by grouping.
\[ x(x + a) + b(x + a) = 0 \] \[ (x + a)(x + b) = 0 \]
Step 6: Equate each factor to zero.
\[ x + a = 0 \implies x = -a \] \[ x + b = 0 \implies x = -b \]
Final Answer: The values of \( x \) are \( -a \) and \( -b \).

Q11.
Places A and B are 100 km apart. Cars start from A and B. Same direction = 5 hours to meet, Opposite direction = 1 hour. Find speeds.
[5 Marks]
Step 1: Let the speed of the car starting from A be \( x \text{ km/h} \) and from B be \( y \text{ km/h} \). Assume \( x > y \).
Step 2: Case I (Same direction):
Relative speed = \( (x - y) \text{ km/h} \). Distance = 100 km. Time = 5 hours.
\[ \text{Distance} = \text{Speed} \times \text{Time} \] \[ 100 = (x - y) \times 5 \] \[ x - y = 20 \quad \text{--- (Eq 1)} \]
Step 3: Case II (Opposite direction):
Relative speed = \( (x + y) \text{ km/h} \). Distance = 100 km. Time = 1 hour.
\[ 100 = (x + y) \times 1 \] \[ x + y = 100 \quad \text{--- (Eq 2)} \]
Step 4: Solve the linear equations. Add (Eq 1) and (Eq 2).
\[ (x - y) + (x + y) = 20 + 100 \] \[ 2x = 120 \implies x = 60 \]
Step 5: Substitute \( x = 60 \) in (Eq 2).
\[ 60 + y = 100 \implies y = 40 \]
Final Answer: Speed of car A is 60 km/h and car B is 40 km/h.
Q12.
Find area of rhombus whose vertices in order are \( (3, 0) \), \( (4, 5) \), \( (-1, 4) \) and \( (-2, -1) \).
[5 Marks]
Step 1: Area of a rhombus = \( \frac{1}{2} \times (\text{Product of its diagonals}) = \frac{1}{2} \times d_1 \times d_2 \).
Let the vertices be \( A(3, 0) \), \( B(4, 5) \), \( C(-1, 4) \) and \( D(-2, -1) \).
The diagonals are AC and BD.
Step 2: Find length of diagonal AC using distance formula.
\[ AC = \sqrt{(-1 - 3)^2 + (4 - 0)^2} \] \[ AC = \sqrt{(-4)^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{16 + 16} = \sqrt{32} = 4\sqrt{2} \text{ units} \]
Step 3: Find length of diagonal BD.
\[ BD = \sqrt{(-2 - 4)^2 + (-1 - 5)^2} \] \[ BD = \sqrt{(-6)^2 + (-6)^2} = \sqrt{36 + 36} = \sqrt{72} = 6\sqrt{2} \text{ units} \]
Step 4: Calculate Area.
\[ \text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times AC \times BD \] \[ \text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times 4\sqrt{2} \times 6\sqrt{2} \] \[ \text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times 24 \times 2 = 24 \text{ sq units} \]
Final Answer: The area of the rhombus is 24 square units.
Q13.
A train travels 360 km. If speed had been 5 km/h more, it would have taken 1 hour less. Find speed.
[5 Marks]
Step 1: Let the original speed of the train be \( x \text{ km/h} \). Distance = 360 km.
Original time taken \( t_1 = \frac{360}{x} \text{ hours} \).
Step 2: New speed = \( (x + 5) \text{ km/h} \).
New time taken \( t_2 = \frac{360}{x + 5} \text{ hours} \).
Step 3: Formulate the equation. The difference in time is 1 hour.
\[ t_1 - t_2 = 1 \] \[ \frac{360}{x} - \frac{360}{x + 5} = 1 \]
Step 4: Solve the equation.
\[ 360 \left( \frac{x + 5 - x}{x(x + 5)} \right) = 1 \] \[ \frac{360 \times 5}{x^2 + 5x} = 1 \] \[ 1800 = x^2 + 5x \] \[ x^2 + 5x - 1800 = 0 \]
Step 5: Factorize the quadratic equation. Numbers multiplying to -1800 and adding to 5 are 45 and -40.
\[ x^2 + 45x - 40x - 1800 = 0 \] \[ x(x + 45) - 40(x + 45) = 0 \] \[ (x - 40)(x + 45) = 0 \]
Step 6: Determine roots. \( x = 40 \) or \( x = -45 \). Since speed cannot be negative, \( x = 40 \).
Final Answer: The original speed of the train is 40 km/h.

\[ \frac{x + 2}{y + 2} = \frac{9}{11} \] \[ 11(x + 2) = 9(y + 2) \implies 11x + 22 = 9y + 18 \] \[ 11x - 9y = -4 \quad \text{--- (Eq 1)} \]
Step 3: Case 2: Add 3 to both.
\[ \frac{x + 3}{y + 3} = \frac{5}{6} \] \[ 6(x + 3) = 5(y + 3) \implies 6x + 18 = 5y + 15 \] \[ 6x - 5y = -3 \quad \text{--- (Eq 2)} \]
Step 4: Solve using substitution. From (Eq 2): \( 6x = 5y - 3 \implies x = \frac{5y - 3}{6} \)
Substitute in (Eq 1):
\[ 11 \left( \frac{5y - 3}{6} \right) - 9y = -4 \] \[ \frac{55y - 33}{6} - 9y = -4 \]
Multiply by 6:
\[ 55y - 33 - 54y = -24 \] \[ y = -24 + 33 \implies y = 9 \]
Step 5: Find x.
\[ x = \frac{5(9) - 3}{6} = \frac{45 - 3}{6} = \frac{42}{6} = 7 \]

Q15.
Find roots using completing the square: \( 2x^2 - 7x + 3 = 0 \)
[5 Marks]
Step 1: Divide the entire equation by the coefficient of \( x^2 \) (which is 2) to make it 1.
\[ x^2 - \frac{7}{2}x + \frac{3}{2} = 0 \]
Step 2: Move the constant term to the right side.
\[ x^2 - \frac{7}{2}x = -\frac{3}{2} \]
Step 3: Take half of the coefficient of \( x \), which is \( -\frac{7}{4} \), square it, and add it to both sides.
\[ x^2 - \frac{7}{2}x + \left(-\frac{7}{4}\right)^2 = -\frac{3}{2} + \left(-\frac{7}{4}\right)^2 \] \[ x^2 - \frac{7}{2}x + \frac{49}{16} = -\frac{3}{2} + \frac{49}{16} \]
Step 4: The left side is now a perfect square. Simplify the right side.
\[ \left(x - \frac{7}{4}\right)^2 = \frac{-24 + 49}{16} \] \[ \left(x - \frac{7}{4}\right)^2 = \frac{25}{16} \]
Step 5: Take the square root of both sides.
\[ x - \frac{7}{4} = \pm \frac{5}{4} \]
Step 6: Solve for x.
\[ x = \frac{7}{4} + \frac{5}{4} = \frac{12}{4} = 3 \] \[ \text{Or, } x = \frac{7}{4} - \frac{5}{4} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2} \]
Final Answer: The roots are \( 3 \) and \( 1/2 \).
