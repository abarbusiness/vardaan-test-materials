Detailed Solutions: Minor Test 12

Class: 7 ICSE | Subject: Mathematics | Topic: Profit and Loss

SECTION A (2 Marks Each)

1. Find the profit or loss percent if Cost Price (CP) = ₹400 and Selling Price (SP) = ₹460.
[2 Marks]
Given: CP = ₹400, SP = ₹460.
Since SP > CP, there is a profit.
\[ \text{Profit} = \text{SP} - \text{CP} = 460 - 400 = \text{₹60} \]
\[ \text{Profit Percent} = \left( \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 \right)\% \]
\[ \text{Profit Percent} = \left( \frac{60}{400} \times 100 \right)\% = 15\% \]
Final Answer: The profit percent is 15%.
2. A shopkeeper buys a toy for ₹250 and sells it at a loss of 12%. Find the selling price of the toy.
[2 Marks]
Given: CP = ₹250, Loss% = 12%.
Method 1: Finding loss amount first.
\[ \text{Loss} = 12\% \text{ of CP} = \frac{12}{100} \times 250 = \text{₹30} \]
\[ \text{SP} = \text{CP} - \text{Loss} = 250 - 30 = \text{₹220} \]
Final Answer: The selling price of the toy is ₹220.
3. What is the cost price of an article which is sold for ₹840 at a profit of 5%?
[2 Marks]
Given: SP = ₹840, Profit% = 5%.
Using the formula for CP when SP and Profit% are given:
\[ \text{CP} = \left( \frac{100}{100 + \text{Profit}\%} \right) \times \text{SP} \]
\[ \text{CP} = \left( \frac{100}{100 + 5} \right) \times 840 = \left( \frac{100}{105} \right) \times 840 \]
\[ \text{CP} = \left( \frac{20}{21} \right) \times 840 = 20 \times 40 = 800 \]
Final Answer: The cost price of the article is ₹800.
4. Define 'Overhead Expenses'. How are they treated while calculating profit or loss?
[2 Marks]
Definition: Overhead expenses are the additional costs incurred by a buyer on an article after purchasing it, such as transportation, repairs, labor charges, or taxes.
Treatment: Overhead expenses are always added to the original Cost Price (CP) to calculate the Total Cost Price (or Effective Cost Price). Profit or loss is calculated on this Total Cost Price.
\[ \text{Total CP} = \text{Original CP} + \text{Overhead Expenses} \]
5. By selling a pen for ₹45, a shopkeeper loses ₹5. Find his loss percentage.
[2 Marks]
Given: SP = ₹45, Loss = ₹5.
First, calculate the Cost Price (CP):
\[ \text{CP} = \text{SP} + \text{Loss} = 45 + 5 = \text{₹50} \]
Now, calculate the Loss Percentage:
\[ \text{Loss Percent} = \left( \frac{\text{Loss}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 \right)\% = \left( \frac{5}{50} \times 100 \right)\% = 10\% \]
Final Answer: The shopkeeper's loss percentage is 10%.

SECTION B (4 Marks Each)

6. A man bought 2 dozen eggs for ₹84. Since 4 of them were broken, he sold the remaining at ₹4 per egg. Find his total profit or loss and the profit/loss percentage.
[4 Marks]
Total eggs bought = 2 dozen = \( 2 \times 12 = 24 \) eggs.
Total Cost Price (CP) of 24 eggs = ₹84.
Number of broken eggs = 4. Remaining good eggs = \( 24 - 4 = 20 \) eggs.
Selling Price (SP) of 1 egg = ₹4.
\[ \text{Total SP of 20 eggs} = 20 \times 4 = \text{₹80} \]
Since Total CP (₹84) > Total SP (₹80), he suffers a loss.
\[ \text{Total Loss} = \text{CP} - \text{SP} = 84 - 80 = \text{₹4} \]
\[ \text{Loss Percent} = \left( \frac{\text{Loss}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 \right)\% = \left( \frac{4}{84} \times 100 \right)\% = \left( \frac{100}{21} \right)\% \]
\[ \text{Loss Percent} = 4\frac{16}{21}\% \approx 4.76\% \]
Final Answer: Total loss is ₹4, and Loss percent is \( 4\frac{16}{21}\% \).
7. The selling price of 12 pens is equal to the cost price of 15 pens. Find the gain percent.
[4 Marks]
Let the Cost Price (CP) of 1 pen be ₹1.
\[ \text{CP of 12 pens} = 12 \times 1 = \text{₹12} \] \[ \text{CP of 15 pens} = 15 \times 1 = \text{₹15} \]
According to the question, SP of 12 pens = CP of 15 pens.
\[ \text{SP of 12 pens} = \text{₹15} \]
Now we compare CP and SP for the same quantity (12 pens):
CP of 12 pens = ₹12, SP of 12 pens = ₹15. Since SP > CP, there is a profit.
\[ \text{Profit} = \text{SP} - \text{CP} = 15 - 12 = \text{₹3} \]
\[ \text{Gain Percent} = \left( \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 \right)\% = \left( \frac{3}{12} \times 100 \right)\% \] \[ \text{Gain Percent} = \left( \frac{1}{4} \times 100 \right)\% = 25\% \]
Final Answer: The gain percent is 25%.
8. A vendor bought lemons at 6 for ₹10 and sold them at 4 for ₹8. Find his gain or loss percent.
[4 Marks]
To find gain or loss, make the quantities same. Let's take the LCM of 6 and 4, which is 12.
Calculating CP for 12 lemons:
\[ \text{CP of 6 lemons} = \text{₹10} \implies \text{CP of 12 lemons} = \frac{10}{6} \times 12 = \text{₹20} \]
Calculating SP for 12 lemons:
\[ \text{SP of 4 lemons} = \text{₹8} \implies \text{SP of 12 lemons} = \frac{8}{4} \times 12 = 2 \times 12 = \text{₹24} \]
Comparing CP and SP for 12 lemons: SP (₹24) > CP (₹20), hence it is a gain.
\[ \text{Gain} = \text{SP} - \text{CP} = 24 - 20 = \text{₹4} \]
\[ \text{Gain Percent} = \left( \frac{\text{Gain}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 \right)\% = \left( \frac{4}{20} \times 100 \right)\% = 20\% \]
Final Answer: The vendor's gain percent is 20%.
9. Rahul sold two bicycles for ₹3,000 each. On one bicycle, he gained 20%, and on the other, he lost 20%. Find his total gain or loss percent in the whole transaction.
[4 Marks]
For the first bicycle (Gain of 20%):
SP = ₹3,000, Gain% = 20%
\[ \text{CP}_1 = \left( \frac{100}{100 + 20} \right) \times 3000 = \left( \frac{100}{120} \right) \times 3000 = \frac{5}{6} \times 3000 = \text{₹2,500} \]
For the second bicycle (Loss of 20%):
SP = ₹3,000, Loss% = 20%
\[ \text{CP}_2 = \left( \frac{100}{100 - 20} \right) \times 3000 = \left( \frac{100}{80} \right) \times 3000 = \frac{5}{4} \times 3000 = \text{₹3,750} \]
Total Transaction:
\[ \text{Total CP} = \text{CP}_1 + \text{CP}_2 = 2500 + 3750 = \text{₹6,250} \] \[ \text{Total SP} = 3000 + 3000 = \text{₹6,000} \]
Since Total CP > Total SP, it is a loss.
\[ \text{Total Loss} = 6250 - 6000 = \text{₹250} \] \[ \text{Loss Percent} = \left( \frac{250}{6250} \times 100 \right)\% = \left( \frac{1}{25} \times 100 \right)\% = 4\% \]
Final Answer: Rahul suffers a total loss of 4%.
10. A dealer buys a machine for ₹8,000. He spends ₹500 on its transport and ₹300 on installation. At what price must he sell it to gain 15%?
[4 Marks]
Initial Cost Price (CP) = ₹8,000.
Overhead expenses = Transport + Installation = ₹500 + ₹300 = ₹800.
\[ \text{Total CP} = \text{Initial CP} + \text{Overhead Expenses} = 8000 + 800 = \text{₹8,800} \]
Desired Gain% = 15%.
\[ \text{SP} = \left( \frac{100 + \text{Gain}\%}{100} \right) \times \text{Total CP} \] \[ \text{SP} = \left( \frac{100 + 15}{100} \right) \times 8800 = \frac{115}{100} \times 8800 = 115 \times 88 = \text{₹10,120} \]
Final Answer: He must sell the machine for ₹10,120 to gain 15%.

SECTION C (5 Marks Each)

11. By selling an article for ₹960, a man incurs a loss of 4%. At what price should he sell it to make a profit of 16%?
[5 Marks]
Step 1: Find the Cost Price (CP)
Given: Initial SP = ₹960, Loss% = 4%.
\[ \text{CP} = \left( \frac{100}{100 - \text{Loss}\%} \right) \times \text{SP} = \left( \frac{100}{100 - 4} \right) \times 960 \] \[ \text{CP} = \left( \frac{100}{96} \right) \times 960 = 100 \times 10 = \text{₹1,000} \]
Step 2: Find the New Selling Price for a 16% Profit
Given: CP = ₹1,000, Desired Profit% = 16%.
\[ \text{New Profit} = 16\% \text{ of } 1000 = \frac{16}{100} \times 1000 = \text{₹160} \] \[ \text{New SP} = \text{CP} + \text{Profit} = 1000 + 160 = \text{₹1,160} \]
Final Answer: He should sell the article for ₹1,160.
12. A grocer bought 50 kg of rice at ₹40 per kg and 50 kg of another variety at ₹60 per kg. He mixed the two varieties and sold the mixture at ₹55 per kg. Find his profit or loss percent on the whole transaction.
[5 Marks]
Step 1: Calculate Total Cost Price (CP)
\[ \text{CP of 1st variety} = 50 \text{ kg} \times 40\text{/kg} = \text{₹2,000} \] \[ \text{CP of 2nd variety} = 50 \text{ kg} \times 60\text{/kg} = \text{₹3,000} \] \[ \text{Total CP} = 2000 + 3000 = \text{₹5,000} \]
Total quantity of mixture = 50 kg + 50 kg = 100 kg.
Step 2: Calculate Total Selling Price (SP)
He sold the entire 100 kg mixture at ₹55 per kg.
\[ \text{Total SP} = 100 \text{ kg} \times 55\text{/kg} = \text{₹5,500} \]
Step 3: Calculate Profit and Profit Percent
Since Total SP > Total CP, there is a profit.
\[ \text{Profit} = \text{Total SP} - \text{Total CP} = 5500 - 5000 = \text{₹500} \] \[ \text{Profit Percent} = \left( \frac{500}{5000} \times 100 \right)\% = 10\% \]
Final Answer: The grocer makes a profit of 10% on the whole transaction.
13. If an article is sold at a gain of 5% instead of being sold at a loss of 5%, a man gets ₹20 more. Find the cost price of the article.
[5 Marks]
Let the Cost Price (CP) of the article be \( x \).
Case 1 (Loss of 5%):
\[ \text{SP}_1 = x - (5\% \text{ of } x) = x - 0.05x = 0.95x \]
Case 2 (Gain of 5%):
\[ \text{SP}_2 = x + (5\% \text{ of } x) = x + 0.05x = 1.05x \]
Forming the Equation:
According to the question, the difference between the two selling prices is ₹20.
\[ \text{SP}_2 - \text{SP}_1 = 20 \] \[ 1.05x - 0.95x = 20 \] \[ 0.10x = 20 \] \[ x = \frac{20}{0.10} = \frac{2000}{10} = 200 \]
Final Answer: The cost price of the article is ₹200.
14. Rohan bought a second-hand car for ₹1,20,000. He spent ₹15,000 on its repairs and ₹5,000 on new tires. He then sold it to his friend at a profit of 10%. His friend later sold it to another person at a loss of 5%. What did the final person pay for the car?
[5 Marks]
Step 1: Rohan's Transaction
\[ \text{Rohan's Total CP} = 1,20,000 \text{ (Purchase)} + 15,000 \text{ (Repairs)} + 5,000 \text{ (Tires)} = \text{₹1,40,000} \]
Rohan sells it at 10% profit.
\[ \text{Profit} = 10\% \text{ of } 1,40,000 = \text{₹14,000} \] \[ \text{Rohan's SP} = 1,40,000 + 14,000 = \text{₹1,54,000} \]
Step 2: Friend's Transaction
The friend buys the car for ₹1,54,000. So, Friend's CP = ₹1,54,000.
The friend sells it at a loss of 5%.
\[ \text{Loss} = 5\% \text{ of } 1,54,000 = \frac{5}{100} \times 1,54,000 = 5 \times 1540 = \text{₹7,700} \] \[ \text{Friend's SP} = \text{Friend's CP} - \text{Loss} = 1,54,000 - 7,700 = \text{₹1,46,300} \]
This Selling Price is exactly what the final person paid.
Final Answer: The final person paid ₹1,46,300 for the car.
