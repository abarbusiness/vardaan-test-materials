SECTION A (10 Marks)
Question 1 [2 Marks]
Write the absolute value of \( -15 \) and the additive inverse of \( 24 \).
Solution:
The absolute value is the distance of a number from zero on the number line, disregarding its sign.
Absolute value of \( -15 \) is \( |-15| = 15 \).
The additive inverse is the number that, when added to the given number, yields zero.
Additive inverse of \( 24 \) is \( -24 \).
Answer: Absolute Value = \( 15 \), Additive Inverse = \( -24 \)
Question 2 [2 Marks]
Without actual division, check if the number \( 3456 \) is divisible by \( 4 \) and \( 9 \) using divisibility rules.
Solution:
Divisibility by 4: A number is divisible by 4 if the number formed by its last two digits is divisible by 4.
Last two digits of \( 3456 \) are \( 56 \). Since \( 56 \div 4 = 14 \), the number is divisible by 4.
Divisibility by 9: A number is divisible by 9 if the sum of its digits is divisible by 9.
Sum of digits = \( 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 = 18 \). Since 18 is divisible by 9, the number is divisible by 9.
Answer: Yes, 3456 is divisible by both 4 and 9.
Question 3 [2 Marks]
Simplify the following using BODMAS rule: \( 18 - [5 + (6 \div 2)] \)
Solution:
According to BODMAS, we solve the inner round bracket first (Division).
\( 18 - [5 + (6 \div 2)] \)
\( = 18 - [5 + 3] \)
Next, solve the square bracket (Addition).
\( = 18 - [8] \)
Finally, perform subtraction.
\( = 18 - 8 = 10 \)
Answer: \( 10 \)
Question 4 [2 Marks]
Find the Highest Common Factor (HCF) of \( 16 \) and \( 24 \) by the prime factorization method.
Solution:
Prime factorization of \( 16 \):
\( 16 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 2^4 \)
Prime factorization of \( 24 \):
\( 24 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 = 2^3 \times 3 \)
The HCF is the product of the lowest powers of common prime factors.
Common prime factor is \( 2 \), and the lowest power is \( 2^3 \).
HCF = \( 2^3 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8 \)
Answer: \( 8 \)
Question 5 [2 Marks]
Arrange the following integers in descending order: \( -7, 0, 5, -12, 14, -1 \)
Solution:
Descending order means arranging from the largest value to the smallest value.
Positive integers (largest to smallest): \( 14, 5 \)
Zero comes next: \( 0 \)
Negative integers (largest to smallest, which means smallest absolute value first): \( -1, -7, -12 \)
Combining them all:
Answer: \( 14, 5, 0, -1, -7, -12 \)
SECTION B (15 Marks)
Question 6 [3 Marks]
Simplify using the BODMAS rule: \( 27 - [38 - \{46 - (15 - \overline{13 - 2})\}] \)
Solution:
According to BODMAS, solve the vinculum (bar bracket) first:
\( \overline{13 - 2} = 11 \)
Expression becomes: \( 27 - [38 - \{46 - (15 - 11)\}] \)
Next, solve the round bracket:
\( 15 - 11 = 4 \)
Expression becomes: \( 27 - [38 - \{46 - 4\}] \)
Next, solve the curly bracket:
\( 46 - 4 = 42 \)
Expression becomes: \( 27 - [38 - 42] \)
Next, solve the square bracket:
\( 38 - 42 = -4 \)
Expression becomes: \( 27 - [-4] \)
Finally, subtract the negative number (which becomes addition):
\( 27 + 4 = 31 \)
Answer: \( 31 \)
Question 7 [3 Marks]
Find the Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) of \( 24, 36 \), and \( 40 \) using the common division method.
Solution:
We divide the numbers by prime numbers until the remainder is 1 for all.
2 | 24, 36, 40
2 | 12, 18, 20
2 | 6, 9, 10
3 | 3, 9, 5
3 | 1, 3, 5
5 | 1, 1, 5
| 1, 1, 1
Multiply all the prime divisors:
LCM = \( 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 5 \)
LCM = \( 8 \times 9 \times 5 \)
LCM = \( 72 \times 5 = 360 \)
Answer: \( 360 \)
Question 8 [3 Marks]
Replace the symbol '*' by the smallest possible digit so that the number \( 56*89 \) becomes divisible by \( 3 \).
Solution:
A number is divisible by 3 if the sum of its digits is a multiple of 3.
Let the unknown digit be \( x \).
Sum of digits = \( 5 + 6 + x + 8 + 9 \)
Sum = \( 28 + x \)
We need to find the smallest digit \( x \) (from 0 to 9) such that \( 28 + x \) is divisible by 3.
The multiples of 3 greater than 28 are 30, 33, 36...
For the sum to be 30: \( 28 + x = 30 \implies x = 2 \)
Since 2 is the smallest possible digit, we don't need to check further.
Answer: The smallest digit is \( 2 \).
Question 9 [3 Marks]
Find the greatest number which divides \( 45 \) and \( 60 \) without leaving any remainder.
Solution:
The greatest number that divides given numbers exactly is their Highest Common Factor (HCF).
Find HCF of \( 45 \) and \( 60 \):
Factors of \( 45 \): \( 1, 3, 5, 9, 15, 45 \)
Factors of \( 60 \): \( 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 15, 20, 30, 60 \)
Common factors: \( 1, 3, 5, 15 \)
The Highest Common Factor is \( 15 \).
Alternatively by prime factorization: \( 45 = 3^2 \times 5 \) and \( 60 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 5 \). HCF = \( 3 \times 5 = 15 \).
Answer: The greatest number is \( 15 \).
Question 10 [3 Marks]
A submarine was situated \( 120 \text{ m} \) below sea level. If it ascends \( 45 \text{ m} \), what is its new position? Express the final position using an appropriate integer.
Solution:
Sea level represents the integer \( 0 \).
Initial position below sea level is represented by a negative integer.
Initial position = \( -120 \text{ m} \)
The submarine ascends, meaning it moves up, which is represented by a positive integer.
Ascent = \( +45 \text{ m} \)
New position = Initial position + Ascent
New position = \( -120 + 45 \)
Since signs are different, we subtract and put the sign of the larger absolute value.
New position = \( -(120 - 45) = -75 \text{ m} \)
Answer: New position is \( -75 \text{ m} \) (or 75 m below sea level).
SECTION C (15 Marks)
Question 11 [5 Marks]
Three bells toll at intervals of \( 12, 15 \), and \( 20 \) minutes respectively. If they start tolling together at 8:00 a.m., at what time will they toll together again? Show your complete working.
Solution:
The bells will toll together again after a time interval that is a common multiple of their individual intervals.
To find the earliest time they toll together, we need to calculate the Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) of \( 12, 15 \), and \( 20 \).
Finding LCM by division method:
2 | 12, 15, 20
2 | 6, 15, 10
3 | 3, 15, 5
5 | 1, 5, 5
| 1, 1, 1
LCM = \( 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 5 \)
LCM = \( 4 \times 15 = 60 \)
The bells will toll together after \( 60 \text{ minutes} \).
Convert minutes to hours: \( 60 \text{ minutes} = 1 \text{ hour} \).
Initial tolling time = 8:00 a.m.
Next tolling time = 8:00 a.m. + 1 hour = 9:00 a.m.
Answer: They will toll together again at 9:00 a.m.
Question 12 [5 Marks]
(a) Simplify: \( 100 - 3 \times [20 + \{50 - (40 - \overline{10 - 5})\}] \) [3 Marks]
Solution (a):
Using BODMAS, solve the vinculum first: \( \overline{10 - 5} = 5 \)
\( = 100 - 3 \times [20 + \{50 - (40 - 5)\}] \)
Solve the round bracket: \( 40 - 5 = 35 \)
\( = 100 - 3 \times [20 + \{50 - 35\}] \)
Solve the curly bracket: \( 50 - 35 = 15 \)
\( = 100 - 3 \times [20 + 15] \)
Solve the square bracket: \( 20 + 15 = 35 \)
\( = 100 - 3 \times 35 \)
Perform multiplication before subtraction: \( 3 \times 35 = 105 \)
\( = 100 - 105 \)
\( = -5 \)
Answer (a): \( -5 \)
(b) Test the divisibility of the number \( 7138965 \) by \( 11 \). State the rule clearly. [2 Marks]
Solution (b):
Rule: A number is divisible by 11 if the difference between the sum of digits at odd places and the sum of digits at even places (starting from the right) is either 0 or a multiple of 11.
Given number: \( 7138965 \)
Sum of digits at odd places (from right: 1st, 3rd, 5th, 7th):
\( 5 + 9 + 3 + 7 = 24 \)
Sum of digits at even places (from right: 2nd, 4th, 6th):
\( 6 + 8 + 1 = 15 \)
Difference between the sums:
\( 24 - 15 = 9 \)
Since the difference (9) is neither 0 nor a multiple of 11, the number is not divisible by 11.
Answer (b): 7138965 is not divisible by 11.
Question 13 [5 Marks]
(a) The product of two numbers is \( 432 \) and their LCM is \( 72 \). Find their HCF. [3 Marks]
Solution (a):
We know the relationship formula between two numbers and their HCF and LCM:
\( \text{HCF} \times \text{LCM} = \text{Product of the two numbers} \)
Given values:
Product = \( 432 \)
LCM = \( 72 \)
Substitute the values into the formula:
\( \text{HCF} \times 72 = 432 \)
To find HCF, divide the product by the LCM:
\( \text{HCF} = \frac{432}{72} \)
\( \text{HCF} = 6 \)
Answer (a): The HCF is \( 6 \).
(b) Evaluate using a suitable property of integers: \( (-15) \times 8 + (-15) \times 2 \) [2 Marks]
Solution (b):
We use the Distributive Property of Multiplication over Addition:
\( a \times b + a \times c = a \times (b + c) \)
Here, \( a = -15 \), \( b = 8 \), and \( c = 2 \).
Take out the common factor \( (-15) \):
\( = (-15) \times (8 + 2) \)
Solve the bracket:
\( = (-15) \times 10 \)
Multiply:
\( = -150 \)
Answer (b): \( -150 \)
