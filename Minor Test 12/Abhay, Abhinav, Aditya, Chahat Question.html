Minor Test 12
Class: 9 ICSE Subject: Physics Time: 1 Hour Max. Marks: 40
General Instructions:
  • All questions are compulsory.
  • The paper is divided into three sections: A, B, and C.
  • Write your answers neatly and legibly. Show working for numericals clearly.
  • Assume \( g = 10 \text{ m s}^{-2} \) and density of water = \( 1000 \text{ kg m}^{-3} \) or \( 1 \text{ g cm}^{-3} \) unless otherwise stated.
SECTION A (10 Marks)(Attempt all questions - 2 Marks Each)
1.
Define upthrust (buoyant force). State its SI unit.
2.
State Archimedes' Principle.
3.
A piece of stone weighs \( 50 \text{ N} \) in air and \( 38 \text{ N} \) when completely immersed in water. Calculate the upthrust acting on the stone.
4.
Why does a block of wood held below the surface of water rise to the surface when released?
5.
State the two fundamental conditions necessary for a body to float equilibrium in a liquid.
SECTION B (15 Marks)(Short Answer Type - 3 Marks Each)
6.
Explain scientifically why a small iron nail sinks in water, but a massive iron ship floats on it.
7.
Define Relative Density. Express the formula for Relative Density in terms of the weight of the solid in air and its weight in water. Why does it have no unit?
8.
A solid metallic block of volume \( 150 \text{ cm}^3 \) is completely immersed in water. Calculate the upthrust acting on the block.
9.
What is a Plimsoll line? Why are different lines marked on the hull of a ship for different water conditions (e.g., fresh water vs. sea water)?
10.
A solid body floats in water with one-third \( (\frac{1}{3}) \) of its volume outside the water surface. Calculate the density of the material of the body in SI units.
SECTION C (15 Marks)(Long Answer / Numerical Type - 5 Marks Each)
11.
(a) Derive the mathematical expression for the upthrust \( (F_B = V \rho g) \) acting on a body completely immersed in a liquid of density \( \rho \). (3)
(b) How does the upthrust acting on a submerged body change when the temperature of the fluid is increased? Give a reason for your answer. (2)
12.
(a) State the Principle of Floatation. (2)
(b) An iceberg floats in sea water. The density of ice is \( 0.92 \text{ g cm}^{-3} \) and the density of sea water is \( 1.025 \text{ g cm}^{-3} \). Determine the percentage of the iceberg's volume that remains submerged underwater. (3)
13.
A piece of brass weighs \( 175 \text{ gf} \) in air and \( 150 \text{ gf} \) when completely immersed in water. Calculate:
(i) The volume of the brass piece. (2)
(ii) The density of the brass piece. (2)
(iii) Its apparent weight when immersed in a liquid of density \( 0.8 \text{ g cm}^{-3} \). (1)
