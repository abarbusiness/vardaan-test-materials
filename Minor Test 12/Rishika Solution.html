SECTION A (10 Marks)
1.
Convert \( \frac{3}{8} \) into a percentage, and express \( 45\% \) as a fraction in its simplest form.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
Part I: Convert fraction to percentage
\( \frac{3}{8} \text{ as a percentage } = \frac{3}{8} \times 100\% \)
\( = \frac{300}{8}\% = 37.5\% \text{ or } 37\frac{1}{2}\% \)
Part II: Convert percentage to fraction
\( 45\% = \frac{45}{100} \)
Dividing numerator and denominator by 5: \( \frac{45 \div 5}{100 \div 5} = \frac{9}{20} \)
Answer: 37.5% and 9/20
2.
Find the number whose \( 12.5\% \) is equal to \( 64 \).
[2 Marks]
Solution:
Let the required number be \( x \).
According to the problem:
\( 12.5\% \text{ of } x = 64 \)
\( \Rightarrow \frac{12.5}{100} \times x = 64 \)
\( \Rightarrow \frac{125}{1000} \times x = 64 \) (removing decimal)
\( \Rightarrow \frac{1}{8} \times x = 64 \)
\( \Rightarrow x = 64 \times 8 \)
\( \Rightarrow x = 512 \)
Answer: The number is 512.
3.
A shopkeeper bought a chair for ₹\( 450 \) and sold it for ₹\( 540 \). Find his profit percentage.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
Cost Price (C.P.) = ₹ 450
Selling Price (S.P.) = ₹ 540
Since S.P. > C.P., there is a profit.
\( \text{Profit} = \text{S.P.} - \text{C.P.} = 540 - 450 = ₹ 90 \)
\( \text{Profit Percentage} = \left( \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{C.P.}} \right) \times 100\% \)
\( = \left( \frac{90}{450} \right) \times 100\% = \frac{1}{5} \times 100\% = 20\% \)
Answer: The profit percentage is 20%.
4.
Calculate the simple interest on ₹\( 2500 \) for \( 2 \) years at the rate of \( 6\% \) per annum.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
Principal (P) = ₹ 2500
Time (T) = 2 years
Rate (R) = 6% per annum
\( \text{Simple Interest (S.I.)} = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100} \)
\( \text{S.I.} = \frac{2500 \times 6 \times 2}{100} \)
\( \text{S.I.} = 25 \times 12 = ₹ 300 \)
Answer: The simple interest is ₹ 300.
5.
If the price of a stationary kit increases from ₹\( 200 \) to ₹\( 250 \), calculate the percentage increase in the price.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
Original Price = ₹ 200
New Price = ₹ 250
\( \text{Increase in price} = \text{New Price} - \text{Original Price} \)
\( = 250 - 200 = ₹ 50 \)
\( \text{Percentage Increase} = \left( \frac{\text{Increase}}{\text{Original Price}} \right) \times 100\% \)
\( = \left( \frac{50}{200} \right) \times 100\% = \frac{1}{4} \times 100\% = 25\% \)
Answer: The percentage increase is 25%.
SECTION B (15 Marks)
6.
Out of \( 800 \) students in a school, \( 45\% \) are girls. Find the exact number of boys studying in the school.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Total number of students = 800
Percentage of girls = 45%
Method 1: Direct approach
Percentage of boys \( = (100\% - \text{Percentage of girls}) = 100\% - 45\% = 55\% \)
Number of boys \( = 55\% \text{ of } 800 \)
\( = \frac{55}{100} \times 800 = 55 \times 8 = 440 \)
Answer: The number of boys in the school is 440.
7.
An article is marked at ₹\( 850 \). The shopkeeper allows a discount of \( 12\% \) on the marked price. Find the final selling price of the article.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Marked Price (M.P.) = ₹ 850
Discount Percentage = 12%
\( \text{Discount Amount} = 12\% \text{ of M.P.} = \frac{12}{100} \times 850 \)
\( = \frac{12 \times 85}{10} = \frac{1020}{10} = ₹ 102 \)
\( \text{Selling Price (S.P.)} = \text{M.P.} - \text{Discount} \)
\( \text{S.P.} = 850 - 102 = ₹ 748 \)
Answer: The selling price is ₹ 748.
8.
A man sells a decorative lamp for ₹\( 396 \) and gains \( 10\% \) on the transaction. Calculate the cost price of the lamp.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Selling Price (S.P.) = ₹ 396
Gain Percentage = 10%
Let the Cost Price (C.P.) be \( x \).
\( \text{S.P.} = \text{C.P.} + \text{Gain} \)
\( 396 = x + 10\% \text{ of } x \)
\( 396 = x + 0.10x \Rightarrow 396 = 1.10x \)
\( x = \frac{396}{1.10} = \frac{3960}{11} = 360 \)
Alternative Formula Method:
\( \text{C.P.} = \frac{100}{100 + \text{Gain\%}} \times \text{S.P.} \)
\( \text{C.P.} = \frac{100}{110} \times 396 = \frac{10}{11} \times 396 = 10 \times 36 = ₹ 360 \)
Answer: The cost price of the lamp is ₹ 360.
9.
At what rate of simple interest per annum will a principal amount of ₹\( 4000 \) yield an amount of ₹\( 5200 \) in \( 3 \) years?
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Principal (P) = ₹ 4000
Amount (A) = ₹ 5200
Time (T) = 3 years
\( \text{Simple Interest (S.I.)} = \text{Amount} - \text{Principal} \)
\( \text{S.I.} = 5200 - 4000 = ₹ 1200 \)
Using the formula for Rate (R):
\( R = \frac{\text{S.I.} \times 100}{P \times T} \)
\( R = \frac{1200 \times 100}{4000 \times 3} \)
\( R = \frac{120000}{12000} = 10\% \)
Answer: The rate of interest is 10% per annum.
10.
The population of a town increases by \( 8\% \) annually. If its present population is \( 54,000 \), what was its population exactly a year ago?
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Let the population a year ago be \( x \).
Annual increase percentage = 8%
Present population = 54,000
\( \text{Present Population} = x + (8\% \text{ of } x) \)
\( 54000 = x + \frac{8}{100}x \)
\( 54000 = \frac{108x}{100} \)
\( x = \frac{54000 \times 100}{108} \)
\( x = 500 \times 100 = 50,000 \)
Answer: The population a year ago was 50,000.
SECTION C (25 Marks)
11.
A dealer bought two tables for a total of ₹\( 3120 \). He sold one of them at a loss of \( 15\% \) and the other at a gain of \( 36\% \). If the selling price of both tables is identical, determine the cost price of each table.
[5 Marks]
Solution:
Let the Cost Price of the first table be \( x \) and the second table be \( y \).
Given: \( x + y = 3120 \)   ---- (Equation 1)
For the first table (Sold at 15% loss):
\( \text{Selling Price (S.P.)}_1 = x - 15\% \text{ of } x = x - 0.15x = 0.85x \)
For the second table (Sold at 36% gain):
\( \text{Selling Price (S.P.)}_2 = y + 36\% \text{ of } y = y + 0.36y = 1.36y \)
According to the question, both selling prices are equal:
\( 0.85x = 1.36y \)
\( \frac{x}{y} = \frac{1.36}{0.85} = \frac{136}{85} \)
Divide numerator and denominator by 17:
\( \frac{x}{y} = \frac{8}{5} \)
This implies the cost prices are in the ratio 8:5. Sum of ratio parts = 8 + 5 = 13.
\( x = \frac{8}{13} \times 3120 = 8 \times 240 = ₹ 1920 \)
\( y = \frac{5}{13} \times 3120 = 5 \times 240 = ₹ 1200 \)
Answer: The cost price of the first table is ₹ 1920 and the second table is ₹ 1200.
12.
A sum of money lent out at simple interest amounts to ₹\( 5750 \) in \( 3 \) years and to ₹\( 6250 \) in \( 5 \) years. Find the principal sum and the rate of interest per annum.
[5 Marks]
Solution:
Let Principal = P and Rate of Interest = R%
Amount after 5 years = P + (S.I. for 5 years) = ₹ 6250
Amount after 3 years = P + (S.I. for 3 years) = ₹ 5750
Subtracting the two equations gives the Simple Interest for the difference in time (2 years):
\( \text{S.I. for 2 years} = 6250 - 5750 = ₹ 500 \)
\( \text{S.I. for 1 year} = \frac{500}{2} = ₹ 250 \)
\( \text{S.I. for 3 years} = 250 \times 3 = ₹ 750 \)
Finding Principal:
\( \text{Principal (P)} = \text{Amount after 3 years} - \text{S.I. for 3 years} \)
\( P = 5750 - 750 = ₹ 5000 \)
Finding Rate of Interest:
\( R = \frac{\text{S.I. for 1 year} \times 100}{P \times 1} = \frac{250 \times 100}{5000} \)
\( R = \frac{25000}{5000} = 5\% \)
Answer: Principal = ₹ 5000, Rate = 5% p.a.
13.
By selling a wall clock for ₹\( 144 \), a retailer loses \( 10\% \). At what price should he sell the same clock to ensure a gain of \( 10\% \)?
[5 Marks]
Solution:
Case 1: Finding Cost Price
Selling Price (\( S.P._1 \)) = ₹ 144, Loss = 10%
\( C.P. = \frac{100}{100 - \text{Loss}\%} \times S.P._1 \)
\( C.P. = \frac{100}{100 - 10} \times 144 = \frac{100}{90} \times 144 \)
\( C.P. = \frac{10}{9} \times 144 = 10 \times 16 = ₹ 160 \)
Case 2: Finding New Selling Price
Cost Price (C.P.) = ₹ 160, Desired Gain = 10%
\( S.P._2 = C.P. \times \left( \frac{100 + \text{Gain}\%}{100} \right) \)
\( S.P._2 = 160 \times \left( \frac{100 + 10}{100} \right) = 160 \times \frac{110}{100} \)
\( S.P._2 = 16 \times 11 = ₹ 176 \)
Answer: He should sell the clock for ₹ 176.
14.
In an entrance examination, \( 72\% \) of the total candidates passed. If the number of failures is \( 392 \), find the total number of candidates who appeared for the exam. Additionally, calculate the number of candidates who passed.
[5 Marks]
Solution:
Let the total number of candidates be \( x \).
Percentage of students who passed = 72%
Percentage of students who failed = \( 100\% - 72\% = 28\% \)
Number of failures is given as 392.
\( 28\% \text{ of } x = 392 \)
\( \frac{28}{100} \times x = 392 \)
\( x = \frac{392 \times 100}{28} = 14 \times 100 = 1400 \)
Total number of candidates = 1400.
Finding Passed Candidates:
Number of passed candidates = Total candidates - Number of failures
\( = 1400 - 392 = 1008 \)
(Alternatively: \( 72\% \text{ of } 1400 = 1008 \))
Answer: Total candidates = 1400. Passed candidates = 1008.
15.
A trader marks his goods \( 40\% \) above the cost price and then allows a discount of \( 25\% \) to the customer. Find his overall gain or loss percentage. If his actual profit is ₹\( 150 \), determine the original cost price of the goods.
[5 Marks]
Solution:
Let the Cost Price (C.P.) be \( 100x \).
Goods marked 40% above C.P.:
Marked Price (M.P.) = \( 100x + 40\% \text{ of } 100x = 100x + 40x = 140x \)
Discount allowed is 25% on M.P.:
\( \text{Discount} = 25\% \text{ of } 140x = \frac{25}{100} \times 140x = \frac{1}{4} \times 140x = 35x \)
Selling Price (S.P.) = M.P. - Discount = \( 140x - 35x = 105x \)
Finding Gain %:
\( \text{Gain} = \text{S.P.} - \text{C.P.} = 105x - 100x = 5x \)
\( \text{Gain } \% = \left( \frac{5x}{100x} \right) \times 100\% = 5\% \)
Finding Original Cost Price:
Actual profit is given as ₹ 150.
\( \therefore 5x = 150 \)
\( x = \frac{150}{5} = 30 \)
Original C.P. = \( 100x = 100 \times 30 = ₹ 3000 \)
Answer: Overall Gain = 5%. Original Cost Price = ₹ 3000.
