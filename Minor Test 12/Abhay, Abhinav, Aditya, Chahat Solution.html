SECTION A (10 Marks)
Q1. Define upthrust (buoyant force). State its SI unit.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
The upward force exerted by a fluid (liquid or gas) on a body when it is partially or completely immersed in it is called upthrust or buoyant force.
SI Unit: The SI unit of upthrust is the Newton (N).
Q2. State Archimedes' Principle.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
Archimedes' Principle states that when a body is partially or completely immersed in a fluid at rest, it experiences an upward thrust (buoyant force) which is equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by the immersed part of the body.
Q3. A piece of stone weighs \( 50 \text{ N} \) in air and \( 38 \text{ N} \) when completely immersed in water. Calculate the upthrust.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
Given:
Weight of stone in air \( (W_1) = 50 \text{ N} \)
Weight of stone in water \( (W_2) = 38 \text{ N} \)
$$ \begin{aligned} \text{Upthrust } (F_B) &= \text{Loss in weight} \\ &= W_1 - W_2 \\ &= 50 \text{ N} - 38 \text{ N} \\ &= 12 \text{ N} \end{aligned} $$
Answer: The upthrust acting on the stone is 12 N.
Q4. Why does a block of wood held below the surface of water rise to the surface when released?
[2 Marks]
Solution:
The density of wood is less than the density of water. When held underwater, the upthrust (buoyant force) exerted by the displaced water is greater than the true weight of the wood block acting downwards. This net upward force pushes the block to the surface when released.
Q5. State the two fundamental conditions necessary for a body to float equilibrium in a liquid.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
1. The weight of the floating body must be equal to the upthrust (or weight of the liquid displaced by its immersed part). i.e., \( W = F_B \).
2. The centre of gravity of the body and the centre of buoyancy of the displaced liquid must lie on the same vertical line.
SECTION B (15 Marks)
Q6. Explain scientifically why a small iron nail sinks in water, but a massive iron ship floats on it.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
An iron nail is solid, so its overall average density is greater than the density of water (\( \rho_{iron} > \rho_{water} \)). The weight of the water displaced by the nail is less than its own weight, so the net downward force causes it to sink.

A ship, though made of iron, is hollow inside and contains a large volume of air. This makes the average density of the ship less than the density of water. When placed in water, it displaces a large volume of water. The upthrust provided by this displaced water becomes equal to the total weight of the ship, satisfying the law of floatation, thereby allowing it to float.
Q7. Define Relative Density. Express the formula for Relative Density in terms of the weight of the solid. Why does it have no unit?
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Definition: Relative density of a substance is defined as the ratio of the density of the substance to the density of water at 4°C.
Formula: By Archimedes' principle, Relative Density can be expressed as:
$$ \text{R.D.} = \frac{\text{Weight of solid in air}}{\text{Weight of solid in air} - \text{Weight of solid in water}} $$
Reason for no unit: It is a pure ratio of two similar physical quantities (densities or weights). Hence, the units cancel out, leaving it dimensionless.
Q8. A solid metallic block of volume \( 150 \text{ cm}^3 \) is completely immersed in water. Calculate the upthrust acting on the block.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Given:
Volume of the block, \( V = 150 \text{ cm}^3 \)
$$ V = 150 \times 10^{-6} \text{ m}^3 = 1.5 \times 10^{-4} \text{ m}^3 $$
Density of water, \( \rho = 1000 \text{ kg m}^{-3} \)
Acceleration due to gravity, \( g = 10 \text{ m s}^{-2} \)
$$ \begin{aligned} \text{Upthrust } (F_B) &= V \cdot \rho \cdot g \\ &= (1.5 \times 10^{-4}) \times 1000 \times 10 \\ &= 1.5 \times 10^{-4} \times 10^4 \\ &= 1.5 \text{ N} \end{aligned} $$
Answer: The upthrust on the block is 1.5 N.
Q9. What is a Plimsoll line? Why are different lines marked on the hull of a ship for different water conditions?
[3 Marks]
Solution:
A Plimsoll line is a reference mark located on a ship's hull that indicates the maximum safe depth to which the vessel may be loaded safely.

Reason for multiple lines: The density of water varies in different environments (e.g., fresh water is less dense than sea water; warm water is less dense than cold water). Because upthrust depends on the density of the fluid \( (F_B = V\rho g) \), a ship will sink deeper in fresh/warm water than in cold sea water to displace the same weight of fluid. Therefore, different lines are marked to ensure safe loading limits across various water densities.
Q10. A solid body floats in water with one-third \( (\frac{1}{3}) \) of its volume outside the water surface. Calculate the density of the material in SI units.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Let the total volume of the body be \( V \).
Volume of the body outside water = \( \frac{1}{3}V \)
Therefore, Volume immersed in water, \( v = V - \frac{1}{3}V = \frac{2}{3}V \).
Density of water, \( \rho_w = 1000 \text{ kg m}^{-3} \).
By the Law of Floatation:
$$ \frac{\text{Volume immersed } (v)}{\text{Total Volume } (V)} = \frac{\text{Density of solid } (\rho_s)}{\text{Density of liquid } (\rho_w)} $$
$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{\frac{2}{3}V}{V} &= \frac{\rho_s}{1000} \\ \frac{2}{3} &= \frac{\rho_s}{1000} \\ \rho_s &= \frac{2000}{3} \\ \rho_s &\approx 666.67 \text{ kg m}^{-3} \end{aligned} $$
Answer: The density of the material is approx \( 666.67 \text{ kg m}^{-3} \).
SECTION C (15 Marks)
Q11. (a) Derive the expression for upthrust. (b) Effect of temperature on upthrust.
[3 + 2 = 5 Marks]
Solution:
(a) Derivation (3 Marks):
Consider a solid cylinder of cross-sectional area \( A \) and height \( h \) completely immersed in a liquid of density \( \rho \). Let the top surface be at depth \( h_1 \) and bottom surface at depth \( h_2 \) from the free surface.
Downward pressure on top surface, \( P_1 = h_1 \rho g \)
Downward thrust on top, \( F_1 = P_1 \cdot A = h_1 \rho g A \)
Upward pressure on bottom surface, \( P_2 = h_2 \rho g \)
Upward thrust on bottom, \( F_2 = P_2 \cdot A = h_2 \rho g A \)
Since \( h_2 > h_1 \), \( F_2 > F_1 \). The net upward force is the Upthrust \( (F_B) \).
$$ \begin{aligned} F_B &= F_2 - F_1 \\ &= h_2 \rho g A - h_1 \rho g A \\ &= A(h_2 - h_1) \rho g \end{aligned} $$
Since \( (h_2 - h_1) = h \) (height of cylinder), and \( A \times h = V \) (volume of cylinder):
$$ F_B = V \rho g $$
(b) Temperature Effect (2 Marks):
When the temperature of the fluid is increased, its volume increases causing its density \( (\rho) \) to decrease. Since Upthrust is directly proportional to the density of the fluid \( (F_B \propto \rho) \), the upthrust decreases with an increase in temperature.
Q12. (a) Principle of Floatation. (b) Iceberg Submerged Percentage.
[2 + 3 = 5 Marks]
Solution:
(a) Principle of Floatation (2 Marks):
It states that the weight of a floating body is equal to the weight of the liquid displaced by its submerged part.

(b) Numerical (3 Marks):
Density of ice, \( \rho_i = 0.92 \text{ g cm}^{-3} \)
Density of sea water, \( \rho_w = 1.025 \text{ g cm}^{-3} \)
Let total volume of iceberg be \( V \) and submerged volume be \( v \).
According to the law of floatation:
$$ \frac{v}{V} = \frac{\rho_i}{\rho_w} $$
$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{v}{V} &= \frac{0.92}{1.025} \\ &= 0.8975 \end{aligned} $$
Percentage submerged = \( \frac{v}{V} \times 100 \)
$$ \text{Percentage} = 0.8975 \times 100 = 89.75\% $$
Answer: Approximately 89.75% of the iceberg's volume remains submerged.
Q13. Brass piece calculations: (i) Volume, (ii) Density, (iii) Apparent weight in another liquid.
[2 + 2 + 1 = 5 Marks]
Solution:
Given:
Weight in air, \( W_a = 175 \text{ gf} \) (Mass = 175 g)
Weight in water, \( W_w = 150 \text{ gf} \)
Density of water, \( \rho_w = 1 \text{ g cm}^{-3} \)
(i) Volume of brass piece (2 Marks):
Upthrust in water = \( W_a - W_w = 175 - 150 = 25 \text{ gf} \)
By Archimedes' Principle, Upthrust = Weight of water displaced.
Volume of water displaced = \( \frac{\text{Mass of water}}{\text{Density of water}} = \frac{25 \text{ g}}{1 \text{ g cm}^{-3}} = 25 \text{ cm}^3 \)
Since it is completely immersed, Volume of brass \( V = 25 \text{ cm}^3 \).

(ii) Density of the brass piece (2 Marks):
$$ \text{Density } (\rho) = \frac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Volume}} = \frac{175 \text{ g}}{25 \text{ cm}^3} = 7 \text{ g cm}^{-3} $$

(iii) Apparent weight in liquid of density \( 0.8 \text{ g cm}^{-3} \) (1 Mark):
Upthrust in liquid = \( V \times \rho_l \times g \)
(In terms of gf, Upthrust = Volume × Density)
$$ \text{Upthrust} = 25 \text{ cm}^3 \times 0.8 \text{ g cm}^{-3} = 20 \text{ gf} $$
Apparent weight = Weight in air - Upthrust in liquid
$$ \text{Apparent Weight} = 175 \text{ gf} - 20 \text{ gf} = 155 \text{ gf} $$
