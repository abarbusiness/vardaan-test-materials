SOLUTION KEY & MARKING SCHEME
Minor Test 12: Force, Pressure & Moment of Force
Class: 8 ICSE | Subject: Physics | Max Marks: 40
SECTION A (10 Marks)
Q1. Define force and state its SI unit.
[2 M]
Solution: A force is a physical quantity that acts as a push or a pull on an object, which tends to change its state of rest, uniform motion, or its shape and size. (1 Mark)
The SI unit of force is Newton (N). (1 Mark)
Q2. State two points of difference between thrust and pressure.
[2 M]
Solution:
1. Definition: Thrust is the total perpendicular force acting on a surface, whereas pressure is the thrust acting per unit area of the surface. (1 Mark)
2. Unit & Dependence: The SI unit of thrust is Newton (N) and it does not depend on the area of contact. The SI unit of pressure is Pascal (Pa) and it is inversely proportional to the area of contact. (1 Mark)
Q3. Give a scientific reason: Why do school bags have wide straps?
[2 M]
Solution: School bags have wide straps to increase the surface area over which the weight of the bag (force) acts on the student's shoulders. (1 Mark) Since pressure is inversely proportional to area (\( P = F/A \)), the wider area decreases the pressure on the shoulders, making it comfortable to carry heavy bags without causing pain. (1 Mark)
Q4. State any two characteristics of liquid pressure.
[2 M]
Solution: (Any two valid points - 1 mark each)
1. Liquid pressure increases with the depth of the liquid.
2. A liquid exerts pressure equally in all directions at the same depth.
3. Liquid pressure depends on the density of the liquid.
Q5. Define 'moment of force' and write down its mathematical formula.
[2 M]
Solution: The turning effect produced by a force on a rigid body about a fixed point (pivot/fulcrum) is called the moment of force (or torque). (1 Mark)
Formula:
\( \text{Moment of Force} = \text{Force (F)} \times \text{Perpendicular Distance (d)} \)
(1 Mark)
SECTION B (15 Marks)
Q6. A force of \( 150 \text{ N} \) acts uniformly over an area of \( 5 \text{ m}^2 \). Calculate the pressure exerted on the surface.
[3 M]
Solution:
Given, Force (\( F \)) = \( 150 \text{ N} \) (0.5 Mark)
Area (\( A \)) = \( 5 \text{ m}^2 \) (0.5 Mark)
Formula:
\( P = \frac{F}{A} \)
(1 Mark)
Substituting values:
\( P = \frac{150}{5} = 30 \text{ N/m}^2 \text{ (or Pascal/Pa)} \)
(1 Mark for correct calculation and unit)
Q7. Explain scientifically why the wall of a water dam is made much thicker at the bottom than at the top.
[3 M]
Solution: We know that liquid pressure increases with an increase in depth. (1 Mark)
As we go deeper into the reservoir of a dam, the pressure exerted by the vast quantity of water becomes extremely high at the bottom. (1 Mark)
To withstand this immense lateral pressure and prevent the dam from collapsing, the walls are constructed much thicker at the base compared to the top. (1 Mark)
Q8. (a) What do you understand by the 'turning effect of a force'?
(b) Give one practical everyday example where the turning effect of force is utilized.
[3 M]
Solution:
(a) When a force is applied to a body that is pivoted at a point, it causes the body to rotate about that pivot. This rotational motion generated by a force is known as the turning effect of a force. (2 Marks)
(b) Example: Opening or closing a door. The force is applied at the handle to rotate the door around its hinges. (Or turning a steering wheel, tightening a nut with a spanner). (1 Mark)
Q9. A boy applies a force of \( 50 \text{ N} \) to open a heavy door. If the perpendicular distance of the point of application of force from the hinges is \( 0.4 \text{ m} \), calculate the moment of force produced.
[3 M]
Solution:
Given, Force (\( F \)) = \( 50 \text{ N} \) (0.5 Mark)
Perpendicular distance (\( d \)) = \( 0.4 \text{ m} \) (0.5 Mark)
Formula:
\( \text{Moment of Force} = F \times d \)
(1 Mark)
\( \text{Moment of Force} = 50 \times 0.4 = 20 \text{ Nm} \)
(1 Mark for correct calculation and unit)
Q10. Briefly explain how the pressure exerted by a liquid varies with: (i) The depth of the liquid. (ii) The density of the liquid.
[3 M]
Solution:
(i) Depth: Pressure exerted by a liquid increases directly with depth. The deeper an object goes into a liquid, the greater the column of liquid pushing down on it, hence greater pressure. (1.5 Marks)
(ii) Density: Pressure exerted by a liquid is directly proportional to its density. A denser liquid (like saltwater) will exert more pressure at a given depth compared to a lighter liquid (like pure water). (1.5 Marks)
SECTION C (15 Marks)
Q11. (a) Define pressure and write its SI unit. (b) Explain why cutting tools like knives and axes have sharp edges. (c) A wooden block weighing \( 200 \text{ N} \) rests on a table. If the area of contact is \( 0.5 \text{ m}^2 \), find the pressure exerted.
[5 M]
Solution:
(a) Pressure: It is defined as the continuous physical force exerted on or against an object by something in contact with it, specifically measured as thrust acting per unit area. (1 Mark) SI Unit: Pascal (Pa) or \( \text{N/m}^2 \). (1 Mark)
(b) Reason: Cutting tools have sharp edges to ensure the area of contact is extremely small. According to \( P = F/A \), a smaller area results in a very high pressure for the same amount of applied force. This high pressure easily cuts through the material. (1.5 Marks)
(c) Numerical:
Thrust (\( F \)) = Weight = \( 200 \text{ N} \)
Area (\( A \)) = \( 0.5 \text{ m}^2 \)
\( P = \frac{F}{A} = \frac{200}{0.5} = 400 \text{ Pa} \)
(1.5 Marks)
Q12. (a) Describe a simple, well-labelled experiment to demonstrate that a liquid exerts pressure equally in all directions at a given depth. (b) State the SI units of (i) Thrust and (ii) Torque.
[5 M]
Solution:
(a) Experiment: (3 Marks)
Apparatus: A large plastic bottle, water, pin.
Procedure: 1. Take an empty plastic bottle. 2. Using a pin, make 4-5 holes around the bottle at exactly the same horizontal level (same height from the base). 3. Fill the bottle completely with water.
Observation: Water squirts out from all the holes simultaneously and falls at the same horizontal distance away from the bottle on all sides.
Conclusion: This proves that liquid exerts equal pressure in all directions at the same depth.
(b) SI Units: (2 Marks)
(i) Thrust = Newton (N) (1 Mark)
(ii) Torque = Newton-metre (Nm) (1 Mark)
Q13. (a) A mechanic uses a wrench of length \( 30 \text{ cm} \) to loosen a tight nut. He applies a perpendicular force of \( 40 \text{ N} \) at the end. Calculate the torque. (b) Why is it always easier to open a heavy door by applying force at the edge farthest from the hinges?
[5 M]
Solution:
(a) Numerical: (3 Marks)
Force (\( F \)) = \( 40 \text{ N} \)
Distance (\( d \)) = \( 30 \text{ cm} \). We must convert this to SI units. (1 Mark)
\( d = \frac{30}{100} \text{ m} = 0.3 \text{ m} \)
Torque = \( F \times d \) (1 Mark)
\( \text{Torque} = 40 \times 0.3 = 12 \text{ Nm} \)
(1 Mark for answer & unit)

(b) Reason: (2 Marks)
The turning effect (torque) depends on two factors: the magnitude of the force and the perpendicular distance from the pivot (hinge). To maximize the torque with minimal force, the perpendicular distance should be as large as possible. Therefore, handles are placed at the edge farthest from the hinges to provide maximum distance, making it easier to turn the door.
