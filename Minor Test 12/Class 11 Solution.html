Q1. (a) Write the ground state electronic configuration of Copper (Cu, Z=29).
(b) State Pauli's Exclusion Principle.
[1 + 1 = 2 Marks]
(a) The ground state electronic configuration of Copper ($Cu$, $Z=29$) is:
$$1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^6 4s^1 3d^{10} \quad \text{or} \quad [Ar] 4s^1 3d^{10}$$
(b) Pauli's Exclusion Principle: It states that no two electrons in an atom can have the same set of all four quantum numbers ($n, l, m_l, m_s$). Alternatively, only two electrons can exist in the same orbital and these electrons must have opposite spins.
Q2. Calculate the number of protons, neutrons, and electrons in $_{35}^{80}Br$.
[2 Marks]
For the given isotope, $_{35}^{80}Br$:
Atomic number ($Z$) = $35$
Mass number ($A$) = $80$
Number of protons = $Z = 35$ [0.5 Mark]
Number of electrons (neutral atom) = Number of protons = $35$ [0.5 Mark]
Number of neutrons = $A - Z = 80 - 35 = 45$ [1 Mark]
Q3. State Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle and write its mathematical expression.
[2 Marks]
Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle: It states that it is impossible to determine simultaneously, the exact position and exact momentum (or velocity) of a microscopic particle like an electron with absolute certainty. [1 Mark]
Mathematical Expression:
$$ \Delta x \times \Delta p \ge \frac{h}{4\pi} \quad \text{or} \quad \Delta x \times \Delta v \ge \frac{h}{4\pi m} $$
where $\Delta x$ is uncertainty in position, $\Delta p$ is uncertainty in momentum. [1 Mark]
Q4. What are degenerate orbitals? Give one example to support your answer.
[2 Marks]
Degenerate orbitals: Orbitals belonging to the same subshell that have exactly the same energy (in the absence of an external magnetic or electric field) are called degenerate orbitals. [1 Mark]
Example: The three $p$-orbitals ($p_x, p_y, p_z$) of a given principal shell (like $2p$) have the same energy and are degenerate. [1 Mark]
Q5. Calculate the frequency ($\nu$) and wave number ($\bar{\nu}$) of yellow radiation having a wavelength of $580 \text{ nm}$.
[2 Marks]
Given: Wavelength $\lambda = 580 \text{ nm} = 580 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}$
Frequency ($\nu$):
$$ \nu = \frac{c}{\lambda} = \frac{3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}}{580 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}} = 5.17 \times 10^{14} \text{ s}^{-1} \text{ (or Hz)} $$ [1 Mark]
Wave number ($\bar{\nu}$):
$$ \bar{\nu} = \frac{1}{\lambda} = \frac{1}{580 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}} = 1.72 \times 10^{6} \text{ m}^{-1} $$ [1 Mark]

Q6. Using s, p, d, f notations, describe the orbital with the following quantum numbers:
(a) $n = 1, l = 0$
(b) $n = 3, l = 1$
(c) $n = 4, l = 2$
[3 Marks]
The principal quantum number ($n$) determines the shell, and the azimuthal quantum number ($l$) determines the subshell notation ($l=0 \rightarrow s, l=1 \rightarrow p, l=2 \rightarrow d, l=3 \rightarrow f$).
(a) $n = 1, l = 0 \implies \mathbf{1s}$ orbital. [1 Mark]
(b) $n = 3, l = 1 \implies \mathbf{3p}$ orbital. [1 Mark]
(c) $n = 4, l = 2 \implies \mathbf{4d}$ orbital. [1 Mark]
Q7. Explain the photoelectric effect. Why is there a threshold frequency required for the ejection of electrons from a metal surface?
[3 Marks]
Photoelectric Effect: The phenomenon of ejection of electrons from the surface of a metal when light of a suitable frequency strikes it is called the photoelectric effect. [1.5 Marks]
Reason for threshold frequency: Electrons in an atom are held by attractive forces from the nucleus. A minimum amount of energy (called work function, $W_0 = h\nu_0$) is required to overcome these attractive forces.
If the incident light has a frequency lower than the threshold frequency ($\nu_0$), its photons do not carry enough energy to break the electron free, regardless of the light's intensity. Hence, emission only occurs if $\nu > \nu_0$. [1.5 Marks]
Q8. Calculate the wavelength of an electron moving with a velocity of $2.05 \times 10^7 \text{ m s}^{-1}$.
(Mass of electron = $9.11 \times 10^{-31} \text{ kg}$, $h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J s}$)
[3 Marks]
According to de Broglie's equation:
$$ \lambda = \frac{h}{mv} $$ [1 Mark]
Substituting the given values:
$$ \lambda = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ kg m}^2\text{s}^{-1}}{(9.11 \times 10^{-31} \text{ kg}) \times (2.05 \times 10^7 \text{ m s}^{-1})} $$ [1 Mark]
$$ \lambda = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}}{18.6755 \times 10^{-24}} \text{ m} $$
$$ \lambda = 3.55 \times 10^{-11} \text{ m} $$ [1 Mark]
Q9. State any three essential postulates of Bohr's model for the hydrogen atom.
[3 Marks]
Three essential postulates of Bohr's model are:
  1. Stationary Orbits: The electron in a hydrogen atom revolves around the nucleus only in certain selected circular paths called orbits or energy levels. These orbits have a fixed energy and radius. [1 Mark]
  2. Quantization of Angular Momentum: Only those orbits are permitted in which the angular momentum of the electron is an integral multiple of $h/2\pi$.
    i.e., $m_e v r = n \frac{h}{2\pi}$ (where $n = 1, 2, 3\dots$) [1 Mark]
  3. Energy Transitions: An electron does not radiate energy while moving in a particular orbit. Energy is emitted or absorbed only when an electron jumps from one stationary orbit to another.
    $\Delta E = E_{final} - E_{initial} = h\nu$. [1 Mark]
Q10. Why is the electronic configuration of Chromium (Cr, Z=24) written as $[Ar] 4s^1 3d^5$ instead of $[Ar] 4s^2 3d^4$? Explain based on orbital stability.
[3 Marks]
The configuration $[Ar] 4s^1 3d^5$ is more stable than $[Ar] 4s^2 3d^4$ due to two main reasons:
1. Symmetrical Distribution of Electrons: A half-filled subshell ($3d^5$) has a completely symmetrical distribution of electrons (one electron in each of the five degenerate d-orbitals). Symmetry leads to greater stability. [1.5 Marks]
2. Higher Exchange Energy: Electrons with parallel spins present in degenerate orbitals tend to exchange their positions. The energy released during this exchange is called exchange energy. The $3d^5$ configuration has more possible exchanges (10 exchanges) compared to $3d^4$ (6 exchanges). Maximum exchange energy corresponds to maximum stability. [1.5 Marks]
SECTION C
Q11. (a) State de Broglie's relation and write its equation. (b) The mass of an electron is $9.1 \times 10^{-31} \text{ kg}$. If its kinetic energy is $3.0 \times 10^{-25} \text{ J}$, calculate its wavelength.
[2 + 3 = 5 Marks]
(a) de Broglie's relation: It states that all material particles in motion possess wave characteristics. The wavelength ($\lambda$) associated with a moving particle is inversely proportional to its momentum ($p$).
$$ \lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{h}{mv} $$ [2 Marks]
(b) Calculation of wavelength:
Given: Kinetic Energy (K.E.) = $3.0 \times 10^{-25} \text{ J}$, Mass ($m$) = $9.1 \times 10^{-31} \text{ kg}$
We know, $K.E. = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$
$$ v^2 = \frac{2 \times K.E.}{m} = \frac{2 \times 3.0 \times 10^{-25}}{9.1 \times 10^{-31}} $$
$$ v^2 = 0.659 \times 10^6 \implies v = \sqrt{6.59 \times 10^5} \approx 8.11 \times 10^2 \text{ m/s} = 811 \text{ m/s} $$ [1.5 Marks]
Now apply de Broglie's equation:
$$ \lambda = \frac{h}{mv} = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}}{9.1 \times 10^{-31} \times 811} $$
$$ \lambda = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}}{7.38 \times 10^{-28}} = 8.97 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m} $$ [1.5 Marks]
(Note: Alternate formula $\lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2m(K.E.)}}$ can also be used for direct calculation.)
Q12. What are quantum numbers? Briefly explain the significance of the principal ($n$), azimuthal ($l$), and magnetic ($m_l$) quantum numbers. What are the possible values of $l$ and $m_l$ for an electron in the $n=3$ principal energy level?
[5 Marks]
Quantum Numbers: A set of four numbers which provide complete information about an electron in an atom, i.e., energy, shape, orientation, and spin of its orbital. [1 Mark]
Significance:
  • Principal Quantum Number ($n$): It determines the main energy level (shell) in which the electron is present. It also largely determines the size of the orbital and energy of the electron. [0.5 Mark]
  • Azimuthal Quantum Number ($l$): It defines the subshell (s, p, d, f) and determines the 3D shape of the orbital. It also contributes to the energy in multi-electron atoms. [0.5 Mark]
  • Magnetic Quantum Number ($m_l$): It describes the spatial orientation of the orbital with respect to a standard set of coordinate axes. [0.5 Mark]
Values for $n = 3$: [2.5 Marks]
For a given $n$, $l$ can take values from $0$ to $(n-1)$.
So, if $n=3$, possible values of $l = 0, 1, 2$.

For a given $l$, $m_l$ can take values from $-l$ to $+l$ (including zero).
  • If $l=0$ (3s subshell), $m_l = 0$
  • If $l=1$ (3p subshell), $m_l = -1, 0, +1$
  • If $l=2$ (3d subshell), $m_l = -2, -1, 0, +1, +2$
Q13. (a) Emission transitions in the Paschen series end at orbit $n=3$ and start from orbit $n$, and can be represented as: $\nu = 3.29 \times 10^{15} (1/3^2 - 1/n^2) \text{ Hz}$. Calculate the value of $n$ if the transition is observed at a wavelength of $1285 \text{ nm}$.
(b) Differentiate between an orbit and an orbital.
[3 + 2 = 5 Marks]
(a) Calculation:
Given: Wavelength $\lambda = 1285 \text{ nm} = 1285 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}$
First, find the frequency ($\nu$):
$$ \nu = \frac{c}{\lambda} = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{1285 \times 10^{-9}} = 2.33 \times 10^{14} \text{ Hz} $$ [1 Mark]
Substitute $\nu$ in the given formula:
$$ 2.33 \times 10^{14} = 3.29 \times 10^{15} \left( \frac{1}{9} - \frac{1}{n^2} \right) $$
$$ \frac{2.33 \times 10^{14}}{3.29 \times 10^{15}} = \frac{1}{9} - \frac{1}{n^2} $$
$$ 0.0708 = 0.1111 - \frac{1}{n^2} $$ [1 Mark]
$$ \frac{1}{n^2} = 0.1111 - 0.0708 = 0.0403 $$
$$ n^2 = \frac{1}{0.0403} \approx 24.8 \approx 25 $$
$$ n = \sqrt{25} = 5 $$ [1 Mark]
The transition occurs from the 5th orbit.
(b) Difference between Orbit and Orbital: [2 Marks, 1 for each point]
Orbit Orbital
1. It is a well-defined circular path around the nucleus in which an electron revolves. 1. It is a 3D region in space around the nucleus where the probability of finding an electron is maximum.
2. It contradicts Heisenberg's uncertainty principle (assumes definite path). 2. It is strictly in accordance with Heisenberg's uncertainty principle.
Q14. (a) What is the maximum number of emission lines observed when the excited electron of an $H$ atom in $n=6$ drops to the ground state? Show calculation.
(b) State Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity. Apply it to show the orbital diagram of Nitrogen (N, Z=7).
[2 + 3 = 5 Marks]
(a) Maximum number of emission lines:
The formula to find the maximum number of emission lines when an electron drops from $n_2$ to $n_1$ (ground state, $n_1=1$) is:
$$ \text{Number of lines} = \frac{(n_2 - n_1)(n_2 - n_1 + 1)}{2} $$ [1 Mark]
Here, $n_2 = 6, n_1 = 1$.
$$ \text{Lines} = \frac{(6 - 1)(6 - 1 + 1)}{2} = \frac{5 \times 6}{2} = 15 \text{ lines} $$ [1 Mark]
(b) Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity:
It states that pairing of electrons in the orbitals belonging to the same subshell (p, d, or f) does not take place until each orbital belonging to that subshell has got one electron each (i.e., it is singly occupied) with parallel spins. [1.5 Marks]
Orbital Diagram of Nitrogen (Z=7):
Electronic configuration: $1s^2 2s^2 2p^3$ [0.5 Mark]
According to Hund's rule, the three electrons in the $2p$ subshell will occupy separate degenerate orbitals ($2p_x, 2p_y, 2p_z$) with parallel spins before pairing.

Q15. (a) Electromagnetic radiation of wavelength $242 \text{ nm}$ is just sufficient to ionize a sodium atom. Calculate the ionization energy of sodium in $\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$.
(b) Mention two major limitations of Bohr's atomic model.
[3 + 2 = 5 Marks]
(a) Ionization Energy Calculation:
Energy of one photon required to ionize one sodium atom:
$$ E = \frac{hc}{\lambda} $$ [0.5 Mark]
$$ E = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J s} \times 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m s}^{-1}}{242 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}} $$
$$ E = \frac{19.878 \times 10^{-26}}{242 \times 10^{-9}} = 8.214 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J/atom} $$ [1 Mark]
Ionization energy per mole (Multiply by Avogadro's number $N_A$):
$$ I.E. = E \times N_A = 8.214 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J} \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} \text{ mol}^{-1} $$
$$ I.E. = 49.46 \times 10^4 \text{ J mol}^{-1} = 494.6 \times 10^3 \text{ J mol}^{-1} $$
$$ I.E. = 494.6 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1} $$ [1.5 Marks]
(b) Limitations of Bohr's Model: (Any two) [2 Marks, 1 for each]
  • It could not explain the line spectra of multi-electron atoms (it was only successful for single-electron species like $H$, $He^+$, $Li^{2+}$).
  • It could not explain the splitting of spectral lines under the influence of an external magnetic field (Zeeman effect) or electric field (Stark effect).
  • It contradicts Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle by assuming well-defined paths and exact velocities for electrons.
  • It failed to explain the dual nature of matter proposed by de Broglie.
