SECTION A SOLUTIONS (2 Marks Each)
Q1. Express the linear equation \( 2x = -5y \) in the standard form \( ax + by + c = 0 \) and indicate the values of \( a, b, \) and \( c \).
[2 Marks]
Step 1: Bring all terms to one side to express it in the form \( ax + by + c = 0 \).
\( 2x + 5y = 0 \)
Step 2: To make the constant term visible, we can write it as:
\( 2x + 5y + 0 = 0 \)
Step 3: Comparing with standard form \( ax + by + c = 0 \):
\( a = 2, b = 5, c = 0 \)
Q2. Check which of the following are solutions of the equation \( x - 2y = 4 \): (i) \( (0, 2) \) (ii) \( (4, 0) \)
[2 Marks]
Given Equation: \( x - 2y = 4 \)
(i) For \( (0, 2) \): Put \( x = 0 \) and \( y = 2 \) in LHS.
\( \text{LHS} = 0 - 2(2) = -4 \)
Since \( \text{LHS} \neq \text{RHS} \) (as \( -4 \neq 4 \)), \( (0, 2) \) is NOT a solution. (1 Mark)
(ii) For \( (4, 0) \): Put \( x = 4 \) and \( y = 0 \) in LHS.
\( \text{LHS} = 4 - 2(0) = 4 \)
Since \( \text{LHS} = \text{RHS} \), \( (4, 0) \) IS a solution. (1 Mark)
Q3. Find the value of \( k \), if \( x = 2, y = 1 \) is a solution of the equation \( 2x + 3y = k \).
[2 Marks]
Since \( (2, 1) \) is a solution, substituting \( x = 2 \) and \( y = 1 \) will satisfy the equation.
Equation: \( 2x + 3y = k \)
\( \Rightarrow 2(2) + 3(1) = k \)
\( \Rightarrow 4 + 3 = k \)
\( k = 7 \)
Q4. Write any two distinct solutions for the linear equation \( 2x + y = 7 \).
[2 Marks]
We can rewrite the equation as \( y = 7 - 2x \). Let's substitute arbitrary values for \( x \).
Solution 1: Let \( x = 0 \). Then, \( y = 7 - 2(0) = 7 \). Point: (0, 7)
Solution 2: Let \( x = 1 \). Then, \( y = 7 - 2(1) = 5 \). Point: (1, 5)
(Note: Students may provide any other valid solutions like (2, 3), (3, 1), etc. Award full marks for any two correct points.)
Q5. At what points does the graph of the linear equation \( x + y = 5 \) meet the x-axis and the y-axis?
[2 Marks]
Intersection with x-axis: The y-coordinate is 0.
Put \( y = 0 \) in \( x + y = 5 \) \( \Rightarrow x + 0 = 5 \Rightarrow x = 5 \).
Point on x-axis: (5, 0) (1 Mark)
Intersection with y-axis: The x-coordinate is 0.
Put \( x = 0 \) in \( x + y = 5 \) \( \Rightarrow 0 + y = 5 \Rightarrow y = 5 \).
Point on y-axis: (0, 5) (1 Mark)
SECTION B SOLUTIONS (3 Marks Each)
Q6. The cost of a notebook is twice the cost of a pen. Write a linear equation in two variables to represent this statement.
[3 Marks]
Let the cost of a notebook be ₹ \( x \). (1/2 Mark)
Let the cost of a pen be ₹ \( y \). (1/2 Mark)
According to the given condition: Cost of notebook = \( 2 \times \) Cost of pen
\( x = 2y \) (1 Mark)
Converting into standard form:
\( x - 2y = 0 \) (1 Mark)

(ii) In two variables:

Q8. Solve the equation \( 2x + 1 = x - 3 \), and represent the solution(s) on the Cartesian plane.
[3 Marks]
Solving the equation:
\( 2x + 1 = x - 3 \)
\( 2x - x = -3 - 1 \)
\( x = -4 \) (1 Mark)
Representation on Cartesian Plane:
The equation \( x = -4 \) can be written as \( 1\cdot x + 0\cdot y = -4 \).
For any value of \( y \), \( x \) is always \( -4 \). Points are \( (-4, 0), (-4, 2), \) etc.
The graph is a straight line parallel to the y-axis, located 4 units to the left of the y-axis.
(Award 2 Marks for correctly plotting a vertical line passing through x = -4)
Q9. If the point \( (3, 4) \) lies on the graph of the equation \( 3y = ax + 7 \), find the value of \( a \).
[3 Marks]
Since the point \( (3, 4) \) lies on the line \( 3y = ax + 7 \), it must satisfy the equation.
Substitute \( x = 3 \) and \( y = 4 \) into the equation: (1 Mark)
\( 3(4) = a(3) + 7 \)
\( 12 = 3a + 7 \) (1 Mark)
\( 12 - 7 = 3a \)
\( 5 = 3a \)
\( a = \frac{5}{3} \) (1 Mark)

SECTION C SOLUTIONS (5 Marks Each)
Q11. Taxi fare: ₹ 8 for 1st km, ₹ 5 per km for subsequent. Distance \( x \), fare ₹ \( y \). (i) Write equation. (ii) Draw graph.
[5 Marks]
(i) Forming the equation:
Total distance = \( x \) km.
Fare for 1st km = ₹ 8
Remaining distance = \( (x - 1) \) km.
Fare for subsequent distance = ₹ \( 5 \times (x - 1) \)
Total fare (\( y \)) = Fare for 1st km + Fare for remaining distance
\( y = 8 + 5(x - 1) \)
\( y = 8 + 5x - 5 \)
\( y = 5x + 3 \) OR \( 5x - y + 3 = 0 \) (2 Marks)
(ii) Drawing the graph:
Find at least two solutions for \( y = 5x + 3 \):
  • If \( x = 0, y = 3 \). Point: \( (0, 3) \)
  • If \( x = 1, y = 8 \). Point: \( (1, 8) \)
  • If \( x = 2, y = 13 \). Point: \( (2, 13) \)
(Award 3 Marks for correctly plotting points, drawing a straight line, and labelling axes. Note: For practical application, \(x\) and \(y\) should be positive in the graph quadrant).
Q12. \( F = \left(\frac{9}{5}\right)C + 32 \). (i) If \( 30^\circ\text{C} \), find F. (ii) If \( 95^\circ\text{F} \), find C. (iii) Find same numerical temp.
[5 Marks]
(i) Temperature is \( 30^\circ\text{C} \): (1.5 Marks)
\( F = \left(\frac{9}{5}\right)(30) + 32 \)
\( F = 9 \times 6 + 32 = 54 + 32 = 86 \)
Temperature in Fahrenheit = \( 86^\circ\text{F} \)
(ii) Temperature is \( 95^\circ\text{F} \): (1.5 Marks)
\( 95 = \left(\frac{9}{5}\right)C + 32 \)
\( 95 - 32 = \left(\frac{9}{5}\right)C \)
\( 63 = \left(\frac{9}{5}\right)C \)
\( C = \frac{63 \times 5}{9} = 7 \times 5 = 35 \)
Temperature in Celsius = \( 35^\circ\text{C} \)
(iii) Numerically same temperature: (2 Marks)
Let \( F = C = x \). Substitute in the equation:
\( x = \left(\frac{9}{5}\right)x + 32 \)
\( x - \left(\frac{9}{5}\right)x = 32 \)
\( \frac{5x - 9x}{5} = 32 \)
\( -4x = 160 \)
\( x = -40 \)
Yes, \( -40^\circ \) is the same in both scales.

  • \( x = 4 \Rightarrow y = 0 \). Point: \( (4, 0) \)

Checking point \( (2, 2) \):
Substitute \( x = 2, y = 2 \) into \( x + y = 4 \).
LHS = \( 2 + 2 = 4 \). RHS = \( 4 \). LHS = RHS.
Yes, \( (2, 2) \) lies on the line. (1.5 Marks)
Q14. Give equations of two lines passing through \( (2, 14) \). How many more? Why?
[5 Marks]
Two equations: We need to create algebraic relations satisfying \( x = 2, y = 14 \).
Example 1: \( y = 7x \) (since \( 14 = 7 \times 2 \))
Example 2: \( x + y = 16 \) (since \( 2 + 14 = 16 \))
Example 3: \( y - x = 12 \)
(Award 2 Marks for any two correct equations).
How many more such lines?
There are infinitely many more such lines. (1 Mark)
Logical geometric explanation:
In Euclidean geometry, through a single given point in a plane, infinitely many straight lines can pass. Since a linear equation in two variables represents a straight line, and the coordinate \( (2, 14) \) represents a single point in the Cartesian plane, an infinite number of lines (and thus, equations) can pass through it in various directions (slopes). (2 Marks)
Q15. (i) Equation where ordinate is 3 times abscissa. (ii) Intersection of \( \frac{x}{2} + \frac{y}{3} = 1 \) on axes.
[5 Marks]
(i) Linear Equation: (2 Marks)
Let abscissa (x-coordinate) = \( x \)
Let ordinate (y-coordinate) = \( y \)
Condition: Ordinate is 3 times the abscissa.
\( y = 3x \) OR \( 3x - y = 0 \)
(ii) Intersection points: (3 Marks)
Equation: \( \frac{x}{2} + \frac{y}{3} = 1 \)
x-axis intersection: Put \( y = 0 \).
\( \frac{x}{2} + \frac{0}{3} = 1 \Rightarrow \frac{x}{2} = 1 \Rightarrow x = 2 \)
Coordinates: \( (2, 0) \)
y-axis intersection: Put \( x = 0 \).
\( \frac{0}{2} + \frac{y}{3} = 1 \Rightarrow \frac{y}{3} = 1 \Rightarrow y = 3 \)
Coordinates: \( (0, 3) \)
