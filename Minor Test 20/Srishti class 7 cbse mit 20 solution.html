Q7. Cause of Muscle Cramps & Hot Water Bath Relief.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Cause of Cramps: During heavy physical exertion, the demand for energy is high, but the supply of oxygen to muscle cells is limited. Therefore, muscle cells respire anaerobically to meet the energy demand, breaking down glucose into lactic acid. Accumulation of lactic acid causes muscle cramps. [1.5 Marks]
  • (b) Relief Mechanism: A hot water bath or massage improves blood circulation in the body. As a result, the supply of oxygen to muscle cells increases, leading to the complete breakdown of lactic acid into carbon dioxide and water. [1.5 Marks]
Q8. Respiration in Earthworm and Cockroach.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Earthworm Respiration: Earthworms breathe through their skin. Their skin feels moist and slimy to touch. Gases can easily pass through this moist surface directly into blood vessels. [1.5 Marks]
  • (b) Insect Respiratory System: Insects like cockroaches have a network of air tubes called tracheae (or tracheal system) inside their body for exchange of gases. [1.5 Marks]
Q9. Movements during Inhalation and Exhalation.
[3 Marks]
  • (i) Inhalation (Breathing In): [1.5 Marks]
    • The ribcage moves upwards and outwards.
    • The diaphragm contracts and moves downwards (flattens), enlarging the chest cavity so air rushes into the lungs.
  • (ii) Exhalation (Breathing Out): [1.5 Marks]
    • The ribcage moves downwards and inwards.
    • The diaphragm relaxes and moves upwards back to its dome shape, pushing air out of the lungs.
Q10. Stomata & Root Respiration in Plants.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Leaf Pores: Tiny pores on the surface of leaves called Stomata (singular: Stoma) facilitate gas exchange. [1 Mark]
  • (b) Root Respiration: Like all living cells, root cells require oxygen to release energy. Roots take in air from the small air spaces present between the soil particles through root hairs. [2 Marks]

Q11. Cellular Respiration Definition & Word Equations.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Cellular Respiration: The process of breakdown of food (glucose) in the cells of an organism to release energy is called cellular respiration. It takes place inside the cells (cytoplasm and mitochondria) of all living organisms. [2 Marks]
  • (b) Word Equations:
    i) Aerobic Respiration (With Oxygen): [1.5 Marks]
    \[\text{Glucose} \xrightarrow{\text{With Oxygen}} \text{Carbon Dioxide} + \text{Water} + \text{Energy}\]

    ii) Anaerobic Respiration in Human Muscles (Without Oxygen): [1.5 Marks]
    \[\text{Glucose} \xrightarrow[\text{in Muscles}]{\text{Without Oxygen}} \text{Lactic Acid} + \text{Energy}\]
Q12. Human Respiratory Pathway & Cause of Sneezing.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Path of Inhaled Air: [3 Marks]
    Air enters through Nostrils \(\rightarrow\) passes into Nasal Cavity \(\rightarrow\) travels down the Windpipe (Trachea) \(\rightarrow\) enters Lungs (via Bronchi and Bronchioles).
  • (b) Reason for Sneezing: [2 Marks]
    • When we breathe in air containing unwanted foreign particles (like dust, pollen, or smoke), these particles get trapped in the hair present in our nasal cavity.
    • Sometimes particles irritate the lining of the cavity, triggering a sneeze response to expel foreign dust particles so that clean air enters our lungs.
Q13. Gills Functioning in Fish & Breathing Rate of Athletes.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Respiration in Fish (Gills): [2.5 Marks]
    • Fish use special respiratory organs called gills to breathe underwater.
    • Gills are projections of the skin that are richly supplied with blood vessels for gas exchange.
    • As water passes over the gills, dissolved oxygen from the water is absorbed into the blood, and carbon dioxide is released into the water.
  • (b) Increased Breathing Rate in Athletes: [2.5 Marks]
    • During a sprint race, the athlete's muscle cells work very hard and require a large amount of extra energy.
    • To release more energy, food needs to be broken down faster, which demands a greater supply of oxygen.
    • Therefore, the athlete breathes faster and deeper to bring more oxygen into the body and quickly clear out extra carbon dioxide generated.
