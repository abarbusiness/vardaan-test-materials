Q3. State the exact location and primary function of the SA Node (Pacemaker).
[2 Marks]
  • Location: Situated in the upper right wall of the Right Atrium near the opening of the superior vena cava. [1 Mark]
  • Function: It generates electrical impulses that initiate and maintain the rhythmic contraction (heartbeat) of the heart muscles. Hence, it is known as the natural pacemaker. [1 Mark]

SECTION B: SHORT ANSWER & REASONING (15 MARKS)
Q6. Give scientific reasons: (a) Thicker ventricular walls (b) Absence of nucleus in mature RBCs.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Ventricular Walls: Atria only pump blood into the adjacent ventricles (short distance/low pressure), whereas ventricles must pump blood with high force to distant organs (left ventricle pumps blood through Aorta to the entire body). Thus, ventricles require thicker muscular walls (\(\text{myocardium}\)) to withstand and exert high pressure. [1.5 Marks]
  • (b) Enucleated RBCs Adaptation:
    1. Loss of nucleus creates a biconcave shape that increases the surface-area-to-volume ratio for rapid diffusion of oxygen. [1 Mark]
    2. It accommodates more Hemoglobin molecules within the cell to carry maximal oxygen. [0.5 Mark]

Q8. (a) What is lymph and tissue fluid formation? (b) Two differences between Blood and Lymph.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Lymph Definition: As blood flows through arterial capillaries under high pressure, water and small soluble solutes filter out through thin capillary walls into intercellular spaces, forming interstitial fluid (tissue fluid). When this fluid enters lymphatic capillaries, it is called lymph. [1 Mark]
  • (b) Differences between Blood and Lymph: [2 Marks - 1 Mark each point]
Parameter Blood Lymph
Composition Contains RBCs, WBCs, Platelets, and high plasma proteins. Red in color. Lacks RBCs and Platelets; contains mainly Lymphocytes. Colorless/pale yellow.
Flow Direction Circulates in a closed circular loop (Heart \(\rightarrow\) Arteries \(\rightarrow\) Tissues \(\rightarrow\) Veins \(\rightarrow\) Heart). Flows in a single direction from body tissues toward the heart.
Q9. ABO Blood group system: (a) Universal Donor (b) Universal Acceptor (c) Rh antigen.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Universal Donor (Group O): RBCs of Group O lack both Antigen A and Antigen B on their surface. Therefore, donating Group O blood does not trigger an immune reaction (agglutination) in any recipient. [1 Mark]
  • (b) Universal Acceptor (Group AB): Plasma of Group AB lacks both Anti-A and Anti-B antibodies. Hence, an individual with AB blood can receive RBCs from any blood group without antibody attack. [1 Mark]
  • (c) Rh Factor Antigen: Rhesus Antigen (Rh D Antigen) first discovered in Rhesus monkeys. [1 Mark]

(b) Valves & Mechanism: [2 Marks - 1 Mark each]

  • (i) Bicuspid (Mitral) Valve: Located between Left Atrium and Left Ventricle. It allows unidirectional flow of oxygenated blood from left atrium to left ventricle and strictly prevents backward flow during ventricular systole.
  • (ii) Pulmonary Semilunar Valve: Located at the opening of the Right Ventricle into the Pulmonary Artery. It prevents blood from flowing back into the right ventricle when the ventricle relaxes (diastole).
Q12. Blood Pressure Dynamics: (a) Definition & Standard values (b) Systolic vs Diastolic (c) Hypertension.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Blood Pressure Definition & Value:
    The lateral pressure exerted by flowing blood against the walls of major arteries as it is pumped by the heart. [1 Mark]
    Standard normal resting reading: 120 / 80 mmHg (Millimeters of Mercury). [1 Mark]
  • (b) Systolic vs Diastolic Pressure: [2 Marks]
    • Systolic Pressure: Maximum pressure exerted in arteries during ventricular contraction (systole). Normal value \(\approx 120\text{ mmHg}\).
    • Diastolic Pressure: Minimum pressure remaining in arteries during ventricular relaxation (diastole). Normal value \(\approx 80\text{ mmHg}\).
  • (c) Hypertension:
    A clinical condition characterized by persistent, abnormally high blood pressure (consistently exceeding \(140/90\text{ mmHg}\)). [0.5 Mark]
    Risk/Damage: Can lead to damage of renal capillaries (kidney failure), rupture of cerebral arteries (stroke/brain hemorrhage), or cardiac arrest. [0.5 Mark]
Q13. (a) Cascade of blood clotting mechanism (b) Structural features of Capillaries.
[5 Marks]

(a) Blood Clotting Mechanism (Coagulation Cascade): [3 Marks]

  1. Step 1 (Release of Thromboplastin): Injured tissue cells and ruptured platelets at the wound site release an enzyme called Thromboplastin / Thrombokinase. [1 Mark]
  2. Step 2 (Conversion of Prothrombin): In the presence of Calcium ions (\(\text{Ca}^{2+}\)) and Vitamin K, thrombokinase converts inactive plasma protein Prothrombin into active Thrombin. [1 Mark]
  3. Step 3 (Meshwork Formation): Thrombin converts soluble plasma protein Fibrinogen into insoluble thread-like Fibrin strands. Fibrin fibers form a mesh trapped with blood cells to form a solid clot (scab), stopping hemorrhage. [1 Mark]

(b) Structural Adaptations of Capillaries for Diffusion: [2 Marks - 1 Mark each]

  • 1. Extremely Thin Walls: Made up of a single layer of squamous epithelial cells called endothelium, allowing rapid passive diffusion of \(\text{O}_2\), \(\text{CO}_2\), glucose, and metabolic wastes.
  • 2. Narrow Diameter & Vast Network: Microscopic diameter (just wide enough for RBCs to pass in single file) reduces flow velocity and brings blood in close contact with surrounding tissue cells.
