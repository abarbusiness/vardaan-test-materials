Q2. True/False: (a) Parallel venation in dicots (b) Transpiration through stomata.
[2 Marks]
  • (a) False. Correction: Parallel venation is a characteristic feature of monocotyledonous plants (e.g., grass, maize, banana), whereas dicot plants show reticulate venation. [1 Mark]
  • (b) True. Stomata open to facilitate gas exchange and release water vapour during transpiration. [1 Mark]
Q3. Definitions: (a) Leaf Venation (b) Phyllotaxy.
[2 Marks]
  • (a) Leaf Venation: The arrangement or pattern of veins and veinlets distributed in the lamina of a leaf. [1 Mark]
  • (b) Phyllotaxy: The systematic arrangement of leaves on the stem or branch of a plant. [1 Mark]

Q6. Simple Leaf vs Compound Leaf.

Criteria Simple Leaf Compound Leaf

Q7. Patterns of Leaf Arrangement (Phyllotaxy).
[3 Marks]
  • 1. Alternate Phyllotaxy: Only a single leaf arises at each node in an alternating sequence (e.g., Sunflower, Mango, Hibiscus). [1 Mark]
  • 2. Opposite Phyllotaxy: A pair of leaves arises opposite to each other at the same node (e.g., Guava, Tulsi/Calotropis). [1 Mark]
  • 3. Whorled Phyllotaxy: Three or more leaves grow at a single node forming a circular whorl or ring (e.g., Oleander/Nerium). [1 Mark]
Q8. Reticulate vs Parallel Venation and Examples.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Comparison: [2 Marks]
    • Reticulate Venation: Veins and veinlets branch randomly to form an irregular network/web. Common in dicot plants.
    • Parallel Venation: Veins run parallel to one another from the leaf base to the apex without forming a network. Common in monocot plants.
  • (b) Identification: [1 Mark]
    • (i) Rose leaf \(\rightarrow\) Reticulate Venation [0.5 Mark]
    • (ii) Maize leaf \(\rightarrow\) Parallel Venation [0.5 Mark]
Q9. Process & Benefits of Transpiration in Plants.
[3 Marks]
  • Process: Excess absorbed water travels up through xylem tissue to leaves and evaporates into the atmosphere as water vapour through microscopic stomata. [1 Mark]
  • Two Vital Benefits: [2 Marks - 1 Mark each]
    1. Cooling Effect: Evaporative loss cools leaf surfaces, protecting cells from scorching heat under bright sunlight.
    2. Transpiration Pull: Creates a strong suction force that pulls water and dissolved mineral nutrients upward from roots to tall branches.
Q10. Insectivorous Plants & Modification in Pitcher Plant.
[3 Marks]
  • Reason for trapping insects: Insectivorous plants grow in swampy, nitrogen-deficient soils. They digest captured insects to satisfy their nitrogen requirement for building proteins. [1.5 Marks]
  • Modification of Pitcher Plant: [1.5 Marks]
    • The leaf lamina modifies into a hollow pitcher-shaped vessel filled with digestive fluids.
    • The apex of the leaf forms a movable lid that closes after an insect enters.
SECTION C: LONG ANSWER SOLUTIONS (15 MARKS)
Q11. Parts of a Typical Leaf & Photosynthesis Word Equation.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Four Main Parts of a Leaf: [3 Marks]
    • Petiole: Stalk attaching lamina to stem, positioning leaf to capture maximum sunlight.
    • Lamina: Flat green area containing chlorophyll where food synthesis occurs.
    • Midrib: Central thick main vein running along the center of the lamina providing strength.
    • Veins: Vascular strands branching from midrib that conduct water, minerals, and synthesized food.
  • (b) Photosynthesis Word Equation: [2 Marks]
    $$\text{Carbon Dioxide} + \text{Water} \xrightarrow[\text{Chlorophyll}]{\text{Sunlight}} \text{Glucose (Food)} + \text{Oxygen}$$
Q12. Leaf Modifications & Vegetative Reproduction in Bryophyllum.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Three Leaf Modifications: [3 Marks - 1 Mark each]
    1. Leaf Tendrils: Leaves modify into coiled thread-like structures for climbing support (e.g., Pea plant).
    2. Leaf Spines: Leaves reduce into sharp needles to minimize transpiration and deter herbivores (e.g., Cactus/Opuntia).
    3. Scale Leaves: Thin dry or fleshy leaves protecting axillary buds or storing food (e.g., Onion, Ginger).
  • (b) Reproduction in Bryophyllum: [2 Marks]
    • Bryophyllum leaves possess notch-like margins containing special **adventitious buds**.
    • When a leaf detaches and falls on moist soil, these buds sprout roots and shoots, growing into new independent plantlets.
Q13. Transpiration Experiment & Gas Exchange in Stomata.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Transpiration Experiment: [3 Marks]
    • Setup: Take a well-watered potted leafy plant. Cover a healthy leafy shoot with a transparent polythene bag and tie its neck tightly around the stem base. Place in bright sunlight for 2-3 hours.
    • Observation: Tiny droplets of water collect on the inner surface of the transparent polythene bag.
    • Inference: Proves that plant leaves lose excess water in the form of water vapour via transpiration.
  • (b) Stomata & Guard Cells Function: [2 Marks]
    • Stomata are microscopic pores guarded by two bean-shaped **guard cells**.
    • Guard cells swell and open stomata during daytime to absorb carbon dioxide (\(\text{CO}_2\)) for photosynthesis, and regulate oxygen (\(\text{O}_2\)) release during plant respiration.
