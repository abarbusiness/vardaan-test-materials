SECTION A (10 Marks)
Q1. Define the terms 'Range' and 'Mean' in the context of statistics.
[2 Marks]
Range: The difference between the highest and the lowest observations in a given set of data is called its range.
$Range = Highest \ observation - Lowest \ observation$
Mean: The mean (or arithmetic mean) is the sum of all observations divided by the total number of observations.
$Mean = \frac{\text{Sum of all observations}}{\text{Total number of observations}}$
Q2. Find the mean of the first five prime numbers.
[2 Marks]
Mean = 5.6
The first five prime numbers are: $2, 3, 5, 7, 11$.
Total number of observations (n) = $5$.
Sum of observations = $2 + 3 + 5 + 7 + 11 = 28$
$Mean = \frac{28}{5} = 5.6$
Q3. A fair coin is tossed once. What is the probability of getting a tail?
[2 Marks]
Probability = 1/2
When a fair coin is tossed, the possible outcomes are Head (H) and Tail (T).
Total number of possible outcomes = 2.
Number of favorable outcomes (getting a Tail) = 1.
$P(\text{Tail}) = \frac{\text{Favorable outcomes}}{\text{Total outcomes}} = \frac{1}{2}$

Q5. The marks obtained by 7 students in a surprise test are: 15, 20, 15, 18, 14, 20, 15. Find the mode of the given data.
[2 Marks]
Mode = 15
Mode is the observation that occurs most frequently in the data set.
Let's count the frequency of each mark:
  • 14 occurs 1 time.
  • 15 occurs 3 times.
  • 18 occurs 1 time.
  • 20 occurs 2 times.
Since 15 occurs the maximum number of times (3 times), the mode is 15.
SECTION B (15 Marks)
Q6. Find the median of the following set of observations: 24, 36, 46, 17, 18, 25, 35.
[3 Marks]
Median = 25
First, arrange the data in ascending order:
$17, 18, 24, 25, 35, 36, 46$. (1 Mark)
Total number of observations (n) = $7$ (which is odd).
Formula for median when n is odd:
$Median = \left( \frac{n + 1}{2} \right)^{\text{th}} \text{term}$ (1 Mark)
$Median = \left( \frac{7 + 1}{2} \right)^{\text{th}} \text{term} = \left( \frac{8}{2} \right)^{\text{th}} \text{term} = 4^{\text{th}} \text{term}$
The 4th term in the ordered data is 25. Therefore, Median = 25. (1 Mark)
Q7. A standard die is rolled once. Find the probability of getting: (i) an even number. (ii) a number strictly greater than 4.
[3 Marks]
Total possible outcomes when a die is rolled = $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$. Total outcomes = $6$. (1 Mark)
(i) Probability of an even number:
Favorable outcomes = $\{2, 4, 6\}$. Number of favorable outcomes = $3$.
$P(\text{Even}) = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$. (1 Mark)
(ii) Probability of a number strictly greater than 4:
Favorable outcomes (numbers > 4) = $\{5, 6\}$. Number of favorable outcomes = $2$.
$P(>4) = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}$. (1 Mark)
Q8. A bag contains 4 red balls, 5 blue balls, and 3 green balls. One ball is drawn at random. Find the probability that the ball drawn is (i) red. (ii) not green.
[3 Marks]
Total number of balls in the bag = $4 \text{ (red)} + 5 \text{ (blue)} + 3 \text{ (green)} = 12$. Total outcomes = 12. (1 Mark)
(i) Probability of drawing a red ball:
Number of red balls = $4$.
$P(\text{Red}) = \frac{4}{12} = \frac{1}{3}$. (1 Mark)
(ii) Probability of drawing a ball that is not green:
A ball is "not green" if it is red or blue. Number of favorable outcomes = $4 + 5 = 9$.
$P(\text{Not Green}) = \frac{9}{12} = \frac{3}{4}$. (1 Mark)
Q9. The mean of 5 observations $x, x+2, x+4, x+6, \text{ and } x+8$ is 11. Find the value of $x$.
[3 Marks]
$x = 7$
Given Mean = $11$ and Total observations = $5$.
$Mean = \frac{\text{Sum of observations}}{\text{Number of observations}}$
$11 = \frac{x + (x+2) + (x+4) + (x+6) + (x+8)}{5}$ (1 Mark)
$11 = \frac{5x + 20}{5}$
$55 = 5x + 20$ (1 Mark)
$5x = 55 - 20$
$5x = 35$
$x = 7$ (1 Mark)

Q12. A box contains 25 identical cards numbered from 1 to 25. Find the probability that the drawn card is (a) a multiple of 5 (b) a prime number (c) a perfect square.
[5 Marks]
Total cards (Total outcomes) = $25$.
(a) A multiple of 5: (2 Marks)
Multiples of 5 from 1 to 25 = $\{5, 10, 15, 20, 25\}$. Favorable outcomes = $5$.
$P(\text{Multiple of 5}) = \frac{5}{25} = \frac{1}{5}$.
(b) A prime number: (2 Marks)
Prime numbers from 1 to 25 = $\{2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23\}$. Favorable outcomes = $9$.
$P(\text{Prime Number}) = \frac{9}{25}$.
(c) A perfect square: (1 Mark)
Perfect squares from 1 to 25 = $\{1, 4, 9, 16, 25\}$. Favorable outcomes = $5$.
$P(\text{Perfect Square}) = \frac{5}{25} = \frac{1}{5}$.
Q13. Runs scored in 7 matches: 45, 89, 12, 105, 56, 34, 78. (a) Mean runs. (b) Median runs. (c) Will the median increase or decrease if 50 runs are scored in the 8th match?
[5 Marks]
(a) Mean Runs: (2 Marks)
Sum of runs = $45 + 89 + 12 + 105 + 56 + 34 + 78 = 419$.
$Mean = \frac{419}{7} = 59.85$ (approx).
(b) Median Runs: (2 Marks)
Arrange in ascending order: $12, 34, 45, 56, 78, 89, 105$.
Number of observations ($n$) = 7 (odd).
$Median = \left(\frac{7+1}{2}\right)^{\text{th}} \text{term} = 4^{\text{th}} \text{term}$.
The 4th term is 56. Median = 56.
(c) Change in Median: (1 Mark)
If 50 runs are scored in the 8th match, the new data in ascending order is:
$12, 34, 45, 50, 56, 78, 89, 105$.
Now $n = 8$ (even). The median is the average of the 4th and 5th terms.
$New \ Median = \frac{50 + 56}{2} = \frac{106}{2} = 53$.
The old median was 56 and the new median is 53. Therefore, the median will decrease.
