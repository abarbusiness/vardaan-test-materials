SECTION B: SHORT ANSWER SOLUTIONS (15 MARKS)
Q6. Definition of Photosynthesis and Chemical Equation.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Definition: Photosynthesis is the biological process by which green plants prepare their own food (glucose) from carbon dioxide and water in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll, releasing oxygen as a byproduct. [1.5 Marks]
  • (b) Equation: [1.5 Marks]
    \[\text{Carbon Dioxide} + \text{Water} \xrightarrow[\text{Chlorophyll}]{\text{Sunlight}} \text{Glucose} + \text{Oxygen}\] \[\text{OR} \quad 6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow[\text{Chlorophyll}]{\text{Sunlight}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2\]
Q7. Role of Stomata and Chlorophyll in Photosynthesis.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Stomata: They are tiny pores located on the leaf surface that facilitate the entry of carbon dioxide gas from atmospheric air into the leaf and the exit of oxygen gas produced during photosynthesis. [1.5 Marks]
  • (b) Chlorophyll: It is a green pigment located inside chloroplasts that traps and absorbs solar energy (sunlight) necessary to drive the chemical reaction of food synthesis. [1.5 Marks]
Q8. Concept of De-starching and Starch Reagent.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Reason for De-starching: The plant is kept in darkness so that it cannot perform fresh photosynthesis. In the absence of light, the plant uses up all the previously stored starch in its leaves for energy. This ensures that any starch detected during the experiment is formed newly. [2 Marks]
  • (b) Reagent: Dilute Iodine Solution. [1 Mark]

Q10. Raw Materials for Photosynthesis and Their Sources.
[3 Marks]
Raw Materials & Sources Breakdown
  • 1. Carbon Dioxide (\(\text{CO}_2\)): Obtained from atmospheric air through stomata on leaves. [0.75 Mark]
  • 2. Water (\(\text{H}_2\text{O}\)): Absorbed from soil by root hairs and transported to leaves via xylem vessels. [0.75 Mark]
  • 3. Sunlight: Radiant energy trapped directly from the Sun. [0.75 Mark]
  • 4. Chlorophyll: Already present inside the chloroplasts of green leaf cells. [0.75 Mark]
SECTION C: LONG ANSWER SOLUTIONS (15 MARKS)
Q11. Experiment: Oxygen Gas Release During Photosynthesis.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Apparatus Required: A large glass beaker, water, short-stemmed glass funnel, test tube, and fresh Hydrilla twigs. [1 Mark]
  • (b) Brief Procedure: [2 Marks]
    • Place fresh Hydrilla twigs inside the beaker filled with water.
    • Invert a glass funnel over the twigs.
    • Fill a test tube completely with water and invert it over the stem of the funnel carefully without letting air enter.
    • Place the entire setup in direct bright sunlight for a few hours.
  • (c) Observation & Gas Test: [2 Marks]
    • Observation: Tiny gas bubbles rise continuously from the twigs and collect at the top of the test tube, displacing water downward.
    • Test: Bring a glowing wooden splint near the mouth of the inverted test tube. The glowing splint bursts into flame, confirming that the collected gas is Oxygen.
Q12. Structural Adaptations of Leaf for Photosynthesis.
[5 Marks]
Leaf Structural Adaptations (Any 4 points)
  • 1. Broad Flat Lamina (Blade): Provides a large surface area to absorb maximum solar light and exchange gases efficiently. [1.25 Marks]
  • 2. Thin Structure: Allows easy penetration of sunlight and rapid diffusion of gases through all cell layers. [1.25 Marks]
  • 3. Presence of Numerous Stomata: Allows rapid intake of carbon dioxide gas and release of oxygen gas. [1.25 Marks]
  • 4. Network of Veins (Xylem & Phloem): Provides continuous transport of water to photosynthetic cells and carries prepared food away to other plant parts. [1.25 Marks]
Q13. Starch Test Procedure in a Leaf & Safety Precaution.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Step-by-Step Procedure: [3.5 Marks]
    i) Pluck a fresh green leaf from a plant exposed to sunlight for a few hours.
    ii) Boil the leaf in water for 2-3 minutes to kill the leaf cells and render them permeable. [1 Mark]
    iii) Transfer the leaf to a beaker containing alcohol and boil it in a water bath to remove chlorophyll (bleaching the leaf pale white). [1 Mark]
    iv) Wash the de-colorized leaf thoroughly in warm water to soften it. [0.5 Mark]
    v) Place the leaf flat on a white tile and add a few drops of dilute iodine solution.
    vi) Observation: The leaf turns blue-black, proving the presence of starch. [1 Mark]
  • (b) Safety Precaution (Water Bath): [1.5 Marks]
    • Alcohol is highly volatile and highly inflammable.
    • If heated directly over a burner flame, alcohol can catch fire immediately. Therefore, it is always heated safely inside a water bath.
