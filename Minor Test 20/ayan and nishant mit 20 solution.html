Q7. Three main functions of a leaf.
[3 Marks]
  • 1. Photosynthesis: Leaves prepare food for the plant using carbon dioxide, water, and sunlight in the presence of chlorophyll. [1 Mark]
  • 2. Transpiration: Leaves release excess water vapour into the air through stomata, creating a cooling effect and pull for nutrients. [1 Mark]
  • 3. Respiration (Gas Exchange): Leaves take in oxygen for respiration and release carbon dioxide through stomata. [1 Mark]
Q8. Simple Leaf vs Compound Leaf.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Simple Leaf: A leaf in which the lamina/blade is single and undivided (or not completely divided down to the midrib). Example: Mango, Peepal, Hibiscus. [1.5 Marks]
  • (b) Compound Leaf: A leaf in which the blade is completely divided into small individual leaf-like segments called leaflets. Example: Rose, Neem, Tamarind. [1.5 Marks]
Q9. Advantages of leaf spines in Cactus.
[3 Marks]
  • 1. Reduces Transpiration: Spines drastically cut down surface area, minimizing loss of water by evaporation. [1 Mark]
  • 2. Protection: Sharp spines protect the plant from being eaten by grazing desert animals. [1 Mark]
  • 3. Trapping Dew/Moisture: Spines help collect tiny drops of morning dew that trickle down to the roots. [1 Mark]
Q10. Insectivorous Plants: Definition and why they trap insects.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Definition: Plants that trap, digest, and absorb nutrients from small insects are called insectivorous (or carnivorous) plants. [1 Mark]
  • (b) Reason: These plants grow in nitrogen-deficient soil (like marshy or boggy areas). Although they make carbohydrate food through photosynthesis, they trap and digest insects to fulfill their nitrogen needs required for growth. [2 Marks]
SECTION C: LONG ANSWER & DETAILED CONCEPTS (15 MARKS)
Q11. Parts of a leaf & Leaf Tendrils explanation.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Main Parts of a Leaf: [3 Marks - 1 Mark each]
    • Petiole: The short stalk that attaches the leaf blade to the stem.
    • Lamina (Blade): The flat, broad, green part of the leaf where food preparation occurs.
    • Midrib and Veins: Midrib is the main central thick vein. Side veins branch off from it to transport water, minerals, and synthesized food throughout the leaf.
  • (b) Leaf Modifications & Leaf Tendrils: [2 Marks]
    • Leaf Modifications: Changes in the structure of leaves to perform functions other than photosynthesis (e.g., support, defense, storage).
    • Leaf Tendril: In weak-stemmed plants (e.g., Pea plant), parts of leaves are modified into thin, coiled, thread-like structures called tendrils that coil around supports to help the plant climb upright.
Q12. Leaf Adaptations in Submerged, Mountain, and Floating Plants.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Submerged Aquatic Leaves (e.g., Hydrilla/Tape grass): [2 Marks]
    • Leaves are thin, narrow, ribbon-like or finely divided so water currents pass through them easily without tearing them.
    • Stomata are absent; gases are absorbed directly across the thin leaf surface.
  • (b) Mountain/Hilly Trees Leaves (e.g., Pine/Fir): [2 Marks]
    • Leaves are thin and needle-like with a thick waxy coating to prevent water loss and survive freezing temperatures.
    • The needle shape prevents heavy snow from accumulating on branches, allowing snow to slide off easily.
  • (c) Floating Water Lily Stomata Position: [1 Mark]
    Stomata are present only on the upper surface exposed to air because the lower surface remains submerged in water where gas exchange with air is impossible.
Q13. Transpiration, Benefits, and Guard Cells.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Transpiration: The biological process by which plants lose excess water in the form of water vapour through stomata present on the leaves. [1.5 Marks]
  • (b) Two Benefits of Transpiration: [2 Marks - 1 Mark each]
    1. Cooling Effect: Evaporation of water keeps the plant cool during hot summer days.
    2. Suction Pull: It creates a transpiration pull that helps draw water and dissolved mineral nutrients upward from roots to top leaves.
  • (c) Guard Cells: [1.5 Marks]
    Each stomatal pore is surrounded by two kidney-shaped Guard Cells. They regulate the opening and closing of the stomata based on the plant's water levels.
