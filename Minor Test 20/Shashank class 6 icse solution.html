SECTION A (10 Marks)
Q1. Define atmosphere. Name the gas in the air that is essential for respiration.
[2 Marks]
Atmosphere: The blanket of air that surrounds the Earth is called the atmosphere.
Gas for respiration: Oxygen ($O_2$) is the gas essential for respiration by living organisms.
Q2. Define the term 'Ore'. Are all minerals considered ores?
[2 Marks]
Ore: A mineral in its natural, unmined form from which metals can be profitably extracted is known as an ore.
Are all minerals ores? No. All ores are minerals, but not all minerals are ores. Only those minerals from which metal extraction is profitable are called ores.
Q3. Differentiate between Ferrous and Non-Ferrous minerals, providing one example of each.
[2 Marks]
Ferrous Minerals: These are metallic minerals that contain iron. Example: Iron ore or Manganese.
Non-Ferrous Minerals: These are metallic minerals that do not contain iron. Example: Gold, Silver, or Copper.
Q4. Give two examples of non-metallic minerals and state one physical property that sets them apart from metallic minerals.
[2 Marks]
Examples: Limestone, Mica, Gypsum, Coal, or Petroleum (any two).
Property: Unlike metallic minerals, non-metallic minerals are brittle (break easily), lack a shiny lustre (appear dull), and are poor conductors of heat and electricity.
Q5. What are fossil fuels? Give two examples of fossil fuels.
[2 Marks]
Fossil Fuels: They are non-metallic minerals of organic origin formed from the remains of dead plants and animals buried deep within the Earth's surface over millions of years under intense heat and pressure.
Examples: Coal, Petroleum, Natural Gas (any two).
SECTION B (15 Marks)
Q6. Write any three important functions or uses of the air/atmosphere for living organisms on Earth.
[3 Marks]
Three important functions are:
  • It provides Oxygen ($O_2$) which is essential for respiration in plants and animals.
  • It provides Carbon dioxide ($CO_2$) which plants use to make food during photosynthesis.
  • It acts as a protective blanket that regulates Earth's temperature and protects living beings from the sun's harmful rays.

[3 Marks]
Metallic Minerals Non-Metallic Minerals
1. They contain extractable metals in raw form. 1. They do not contain extractable metals.
2. They are malleable, ductile, and generally hard. 2. They are brittle and break easily.
3. They are good conductors of heat and electricity. 3. They are poor conductors of heat and electricity.
Q8. State one major industrial use for each of the following minerals: (a) Bauxite (b) Manganese (c) Mica
[3 Marks]
(a) Bauxite: Used in manufacturing aircraft, automobiles, household appliances, and electrical equipment (as it is the main ore of aluminium).
(b) Manganese: Used to make steel tough and rust-resistant, or used in bleaching powder, black enamel, and dry cell batteries.
(c) Mica: Heavily used in the electrical industry because it is an excellent insulator.
Q9. Explain the difference between 'Quarrying' and 'Open-cast Mining'.
[3 Marks]
Quarrying: This is a method of mining used when minerals occur very close to the surface; they are simply dug out.
Open-cast Mining: This method is used when minerals are found at slightly shallow depths. They are extracted by removing the top layer of soil and rocks (overburden) to reach the deposits below.
Q10. Why is Petroleum often referred to as "Liquid Gold"? Name two places in India where petroleum is found.
[3 Marks]
Why "Liquid Gold": Petroleum is called "Liquid Gold" due to its immense economic value and high demand. It is crucial as a fuel for vehicles, ships, and airplanes, and yields valuable by-products like kerosene, wax, and lubricants.
Places in India: Digboi in Assam and Mumbai High.
SECTION C (15 Marks)
Q11. (a) Name the two most abundant gases present in the Earth's atmosphere. (b) Suggest and explain any four ways to conserve minerals.
[5 Marks]
(a) Gases: Nitrogen and Oxygen. (1 Mark)
(b) Four ways to conserve minerals: (4 Marks)
  1. Reduce Wastage: Adopt better and more efficient mining and processing methods to minimize the loss of minerals.
  2. Recycling: Recycle scrap metals and reuse old products made from metals to reduce the need for fresh extraction.
  3. Use Alternatives: Replace scarce and rare minerals with more abundant ones (e.g., substituting copper wires with aluminium for certain electrical goods).
  4. Alternative Energy: Shift our dependence from exhaustible fossil fuels (coal, petroleum) to renewable and clean energy sources like solar power, wind energy, and hydroelectric power.
Q12. Discuss Coal and Natural Gas under the given headings: Formation, Uses, and Producers.
[5 Marks]
(a) Formation: Both Coal and Natural Gas are fossil fuels formed from the organic remains of dead plants and animals that were buried deep within the Earth's surface under intense heat and pressure over millions of years. (1 Mark)
(b) Coal (Uses & Producers):
  • Uses: Used as a fuel in thermal power plants to generate electricity, for domestic heating, and in iron and steel manufacturing.
  • Producers: China, USA, India (Jharkhand - Jharia, Bokaro). (2 Marks)
(c) Natural Gas (Uses):
  • It is a cleaner, non-polluting fuel used for domestic heating and cooking.
  • It is used as CNG (Compressed Natural Gas) as fuel for vehicles. (2 Marks)
Q13. (a) Define the term 'Mining'. (b) Explain 'Shaft Mining' and 'Drilling' in detail. Mention the conditions under which each method is applied.
[5 Marks]
(a) Mining: The process of extracting useful minerals out of the Earth's surface is called mining. (1 Mark)
(b) Extraction Methods: (4 Marks)
  • Shaft Mining: This method is applied when minerals are found deep underground. To reach these deep mineral deposits, deep bores known as 'shafts' are made into the earth. Miners and machinery travel down these shafts to extract the minerals.
  • Drilling: This method is specifically applied to extract liquid or gaseous minerals like petroleum and natural gas that lie far below the Earth's surface. Deep wells are bored (drilled) into the earth to pump out these resources.
