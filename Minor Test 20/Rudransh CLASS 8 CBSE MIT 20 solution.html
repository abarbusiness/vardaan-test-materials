Q6. Three Beneficial Uses of Microorganisms in Daily Life.
[3 Marks]
Key Uses of Microorganisms (Any 3 points)
  • 1. Food Production: Lactobacillus bacteria convert milk into curd, and Yeast causes fermentation in bread dough making it fluffy. [1 Mark]
  • 2. Commercial & Medicinal Uses: Microorganisms are used in manufacturing alcohol, wines, vaccines, and antibiotics (e.g., Penicillin). [1 Mark]
  • 3. Agriculture & Environment: Nitrogen-fixing bacteria (like Rhizobium) enrich soil fertility, while decomposer microbes clean the environment by breaking down dead organic waste into simple nutrients. [1 Mark]

Q8. Antibiotics and Vaccines Explanation.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Antibiotics: Medicines produced by microorganisms (fungi/bacteria) that kill or stop the growth of disease-causing microorganisms. [1 Mark]
    • First Discovered Antibiotic: Penicillin (discovered by Alexander Fleming). [0.5 Mark]
  • (b) Vaccine: A biological preparation containing dead or weakened microbes introduced into the body to stimulate antibody production. [1 Mark]
    • Protection Mechanism: The antibodies created remain in the body to protect against future infection by the same pathogen (immunity). [0.5 Mark]
Q9. Functions of Cell Membrane, Cell Wall, and Vacuole.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Cell Membrane (Plasma Membrane): It bounds the cell contents, gives shape to the cell, and regulates the entry and exit of selected substances in and out of the cell. [1 Mark]
  • (b) Cell Wall: Provides rigidity, mechanical strength, structural support, and protection against high temperature, wind, and moisture to plant cells. [1 Mark]
  • (c) Large Central Vacuole: Stores cell sap, water, waste products, and nutrients, maintaining turgidity and shape of the plant cell. [1 Mark]
Q10. Food Spoilage and Preservation Techniques.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Cause of Food Spoilage: Microorganisms (bacteria and fungi) grow rapidly on moist food at room temperature, releasing toxic substances that spoil taste, smell, and color. [1 Mark]
  • (b) Preservation Methods:
    i) Preservation by Salt/Sugar: Removes moisture from food cells through osmosis, preventing microbial growth in pickles, jams, and jellies. [1 Mark]
    ii) Pasteurisation: Heating milk to about \(70^\circ\text{C}\) for 15 to 30 seconds and then suddenly chilling it to prevent microbial growth. Discovered by Louis Pasteur. [1 Mark]
SECTION C: LONG ANSWER SOLUTIONS (15 MARKS)
Q11. Pathogens, Communicable Diseases, and Housefly Transmission.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Pathogens: Disease-causing microorganisms present in air, water, food, or vectors are called pathogens. [1 Mark]
  • (b) Disease Examples: [2 Marks]
    • Bacterial Diseases: Tuberculosis (TB), Cholera / Typhoid. [1 Mark]
    • Viral Diseases: Polio, Chickenpox / Common Cold. [1 Mark]
  • (c) Role of Houseflies as Disease Carriers: [2 Marks]
    • Houseflies sit on garbage and animal excreta, where disease-causing pathogens stick to their legs and bodies.
    • When these flies land on uncovered food, they transfer pathogens to the food. Consuming this contaminated food leads to diseases like cholera and dysentery.
    • Prevention Measure: Keep food covered at all times and keep surroundings clean to avoid fly breeding.
Q12. Structural Components of Cell and Nucleus.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Three Basic Components of a Cell: [1.5 Marks]
    1. Cell Membrane (Plasma Membrane) [0.5 Mark]
    2. Cytoplasm [0.5 Mark]
    3. Nucleus [0.5 Mark]
  • (b) Structure and Components of Nucleus: [3.5 Marks]
    • Nuclear Membrane: Double-layered porous membrane that separates the nucleus from cytoplasm and allows selective substance exchange. [1 Mark]
    • Nucleolus: Small, dense spherical body located inside the nucleus involved in protein synthesis. [0.5 Mark]
    • Chromosomes & Genes: Thread-like structures present inside nucleoplasm carrying Genes. [1 Mark]
    • Function of Genes: Gene is the basic unit of inheritance in living organisms; it controls the transfer of hereditary characters from parents to offspring. [1 Mark]
Q13. Scientific Discoveries & Nerve Cell Structure/Function.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Discovery of Cell: [2 Marks]
    • Discovered by Robert Hooke in the year 1665. [1 Mark]
    • He examined thin slices of cork (bark of a tree) under his simple magnifying device and observed honeycomb-like compartmental boxes. [1 Mark]
  • (b) Discovery of Fermentation: [1 Mark]
    • Discovered by French scientist Louis Pasteur in 1857. [1 Mark]
  • (c) Nerve Cell (Neuron) - Shape and Function: [2 Marks]
    • Shape: Long and branched structure with thin extensions. [1 Mark]
    • Function: Receives, conducts, and transfers messages/nerve impulses between organs and the brain to control and coordinate body actions. [1 Mark]
