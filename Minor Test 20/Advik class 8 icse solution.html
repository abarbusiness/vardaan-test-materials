Q1. Define Valency. State the variable valency exhibited by Iron in (a) $\text{FeCl}_2$ and (b) $\text{FeCl}_3$.
[2 Marks]
✔ Correct Answer: Valency = Combining capacity of an atom. Valency of Fe in $\text{FeCl}_2$ = 2 (Ferrous); in $\text{FeCl}_3$ = 3 (Ferric).

1. Definition of Valency: Valency is defined as the combining capacity of an element with other atoms to form a chemical bond. It corresponds to the number of electrons lost, gained, or shared by an atom to achieve a stable octet or duplet configuration. (1 Mark)

2. Variable Valency of Iron ($\text{Fe}$):

  • In $\text{FeCl}_2$ (Iron(II) chloride / Ferrous chloride), Iron exhibits a valency of 2 ($\text{Fe}^{2+}$). (0.5 Mark)
  • In $\text{FeCl}_3$ (Iron(III) chloride / Ferric chloride), Iron exhibits a valency of 3 ($\text{Fe}^{3+}$). (0.5 Mark)
Q2. Aluminium atom ($Z = 13, A = 27$). Find protons, electrons, neutrons and give electronic configuration.
[2 Marks]
✔ Correct Answer: Protons = 13, Electrons = 13, Neutrons = 14. Electronic Configuration = 2, 8, 3.

Step-by-step Calculation:

Given: Atomic Number ($Z$) = 13, Mass Number ($A$) = 27.

  • Number of Protons ($p$) = Atomic Number ($Z$) = 13 (0.5 Mark)
  • Number of Electrons ($e$) = Number of Protons (for a neutral atom) = 13 (0.5 Mark)
  • Number of Neutrons ($n$) = Mass Number ($A$) - Atomic Number ($Z$) = $27 - 13 = \mathbf{14}$ (0.5 Mark)

Electronic Configuration: With 13 electrons distributed in shells $K, L, M$:

$$\text{Configuration} = K: 2,\; L: 8,\; M: 3 \quad \text{or simply } (2, 8, 3)$$
(0.5 Mark)

Q7. State three postulates of Dalton's Atomic Theory and one modern modification.
[3 Marks]
✔ Key Points: Matter made of tiny indivisible atoms; Atoms of same element identical; Chemical combination in whole numbers.

Three Main Postulates of Dalton's Atomic Theory:

  1. Matter is made up of extremely small, indivisible particles called atoms.
  2. Atoms of a given element are identical in mass, size, and chemical properties.
  3. Atoms combine in simple whole-number ratios to form compounds. (2 Marks, 0.67 marks each)

One Modern Modification:

Dalton stated that atoms are indivisible. Modern atomic theory modified this: Atoms are divisible into smaller subatomic particles—protons, neutrons, and electrons. (Alternatively: Atoms of the same element can have different atomic masses, known as isotopes). (1 Mark)

Q12. Law of Conservation of Mass definition, numerical calculation, and importance of balancing equations.
[5 Marks]
✔ Mass of $\text{CO}_2 = 10.0\,\text{g} - 5.6\,\text{g} = 4.4\,\text{g}$. Mass of Reactants = Mass of Products.

(a) Law of Conservation of Mass:

The Law of Conservation of Mass states that matter can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction. Consequently, the total mass of reactants before a reaction is always equal to the total mass of products formed after the reaction. (1 Mark)

(b) Numerical Verification:

Reaction Equation: $\text{CaCO}_3\,(s) \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{CaO}\,(s) + \text{CO}_2\,(g)$

  • Mass of Reactant ($\text{CaCO}_3$) = $10.0\,\text{g}$
  • Mass of Solid Product ($\text{CaO}$) = $5.6\,\text{g}$
  • Let mass of $\text{CO}_2$ produced = $x\,\text{g}$

According to Law of Conservation of Mass:

$$\text{Total Mass of Reactants} = \text{Total Mass of Products}$$ $$10.0\,\text{g} = 5.6\,\text{g} + x$$ $$x = 10.0\,\text{g} - 5.6\,\text{g} = \mathbf{4.4\,\text{g}}$$

Conclusion: The mass of $\text{CO}_2$ gas evolved is $4.4\,\text{g}$. Since total initial mass ($10\,\text{g}$) equals final total mass ($5.6\,\text{g} + 4.4\,\text{g} = 10\,\text{g}$), the experiment verifies the Law of Conservation of Mass. (2 Marks)

(c) Why Equations Must Be Balanced:

In a chemical reaction, atoms are neither created nor destroyed; they are simply rearranged. Therefore, the total number of atoms of each element on the reactant side (left) must equal the total number of atoms of that element on the product side (right). Balancing ensures compliance with the Law of Conservation of Mass. (2 Marks)
