SECTION B: SHORT ANSWER SOLUTIONS (15 MARKS)
Q6. Four Whorls of a Flower & Classification.
[3 Marks]
  • Four Whorls (Outside to Inside): [1.5 Marks - 0.5 Mark per pair]
    1. Calyx (made of Sepals)
    2. Corolla (made of Petals)
    3. Androecium (made of Stamens)
    4. Gynoecium / Pistil (made of Carpels)
  • Classification: [1.5 Marks]
    • Non-essential Whorls: Calyx and Corolla (They protect reproductive organs and attract insects).
    • Essential Whorls: Androecium and Gynoecium (They produce male and female cells for reproduction).
Q7. Breathing vs Respiration Comparison.
[3 Marks]
Criteria Breathing Respiration
Nature A physical/mechanical process of taking in oxygen and giving out \(\text{CO}_2\). [1 Mark] A biochemical process where glucose is broken down to release energy. [1 Mark]
Location & Energy Occurs outside cells (in respiratory organs); No energy is released. [0.5 Mark] Occurs inside living cells; Energy is released in the form of ATP. [0.5 Mark]
Q8. Unisexual Flowers vs Bisexual Flowers.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Difference: [2 Marks]
    • Unisexual Flower: Contains either male parts (stamens) or female parts (pistil) only.
    • Bisexual Flower: Contains both male (stamens) and female (pistil) parts in the same flower.
  • (b) Examples: [1 Mark - 0.5 Mark each]
    • Unisexual: Papaya / Cucumber / Corn.
    • Bisexual: Mustard / Rose / Hibiscus.

Q10. Inhalation and Exhalation Movements.
[3 Marks]
  • (i) During Inhalation (Taking air in): [1.5 Marks]
    • Ribcage moves upwards and outwards.
    • Diaphragm moves downwards (flattens) to enlarge chest cavity.
  • (ii) During Exhalation (Giving air out): [1.5 Marks]
    • Ribcage moves downwards and inwards.
    • Diaphragm moves upwards back to its dome-shaped resting position.
SECTION C: LONG ANSWER SOLUTIONS (15 MARKS)
Q11. Stamen, Pistil Structure & Pollination.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Parts of Stamen and Pistil: [2.5 Marks]
    • Stamen: Made of two parts — Anther (contains pollen grains) and Filament (thin stalk). [1 Mark]
    • Pistil: Made of three parts — Stigma (sticky top), Style (middle tube), and Ovary (swollen base with ovules). [1.5 Marks]
  • (b) Pollination & Types: [2.5 Marks]
    • Pollination: Transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma of a flower. [0.5 Mark]
    • Self-pollination: Pollen lands on the stigma of the same flower or another flower on the same plant. [1 Mark]
    • Cross-pollination: Pollen lands on the stigma of a flower on a different plant of the same kind. [1 Mark]
Q12. Human Respiratory Air Pathway & Nasal Hair/Mucus.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Pathway of Air in Humans: [3 Marks]
    Nostrils \(\rightarrow\) Nasal Cavity \(\rightarrow\) Pharynx (Throat) \(\rightarrow\) Windpipe (Trachea) \(\rightarrow\) Bronchi \(\rightarrow\) Lungs \(\rightarrow\) Alveoli.
  • (b) Role of Fine Hair and Mucus in Nasal Cavity: [2 Marks]
    • They trap dust particles, dirt, and microscopic germs present in inhaled air.
    • Mucus also moistens and warms the air entering the respiratory tract, protecting lungs from harm.
Q13. Fertilisation & Respiration in Plant Organs.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Fertilisation & Post-fertilisation: [2.5 Marks]
    • Fertilisation: The process of fusion of male gamete with female gamete inside the ovule to form a zygote. [0.5 Mark]
    • Ovary develops into the Fruit. [1 Mark]
    • Ovules develop into Seeds. [1 Mark]
  • (b) Structures for Plant Respiration: [2.5 Marks]
    • Plants take in oxygen and give out carbon dioxide during respiration. [0.5 Mark]
    • Leaves: Gas exchange occurs through microscopic pores called Stomata. [1 Mark]
    • Woody Stems: Gas exchange occurs through tiny openings called Lenticels on the bark. [1 Mark]
