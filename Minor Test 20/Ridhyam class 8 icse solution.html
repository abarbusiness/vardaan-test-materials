SECTION A: OBJECTIVE & CONCEPT RECALL (10 MARKS)
Q1. Name the following terms: (a) Chemical messengers secreted by ductless glands (b) Gland controlling other glands.
[2 Marks]
Expected Terminology
  • (a) Hormones (Chemical regulatory substances released directly into blood). [1 Mark]
  • (b) Pituitary Gland (Located at base of brain). [1 Mark]
Q2. Differentiate between Exocrine and Endocrine glands.
[2 Marks]
Criteria Exocrine Gland Endocrine Gland
(a) Presence of Ducts Glands with ducts (Duct glands). [0.5 Mark] Ductless glands. [0.5 Mark]
(b) Transport Medium Secretions carried through ducts to site of action. [0.5 Mark] Secretions poured directly into bloodstream. [0.5 Mark]
Q3. Location and main hormone of Thyroid Gland.
[2 Marks]
  • Location: Situated in the neck region, attached to the windpipe (trachea) just below the larynx. [1 Mark]
  • Hormone Secreted: Thyroxine. [1 Mark]
Q4. Two key characteristics of hormones.
[2 Marks]
  • 1. Secreted in extremely small quantities and act on specific target organs/tissues. [1 Mark]
  • 2. They are non-protein or protein compounds carried by blood, and are neither stored (except thyroxine) nor reused in the body. [1 Mark]
Q5. Technical biological terms for: (a) Deficiency of Insulin (b) Neck swelling due to Iodine lack.
[2 Marks]
  • (a) Diabetes Mellitus (characterized by high blood sugar). [1 Mark]
  • (b) Simple Goitre (enlargement of thyroid gland). [1 Mark]
SECTION B: SHORT ANSWER & REASONING (15 MARKS)
Q6. Give reasons: (a) Pituitary as 'Master Gland' (b) Adrenaline as 'Emergency Hormone'.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Master Gland Reason: Pituitary gland secretes tropic hormones (like TSH, ACTH) that control and regulate the secretions and functioning of all other major endocrine glands in the body. [1.5 Marks]
  • (b) Emergency Hormone Reason: Adrenaline prepares the body to face stressful, fear, or emergency situations ("fight or flight") by rapidly increasing heart rate, breathing, and blood sugar levels. [1.5 Marks]

Q8. Pancreas as Heterocrine Gland & Islets of Langerhans.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Heterocrine Gland: It possesses both exocrine parts (secretes pancreatic juice via duct into duodenum for digestion) and endocrine parts (secretes hormones directly into blood). [1.5 Marks]
  • (b) Cell Clusters & Hormones: Endocrine part consists of Islets of Langerhans, which secrete:
    1. Insulin (Lowers blood glucose).
    2. Glucagon (Raises blood glucose). [1.5 Marks]

SECTION C: LONG ANSWER & DETAILED EXPLANATIONS (15 MARKS)
Q11. Thyroid Gland: Essential element, Disorders (Goitre, Cretinism), and Hypersecretion.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Essential Element: Iodine. [1 Mark]
  • (b) Disorders Description: [2 Marks]
    • (i) Simple Goitre: Caused due to deficiency of iodine in food/water. Leads to enlargement of the thyroid gland, causing a swollen neck. [1 Mark]
    • (ii) Cretinism: Hyposecretion of thyroxine in infants/young children. Leads to dwarfism, mental retardation, and defective physical development. [1 Mark]
  • (c) Hypersecretion Symptoms (Exophthalmic Goitre): [2 Marks]
    Excess secretion causes hyperthyroidism.
    • Symptoms: Bulging eyes (exophthalmos), increased rate of heartbeat, high metabolism, and weight loss.
Q12. Pituitary Growth Hormone: Dwarfism, Gigantism, and Acromegaly.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Growth Hormone: Somatotropin / Growth Hormone (GH). [1 Mark]
  • (b) Dwarfism vs Gigantism: [2 Marks]
    Feature Dwarfism Gigantism
    Secretion Level Hyposecretion (under-secretion) during childhood. Hypersecretion (over-secretion) during childhood.
    Physical Feature Stunted physical growth; small height with normal proportion. Abnormal increase in bone length; extra tall height.
  • (c) Acromegaly: [2 Marks]
    • Condition caused by hypersecretion of Growth Hormone in adults after skeletal growth is complete.
    • Symptoms: Abnormal enlargement of facial bones, jaw, hands, and feet (gorilla-like appearance).
Q13. Adrenal Glands: Location, Structure, and Physiological Actions of Adrenaline.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Location: Situated like a cap on top of each of the two kidneys (suprarenal glands). [1 Mark]
  • (b) Structure Parts: [1 Mark]
    • Outer part: Adrenal Cortex.
    • Inner part: Adrenal Medulla.
  • (c) Physiological Actions of Adrenaline: [3 Marks - 1 Mark each point]
    1. Increased Heartbeat & BP: Pumps more blood to muscles to supply oxygen and glucose.
    2. Increased Breathing Rate: Dilates bronchioles to absorb maximum oxygen.
    3. Elevated Blood Sugar: Stimulates liver to break down glycogen into glucose for quick energy.
