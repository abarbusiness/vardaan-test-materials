SECTION A (16 MARKS) — COMPULSORY SHORT ANSWER QUESTIONS
Question 1 [8 Marks]
Answer the following short concept-based questions brief and precisely:
(i) Define 1 calorie of heat energy and write its exact quantitative relationship with Joule ($\text{J}$).
[1 Mark]
(ii) State the S.I. unit of temperature and write its symbol.
[1 Mark]
(iii) Write the mathematical relationship between coefficient of linear expansion ($\alpha$) and coefficient of cubical expansion ($\gamma$) for a solid.
[1 Mark]
(iv) At what temperature does pure water exhibit its maximum density?
[1 Mark]
(v) Name the laboratory apparatus designed to demonstrate the anomalous expansion of water.
[1 Mark]
(vi) Why are small gaps left between consecutive steel rails on a railway track?
[1 Mark]
(vii) Convert normal human body temperature ($37^\circ\text{C}$) into the Fahrenheit scale.
[1 Mark]
(viii) What happens to the volume of a given mass of water when it is cooled from $4^\circ\text{C}$ down to $0^\circ\text{C}$?
[1 Mark]
Question 2 [8 Marks]
Answer the following analytical and conceptual reasoning questions:
(a) Differentiate between Heat and Temperature based on (1) Definition, and (2) S.I. Unit.
[2 Marks]
(b) Explain why a bimetallic strip made of brass and iron bends on heating. Mention one common practical application of a bimetallic strip.
[2 Marks]
(c) Explain how aquatic plants and animals survive at the bottom of deep ponds and lakes during freezing winter conditions.
[2 Marks]
(d) A brass rod of initial length $100\text{ cm}$ is heated from $20^\circ\text{C}$ to $120^\circ\text{C}$. Calculate the increase in length of the rod if coefficient of linear expansion for brass $\alpha = 1.9 \times 10^{-5}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$.
[2 Marks]
SECTION B (24 MARKS) — STRUCTURED LONG ANSWER QUESTIONS
Question 3 [8 Marks]
(a) Derive the general conversion formula between Celsius ($C$) and Fahrenheit ($F$) temperature scales using their lower fixed points (LFP) and upper fixed points (UFP).
[3 Marks]
(b) At what numerical temperature do both the Celsius and Fahrenheit thermometers read the exact same value? Show complete algebraic calculation.
[3 Marks]
(c) Convert $-40^\circ\text{C}$ into the Kelvin scale ($K$).
[2 Marks]
Question 4 [8 Marks]
(a) What is meant by the Anomalous Expansion of Water? Sketch a neat line graph showing the variation of volume of $1\text{ g}$ of water as its temperature increases from $0^\circ\text{C}$ to $10^\circ\text{C}$.
[3 Marks]
(b) Describe Hope's Apparatus experiment. What are the steady temperature readings recorded by the upper and lower thermometers when ice-salt freezing mixture is added to the central trough?
[3 Marks]
(c) Give a scientific reason why glass water bottles filled completely with water often burst when kept in the freezer section of a refrigerator.
[2 Marks]
Question 5 [8 Marks]
(a) Define the three coefficients of thermal expansion for solids:
1. Linear Expansion ($\alpha$) • 2. Superficial Expansion ($\beta$) • 3. Cubical Expansion ($\gamma$).
Write the simple ratio connecting $\alpha$, $\beta$, and $\gamma$.
[3 Marks]
(b) Give sound physical reasons for the following daily life observations:
1. Telephone and electric transmission wires are left loose (sagging) during summer installation.
2. Thick glass tumblers often crack when boiling water is suddenly poured into them.
[3 Marks]
(c) A cubical metal block of volume $500\text{ cm}^3$ at $10^\circ\text{C}$ is heated to $110^\circ\text{C}$. If coefficient of cubical expansion $\gamma = 3.6 \times 10^{-5}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$, calculate the increase in volume of the block.
[2 Marks]
