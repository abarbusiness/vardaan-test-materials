Expected Answers

Q2. True/False: (a) Plant cells lack cell wall (b) Yeast dough rising due to CO₂.
[2 Marks]
  • (a) False. Correction: Plant cells have a rigid cell wall outside the plasma membrane, whereas animal cells lack a cell wall. [1 Mark]
  • (b) True. (Yeast produces \(\text{CO}_2\) gas during respiration which fills the dough and makes it rise). [1 Mark]
Q3. Define: (a) Pseudopodia (b) Fermentation.
[2 Marks]
  • (a) Pseudopodia: Temporary finger-like projections or "false feet" extended by unicellular organisms like Amoeba for movement and capturing food. [1 Mark]
  • (b) Fermentation: The biological process of conversion of sugars into alcohol and carbon dioxide by microorganisms like yeast in anaerobic conditions. [1 Mark]
Q4. Microorganisms for: (a) Malaria (b) Citrus Canker.
[2 Marks]
  • (a) Plasmodium (Protozoan carried by female Anopheles mosquito). [1 Mark]
  • (b) Xanthomonas citri / Bacteria. [1 Mark]
Q5. Technical terms for: (a) Control centre of cell (b) Green plastids.
[2 Marks]
  • (a) Nucleus. [1 Mark]
  • (b) Chloroplasts. [1 Mark]

Q7. Antibiotics: Definition, example, and two precautions.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Definition & Example: Medicines that kill or stop the growth of disease-causing bacteria. Example: Penicillin / Streptomycin / Amoxicillin. [1 Mark]
  • (b) Precautions: [2 Marks - 1 Mark each]
    1. Antibiotics should be taken only on the advice of a qualified doctor.
    2. Complete the full course prescribed by the doctor, even if symptoms disappear earlier.
Q8. Unicellular vs Multicellular & nerve cell shape.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Unicellular vs Multicellular: [1.5 Marks]
    • Unicellular: Organisms composed of a single cell (e.g., Amoeba, Paramecium).
    • Multicellular: Organisms composed of many cells working together (e.g., Humans, Rose plant).
  • (b) Nerve Cell Function & Structure: [1.5 Marks]
    Nerve cells are long and branched so they can receive and transfer control signals (impulses) efficiently across long distances in the body, helping coordinate brain activities.
Q9. Pasteurisation & action of salt/sugar in preservation.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Pasteurisation: Milk is heated to about \(70^\circ\text{C}\) for 15 to 30 seconds and then suddenly chilled and stored. Discovered by Louis Pasteur. [1.5 Marks]
  • (b) Preservation Mechanism: Salt and sugar draw moisture out of microbial cells by osmosis, creating a dry environment that inhibits the growth of bacteria and fungi. [1.5 Marks]
Q10. Nucleus function, Chromosomes, and Genes.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Primary Function: Controls all metabolic activities of the cell and regulates cell division. [1 Mark]
  • (b) Chromosomes & Genes: Thread-like structures present inside the nucleus that are visible during cell division. They carry Genes, which are functional units of inheritance that transfer genetic traits from parents to offspring. [2 Marks]
SECTION C: LONG ANSWER & DETAILED EXPLANATIONS (15 MARKS)
Q11. Detailed Cell Structure: Plasma Membrane, Cytoplasm, and Cell Wall.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Cell Membrane: [2 Marks]
    • Outer delicate boundary of animal cells. It encloses the cytoplasm and nucleus.
    • Selectively Permeable: It allows only specific substance molecules to pass in and out while blocking unwanted substances.
  • (b) Cytoplasm & Organelles: [1.5 Marks]
    • Jelly-like fluid matrix between the plasma membrane and nucleus.
    • Organelles present: Mitochondria, Endoplasmic Reticulum, Golgi Apparatus, Vacuoles.
  • (c) Need for Cell Wall in Plants: [1.5 Marks]
    Plants cannot move to shelter themselves from weather extremes. The rigid cellulose cell wall protects plant cells against variations in temperature, high wind speed, atmospheric moisture, and mechanical pressure.
Q12. Microorganisms: Nitrogen Fixation, Decomposers, and Vaccines.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Nitrogen Fixation by Rhizobium: [2 Marks]
    Rhizobium bacteria live in the root nodules of leguminous plants. They convert free atmospheric nitrogen gas (\(\text{N}_2\)) into usable nitrogenous compounds (nitrates) in the soil, which plants absorb to make proteins, thereby increasing soil fertility naturally.
  • (b) Role of Decomposers: [1.5 Marks]
    Decomposers break down dead plant materials, dead animals, and organic wastes into simple inorganic substances that mix with the soil and recycle nutrients back into the ecosystem, keeping the environment clean.
  • (c) Vaccines & Immunity: [1.5 Marks]
    A vaccine contains dead or weakened microbes introduced into the body. The body produces protective proteins called antibodies to fight them. These antibodies remain stored in the body, providing immunity against future infections by the same pathogen.
Q13. Pathogens, Disease Transmission Table, and Mosquito Control.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Pathogens & Communicable Diseases: [2 Marks]
    • Pathogens: Disease-causing microorganisms.
    • Microbes spread through air (coughing/sneezing droplets), water, food, or direct contact from an infected person to a healthy person.
  • (b) Completed Table: [2 Marks - 0.5 Mark each]
    Human Disease Causative Microorganism Mode of Transmission
    Tuberculosis (i) Bacteria Air
    Chicken Pox Virus (ii) Air / Contact
    Cholera (iii) Bacteria Water / Food
    Malaria Protozoa (iv) Female Anopheles Mosquito
  • (c) Preventive Measure against Mosquitoes: Do not allow water to collect in coolers, tires, or flower pots. Keeping surroundings dry prevents mosquitoes from laying eggs. [1 Mark]
