SECTION A (10 Marks)
Q1. Define 'Force'. Give one example of a push and one example of a pull from your daily life.
[2 Marks]
Force: A push or a pull acting on an object is called force. It tends to change the object's state of rest or uniform motion. (1 Mark)
Example of Push: Pushing a door to close it. (0.5 Mark)
Example of Pull: Pulling a drawer to open it. (0.5 Mark)
(Accept any other valid daily life examples)
Q2. What do you understand by a non-contact force? Give two examples of non-contact forces.
[2 Marks]
Non-contact Force: A force that can act on an object from a distance without coming into physical contact with it is called a non-contact force. (1 Mark)
Examples: (1) Gravitational force, (2) Magnetic force, (3) Electrostatic force. (Any two for 1 Mark)
Q3. A rubber band stretches when it is pulled from both ends. Which effect of force is demonstrated here? State one other effect a force can have on an object.
[2 Marks]
Effect demonstrated: Force can change the shape and size of an object. (1 Mark)
Other effect: Force can change the speed or direction of a moving object (or it can start/stop motion). (1 Mark)
Q4. Define 'Frictional force'. Does this force oppose or support the motion of a moving object?
[2 Marks]
Frictional force: The force that acts between two surfaces in contact and opposes the relative motion of the objects is called frictional force (or friction). (1 Mark)
It always opposes the motion of a moving object. (1 Mark)
Q5. What is electrostatic force? Give a common everyday activity that demonstrates this force.
[2 Marks]
Electrostatic force: The force exerted by a charged body on another charged or uncharged body is called electrostatic force. (1 Mark)
Example: Rubbing a plastic scale on dry hair makes the scale electrically charged, which then attracts tiny pieces of paper. (1 Mark)

Q8. Why do we tend to slip easily when we walk on a smooth, wet floor? Explain this phenomenon in terms of force.
[3 Marks]
We are able to walk on the ground safely due to the frictional force between our shoes and the ground, which gives us a proper grip. (1 Mark)
When a floor is smooth and wet, the layer of water acts as a lubricant and severely reduces the friction between our feet and the floor. (1 Mark)
Due to this lack of sufficient opposing frictional force, our feet fail to grip the floor, causing us to slip. (1 Mark)

[3 Marks]
1. Force can make a stationary object move: Kicking a resting football makes it move. (1 Mark)
2. Force can change the direction of a moving object: A batsman hitting a cricket ball changes its direction of motion. (1 Mark)
3. Force can change the shape and size of an object: Squeezing a toothpaste tube or stretching a rubber band. (1 Mark)

[3 Marks]

SECTION C (15 Marks)
Q11. (a) What is meant by 'Muscular Force'? Provide two instances. (b) Explain why a football rolling gradually slows down and halts. Name the force and state its type.
[5 Marks]
(a) Muscular Force: The force resulting from the action of muscles in humans or animals is called muscular force. (1 Mark)
Instances: Lifting a school bag, a bullock pulling a cart. (1 Mark)
(b) Slowing of football: When a football rolls on the ground, the surface of the ground rubs against the surface of the ball. This rubbing creates a force that acts in the direction opposite to the motion of the ball. (1.5 Marks)
This opposing force is called Frictional Force. (1 Mark)
Since friction occurs only when the ball is touching the ground, it is a Contact Force. (0.5 Mark)
Q12. Explain the concept of gravitational force in detail. How is gravitational force different from magnetic force? Provide examples.
[5 Marks]
Gravitational Force: Every object in the universe pulls every other object towards itself. This force of attraction is called gravitational force. In our daily lives, the Earth pulls all objects towards its center with a massive gravitational force, called gravity. (2 Marks)
Differences:
1. Attraction/Repulsion: Gravitational force is always an attractive force. Magnetic force can be both attractive (unlike poles) and repulsive (like poles). (1.5 Marks)
2. Material Dependency: Gravitational force acts between all objects having mass. Magnetic force acts only between magnets or specific magnetic materials (like iron, nickel, cobalt). (1.5 Marks)
Examples: A leaf falling from a tree (Gravity). A magnet picking up iron pins (Magnetic).
Q13. (a) "Force can change the state of motion and shape." Justify with examples. (b) Classify forces as Contact/Non-Contact: (i) Kicking a ball (ii) Leaves falling (iii) Magnets repelling.
[5 Marks]
(a) Changing state of motion: A goalkeeper catching a fast-moving football applies force to bring it to a state of rest. (1 Mark)
Changing shape: A potter applies force with his hands to change a lump of wet clay into a beautifully shaped pot. (1 Mark)
(b) Classification:
(i) Kicking a football — Contact Force (muscular interaction). (1 Mark)
(ii) Leaves falling from a branch — Non-Contact Force (gravity pulls from a distance). (1 Mark)
(iii) Two magnets repelling each other — Non-Contact Force (magnetic force acts across space). (1 Mark)
