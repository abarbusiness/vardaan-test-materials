Q7. Definitions of Breathing and Respiration + Major Difference.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Breathing: It is a physical process of taking in air rich in oxygen and giving out air rich in carbon dioxide. [1 Mark]
  • (b) Respiration: It is a chemical process taking place in cells where glucose is broken down in the presence of oxygen to release energy. [1 Mark]
  • (c) Key Difference: Breathing is a physical process occurring outside the cells (extracellular), whereas respiration is a biochemical process occurring inside the cells (intracellular). [1 Mark]
Q8. Functions of Cell Membrane, Cytoplasm, and Nucleus.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Cell Membrane: Protects the cell and selectively regulates the entry and exit of substances into and out of the cell. [1 Mark]
  • (b) Cytoplasm: A jelly-like liquid where most cellular chemical reactions and metabolic processes occur. [1 Mark]
  • (c) Nucleus: Controls all vital functions and activities of the cell and contains genetic material (chromosomes). [1 Mark]

Q10. Respiration in Insects and Earthworms.
[3 Marks]
  • (a) Insect Openings: Tiny air holes present on the sides of a cockroach or insect's body are called Spiracles. Air enters through spiracles into air tubes called tracheae. [1.5 Marks]
  • (b) Earthworm Respiration: Earthworms absorb oxygen directly through their moist, thin, and slimy skin, which has a rich supply of blood capillaries. [1.5 Marks]
SECTION C: LONG ANSWER SOLUTIONS (15 MARKS)
Q11. Discovery of Cell & Types of Organisms.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Discovery of Cell: [2 Marks]
    • The cell was discovered by Robert Hooke in 1665. [1 Mark]
    • He examined thin slices of cork (bark of an oak tree) under his primitive microscope and observed honeycomb-like compartments. [1 Mark]
  • (b) Types of Organisms: [3 Marks]
    • Unicellular Organisms: Organisms made up of a single cell that performs all life functions. Example: Amoeba or Paramecium. [1.5 Marks]
    • Multicellular Organisms: Organisms made up of many cells working together. Example: Humans, plants, or dogs. [1.5 Marks]
Q12. Mechanism of Inhalation/Exhalation & Nasal Hair Role.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Movements during Breathing: [3 Marks]
    i) Inhalation (Breathing In): Ribcage moves upward and outward. Diaphragm contracts and moves downward (flattens), expanding the chest cavity. [1.5 Marks]
    ii) Exhalation (Breathing Out): Ribcage moves downward and inward. Diaphragm relaxes and moves upward (dome-shaped), reducing chest cavity size. [1.5 Marks]
  • (b) Role of Fine Hairs and Mucus: [2 Marks]
    • Fine hairs and sticky mucus inside the nasal cavity trap dust, dirt, germs, and foreign particles present in the air.
    • They filter and warm the inhaled air so that clean, humidified air enters the delicate lungs.
Q13. Stomata in Plants & Aerobic Respiration Equation.
[5 Marks]
  • (a) Stomata: [2.5 Marks]
    • Stomata are microscopic, minute pores surrounded by guard cells, located mostly on the underside of leaves. [1 Mark]
    • Role: They facilitate the exchange of gases (\(\text{O}_2\) and \(\text{CO}_2\)) during respiration/photosynthesis and allow water vapor to escape during transpiration. [1.5 Marks]
  • (b) Respiration Equation & Energy: [2.5 Marks]
    • Word Equation: [1.5 Marks]
    \[\text{Glucose} + \text{Oxygen} \rightarrow \text{Carbon Dioxide} + \text{Water} + \text{Energy (ATP)}\]

    • Energy Currency: The main energy released during respiration is stored in the form of ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate) molecules, which power cellular functions. [1 Mark]
