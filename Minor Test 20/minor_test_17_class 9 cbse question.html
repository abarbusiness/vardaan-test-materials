MINOR TEST 17 (CHAPTER 8: JOURNEY INSIDE THE ATOM)
Class: 9 CBSE Subject: Science (Chemistry) Time Allowed: 1 Hour Max. Marks: 40 Marks
General Instructions:
  • All questions are compulsory. Read the paper carefully before answering.
  • The question paper consists of 13 questions divided into three sections: A, B, and C.
  • Section A: Q1 to Q5 carry 2 marks each (Short Concept Questions).
  • Section B: Q6 to Q10 carry 3 marks each (Analytical & Numerical Questions).
  • Section C: Q11 to Q13 carry 5 marks each (Long Answer & Descriptive Questions).
  • Write clear, legible, and structurally organized responses. Use neat chemical notations where necessary.
SECTION A (10 Marks) (Concept Recall & Rapid Assessment — 2 Marks Each)
Q1.
[2 Marks] State any two key postulates proposed by Niels Bohr that successfully explained why electrons do not lose energy and fall into the nucleus of an atom.
Q2.
[2 Marks] Define Atomic Number ($Z$) and Mass Number ($A$). Write the standard chemical notation ($_{Z}^{A}\text{X}$) for a Carbon atom that contains $6$ protons and $6$ neutrons.
Q3.
[2 Marks] An atom of an element contains $12$ protons and $12$ neutrons in its nucleus. Calculate:
(a) Its atomic number and mass number.
(b) Its electronic configuration in K, L, and M shells.
Q4.
[2 Marks] Differentiate between Isotopes and Isobars on the basis of their atomic number and mass number. Give one example of an isobaric pair.
Q5.
[2 Marks] Write the official IUPAC chemical symbols and their Latin/German name origins for the following elements:
(a) Iron        (b) Sodium        (c) Potassium        (d) Gold
SECTION B (15 Marks) (Short Answer & Conceptual Reasoning — 3 Marks Each)
Q6.
[3 Marks] Briefly describe J.J. Thomson's 'Plum Pudding' or 'Watermelon' model of an atom. Explain why this model was eventually discarded after Geiger and Marsden conducted Rutherford's gold foil experiment.
Q7.
[3 Marks] State the rules proposed by Bohr and Bury for the distribution of electrons in different shells of an atom. Calculate the maximum electron holding capacity for the K-shell ($n=1$), L-shell ($n=2$), and M-shell ($n=3$).
Q8.
[3 Marks] Naturally occurring Chlorine exists in two isotopic forms: $^{35}_{17}\text{Cl}$ with relative abundance of $75\%$, and $^{37}_{17}\text{Cl}$ with relative abundance of $25\%$. Show step-by-step mathematical calculation to determine the weighted average atomic mass of Chlorine in unified atomic mass units ($\text{u}$).
Q9.
[3 Marks] Define the term Valency. Explain how valency is determined for:
(a) An element with valence electrons $\le 4$ (e.g., Sodium, $Z=11$).
(b) An element with valence electrons $> 4$ (e.g., Oxygen, $Z=8$).
Q10.
[3 Marks] An atom of element $\text{X}$ has a mass number of $35$ and contains $18$ neutrons in its nucleus.
(a) Determine its atomic number and total number of electrons.
(b) Write its electronic configuration and state its number of valence electrons.
(c) Identify element $\text{X}$ and write its valency.
SECTION C (15 Marks) (Long Answer & Analytical Derivations — 5 Marks Each)
Q11.
[5 Marks] Rutherford's $\alpha$-particle scattering (gold foil) experiment led to the discovery of the atomic nucleus.
(a) List the three main observations made during the gold foil experiment.
(b) Write the corresponding structural conclusion drawn from each observation.
(c) What major drawback/limitation of Rutherford's nuclear model prevented it from explaining atomic stability?
Q12.
[5 Marks] Answer the following questions:
(a) [2.5 Marks] Who discovered the neutron? State its relative charge, symbol, and location inside an atom. Why is Protium ($^{1}_{1}\text{H}$) unique among all neutral atoms regarding neutrons?
(b) [2.5 Marks] Define Isotopes. State three important applications of specific radioactive isotopes in the fields of medicine, energy, and archaeology.
Q13.
[5 Marks] Answer the following questions:
(a) [2.5 Marks] Draw structured schematic shell diagrams showing electron distribution for:
       (i) Nitrogen atom ($Z=7$)
       (ii) Magnesium atom ($Z=12$)
(b) [2.5 Marks] Explain why noble gases like Neon ($Z=10$) and Argon ($Z=18$) exhibit zero valency and do not react easily with other elements under normal conditions.
