SECTION A: SHORT CONCEPT SOLUTIONS (16 MARKS)
Q1. Short Concept Questions (8 Sub-parts)
[8 Marks]

(i) Definition of 1 calorie:
1 calorie is the amount of heat energy required to raise the temperature of $1\text{ g}$ of pure water through $1^\circ\text{C}$ (specifically from $14.5^\circ\text{C}$ to $15.5^\circ\text{C}$).
Relationship: $1\text{ cal} = 4.186\text{ J} \approx \mathbf{4.2\text{ J}}$. [1 Mark]

(ii) S.I. unit of temperature:
The S.I. unit of temperature is Kelvin (Symbol: $\mathbf{K}$). [1 Mark]

(iii) Relationship between $\alpha$ and $\gamma$:
$\gamma = 3\alpha$   or   $\alpha : \gamma = 1 : 3$. [1 Mark]

(iv) Temperature of maximum density of water:
At $\mathbf{4^\circ\text{C}}$ (or $277\text{ K}$), water has its maximum density ($1000\text{ kg/m}^3$ or $1\text{ g/cm}^3$). [1 Mark]

(v) Apparatus name:
Hope's Apparatus. [1 Mark]

(vi) Small gaps in railway tracks:
Gaps allow space for linear expansion of steel rails during hot summers without bending or buckling the tracks. [1 Mark]

(vii) Converting $37^\circ\text{C}$ to Fahrenheit:

$$\frac{C}{5} = \frac{F - 32}{9} \implies \frac{37}{5} = \frac{F - 32}{9} \implies 7.4 \times 9 = F - 32 \implies 66.6 + 32 = \mathbf{98.6^\circ\text{F}}$$

[1 Mark]

(viii) Volume change of water from $4^\circ\text{C}$ to $0^\circ\text{C}$:
When cooled from $4^\circ\text{C}$ to $0^\circ\text{C}$, the volume of water expands / increases (Anomalous behavior). [1 Mark]

Q2. Reasoning & Short Calculation Questions
[8 Marks]

(a) Difference between Heat and Temperature:

  • Heat: Form of internal energy transferred due to temperature difference. S.I. unit: Joule ($\text{J}$).
  • Temperature: Thermal condition/degree of hotness or coldness that determines direction of heat flow. S.I. unit: Kelvin ($\text{K}$).

[2 Marks: 1 mark per point]

(b) Bimetallic Strip Mechanism & Application:

Reason: Brass expands more than iron for the same rise in temperature because coefficient of linear expansion for brass is greater than iron ($\alpha_{\text{brass}} > \alpha_{\text{iron}}$). Thus, the strip bends with brass on the outer convex curve.

Application: Used in electric irons, thermostats, or fire alarms as an automatic switch. [2 Marks]

(c) Survival of Aquatic Life in Cold Winter:

As weather cools, surface water reaches $4^\circ\text{C}$, becomes densest, and sinks to the bottom. Further cooling below $4^\circ\text{C}$ makes top water lighter, freezing into ice at $0^\circ\text{C}$. Since ice is a poor conductor of heat, the bottom layer remains liquid at $4^\circ\text{C}$, allowing aquatic organisms to survive safely underneath. [2 Marks]

(d) Numerical Calculation (Linear Expansion):

Given: Initial length $L_0 = 100\text{ cm}$, Temperature change $\Delta T = 120^\circ\text{C} - 20^\circ\text{C} = 100^\circ\text{C}$, $\alpha = 1.9 \times 10^{-5}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$.

$$\text{Increase in length } (\Delta L) = L_0 \times \alpha \times \Delta T$$
$$\Delta L = 100 \times (1.9 \times 10^{-5}) \times 100 = 1.9 \times 10^{-1} = \mathbf{0.19\text{ cm}} \quad (\text{or } 1.9\text{ mm})$$

[2 Marks: 1 mark for formula substitution, 1 mark for final answer]

SECTION B: STRUCTURED LONG ANSWER SOLUTIONS (24 MARKS)
Q3. Temperature Scales & Numerical Conversions
[8 Marks]

(a) Derivation of Celsius to Fahrenheit Formula:

For any temperature scale: $\frac{\text{Reading} - \text{LFP}}{\text{UFP} - \text{LFP}} = \text{Constant}$.

For Celsius scale: LFP $= 0^\circ\text{C}$, UFP $= 100^\circ\text{C}$.

For Fahrenheit scale: LFP $= 32^\circ\text{F}$, UFP $= 212^\circ\text{F}$.

$$\frac{C - 0}{100 - 0} = \frac{F - 32}{212 - 32} \implies \frac{C}{100} = \frac{F - 32}{180} \implies \mathbf{\frac{C}{5} = \frac{F - 32}{9}}$$

[3 Marks: 1 mark for LFP/UFP identification, 2 marks for derivation]

(b) Equal Reading Temperature Calculation:

Let the temperature at which $C = F = x$.

$$\frac{x}{5} = \frac{x - 32}{9} \implies 9x = 5(x - 32) \implies 9x = 5x - 160$$
$$4x = -160 \implies x = \mathbf{-40}$$
Thus, at $-40^\circ\text{C}$ and $-40^\circ\text{F}$, both thermometers show identical numerical value.

[3 Marks]

(c) Converting $-40^\circ\text{C}$ to Kelvin:

$$T(K) = t(^\circ\text{C}) + 273.15 \approx -40 + 273 = \mathbf{233\text{ K}}$$

[2 Marks]

Q4. Anomalous Expansion of Water & Hope's Experiment
[8 Marks]

(a) Definition & Volume-Temperature Graph:

Anomalous Expansion: The unusual behavior of water by which it contracts on heating from $0^\circ\text{C}$ to $4^\circ\text{C}$ (volume decreases) and expands when heated above $4^\circ\text{C}$.

Graph: Volume of $1\text{ g}$ Water vs Temperature
$$\text{At } 0^\circ\text{C}: V \approx 1.00013\text{ cm}^3 \longrightarrow \text{At } 4^\circ\text{C}: V_{\text{min}} = 1.00000\text{ cm}^3 \longrightarrow \text{At } 10^\circ\text{C}: V \uparrow$$

(U-shaped curve reaching minimum volume strictly at $4^\circ\text{C}$)

[3 Marks: 1.5 marks definition, 1.5 marks graph]

(b) Hope's Apparatus Readings:

1. Initially, water is filled at room temperature ($\approx 12^\circ\text{C}$). Both thermometers read $12^\circ\text{C}$.

2. When freezing mixture (ice + salt) is added to central trough, water near middle cools, becomes denser, and sinks down. Thus, lower thermometer temperature drops rapidly to $4^\circ\text{C}$ and remains steady.

3. Water further cooling from $4^\circ\text{C}$ to $0^\circ\text{C}$ becomes lighter, moves upwards. Thus, upper thermometer temperature drops to $0^\circ\text{C}$ and ice begins to form at top.

[3 Marks: 1.5 marks upper thermometer $0^\circ\text{C}$, 1.5 marks lower $4^\circ\text{C}$]

(c) Bursting of Glass Water Bottles in Freezer:

When water freezes into ice at $0^\circ\text{C}$, its volume expands due to anomalous expansion. Since glass is rigid and cannot stretch, the enormous outward pressure exerted by expanding ice breaks the bottle. [2 Marks]

Q5. Coefficients of Expansion & Practical Applications
[8 Marks]

(a) Definitions & Ratio of Coefficients:

  1. Linear Expansion ($\alpha$): Increase in length per unit original length per degree rise in temperature ($\alpha = \frac{\Delta L}{L_0 \Delta T}$).
  2. Superficial Expansion ($\beta$): Increase in surface area per unit original area per degree rise in temperature ($\beta = \frac{\Delta A}{A_0 \Delta T}$).
  3. Cubical Expansion ($\gamma$): Increase in volume per unit original volume per degree rise in temperature ($\gamma = \frac{\Delta V}{V_0 \Delta T}$).
Ratio: $\alpha : \beta : \gamma = 1 : 2 : 3$   or   $\beta = 2\alpha$, $\gamma = 3\alpha$.

[3 Marks]

(b) Reasoning for Practical Observations:

1. Telephone wires sagging: In winter, wires contract. If installed tight in summer, they would snap in winter due to tension. Hence, left loose in summer.

2. Cracking of thick glass tumbler: Glass is a poor conductor of heat. When hot water is poured, inner surface expands rapidly while outer surface remains cold. Unequal thermal expansion creates internal stress, cracking the glass. [3 Marks]

(c) Numerical Calculation (Volume Expansion):

Given: Initial volume $V_0 = 500\text{ cm}^3$, $\Delta T = 110^\circ\text{C} - 10^\circ\text{C} = 100^\circ\text{C}$, $\gamma = 3.6 \times 10^{-5}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$.

$$\Delta V = V_0 \times \gamma \times \Delta T$$
$$\Delta V = 500 \times (3.6 \times 10^{-5}) \times 100 = 50000 \times 3.6 \times 10^{-5} = \mathbf{1.8\text{ cm}^3}$$

[2 Marks]
