SECTION A
Q14. (a) Derive de Broglie's equation. (b) Calculate mass of photon with \(\lambda = 3.6 \mathring{A}\).
Given:
Wavelength (\(\lambda\)) = \(400 \text{ nm} = 400 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m} = 4 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m}\)
\(h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J s}\)
\(c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}\)
1. Calculation of Frequency (\(\nu\)):
\[ \nu = \frac{c}{\lambda} = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{4 \times 10^{-7}} \] \[ \nu = 0.75 \times 10^{15} \text{ Hz} = 7.5 \times 10^{14} \text{ Hz (or s}^{-1}\text{)} \] [1.5 Marks]
2. Calculation of Energy (\(E\)):
\[ E = h\nu = (6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J s}) \times (7.5 \times 10^{14} \text{ s}^{-1}) \] \[ E = 49.695 \times 10^{-20} \text{ J} = 4.97 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J} \] [1.5 Marks]
Q10. Explain the Aufbau Principle. Which orbital will be filled first among \(4s\), \(3d\), and \(4p\)? Justify your answer using the \((n+l)\) rule.
Aufbau Principle: It states that in the ground state of atoms, orbitals are filled with electrons in the increasing order of their energies. Electrons first occupy the lowest energy orbital available. [1 Mark]
\((n+l)\) Rule Application:
For \(4s\): \(n=4, l=0 \implies (n+l) = 4 + 0 = 4\)
For \(3d\): \(n=3, l=2 \implies (n+l) = 3 + 2 = 5\)
For \(4p\): \(n=4, l=1 \implies (n+l) = 4 + 1 = 5\) [1 Mark]
Conclusion: The \(4s\) orbital has the lowest \((n+l)\) value, hence it has the lowest energy and will be filled first.
Note: Between \(3d\) and \(4p\), both have \((n+l) = 5\), but \(3d\) has a lower \(n\) value, so it is filled before \(4p\). [1 Mark]
SECTION C
Q11. (a) Define Quantum numbers. (b) Name the four quantum numbers and explain in detail what information each provides.
(a) Definition: Quantum numbers are a set of four numbers which give complete information about the electron in an atom, i.e., its energy, shape, orientation, and spin. [1 Mark]
(b) The Four Quantum Numbers:
  • Principal Quantum Number (\(n\)): Indicates the main energy shell. It determines the size and largely the energy of the orbital. Values: \(n = 1, 2, 3...\) [1 Mark]
  • Azimuthal/Angular Momentum Quantum Number (\(l\)): Indicates the subshell and defines the three-dimensional shape of the orbital (s=spherical, p=dumbbell, etc.). Values: \(0\) to \((n-1)\). [1 Mark]
  • Magnetic Quantum Number (\(m_l\)): Indicates the spatial orientation of the orbital with respect to a standard set of coordinate axes. It also determines the number of orbitals in a subshell. Values: \(-l\) to \(+l\) including zero. [1 Mark]
  • Electron Spin Quantum Number (\(m_s\)): Indicates the direction of the electron's spin on its own axis (clockwise or anticlockwise). Values: \(+\frac{1}{2}\) or \(-\frac{1}{2}\). [1 Mark]
Q12. (a) What is the photoelectric effect? (b) Photon \(\lambda = 4 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m}\), Work function \(W_0 = 2.13 \text{ eV}\). Calculate Energy of photon in eV and K.E. of emission.
(a) Photoelectric Effect: The phenomenon of ejection of electrons from the surface of a metal when light of suitable frequency (threshold frequency) strikes it is called the photoelectric effect. The emitted electrons are called photoelectrons. [2 Marks]
(b) Calculations:
Given: \(\lambda = 4 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m}\), \(W_0 = 2.13 \text{ eV}\).

(i) Energy of the photon (\(E\)):
\[ E = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34} \times 3 \times 10^8}{4 \times 10^{-7}} \text{ Joules} \] \[ E = \frac{19.878 \times 10^{-26}}{4 \times 10^{-7}} = 4.969 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J} \] To convert to eV, divide by \(1.6 \times 10^{-19}\):
\[ E (\text{in eV}) = \frac{4.969 \times 10^{-19}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}} \approx 3.10 \text{ eV} \] [1.5 Marks]
(ii) Kinetic Energy (K.E.) of emission:
According to Einstein's photoelectric equation: \(E = W_0 + K.E.\)
\[ K.E. = E - W_0 \] \[ K.E. = 3.10 \text{ eV} - 2.13 \text{ eV} = 0.97 \text{ eV} \] (Or in Joules: \(0.97 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19} = 1.55 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J}\)) [1.5 Marks]
Q13. (a) Draw shapes of s and p orbitals. (b) Four quantum numbers for valence electron of Na. (c) Which orbitals are not possible: \(1p, 2s, 3f, 4d\)? Reason.
(a) Shapes:
- \(s\)-orbital is strictly spherical and symmetrical around the nucleus.
- \(p\)-orbital is dumbbell-shaped consisting of two lobes on opposite sides of the nucleus (e.g., \(p_x\) along the x-axis).
(Examiner notes: 1 mark for correct description/rough sketch of spherical s, 1 mark for dumbbell p-orbital). [2 Marks]
(b) Quantum numbers for Na (Z=11) valence electron:
Electronic configuration: \(1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 \mathbf{3s^1}\)
Valence electron is in \(3s\).
\(n = 3\), \(l = 0\), \(m_l = 0\), \(m_s = +\frac{1}{2}\) (or \(-\frac{1}{2}\)) [1 Mark]
(c) Impossible Orbitals:
- \(1p\) is not possible: For \(n=1\), \(l\) can only be \(0\) (s-orbital). \(l\) cannot be equal to \(n\).
- \(3f\) is not possible: For \(n=3\), max value of \(l\) is \(2\) (\(3d\)). For f-orbital, \(l=3\), which requires \(n\) to be at least \(4\).
(\(2s\) and \(4d\) are perfectly possible). [2 Marks]
Q14. (a) Derive de Broglie's equation. (b) Calculate mass of photon with \(\lambda = 3.6 \text{ \AA}\).
(a) Derivation of de Broglie's equation:
According to Planck's quantum theory for a photon: \(E = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda}\)     ...(i)
According to Einstein's mass-energy relationship: \(E = mc^2\)     ...(ii)
Equating (i) and (ii):
\[ mc^2 = \frac{hc}{\lambda} \implies \lambda = \frac{h}{mc} \] For a material particle of mass '\(m\)' moving with velocity '\(v\)', \(c\) is replaced by \(v\):
\[ \lambda = \frac{h}{mv} \quad \text{or} \quad \lambda = \frac{h}{p} \] [2 Marks]
(b) Calculation of mass of photon:
Given: \(\lambda = 3.6 \mathring{A} = 3.6 \times 10^{-10} \text{ m}\)
\(h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ kg m}^2 \text{s}^{-1}\)
\(c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}\)

Using formula: \(m = \frac{h}{\lambda c}\)
\[ m = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}}{3.6 \times 10^{-10} \times 3 \times 10^8} \] \[ m = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}}{10.8 \times 10^{-2}} \] \[ m = 0.6135 \times 10^{-32} \text{ kg} = 6.135 \times 10^{-33} \text{ kg} \] [3 Marks]
Q15. (a) Postulates of Bohr's model for hydrogen atom. (b) Limitations of Bohr's atomic model.
(a) Main Postulates of Bohr's Model:
  • The electron in a hydrogen atom can move around the nucleus in a circular path of fixed radius and energy, called orbits or stationary states.
  • The energy of an electron in the orbit does not change with time (remains constant unless it absorbs/emits energy).
  • Only those orbits are permitted in which the angular momentum of the electron is an integral multiple of \(\frac{h}{2\pi}\). i.e., \(mvr = \frac{nh}{2\pi}\).
  • Radiation is absorbed or emitted only when an electron jumps from one stationary state to another. \(\Delta E = E_2 - E_1 = h\nu\).
[3 Marks]
(b) Limitations of Bohr's Model (Any two):
  • It could not explain the line spectra of multi-electron atoms (atoms containing more than one electron).
  • It could not explain the splitting of spectral lines in a magnetic field (Zeeman effect) or electric field (Stark effect).
  • It contradicts Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle as it assumes well-defined circular paths (orbits) with exact position and velocity simultaneously.
  • It could not explain the ability of atoms to form molecules by chemical bonds.
[2 Marks]
