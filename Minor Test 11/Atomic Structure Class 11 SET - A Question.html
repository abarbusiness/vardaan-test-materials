Minor Test 11 - Set A: Structure of Atom
Class: 11 CBSE Subject: Chemistry Time: 2 Hours Max. Marks: 50
General Instructions:
  • All questions are compulsory.
  • The paper is divided into three sections: A, B, and C.
  • Section A consists of 5 questions of 2 marks each.
  • Section B consists of 5 questions of 3 marks each.
  • Section C consists of 5 questions of 5 marks each.
  • Use of log tables and basic non-programmable calculators is permitted if necessary.
SECTION A (10 Marks)(Attempt all questions - 2 Marks Each)
1.
State Heisenberg’s Uncertainty Principle and write its mathematical expression.
2.
Write the complete electronic configuration of Copper (\(Cu\), \(Z=29\)) and Chromium (\(Cr\), \(Z=24\)). State the reason for their anomalous configurations.
3.
Calculate the number of protons, neutrons, and electrons in \(^{80}_{35}Br\).
4.
What is the physical significance of \(\psi\) and \(\psi^2\) in the quantum mechanical model of an atom?
5.
An atomic orbital has principal quantum number \(n = 3\). What are the possible values of the azimuthal quantum number (\(l\)) and magnetic quantum number (\(m_l\))?
SECTION B (15 Marks)(Short Answer Type - 3 Marks Each)
6.
State Pauli's Exclusion Principle and Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity. Provide one brief example to illustrate Hund's rule.
7.
Calculate the wavelength of a particle moving with a velocity of \(10^3 \text{ m/s}\). The mass of the particle is \(6.626 \times 10^{-31} \text{ kg}\).
(Given: \(h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J s}\))
8.
Differentiate between an orbit (Bohr's concept) and an orbital (Quantum mechanical concept). Give any three points of difference.
9.
Calculate the energy and frequency of a photon of light having a wavelength of \(400 \text{ nm}\).
(Given: \(h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J s}, c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}\))
10.
Explain the Aufbau Principle. Which orbital will be filled first among \(4s\), \(3d\), and \(4p\)? Justify your answer using the \((n+l)\) rule.
SECTION C (25 Marks)(Long Answer Type - 5 Marks Each)
11.
(a) Define Quantum numbers. [1]
(b) Name the four quantum numbers and explain in detail what information each provides about an electron in an atom. [4]
12.
(a) What is the photoelectric effect? [2]
(b) A photon of wavelength \(4 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m}\) strikes on a metal surface. The work function of the metal is \(2.13 \text{ eV}\). Calculate:
(i) The energy of the photon in eV. [1.5]
(ii) The kinetic energy of the emitted photoelectron. [1.5]
(Given: \(1 \text{ eV} = 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J}\))
13.
(a) Draw the geometrical shapes of \(s\)-orbital and any two \(p\)-orbitals (\(p_x, p_y\), or \(p_z\)). [2]
(b) Write the correct set of four quantum numbers for the outermost electron (valence electron) of Sodium (\(Na\), \(Z=11\)). [1]
(c) Which of the following orbitals are not possible: \(1p, 2s, 3f, 4d\)? Give appropriate reasons. [2]
14.
(a) Derive de Broglie's equation relating the wavelength and momentum of a moving particle. [2]
(b) Calculate the mass of a photon with a wavelength of \(3.6 \mathring{A}\). [3]
(Given: \(h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ kg m}^2 \text{s}^{-1}\), \(c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}\), \(1 \mathring{A} = 10^{-10} \text{ m}\))
15.
(a) State the main postulates of Bohr's model for the hydrogen atom. [3]
(b) What are the major limitations of Bohr's atomic model that led to the development of the quantum mechanical model? (Give at least two limitations). [2]
