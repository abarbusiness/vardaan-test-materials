DETAILED SOLUTION KEY: Minor Test 11 - Set A
Class: 11 CBSE Subject: Chemistry Time: 2 Hours Max. Marks: 50
SECTION A
Q1. State Heisenberg’s Uncertainty Principle and write its mathematical expression.
Statement: It is impossible to determine simultaneously, the exact position and exact momentum (or velocity) of an electron (or any other microscopic particle). [1 Mark]
Mathematical Expression: \( \Delta x \cdot \Delta p \ge \frac{h}{4\pi} \)   OR   \( \Delta x \cdot (m\Delta v) \ge \frac{h}{4\pi} \) [1 Mark]
Q2. Complete electronic configuration of Cu (Z=29) and Cr (Z=24). Reason for anomalous configurations.
Chromium (Cr): \( 1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^6 \mathbf{4s^1 3d^5} \) (or \( [Ar] 4s^1 3d^5 \))
Copper (Cu): \( 1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^6 \mathbf{4s^1 3d^{10}} \) (or \( [Ar] 4s^1 3d^{10} \)) [1 Mark]
Reason: Exactly half-filled (\(3d^5\)) and fully-filled (\(3d^{10}\)) orbitals provide extra stability due to symmetrical distribution of electrons and greater exchange energy. [1 Mark]
Q3. Calculate the number of protons, neutrons, and electrons in \(^{80}_{35}Br\).
Atomic Number (\(Z\)) = 35; Mass Number (\(A\)) = 80.
Number of protons (\(p\)) = \(Z = \mathbf{35}\).
Number of electrons (\(e\)) in a neutral atom = \(Z = \mathbf{35}\). [1 Mark]
Number of neutrons (\(n\)) = \(A - Z = 80 - 35 = \mathbf{45}\). [1 Mark]
Q4. Physical significance of \(\psi\) and \(\psi^2\).
\(\psi\) (Wave function): It represents the amplitude of the electron wave. It has no physical meaning or physical significance on its own. [1 Mark]
\(\psi^2\) (Probability density): It gives the probability of finding an electron at a point within a given region of space around the nucleus. [1 Mark]
Q5. For \(n = 3\), what are the possible values of \(l\) and \(m_l\)?
If \(n = 3\), the possible values of \(l\) are \(0, 1, \text{ and } 2\). [1 Mark]
Corresponding \(m_l\) values:
For \(l = 0 \implies m_l = 0\)
For \(l = 1 \implies m_l = -1, 0, +1\)
For \(l = 2 \implies m_l = -2, -1, 0, +1, +2\) [1 Mark]
SECTION B
Q6. State Pauli's Principle and Hund's Rule. Example for Hund's rule.
Pauli's Exclusion Principle: No two electrons in an atom can have the same set of all four quantum numbers. (An orbital can hold a maximum of 2 electrons with opposite spins). [1 Mark]
Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity: Electron pairing in any subshell (p, d, f) cannot take place until each degenerate orbital is singly occupied with parallel spin. [1 Mark]
Example: Nitrogen (\(Z=7\)) is \(1s^2 2s^2 2p^3\). The 2p electrons will be filled as \(2p_x^1 2p_y^1 2p_z^1\) (all unpaired) rather than pairing them up in one orbital. [1 Mark]
Q7. Calculate wavelength. \(v = 10^3 \text{ m/s}, m = 6.626 \times 10^{-31} \text{ kg}\).
According to de Broglie's equation: \( \lambda = \frac{h}{mv} \) [1 Mark]
Given: \( h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J s} \), \( m = 6.626 \times 10^{-31} \text{ kg} \), \( v = 10^3 \text{ m/s} \).
\( \lambda = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}}{6.626 \times 10^{-31} \times 10^3} \) [1 Mark]
\( \lambda = \frac{10^{-34}}{10^{-28}} = \mathbf{10^{-6} \text{ m}} \) [1 Mark]
Q8. Differentiate between an orbit and an orbital (3 points).
1. Definition: Orbit is a well-defined circular path around the nucleus. Orbital is a 3D region in space where the probability of finding an electron is maximum. [1 Mark]
2. Shape: Orbits are circular or elliptical (planar). Orbitals have different shapes (s is spherical, p is dumbbell, etc.). [1 Mark]
3. Heisenberg Principle: The concept of an orbit contradicts Heisenberg's uncertainty principle. The concept of an orbital is strictly in accordance with Heisenberg's principle. [1 Mark]
Q9. Energy and frequency of photon \(\lambda = 400 \text{ nm}\).
Given \(\lambda = 400 \text{ nm} = 400 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m} = 4 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m}\).
Frequency (\(\nu\)): \( \nu = \frac{c}{\lambda} = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{4 \times 10^{-7}} = 0.75 \times 10^{15} = \mathbf{7.5 \times 10^{14} \text{ s}^{-1}} (\text{Hz}) \). [1.5 Marks]
Energy (\(E\)): \( E = h\nu = (6.626 \times 10^{-34}) \times (7.5 \times 10^{14}) \)
\( E = 49.695 \times 10^{-20} = \mathbf{4.969 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J}} \). [1.5 Marks]
Q10. Aufbau Principle. Which fills first: \(4s, 3d, 4p\)? Justify with \((n+l)\) rule.
Aufbau Principle: In the ground state of the atoms, the orbitals are filled in order of their increasing energies (lowest energy orbital is filled first). [1 Mark]
Applying \((n+l)\) Rule:
For \(4s\): \(n=4, l=0 \implies (n+l) = 4\)
For \(3d\): \(n=3, l=2 \implies (n+l) = 5\)
For \(4p\): \(n=4, l=1 \implies (n+l) = 5\) [1 Mark]
Justification: The \(4s\) orbital has the lowest \((n+l)\) value, meaning it has the lowest energy among the three. Therefore, \(4s\) will be filled first. [1 Mark]
SECTION C
Q11. (a) Define Quantum numbers. (b) Explain the four quantum numbers in detail.
(a) Definition: A set of four numbers which provide complete information about an electron in an atom, i.e., its energy, shape, spatial orientation, and spin. [1 Mark]
(b) The Four Quantum Numbers:
1. Principal Quantum Number (\(n\)): Indicates the main energy shell. Gives the size and energy of the orbital. Values: \(n = 1, 2, 3...\) [1 Mark]
2. Azimuthal Quantum Number (\(l\)): Indicates the subshell (s, p, d, f) and determines the 3D shape of the orbital. Values: \(0\) to \((n-1)\). [1 Mark]
3. Magnetic Quantum Number (\(m_l\)): Indicates the spatial orientation of the orbital in an applied magnetic field. Gives the exact number of orbitals in a subshell. Values: \(-l\) to \(+l\) including zero. [1 Mark]
4. Electron Spin Quantum Number (\(m_s\)): Indicates the direction of the electron's spin on its own axis (clockwise/anticlockwise). Values: \(+\frac{1}{2}\) or \(-\frac{1}{2}\). [1 Mark]
Q12. Photoelectric effect. \( \lambda = 4 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m} \), \( W_0 = 2.13 \text{ eV} \). Find E (in eV) and KE.
(a) Photoelectric effect: The phenomenon of ejection of electrons from the surface of a metal when light of suitable threshold frequency strikes it. [2 Marks]
(b) (i) Energy of Photon: \( E = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34} \times 3 \times 10^8}{4 \times 10^{-7}} \)
\( E = 4.969 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J} \)
To convert to eV: \( \frac{4.969 \times 10^{-19}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}} = \mathbf{3.10 \text{ eV}} \). [1.5 Marks]
(b) (ii) Kinetic Energy: According to photoelectric equation, \( E = W_0 + K.E. \)
\( K.E. = E - W_0 = 3.10 \text{ eV} - 2.13 \text{ eV} = \mathbf{0.97 \text{ eV}} \). [1.5 Marks]
Q13. Shapes of s & p. QN for Na valence. Impossible orbitals.
(a) Shapes:
- s-orbital: Spherical and symmetrical around the nucleus.
- p-orbital: Dumbbell shaped consisting of two lobes on opposite sides of the nucleus. (e.g., \(p_x\) oriented along the x-axis).
(Examiner notes: 1 mark for each valid rough sketch or correct description). [2 Marks]
(b) Na (\(Z=11\)) Valence Electron:
Configuration: \(1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 \mathbf{3s^1}\). The valence electron is in \(3s\).
Quantum numbers: \( n=3, l=0, m_l=0, m_s=+\frac{1}{2} \) (or \(-\frac{1}{2}\)). [1 Mark]
(c) Impossible Orbitals:
- 1p is not possible: For \(n=1\), \(l\) can only be 0. It cannot be 1 (p).
- 3f is not possible: For \(n=3\), maximum \(l\) is 2 (\(3d\)). f-orbital requires \(l=3\), which is only possible from \(n=4\) onwards.
(\(2s\) and \(4d\) are fully valid). [2 Marks]
Q14. Derive de Broglie's equation. Find mass of photon with \(\lambda = 3.6 \mathring{A}\).
(a) Derivation:
From Planck's theory: \( E = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda} \)  ---(i)
From Einstein's mass-energy relation: \( E = mc^2 \)  ---(ii)
Equating both: \( mc^2 = \frac{hc}{\lambda} \implies \lambda = \frac{h}{mc} \)
For a material particle replacing \(c\) with velocity \(v\): \( \mathbf{\lambda = \frac{h}{mv} = \frac{h}{p}} \). [2 Marks]
(b) Mass Calculation:
Given \(\lambda = 3.6 \mathring{A} = 3.6 \times 10^{-10} \text{ m}\).
From \( \lambda = \frac{h}{mc} \implies m = \frac{h}{\lambda c} \)
\( m = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}}{(3.6 \times 10^{-10}) \times (3 \times 10^8)} \) [1.5 Marks]
\( m = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}}{10.8 \times 10^{-2}} \)
\( m = 0.6135 \times 10^{-32} \text{ kg} = \mathbf{6.135 \times 10^{-33} \text{ kg}} \). [1.5 Marks]
Q15. Bohr's model postulates and limitations.
(a) Postulates (Any 3):
1. Electrons revolve in specific, non-radiating circular paths called stationary orbits.
2. Energy of the electron in an orbit remains constant.
3. Angular momentum is quantized: \( mvr = \frac{nh}{2\pi} \).
4. Energy is emitted/absorbed only when an electron jumps between orbits (\(\Delta E = h\nu\)). [3 Marks]
(b) Limitations (Any 2):
1. Failed to explain the spectrum of atoms containing more than one electron.
2. Could not explain Zeeman effect (magnetic field splitting) or Stark effect (electric field splitting).
3. Contradicts Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle (assumes well-defined orbits). [2 Marks]
