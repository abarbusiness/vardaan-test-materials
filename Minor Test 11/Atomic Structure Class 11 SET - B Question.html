Minor Test 11 - Set B: Structure of Atom
Class: 11 CBSE Subject: Chemistry Time: 2 Hours Max. Marks: 50
General Instructions:
  • All questions are compulsory.
  • The paper is divided into three sections: A, B, and C.
  • Section A consists of 5 questions of 2 marks each.
  • Section B consists of 5 questions of 3 marks each.
  • Section C consists of 5 questions of 5 marks each.
  • Use of log tables and basic non-programmable calculators is permitted if necessary.
SECTION A (10 Marks)(Attempt all questions - 2 Marks Each)
1.
Define Isotopes and Isobars with one suitable example for each.
2.
State Planck's Quantum Theory and write its mathematical expression defining all terms.
3.
Write the electronic configuration of \(Fe^{2+}\) and \(Fe^{3+}\) ions (\(Z=26\) for Iron). Which one of these is more stable and why?
4.
Calculate the total number of angular nodes and radial nodes present in a \(3p\) orbital.
5.
Define wave number (\(\bar{\nu}\)). What is its relationship with the wavelength (\(\lambda\)) and frequency (\(\nu\)) of an electromagnetic wave?
SECTION B (15 Marks)(Short Answer Type - 3 Marks Each)
6.
State the \((n+l)\) rule. Using this rule, arrange the following orbitals in increasing order of their energy: \(4d, 5p, 5s,\) and \(6s\).
7.
Calculate the wavelength of an electron moving with a velocity of \(2.05 \times 10^7 \text{ m/s}\).
(Given: \(h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ kg m}^2\text{s}^{-1}\), mass of electron \(m_e = 9.11 \times 10^{-31} \text{ kg}\))
8.
Briefly explain the line emission spectrum of hydrogen. Name the spectral series that lies in the visible region of the electromagnetic spectrum.
9.
A microscope using suitable photons is employed to locate an electron in an atom within a distance of \(0.1 \mathring{A}\). What is the uncertainty involved in the measurement of its velocity?
(Given: \(h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J s}\), \(m_e = 9.11 \times 10^{-31} \text{ kg}\))
10.
The threshold frequency (\(\nu_0\)) for a metal is \(7.0 \times 10^{14} \text{ s}^{-1}\). Calculate the kinetic energy of an electron emitted when radiation of frequency \(\nu = 1.0 \times 10^{15} \text{ s}^{-1}\) hits the metal surface.
(Given: \(h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J s}\))
SECTION C (25 Marks)(Long Answer Type - 5 Marks Each)
11.
(a) What are degenerate orbitals? Give one example. [2]
(b) Write the four quantum numbers for the 19th electron of Potassium (\(K\), \(Z=19\)). [2]
(c) How many maximum electrons in an atom can have the quantum numbers \(n=4, m_s=-\frac{1}{2}\)? [1]
12.
(a) Write the Rydberg equation used for calculating wave number of lines in the hydrogen spectrum. [1]
(b) What is the maximum number of emission lines observed when the excited electron of an H atom in \(n=6\) drops to the ground state? [2]
(c) Calculate the energy associated with the first orbit of \(He^+\) ion. [2]
(Given: Rydberg constant for energy \(R_H = 2.18 \times 10^{-18} \text{ J}\))
13.
(a) Why is the wave nature of macroscopic objects (like a moving cricket ball) not observable in our daily life? [2]
(b) State Heisenberg's uncertainty principle. [1]
(c) Show mathematically that the circumference of the Bohr orbit for the hydrogen atom is an integral multiple of the de Broglie wavelength associated with the electron revolving around that orbit. [2]
14.
(a) Differentiate between emission spectra and absorption spectra (Give two points). [2]
(b) A 25-watt bulb emits monochromatic yellow light of wavelength \(0.57 \mu\text{m}\). Calculate the rate of emission of quanta per second. [3]
(Given: \(1 \text{ watt} = 1 \text{ J/s}\), \(h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J s}\), \(c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}\))
15.
(a) Describe the boundary surface diagrams (shapes) of \(d\)-orbitals. Draw neat sketches for any two \(d\)-orbitals. [2]
(b) Explain the physical significance of the magnetic quantum number (\(m_l\)). [1]
(c) What is a continuous spectrum? Give an example of a natural phenomenon that produces a continuous spectrum. [2]
