26.
State the functions of the three main parts of a neuron: Cyton, Dendrites, and Axon.
[3 Marks]
Solution:

The functions of the three main parts of a neuron are:

  • (i) Cyton (Cell Body): It is the main metabolic region of the neuron. It controls the vital functions of the cell and processes the electrical signals received from the dendrites. (1 Mark)
  • (ii) Dendrites: These are short, highly branched extensions that act as receptors. They receive nerve impulses (messages) from other neurons and conduct them towards the cell body. (1 Mark)
  • (iii) Axon: It is a long, single nerve fiber that carries nerve impulses away from the cell body and transmits them to another neuron, muscle, or gland. (1 Mark)
27.
Differentiate between Cerebrum and Cerebellum based on their primary functions. (Give at least two points of difference).
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Feature Cerebrum Cerebellum
Primary Function It controls memory, intelligence, thinking, reasoning, and all conscious (voluntary) activities. It controls the coordination of muscular movements and maintains the posture and balance of the body.
Location / Size It is the largest part of the brain, occupying the uppermost region. It is a smaller region located at the back of the head, under the cerebrum.

28.
What is a Reflex Action? Give two common examples of reflex actions in our daily life.
[3 Marks]
Solution:

Definition: A reflex action is an automatic, quick, and involuntary response of the body to a stimulus, primarily controlled by the spinal cord without the immediate involvement of the conscious areas of the brain. (1.5 Marks)

Examples (Any two): (0.75 Marks each)

  1. Immediate withdrawal of the hand when it touches a hot or sharp object.
  2. Watering of the mouth upon seeing or smelling delicious food.
  3. Blinking of eyes when a strong beam of light flashes across them.
29.
Name the three types of nerves found in the human body. Briefly state the function of each type.
[3 Marks]
Solution:

The three types of nerves and their functions are:

  • 1. Sensory Nerves: They carry nerve impulses from the sense organs (receptors) to the Central Nervous System (Brain and Spinal Cord). (1 Mark)
  • 2. Motor Nerves: They carry instructions (motor impulses) from the Central Nervous System back to the effector organs (muscles or glands) to initiate a response. (1 Mark)
  • 3. Mixed Nerves: These nerves contain both sensory and motor fibers. They perform dual functions, i.e., transmitting impulses to and from the Central Nervous System. (Example: Spinal nerves). (1 Mark)
30.
Define the Central Nervous System (CNS). What is the main function of the spinal cord?
[3 Marks]
Solution:

Central Nervous System (CNS): The CNS is the main control center of the body that integrates and processes information. It consists of the Brain and the Spinal Cord. (1.5 Marks)

Main Functions of the Spinal Cord: (1.5 Marks)

  • It controls involuntary actions below the neck region, specifically managing reflex actions.
  • It acts as a relay channel, conducting sensory impulses from the skin and muscles to the brain, and carrying motor impulses from the brain to the muscles of the trunk and limbs.
