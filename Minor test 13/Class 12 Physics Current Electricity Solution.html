SECTION A
Q1. A metallic wire of resistance \(R\) is stretched to uniformly double its length. New resistance and resistivity?
Resistivity: Remains unchanged as it is a characteristic property of the material and depends only on material and temperature, not on dimensions. [1 Mark]
Resistance: When stretched, volume \(V = A \cdot l\) remains constant.
If \(l' = 2l\), then new area \(A' = A/2\).
\(R' = \rho \frac{l'}{A'} = \rho \frac{2l}{A/2} = 4 \left( \rho \frac{l}{A} \right) = 4R\). The new resistance becomes 4 times the original. [1 Mark]
Q2. Plot variation of resistivity (\(\rho\)) with temperature (\(T\)) for (i) Copper and (ii) Silicon.
(i) Copper (Metal): Resistivity increases with temperature. The graph is a curve bending upwards at lower temperatures and becoming somewhat linear at higher temperatures. [1 Mark]
(ii) Silicon (Semiconductor): Resistivity decreases exponentially with an increase in temperature. The graph is a smooth curve sloping downwards. [1 Mark]
Q3. Define mobility of charge carriers. Write its S.I. unit.
Definition: Mobility (\(\mu\)) of charge carriers is defined as the magnitude of drift velocity (\(v_d\)) acquired by them per unit applied electric field (\(E\)). i.e., \(\mu = \frac{v_d}{E}\). [1 Mark]
S.I. Unit: \(m^2 V^{-1} s^{-1}\) (or \(m^2 / (V \cdot s)\)). [1 Mark]
Q4. Graph showing variation of terminal potential difference '\(V\)' with external resistance '\(R\)'.
Reasoning: \(V = I R = \left(\frac{E}{R+r}\right)R = \frac{E}{1 + \frac{r}{R}}\). [1 Mark]
Graph: The graph starts from origin (\(V=0\) when \(R=0\)), curves upwards and asymptotically approaches the maximum value \(V = E\) as \(R \to \infty\). [1 Mark]
Q5. Microscopic form of Ohm's law and connection to macroscopic form.
Microscopic form: \(\vec{J} = \sigma \vec{E}\) [1 Mark]
Connection: We know \(J = \frac{I}{A}\) and \(E = \frac{V}{l}\).
Substituting in \(J = \sigma E \implies \frac{I}{A} = \sigma \frac{V}{l} \implies V = \left(\frac{l}{\sigma A}\right)I\).
Since \(\frac{l}{\sigma A} = \rho \frac{l}{A} = R\), we get \(V = IR\). [1 Mark]
SECTION B
Q6. Define relaxation time. Relation to drift velocity. Derive \(v_d\).
Relaxation Time (\(\tau\)): The average time interval between two successive collisions of a free electron with the positive ions of the conductor. [1 Mark]
Derivation: Force on an electron in electric field \(E\) is \(\vec{F} = -e\vec{E}\).
Acceleration of electron \(\vec{a} = \frac{\vec{F}}{m} = -\frac{e\vec{E}}{m}\). [1 Mark]
Average velocity acquired is drift velocity \(v_d\). Using \(v = u + at\), average initial velocity \(u = 0\) (thermal motion averages to zero).
\(\vec{v_d} = 0 + \vec{a}\tau = -\frac{e\vec{E}}{m}\tau\) [1 Mark]
Q7. Two identical cells, same current in series and parallel across \(2 \, \Omega\). Find \(r\).
Let emf of each cell be \(E\) and internal resistance be \(r\).
In series: Total emf = \(2E\), Total internal resistance = \(2r\).
Current \(I_s = \frac{2E}{R + 2r}\) [1 Mark]
In parallel: Total emf = \(E\), Total internal resistance = \(r/2\).
Current \(I_p = \frac{E}{R + r/2} = \frac{2E}{2R + r}\) [1 Mark]
Given \(I_s = I_p \implies \frac{2E}{R + 2r} = \frac{2E}{2R + r}\)
\(\implies R + 2r = 2R + r \implies r = R\).
Since \(R = 2 \, \Omega\), therefore \(r = 2 \, \Omega\). [1 Mark]
Q8. State Kirchhoff's rules. Fundamental conservation principle.
Junction Rule (KCL): The algebraic sum of currents meeting at a junction in a closed circuit is zero (\(\sum I = 0\)).
Principle: Based on the Law of Conservation of Charge. [1.5 Marks]
Loop Rule (KVL): The algebraic sum of changes in potential around any closed loop involving resistors and cells in the loop is zero (\(\sum \Delta V = 0\)).
Principle: Based on the Law of Conservation of Energy. [1.5 Marks]
Q9. Wheatstone bridge derivation.
Diagram: (Examiner notes: 1 mark for correct diamond shape circuit with 4 resistors P,Q,R,S, a galvanometer G and a battery). [1 Mark]
Derivation: In balanced condition, current through galvanometer \(I_g = 0\).
Applying KVL to loop ABDA: \(-I_1 P + 0 \cdot G + I_2 R = 0 \implies I_1 P = I_2 R\)  --- (i) [1 Mark]
Applying KVL to loop BCDB: \(-I_1 Q - 0 \cdot G + I_2 S = 0 \implies I_1 Q = I_2 S\)  --- (ii)
Dividing (i) by (ii): \(\frac{I_1 P}{I_1 Q} = \frac{I_2 R}{I_2 S} \implies \frac{P}{Q} = \frac{R}{S}\). [1 Mark]
Q10. Nichrome heating element numerical.
Initial Resistance \(R_1\) (at \(27^\circ \text{C}\)) = \(\frac{V}{I_1} = \frac{220}{3.2} = 68.75 \, \Omega\) [1 Mark]
Steady Resistance \(R_2\) (at steady temp \(T\)) = \(\frac{V}{I_2} = \frac{220}{2.8} = 78.57 \, \Omega\) [1 Mark]
Using formula: \(R_2 = R_1[1 + \alpha(T - T_1)]\)
\(78.57 = 68.75[1 + 1.7 \times 10^{-4} (T - 27)]\)
\(\frac{78.57}{68.75} - 1 = 1.7 \times 10^{-4} (T - 27)\)
\(0.1428 = 1.7 \times 10^{-4} (T - 27) \implies T - 27 = \frac{0.1428}{1.7 \times 10^{-4}} \approx 840^\circ \text{C}\)
Steady Temperature \(T = 840 + 27 = \mathbf{867^\circ \text{C}}\) [1 Mark]
SECTION C
Q11. Kirchhoff's laws circuit numerical.
Statement of Laws: KCL (sum of currents at junction = 0) and KVL (sum of potential drops in closed loop = 0). [1 Mark]
Setting up currents: Let current \(I_1\) originate from the 10V battery (moving up) and \(I_2\) originate from the 5V battery (moving up). Both currents meet at the top nodes and flow down through the middle \(4 \, \Omega\) branch as \((I_1 + I_2)\).
Applying KVL in Loop 1 (Left loop, clockwise):
Starting from bottom left, going up: \(+10 - 3I_1 - 4(I_1 + I_2) = 0\)
\(\implies 10 = 7I_1 + 4I_2\)    --- (Eq 1) [1.5 Marks]
Applying KVL in Loop 2 (Right loop, anti-clockwise):
Starting from bottom right, going up: \(+5 - 2I_2 - 4(I_1 + I_2) = 0\)
\(\implies 5 = 4I_1 + 6I_2\)    --- (Eq 2) [1.5 Marks]
Solving equations:
Multiply Eq 1 by 3: \(21I_1 + 12I_2 = 30\)
Multiply Eq 2 by 2: \(8I_1 + 12I_2 = 10\)
Subtracting: \(13I_1 = 20 \implies \mathbf{I_1 = \frac{20}{13} \text{ A} \approx 1.54 \text{ A}}\)
Substitute \(I_1\) in Eq 2: \(4\left(\frac{20}{13}\right) + 6I_2 = 5 \implies 6I_2 = 5 - \frac{80}{13} = \frac{-15}{13} \implies \mathbf{I_2 = -\frac{15}{78} = -\frac{5}{26} \text{ A} \approx -0.19 \text{ A}}\) (flows opposite to assumed direction).
Middle branch current = \(I_1 + I_2 = \frac{40}{26} - \frac{5}{26} = \mathbf{\frac{35}{26} \text{ A} \approx 1.35 \text{ A}}\) (downwards). [1 Mark]
Q12. (a) Relation \(I = neAv_d\). (b) Effect of doubling length on drift velocity.
(a) Derivation: Consider a conductor of length \(L\), cross-section \(A\), and electron density \(n\).
Volume of conductor = \(AL\). Total number of electrons \(N = nAL\).
Total charge \(Q = Ne = nALe\). [1 Mark]
Time taken by an electron to cross the conductor \(t = \frac{L}{v_d}\). [1 Mark]
Current \(I = \frac{Q}{t} = \frac{nALe}{L/v_d} = \mathbf{neAv_d}\). [1 Mark] [3 Marks]
(b) Doubling Length: We know \(v_d = \frac{eE}{m}\tau = \frac{e}{m} \left(\frac{V}{L}\right) \tau\).
If potential difference (\(V\)) is constant, \(v_d \propto \frac{1}{L}\).
When length \(L\) is doubled (\(2L\)), the drift velocity is halved. [2 Marks]
Q13. Wheatstone bridge circuit.
(a) Equivalent Resistance: The given circuit represents a Wheatstone bridge.
Check for balance: Ratio of arms \(\frac{R_1}{R_3} = \frac{10}{15} = \frac{2}{3}\). Ratio of arms \(\frac{R_2}{R_4} = \frac{20}{30} = \frac{2}{3}\).
Since \(\frac{R_1}{R_3} = \frac{R_2}{R_4}\), the bridge is balanced. Therefore, no current flows through the middle \(50 \, \Omega\) resistor. [1.5 Marks]
The circuit simplifies to \((10+20) \, \Omega\) branch in parallel with \((15+30) \, \Omega\) branch.
Upper branch = \(30 \, \Omega\), Lower branch = \(45 \, \Omega\).
\(R_{eq} = \frac{30 \times 45}{30 + 45} = \frac{1350}{75} = \mathbf{18 \, \Omega}\). [1.5 Marks] [3 Marks]
(b) Total Current: \(I = \frac{V}{R_{eq}} = \frac{9 \text{ V}}{18 \, \Omega} = \mathbf{0.5 \text{ A}}\). [1 Mark]
(c) Null Method advantage: In a balanced state, the galvanometer draws zero current from the circuit. Thus, the measurement does not alter the actual voltage/current in the main circuit, unlike a voltmeter which has finite resistance and draws some current, causing errors. [1 Mark]
Q14. (a) Parallel cells derivation. (b) Equivalent cell calculation.
(a) Derivation: For two cells in parallel, terminal voltage \(V\) is same.
\(I_1 = \frac{E_1 - V}{r_1}\) and \(I_2 = \frac{E_2 - V}{r_2}\).
Total current \(I = I_1 + I_2 = \frac{E_1 - V}{r_1} + \frac{E_2 - V}{r_2} = \left(\frac{E_1}{r_1} + \frac{E_2}{r_2}\right) - V\left(\frac{1}{r_1} + \frac{1}{r_2}\right)\). [1.5 Marks]
Comparing with standard equation \(V = E_{eq} - I r_{eq}\), we rearrange to get:
\(V = \frac{E_1 r_2 + E_2 r_1}{r_1 + r_2} - I \left( \frac{r_1 r_2}{r_1 + r_2} \right)\).
Thus, \(\mathbf{E_{eq} = \frac{E_1 r_2 + E_2 r_1}{r_1 + r_2}}\) and \(\mathbf{r_{eq} = \frac{r_1 r_2}{r_1 + r_2}}\). [1.5 Marks] [3 Marks]
(b) Calculation:
\(E_{eq} = \frac{1.5 \times 0.3 + 2.0 \times 0.2}{0.2 + 0.3} = \frac{0.45 + 0.40}{0.5} = \frac{0.85}{0.5} = \mathbf{1.7 \text{ V}}\).
\(r_{eq} = \frac{0.2 \times 0.3}{0.2 + 0.3} = \frac{0.06}{0.5} = \mathbf{0.12 \, \Omega}\). [2 Marks]
Q15. (a) Conductivity. (b) Series power derivation. (c) Brighter bulb.
(a) Conductivity: It is the reciprocal of electrical resistivity. \(\sigma = \frac{1}{\rho}\). [1 Mark]
(b) Derivation: Resistance of bulbs: \(R_1 = \frac{V^2}{P_1}\) and \(R_2 = \frac{V^2}{P_2}\).
In series, total resistance \(R = R_1 + R_2 = \frac{V^2}{P_1} + \frac{V^2}{P_2}\).
Total power consumed \(P = \frac{V^2}{R}\). Therefore, \(R = \frac{V^2}{P}\).
Equating the two: \(\frac{V^2}{P} = \frac{V^2}{P_1} + \frac{V^2}{P_2}\). Canceling \(V^2\), we get \(\mathbf{\frac{1}{P} = \frac{1}{P_1} + \frac{1}{P_2}}\). [2 Marks]
(c) Brighter bulb: In a series combination, the current (\(I\)) remains constant through both bulbs.
Power dissipated (Brightness) \(P' = I^2 R\). So, brightness \(\propto R\).
Since \(R = \frac{V^2}{P}\), the bulb with the lower power rating has a higher resistance.
Therefore, the bulb with the lower power rating will dissipate more power (\(I^2 R\)) and glow brighter. [2 Marks]
