29. Differentiate between parenchyma, collenchyma, and sclerenchyma purely on the basis of their cell wall characteristics.
[2 Marks]
Ans:
  • Parenchyma: Cell wall is thin and made up of cellulose.
  • Collenchyma: Cell wall is irregularly thickened at the corners due to the deposition of pectin and cellulose.
  • Sclerenchyma: Cell wall is heavily thickened, hard, and rigid due to the deposition of lignin.

33. Explain what complex permanent tissues are. Name the two types of complex tissues found in plants and state their respective functions.
[3 Marks]
Ans:
Complex Permanent Tissues: Tissues that are made up of more than one type of cells which coordinate together to perform a common specific function are called complex permanent tissues. (1 Mark)

The two types of complex tissues in plants are Xylem and Phloem. (0.5 Mark for naming both)

Functions:
  • Xylem: It is primarily responsible for the unidirectional conduction of water and dissolved minerals from the roots to the stem and leaves. (0.75 Mark)
  • Phloem: It is responsible for the bidirectional translocation of prepared food (sugars) from the leaves to other parts of the plant. (0.75 Mark)
SECTION D (Long Answer Type)
34. (a) Describe the structure and general function of the various types of epithelial tissues in animals. Mention where squamous and columnar epithelium are specifically found. (3 Marks)

(b) How does cork act as a protective tissue? Explain the process of its formation and name the chemical that makes it impervious to gases and water. (3 Marks)
[6 Marks]
Ans:
Part (a): Epithelial Tissues (3 Marks)
Epithelial tissues are covering or protective tissues separated from underlying tissues by an extracellular fibrous basement membrane. They are tightly packed with almost no intercellular spaces.
Types based on structure:
  • Squamous Epithelium: Thin, flat, and scale-like cells forming a delicate lining. Location: Alveoli of lungs, inner lining of blood vessels, and lining of mouth/esophagus. (1 Mark)
  • Cuboidal Epithelium: Cube-shaped cells that provide mechanical support and sometimes secretion. Location: Lining of kidney tubules and ducts of salivary glands. (1 Mark)
  • Columnar Epithelium: Tall, pillar-like cells facilitating absorption and secretion. Location: Inner lining of the intestine. If bearing cilia (ciliated columnar), found in the respiratory tract. (1 Mark)
Part (b): Cork as Protective Tissue (3 Marks)
  • Formation: As plants grow older, the outer protective tissues undergo changes. A strip of secondary meristem (cork cambium) replaces the epidermis of the stem. Cells on the outside are cut off from this layer to form a multi-layered thick bark or cork. (1.5 Marks)
  • Protective Nature: Cells of cork are dead and compactly arranged without intercellular spaces. This physically protects the inner tissues from mechanical injury, extreme temperatures, and parasitic fungal/bacterial infections. (0.5 Mark)
  • Chemical Imperviousness: Their cell walls possess a waxy chemical called Suberin. (1 Mark). Suberin makes the cork cells highly impervious (waterproof and leakproof) to gases and water, preventing excessive water loss (desiccation).
