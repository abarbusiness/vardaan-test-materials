SECTION A - MULTIPLE CHOICE QUESTIONS (25 Marks)
Q1. Yeast is used in the production of:
[1 Mark]
Answer: (b) Alcohol
Explanation: Yeast converts natural sugars present in grains (barley, wheat, etc.) into alcohol through anaerobic respiration (fermentation).
Q2. The following is an antibiotic:
[1 Mark]
Answer: (b) Streptomycin
Explanation: Streptomycin, tetracycline, and erythromycin are common antibiotics made from fungi and bacteria.
Q3. Carrier of malaria-causing protozoan is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: (a) Female Anopheles mosquito
Explanation: It carries the parasite Plasmodium which causes malaria.
Q4. The most common carrier of communicable diseases is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: (b) Housefly
Explanation: Houseflies sit on garbage and animal excreta, pick up pathogens, and transfer them to uncovered food.
Q5. The bread or idli dough rises because of:
[1 Mark]
Answer: (c) Growth of yeast cells
Explanation: Yeast reproduces rapidly and produces \( CO_2 \) gas during respiration. Bubbles of the gas fill the dough and increase its volume.

[1 Mark]
Answer: (c) Fermentation
Reaction: \( C_6H_{12}O_6 \xrightarrow{\text{Yeast}} 2C_2H_5OH \text{ (Alcohol)} + 2CO_2 \)

SECTION B - SUBJECTIVE QUESTIONS (15 Marks)
Q26. (a) What are microorganisms? (b) Name the four major groups into which microorganisms are classified.
[3 Marks]
Answer:

(a) Microorganisms: Living organisms around us which are too small to be seen with the naked eye are called microorganisms or microbes. (1 Mark)

(b) Four major groups: (0.5 Marks each = 2 Marks)

  • Bacteria (e.g., Lactobacillus)
  • Fungi (e.g., Bread mould, Penicillium)
  • Protozoa (e.g., Amoeba, Paramecium)
  • Algae (e.g., Spirogyra, Chlamydomonas)

Q27. Write any three commercial or industrial uses of microorganisms in our daily lives.
[3 Marks]
Answer: (Any three valid points, 1 Mark each)
  • Production of Alcohol/Wine: Yeast is used for large-scale production of alcohol, wine, and acetic acid (vinegar) through fermentation.
  • Baking Industry: Yeast is used in the baking industry for making breads, pastries, and cakes as it produces carbon dioxide which makes the dough rise.
  • Dairy Industry: Bacteria like Lactobacillus promote the conversion of milk into curd and are also involved in cheese making.
  • Medicines (Antibiotics): Fungi and bacteria are used to manufacture antibiotics like penicillin, streptomycin, etc.
Q28. What are communicable diseases? Give any two examples and suggest one primary way to prevent their spread.
[3 Marks]
Answer:

Communicable Diseases: Microbial diseases that can spread from an infected person to a healthy person through air, water, food, or physical contact are called communicable diseases. (1 Mark)

Examples: Cholera, Common cold, Chickenpox, Tuberculosis (TB). (Any two, 0.5 + 0.5 = 1 Mark)

Prevention: (Any one valid point = 1 Mark)

  • Keep a handkerchief on the nose and mouth while sneezing.
  • Keep infected persons separated and maintain personal hygiene.
  • Do not let water collect in the surroundings to prevent mosquito breeding.

Q29. (a) What is pasteurization? Briefly explain the process. (b) Name the scientist who discovered this process.
[3 Marks]
Answer:

(a) Pasteurization: It is a process used to preserve milk and prevent it from spoiling by destroying harmful microbes.
Process: The milk is heated to about \( 70^\circ \text{C} \) for 15 to 30 seconds and then suddenly chilled and stored. This sudden change in temperature prevents the growth of microbes. (2 Marks)

(b) Discoverer: The process was discovered by the French scientist Louis Pasteur. (1 Mark)

Q30. What is a vaccine? How does it help our body fight against harmful diseases?
[3 Marks]
Answer:

Vaccine: A biological preparation comprising dead or weakened (attenuated) microbes of a specific disease, which is introduced into a healthy body to produce immunity. (1 Mark)

Working Mechanism: (2 Marks)

  • When dead or weakened microbes are introduced, the body's immune system fights and kills the invading bacteria by producing specific protective proteins called antibodies.
  • These antibodies remain in the body, and the body "remembers" how to fight the microbe.
  • If the actual disease-causing microbe enters the body later, the pre-existing antibodies quickly recognize and destroy it, thus preventing the disease.
