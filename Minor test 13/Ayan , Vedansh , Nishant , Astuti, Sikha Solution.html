26. Define venation. Name and briefly explain the two main types of venation found in leaves with one example of each.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Venation: The arrangement or pattern of veins and veinlets in the lamina (leaf blade) of a leaf is called venation. (1 Mark)

Types of Venation:
  • Reticulate Venation: When the veins form a net-like design on both sides of the midrib.
    Example: Peepal, Mango, Rose. (1 Mark)
  • Parallel Venation: When the veins run parallel to one another from the base to the tip of the leaf.
    Example: Grass, Wheat, Banana. (1 Mark)
(Marking Scheme: 1 mark for definition, 1 mark each for correct explanation + example of the two types.)
27. State three important functions performed by the leaves of a plant.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
The three main functions of a leaf are:
  1. Photosynthesis: Leaves prepare food for the plant using carbon dioxide and water in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll.
  2. Transpiration: Leaves release excess water into the atmosphere in the form of water vapour through tiny pores called stomata. This helps in cooling the plant and pulling water up from the roots.
  3. Respiration / Gaseous Exchange: Leaves facilitate the exchange of gases (taking in oxygen and releasing carbon dioxide during respiration, and vice versa during photosynthesis) through stomata.
(Marking Scheme: 1 mark for each correctly stated function.)

29. Name the four main whorls (parts) of a complete flower. State the primary function of petals.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
The four main whorls of a complete flower from outside to inside are:
  1. Sepals (Calyx)
  2. Petals (Corolla)
  3. Stamens (Androecium - Male part)
  4. Pistil / Carpel (Gynoecium - Female part)
(2 Marks for listing all four whorls correctly. 1/2 mark for each.)

Function of Petals: Petals are usually brightly colored and scented to attract insects and birds for pollination. They also protect the inner reproductive parts when the flower is young. (1 Mark)
30. Differentiate between the Stamen and the Pistil by writing any three key differences.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Feature Stamen Pistil (Carpel)
1. Role It is the male reproductive part of the flower. It is the female reproductive part of the flower.
2. Parts/Structure It consists of two parts: the anther and the filament. It consists of three parts: the stigma, style, and ovary.
3. Gamete Production The anther produces pollen grains (which contain male gametes). The ovary contains ovules (which contain female gametes/eggs).

(Marking Scheme: 1 mark for each correct, corresponding point of difference.)
