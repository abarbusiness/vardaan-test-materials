SOLUTIONS & ANSWER KEY : MINOR TEST 13

SECTION A - SOLUTIONS (25 Marks)
1.
Who proposed the Five-Kingdom classification of living organisms?
[1]
Answer: (b) Robert Whittaker
Explanation: Robert H. Whittaker proposed the 5-kingdom classification (Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia) in 1969.
2.
Which of the following is the basic or smallest unit of classification?
[1]
Answer: (d) Species
Explanation: Species is the lowest and most fundamental unit of biological classification.
3.
Amoeba belongs to which of the following kingdoms?
[1]
Answer: (b) Protista
Explanation: Amoeba is a unicellular eukaryotic organism, which places it in Kingdom Protista.
4.
Organisms in Kingdom Fungi obtain their food by:
[1]
Answer: (b) Absorbing decaying matter
Explanation: Fungi are heterotrophic and typically saprophytic, absorbing nutrients from dead and decaying matter.
5.
Animals that do not possess a backbone are classified as:
[1]
Answer: (c) Invertebrates
6.
Sponges, which have pores all over their body, belong to Phylum:
[1]
Answer: (a) Porifera
Explanation: The word 'Porifera' means pore-bearing animals.
7.
Hydra and Jellyfish belong to which phylum?
[1]
Answer: (c) Cnidaria (Coelenterata)
8.
Tapeworm is an example of a flatworm belonging to Phylum:
[1]
Answer: (b) Platyhelminthes
9.
Earthworms have a segmented body. They belong to Phylum:
[1]
Answer: (a) Annelida
10.
Which is the largest phylum of the animal kingdom, containing insects?
[1]
Answer: (c) Arthropoda
11.
The term 'Arthropoda' literally means:
[1]
Answer: (b) Jointed legs
Explanation: 'Arthro' means jointed and 'poda' means legs/feet.
12.
Animals with a soft, unsegmented body often enclosed in a hard shell (e.g., Snail) belong to:
[1]
Answer: (c) Mollusca
13.
Starfish belongs to Phylum Echinodermata. A key characteristic of this phylum is:
[1]
Answer: (b) Spiny skin
Explanation: 'Echino' means spiny and 'derma' means skin.
14.
Animals possessing a backbone (vertebral column) belong to:
[1]
Answer: (c) Vertebrata
15.
Fishes (Class Pisces) primarily breathe using:
[1]
Answer: (c) Gills
16.
Amphibians, such as frogs, are unique because they can live:
[1]
Answer: (c) Both on land and in water
17.
Which class includes cold-blooded animals with dry, scaly skin (e.g., Snakes, Lizards)?
[1]
Answer: (b) Reptilia
18.
Birds belong to Class Aves. Which of the following is true for them?
[1]
Answer: (c) Forelimbs modified into wings
19.
Which of these is a distinct characteristic of Mammals?
[1]
Answer: (b) Presence of mammary glands
20.
Which kingdom contains unicellular organisms that lack a distinct, well-defined nucleus (Prokaryotes)?
[1]
Answer: (a) Monera
21.
The system of providing a two-word scientific name to organisms is called:
[1]
Answer: (c) Binomial nomenclature
22.
In the scientific name Homo sapiens, the word 'Homo' represents the:
[1]
Answer: (b) Genus
23.
A bat is capable of flying in the air, yet it is classified as a:
[1]
Answer: (b) Mammal
Explanation: Bats give birth to young ones and have mammary glands.
24.
Yeast and Mushroom are examples of which kingdom?
[1]
Answer: (d) Fungi
25.
Which of the following animals has a streamlined body to help it swim easily in water?
[1]
Answer: (c) Fish
SECTION B - SOLUTIONS (6 Marks)
26.
Define 'Species'. Who introduced the system of Binomial Nomenclature?
[2]
Solution:
  • Species: It is the basic unit of classification. A species is defined as a group of organisms that are structurally similar and can interbreed among themselves to produce fertile offspring. (1 Mark)
  • Binomial Nomenclature: The system was introduced by Carolus Linnaeus. (1 Mark)
27.
State any two main characteristics of Kingdom Fungi. Give one example of an organism from this kingdom.
[2]
Solution:
Characteristics (Any Two - 1 Mark Total; 0.5 each):
  • They are eukaryotic and mostly multicellular (except yeast which is unicellular).
  • They lack chlorophyll, hence they cannot photosynthesize (Heterotrophic/Saprophytic).
  • Their cell walls are made up of chitin.
Example (1 Mark): Mushroom, Yeast, or Bread mould (Rhizopus).
28.
Write any two fundamental differences between Invertebrates and Vertebrates.
[2]
Solution:
Invertebrates Vertebrates
They do not have a backbone (vertebral column). They possess a well-defined backbone (vertebral column).
They do not have a skeletal framework inside their body (endoskeleton). They have an internal skeleton made of bone or cartilage.

(1 Mark for each correct difference pairing)
SECTION C - SOLUTIONS (9 Marks)
29.
Write three distinguishing features of Phylum Arthropoda. Give two common examples of animals belonging to this phylum.
[3]
Solution:
Distinguishing Features (Any Three - 2 Marks; ~0.66 each):
  • They possess jointed appendages (legs).
  • Their body is segmented, usually divided into head, thorax, and abdomen.
  • They have a hard outer covering called an exoskeleton made of chitin.
  • It is the largest phylum in the animal kingdom.
Examples (1 Mark; 0.5 each): Ant, Spider, Butterfly, Cockroach, Crab. (Any two).
30.
Classify the following animals into their correct respective classes under Vertebrata:

(a) Shark    (b) Frog    (c) Snake    (d) Pigeon    (e) Bat    (f) Human
[3]
Solution:
(0.5 Marks for each correct classification, Total 3 Marks)
  • (a) Shark: Class Pisces (Fishes)
  • (b) Frog: Class Amphibia
  • (c) Snake: Class Reptilia
  • (d) Pigeon: Class Aves (Birds)
  • (e) Bat: Class Mammalia
  • (f) Human: Class Mammalia
31.
Name the five kingdoms of the living world as proposed by R.H. Whittaker. Give one example of an organism for each of the first three kingdoms you list.
[3]
Solution:
The Five Kingdoms (1.5 Marks):
  1. Kingdom Monera
  2. Kingdom Protista
  3. Kingdom Fungi
  4. Kingdom Plantae
  5. Kingdom Animalia
Examples of the first three (1.5 Marks; 0.5 each):
  • Monera Example: Bacteria, Blue-green algae.
  • Protista Example: Amoeba, Paramecium.
  • Fungi Example: Mushroom, Yeast, Bread mould.
