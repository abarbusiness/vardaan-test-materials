SECTION A (2 Marks Each)
Q1. A boat has a velocity of \( 5\text{ m/s} \) in still water. It crosses a river of width \( 200\text{ m} \) flowing at \( 3\text{ m/s} \) along the shortest possible path. Find the time taken to cross the river.
[2 Marks]
Velocity of boat in still water, \( v_b = 5 \text{ m/s} \).
Velocity of river, \( v_r = 3 \text{ m/s} \).
Width of river, \( d = 200 \text{ m} \).

For the shortest path (straight across), the resultant velocity \( v_{res} \) must be strictly perpendicular to the river flow.
$$ v_{res} = \sqrt{v_b^2 - v_r^2} $$ $$ v_{res} = \sqrt{5^2 - 3^2} = \sqrt{25 - 9} = \sqrt{16} = 4 \text{ m/s} $$
Time taken, \( t = \frac{\text{Distance}}{v_{res}} \)
$$ t = \frac{200}{4} = 50 \text{ s} $$
Final Answer: 50 seconds
Q2. A bullet is dropped from a certain height. Simultaneously, another bullet is fired horizontally from exactly the same height. Which one will hit the ground first and why?
[2 Marks]
Both bullets will hit the ground simultaneously.

Reason: The vertical motion of an object is completely independent of its horizontal motion. For both bullets, the initial vertical velocity (\( u_y \)) is exactly zero.
They are subjected to the same vertical acceleration due to gravity (\( a_y = g \)) and have to cover the same vertical downward displacement (\( y \)).
Using \( y = u_y t + \frac{1}{2}gt^2 \), we get \( y = 0 + \frac{1}{2}gt^2 \implies t = \sqrt{\frac{2y}{g}} \).
Since \( y \) and \( g \) are the same for both, the time taken (\( t \)) is identical.
Q3. Two projectiles are thrown with the same initial velocity at angles \( (45^\circ - \theta) \) and \( (45^\circ + \theta) \) with the horizontal. What is the ratio of their horizontal ranges? Show your reasoning.
[2 Marks]
Let the first angle be \( \alpha_1 = 45^\circ - \theta \) and the second angle be \( \alpha_2 = 45^\circ + \theta \).
Sum of the angles: \( \alpha_1 + \alpha_2 = (45^\circ - \theta) + (45^\circ + \theta) = 90^\circ \).

Because the angles are complementary (they add up to \( 90^\circ \)), their horizontal ranges are strictly equal.
Proof:
$$ R_1 = \frac{u^2 \sin(2\alpha_1)}{g} = \frac{u^2 \sin(90^\circ - 2\theta)}{g} = \frac{u^2 \cos(2\theta)}{g} $$ $$ R_2 = \frac{u^2 \sin(2\alpha_2)}{g} = \frac{u^2 \sin(90^\circ + 2\theta)}{g} = \frac{u^2 \cos(2\theta)}{g} $$
Therefore, \( R_1 = R_2 \).
Ratio = 1 : 1
Q4. Rain is falling vertically downwards with a speed of \( 30\text{ m/s} \). A woman rides a bicycle with a speed of \( 10\text{ m/s} \) in the east to west direction. What is the magnitude and direction of the relative velocity of the rain with respect to the woman?
[2 Marks]
Let vertically downward be the negative y-axis (\( -\hat{j} \)) and West be the negative x-axis (\( -\hat{i} \)).
Velocity of rain, \( \vec{v}_r = -30\hat{j} \text{ m/s} \).
Velocity of woman, \( \vec{v}_w = -10\hat{i} \text{ m/s} \).

Relative velocity of rain w.r.t woman, \( \vec{v}_{rw} = \vec{v}_r - \vec{v}_w \)
$$ \vec{v}_{rw} = -30\hat{j} - (-10\hat{i}) = 10\hat{i} - 30\hat{j} $$
Magnitude:
$$ |\vec{v}_{rw}| = \sqrt{(10)^2 + (-30)^2} = \sqrt{100 + 900} = \sqrt{1000} \approx 31.62 \text{ m/s} $$
Direction: Let \( \alpha \) be the angle with the vertical.
$$ \tan\alpha = \frac{v_w}{v_r} = \frac{10}{30} = \frac{1}{3} $$
Magnitude = 31.62 m/s | Direction = \( \tan^{-1}(1/3) \) from the vertical towards West.
Q5. A ball is thrown at an angle of \( 30^\circ \) to the horizontal. Calculate the ratio of the kinetic energy of the ball at the highest point of its trajectory to its initial kinetic energy.
[2 Marks]
Let initial velocity be \( u \) and mass be \( m \).
Initial Kinetic Energy, \( K_i = \frac{1}{2}mu^2 \).

At the highest point, the vertical component of velocity is zero. The ball only has its horizontal velocity component, which remains constant throughout the flight.
Velocity at highest point, \( v = u_x = u\cos(30^\circ) = u\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) \).

Kinetic Energy at highest point, \( K_f = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 \)
$$ K_f = \frac{1}{2}m\left(u\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{2}m\left(\frac{3u^2}{4}\right) = \frac{3}{4} \left(\frac{1}{2}mu^2\right) = \frac{3}{4} K_i $$
Ratio (\( K_f : K_i \)) = 3 : 4
SECTION B (3 Marks Each)
Q6. A man can swim at \( 4\text{ km/h} \) in still water. How long does he take to cross a river \( 1\text{ km} \) wide if the river flows steadily at \( 3\text{ km/h} \) and he makes his strokes strictly normal to the river current? How far down the river does he go when he reaches the other bank?
[3 Marks]
Velocity of man in still water, \( v_m = 4 \text{ km/h} \) (perpendicular to flow).
Velocity of river, \( v_r = 3 \text{ km/h} \).
Width of river, \( d = 1 \text{ km} \).

Step 1: Time taken to cross
The man crosses the river due to his perpendicular velocity component, which is entirely \( v_m \).
$$ t = \frac{d}{v_m} = \frac{1 \text{ km}}{4 \text{ km/h}} = 0.25 \text{ hours} = 15 \text{ minutes} $$
Step 2: Drift (Distance moved down the river)
The drift is caused by the river current pushing him sideways during the time \( t \) he takes to cross.
$$ \text{Drift } (x) = v_r \times t = 3 \text{ km/h} \times 0.25 \text{ h} = 0.75 \text{ km} = 750 \text{ m} $$
Time = 15 mins | Drift = 750 meters
Q7. An airplane is flying horizontally at a height of \( 490\text{ m} \) with a velocity of \( 360\text{ km/h} \). A food packet is released from it. Calculate the time taken by the packet to reach the ground and its horizontal range. (Take \( g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2 \)).
[3 Marks]
This is a case of horizontal projection.
Initial horizontal velocity, \( u_x = 360 \text{ km/h} = 360 \times \frac{5}{18} = 100 \text{ m/s} \).
Initial vertical velocity, \( u_y = 0 \).
Vertical displacement, \( y = 490 \text{ m} \).

1. Time to reach ground (\( t \)):
Using vertical motion equation: \( y = u_y t + \frac{1}{2}gt^2 \)
$$ 490 = 0 + \frac{1}{2}(9.8)t^2 $$ $$ 490 = 4.9t^2 \implies t^2 = \frac{490}{4.9} = 100 $$ $$ t = 10 \text{ s} $$
2. Horizontal Range (\( R \)):
Horizontal velocity remains constant.
$$ R = u_x \times t = 100 \text{ m/s} \times 10 \text{ s} = 1000 \text{ m} $$
Time = 10 s | Horizontal Range = 1000 m (or 1 km)
Q8. The equation of trajectory of a projectile is given by \( y = \sqrt{3}x - \frac{g x^2}{2} \). Calculate the angle of projection and the initial velocity of the projectile.
[3 Marks]
The standard equation of trajectory for a projectile fired at an angle \( \theta \) with velocity \( u \) is:
$$ y = x\tan\theta - \frac{gx^2}{2u^2\cos^2\theta} $$
Given equation: \( y = \sqrt{3}x - \frac{g x^2}{2} \).

1. Angle of Projection (\( \theta \)):
Comparing the coefficient of \( x \):
$$ \tan\theta = \sqrt{3} \implies \theta = 60^\circ $$
2. Initial Velocity (\( u \)):
Comparing the coefficient of \( x^2 \):
$$ \frac{g}{2u^2\cos^2\theta} = \frac{g}{2} $$ $$ u^2\cos^2(60^\circ) = 1 $$ $$ u^2 \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = 1 \implies u^2 \left(\frac{1}{4}\right) = 1 $$ $$ u^2 = 4 \implies u = 2 \text{ m/s} $$
Angle = 60° | Initial Velocity = 2 m/s
Q9. An aircraft is flying at a speed of \( 400\text{ km/h} \) due north. A strong wind starts blowing at \( 100\text{ km/h} \) from east to west. Find the magnitude and direction of the resultant velocity of the aircraft.
[3 Marks]
Velocity of aircraft, \( \vec{v}_a = 400\hat{j} \text{ km/h} \) (North).
Velocity of wind, \( \vec{v}_w = -100\hat{i} \text{ km/h} \) (East to West means towards West).

Resultant velocity of the aircraft relative to the ground, \( \vec{v}_{res} = \vec{v}_a + \vec{v}_w \).
$$ \vec{v}_{res} = -100\hat{i} + 400\hat{j} $$
Magnitude:
$$ |\vec{v}_{res}| = \sqrt{(-100)^2 + (400)^2} = \sqrt{10000 + 160000} = \sqrt{170000} $$ $$ |\vec{v}_{res}| = 100\sqrt{17} \approx 412.31 \text{ km/h} $$
Direction: Let \( \alpha \) be the angle made with North (y-axis).
$$ \tan\alpha = \frac{|\vec{v}_w|}{|\vec{v}_a|} = \frac{100}{400} = \frac{1}{4} = 0.25 $$
Velocity = 412.31 km/h | Direction = \( \tan^{-1}(0.25) \) West of North
Q10. Mathematically prove that the maximum horizontal range is exactly four times the maximum height attained by a projectile when it is fired at an angle that provides the maximum horizontal range.
[3 Marks]
For maximum horizontal range, the angle of projection must be \( \theta = 45^\circ \).

Step 1: Formula for Maximum Range (\( R_{max} \))
$$ R = \frac{u^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g} $$ $$ R_{max} = \frac{u^2 \sin(2 \times 45^\circ)}{g} = \frac{u^2 \sin(90^\circ)}{g} = \frac{u^2}{g} \quad \text{--- (Eq 1)} $$
Step 2: Formula for Height at \( 45^\circ \) (\( H \))
$$ H = \frac{u^2 \sin^2(\theta)}{2g} = \frac{u^2 \sin^2(45^\circ)}{2g} $$ $$ H = \frac{u^2 (1/\sqrt{2})^2}{2g} = \frac{u^2 (1/2)}{2g} = \frac{u^2}{4g} $$
From Eq 1, we can substitute \( \frac{u^2}{g} \) with \( R_{max} \):
$$ H = \frac{R_{max}}{4} \implies R_{max} = 4H $$
Hence, verified that maximum horizontal range is four times the maximum height at this angle.
SECTION C (5 Marks Each)
Q11. A projectile is fired from the top of a \( 40\text{ m} \) high tower with an initial velocity of \( 20\text{ m/s} \) at an angle of \( 30^\circ \) above the horizontal. Find: (a) Total time taken to hit the ground. (b) Horizontal distance from the base. (c) Magnitude of striking velocity. (Take \( g = 10\text{ m/s}^2 \))
[5 Marks]
Let the point of projection be the origin (0,0). Taking upward as positive and downward as negative.
Initial velocity, \( u = 20 \text{ m/s} \). Angle, \( \theta = 30^\circ \).
\( u_x = 20\cos(30^\circ) = 20(\sqrt{3}/2) = 10\sqrt{3} \text{ m/s} \).
\( u_y = 20\sin(30^\circ) = 20(1/2) = 10 \text{ m/s} \).
Vertical displacement to reach the ground, \( s_y = -40 \text{ m} \). Acceleration, \( a_y = -10 \text{ m/s}^2 \).

(a) Time of flight (\( t \)):
$$ s_y = u_yt + \frac{1}{2}a_yt^2 $$ $$ -40 = 10t + \frac{1}{2}(-10)t^2 $$ $$ -40 = 10t - 5t^2 $$
Divide by 5 and rearrange:
$$ t^2 - 2t - 8 = 0 $$ $$ (t-4)(t+2) = 0 $$
Since time cannot be negative, \( t = 4 \text{ s} \).

(b) Horizontal distance (Range, \( R \)):
$$ R = u_x \times t = 10\sqrt{3} \times 4 = 40\sqrt{3} \text{ m} \approx 69.28 \text{ m} $$

(c) Striking Velocity (\( v \)):
Final horizontal velocity, \( v_x = u_x = 10\sqrt{3} \text{ m/s} \).
Final vertical velocity, \( v_y = u_y + a_yt = 10 + (-10)(4) = 10 - 40 = -30 \text{ m/s} \).
Magnitude of velocity:
$$ v = \sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2} = \sqrt{(10\sqrt{3})^2 + (-30)^2} = \sqrt{300 + 900} = \sqrt{1200} $$ $$ v = 20\sqrt{3} \text{ m/s} \approx 34.64 \text{ m/s} $$
Time = 4s | Range = \( 40\sqrt{3} \) m | Velocity = \( 20\sqrt{3} \) m/s
Q12. A river is flowing due east with a steady speed of \( 3\text{ m/s} \). A swimmer can swim in still water at a speed of \( 4\text{ m/s} \).
(a) Shortest possible path angle.
(b) Resultant velocity.
(c) Time for shortest path (river width = 100m).
(d) Horizontal drift for shortest time.
[5 Marks]
Given: \( v_r = 3 \text{ m/s} \) (East), \( v_b = 4 \text{ m/s} \), Width \( d = 100 \text{ m} \).

(a) Angle for shortest path:
To cross straight across, the swimmer must swim upstream at an angle \( \alpha \) with the vertical (y-axis).
$$ \sin\alpha = \frac{v_r}{v_b} = \frac{3}{4} $$ $$ \alpha = \sin^{-1}(0.75) $$
Angle with the direction of river flow = \( 90^\circ + \sin^{-1}(3/4) \).

(b) Resultant Velocity (\( v_{res} \)):
For shortest path, the resultant velocity is perpendicular to the flow.
$$ v_{res} = \sqrt{v_b^2 - v_r^2} = \sqrt{4^2 - 3^2} = \sqrt{16 - 9} = \sqrt{7} \text{ m/s} \approx 2.65 \text{ m/s} $$

(c) Time taken for shortest path:
$$ t_{shortest\_path} = \frac{d}{v_{res}} = \frac{100}{\sqrt{7}} \text{ s} \approx 37.8 \text{ s} $$

(d) Horizontal drift for shortest time:
For shortest time, he must swim exactly perpendicular to the flow.
$$ t_{min} = \frac{d}{v_b} = \frac{100}{4} = 25 \text{ s} $$
The drift is purely due to the river's flow during this minimum time:
$$ \text{Drift} = v_r \times t_{min} = 3 \times 25 = 75 \text{ m} $$
Q13. Based on the principles of projectile motion:
(a) Prove that there are two angles of projection for a given initial velocity for which the horizontal range is the same.
(b) A body is projected with a velocity \( u \) such that its horizontal range is \( R \). If \( t_1 \) and \( t_2 \) are the times of flight for the two possible angles of projection, derive \( t_1 t_2 = \frac{2R}{g} \).
[5 Marks]
(a) Proof of Two Angles for Same Range:
The horizontal range \( R \) for an angle \( \theta \) is:
$$ R = \frac{u^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g} \quad \text{--- (Eq 1)} $$
Let's substitute another angle \( (90^\circ - \theta) \) into the range formula:
$$ R' = \frac{u^2 \sin(2(90^\circ - \theta))}{g} = \frac{u^2 \sin(180^\circ - 2\theta)}{g} $$
Since \( \sin(180^\circ - x) = \sin(x) \):
$$ R' = \frac{u^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g} \quad \text{--- (Eq 2)} $$
Comparing (Eq 1) and (Eq 2), \( R = R' \). Thus, for complementary angles \( \theta \) and \( (90^\circ - \theta) \), the horizontal range is the same.

(b) Derivation of \( t_1 t_2 = \frac{2R}{g} \):
Time of flight for first angle (\( \theta \)):
$$ t_1 = \frac{2u \sin\theta}{g} $$
Time of flight for second angle (\( 90^\circ - \theta \)):
$$ t_2 = \frac{2u \sin(90^\circ - \theta)}{g} = \frac{2u \cos\theta}{g} $$
Now, find the product \( t_1 \times t_2 \):
$$ t_1 t_2 = \left( \frac{2u \sin\theta}{g} \right) \left( \frac{2u \cos\theta}{g} \right) = \frac{4u^2 \sin\theta \cos\theta}{g^2} $$
Rearranging to group terms for the Range formula \( R = \frac{u^2 (2\sin\theta\cos\theta)}{g} \):
$$ t_1 t_2 = \frac{2}{g} \left[ \frac{u^2 (2 \sin\theta \cos\theta)}{g} \right] $$ $$ t_1 t_2 = \frac{2}{g} [ R ] $$ $$ t_1 t_2 = \frac{2R}{g} $$
Hence Derived.
