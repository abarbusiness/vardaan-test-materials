ANSWER KEY & DETAILED SOLUTIONS : Minor Test 13

Class: 9 ICSE | Subject: Biology | Topic: Five Kingdom Classification (upto Plantae)

SECTION A (25 Marks) - Multiple Choice Questions
1. The Five-Kingdom classification was proposed by:
[1 Mark]
Ans: (b) Robert Whittaker.
Explanation: R.H. Whittaker proposed the Five-Kingdom classification in 1969 comprising Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalia.
2. Organisms lacking a well-defined nucleus and membrane-bound organelles are grouped under Kingdom:
[1 Mark]
Ans: (b) Monera.
Explanation: Monerans are prokaryotes, meaning their genetic material is not enclosed in a nuclear membrane (nucleoid).
3. The cell wall of fungi is primarily composed of:
[1 Mark]
Ans: (c) Chitin.
Explanation: Fungal cell walls are made of a tough, complex sugar called chitin, unlike plants which have cellulose.
4. Which of the following is an example of a unicellular eukaryote?
[1 Mark]
Ans: (b) Amoeba.
Explanation: Amoeba belongs to Kingdom Protista, which consists of primarily unicellular, eukaryotic organisms.
5. Plants belonging to the division Thallophyta are commonly called:
[1 Mark]
Ans: (c) Algae.
Explanation: Thallophytes have an undifferentiated plant body (thallus) and are mostly aquatic, predominantly known as algae.
6. Which group of plants is known as the 'Amphibians of the Plant Kingdom'?
[1 Mark]
Ans: (b) Bryophytes.
Explanation: They live on land but require water for sexual reproduction (swimming of male gametes to the female organ).
7. The reserve food material in fungi is typically stored as:
[1 Mark]
Ans: (b) Glycogen.
Explanation: Like animals, fungi store their excess food as glycogen, not as starch.
8. Plants bearing 'naked seeds' belong to which division?
[1 Mark]
Ans: (b) Gymnosperms.
Explanation: 'Gymno' means naked and 'sperma' means seed. Their ovules are not enclosed within an ovary wall.
9. Which of the following belongs to Kingdom Monera?
[1 Mark]
Ans: (c) Lactobacillus.
Explanation: Lactobacillus is a bacterium, which is a prokaryote belonging to Monera.
10. The first terrestrial plants to possess vascular tissues (xylem and phloem) are:
[1 Mark]
Ans: (b) Pteridophytes.
Explanation: Pteridophytes (like ferns) are the earliest vascular cryptogams.
11. A defining feature of Angiosperms is:
[1 Mark]
Ans: (b) Seeds enclosed within a fruit.
Explanation: Angiosperms are flowering plants where the ovary ripens into a fruit, enclosing the seeds.
12. Lichen is a symbiotic association between:
[1 Mark]
Ans: (c) Algae and Fungi.
Explanation: The alga prepares food (photosynthesis), and the fungus provides shelter and absorbs minerals/water.
13. Euglena is placed under Kingdom Protista because it is:
[1 Mark]
Ans: (b) Unicellular and eukaryotic.
Explanation: Protista is the kingdom for all unicellular eukaryotes, irrespective of their mode of nutrition.
14. The basic unit of biological classification is:
[1 Mark]
Ans: (c) Species.
Explanation: Species is the lowest and basic unit of taxonomy containing organisms capable of interbreeding.
15. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of Kingdom Plantae?
[1 Mark]
Ans: (b) Heterotrophic nutrition.
Explanation: Plants are characteristically autotrophic (photosynthetic).
16. Cycas and Pinus are examples of:
[1 Mark]
Ans: (c) Gymnosperms.
Explanation: These are non-flowering plants that bear naked seeds in cones.
17. The mode of nutrition in mushrooms and bread moulds is:
[1 Mark]
Ans: (c) Saprophytic.
Explanation: They secrete digestive enzymes onto dead/decaying organic matter and absorb the soluble nutrients.
18. Which division of Plantae has a plant body that is NOT differentiated into true root, stem, and leaves?
[1 Mark]
Ans: (d) Thallophyta.
Explanation: The undifferentiated plant body is called a 'thallus'.
19. Pseudopodia are the locomotory organs of:
[1 Mark]
Ans: (a) Amoeba.
Explanation: Amoeba uses 'false feet' (pseudopodia) for movement and engulfing food.
20. Yeast is distinct from other fungi because it is:
[1 Mark]
Ans: (b) Unicellular.
Explanation: While most fungi are multicellular with hyphae, yeast is uniquely unicellular.
21. The thread-like structures forming the body of a fungus are called:
[1 Mark]
Ans: (a) Hyphae.
Explanation: A network of hyphae forms the mycelium, which is the main body of a fungus.
22. In the binomial nomenclature system, the first name indicates the:
[1 Mark]
Ans: (c) Genus.
Explanation: The scientific name consists of the generic name (starts with a capital letter) and the specific epithet.
23. Which of the following is an example of an Angiosperm?
[1 Mark]
Ans: (b) Mango tree.
Explanation: Mango trees produce flowers and fruits enclosing seeds, characteristic of Angiosperms.
24. Spores in a fern are typically found on the:
[1 Mark]
Ans: (b) Underside of leaves.
Explanation: Ferns produce spores in structures called sporangia, clustered into sori on the lower surface of leaves (fronds).
25. Organisms that survive in extreme environments, like hot springs, belong to which group?
[1 Mark]
Ans: (a) Archaebacteria (Monera).
Explanation: Archaebacteria have unique cell wall components that allow them to endure extreme heat (thermophiles) or salinity.
SECTION B (15 Marks) - Short Answer Type
26. Differentiate between organisms of Kingdom Monera and Kingdom Protista based on their cellular structure. Give one example of each.
[3 Marks]
Ans:
Feature Kingdom Monera Kingdom Protista
Cellular Structure Prokaryotic: Lack a well-defined nucleus (genetic material is a nucleoid) and lack membrane-bound organelles (like mitochondria, chloroplasts). (1 mark) Eukaryotic: Have a true well-defined nucleus enclosed in a nuclear membrane and contain membrane-bound organelles. (1 mark)
Example Bacteria (e.g., Lactobacillus, E. coli) (0.5 mark) Amoeba, Paramecium, Euglena (0.5 mark)
27. State three distinct characteristic features of Kingdom Fungi regarding their cell wall, mode of nutrition, and body structure.
[3 Marks]
Ans: (1 mark for each point)
  1. Cell Wall: They have a rigid cell wall primarily made of a complex sugar called chitin (not cellulose).
  2. Mode of Nutrition: They are heterotrophic, specifically saprophytic or parasitic. They secrete enzymes outside their body to digest dead organic matter and then absorb the nutrients.
  3. Body Structure: The main plant body is mostly multicellular (except yeast) and consists of a network of fine, thread-like structures called hyphae, which together form a mass known as mycelium.
28. Why are Bryophytes referred to as the "amphibians of the plant kingdom"? Explain briefly.
[3 Marks]
Ans: Bryophytes (like mosses and liverworts) are called the "amphibians of the plant kingdom" because:
  • Terrestrial Habitat (1 Mark): They primarily grow on land in moist and shady places, functioning as terrestrial plants for their vegetative growth.
  • Dependency on Water for Reproduction (2 Marks): Just like amphibians in the animal kingdom, bryophytes absolutely require an external layer of water to complete their life cycle. The male gametes (antherozoids) are motile and must swim through water to reach the female reproductive organ (archegonium) for fertilization to occur.
29. What are Gymnosperms? State any two features that differentiate them from Angiosperms.
[3 Marks]
Ans: Gymnosperms are a group of vascular plants that bear seeds which are not enclosed within an ovary wall (i.e., they bear "naked seeds"). (1 Mark)

Differences from Angiosperms: (2 Marks)
Feature Gymnosperms Angiosperms
Seeds Seeds are naked (exposed on cone scales). Seeds are enclosed within a fruit (ripened ovary).
Reproductive Organ Do not produce flowers; bear cones (strobili). Produce true flowers.
30. List three main criteria used by Robert Whittaker to classify organisms into Five Kingdoms.
[3 Marks]
Ans: (1 mark for each valid criterion) Robert Whittaker's Five-Kingdom classification is based on the following key criteria:
  1. Cell Structure: Whether the organism is Prokaryotic (lacking a true nucleus) or Eukaryotic (having a true nucleus).
  2. Body Organization (Thallus organization): Whether the organism is Unicellular (single-celled) or Multicellular (complex body design).
  3. Mode of Nutrition: How the organism obtains food - Autotrophic (photosynthetic), Heterotrophic (saprophytic or holozoic ingestion).
(Note: Reproduction and phylogenetic relationships were also criteria, accepting any three gives full marks.)
SECTION C (10 Marks) - Long Answer Type
31. Explain the classification of Kingdom Plantae into its major divisions. Describe the defining characteristics of Thallophyta, Bryophyta, and Pteridophyta with one example for each.
[5 Marks]
Ans: Kingdom Plantae is broadly classified based on plant body differentiation, presence of vascular tissues, and ability to bear seeds. (0.5 Mark)

1. Thallophyta (Algae): (1.5 Marks)
  • Characteristics: The plant body is not differentiated into true roots, stem, and leaves; it is a simple mass called a 'thallus'. They lack vascular tissues (xylem and phloem). Predominantly aquatic.
  • Example: Spirogyra (or Ulothrix, Chara).
2. Bryophyta: (1.5 Marks)
  • Characteristics: The plant body is differentiated into stem-like and leaf-like structures but lacks true roots; instead, they have thread-like structures called rhizoids for anchorage. They lack specialized vascular tissues. Known as amphibians of the plant kingdom.
  • Example: Moss (Funaria), Riccia (Liverwort).
3. Pteridophyta: (1.5 Marks)
  • Characteristics: The plant body is well-differentiated into true roots, stems, and leaves. They are the first terrestrial plants to possess vascular tissues (xylem for water, phloem for food). They reproduce via spores and do not produce seeds.
  • Example: Ferns (Dryopteris), Horsetails.
32. Describe the general characteristics of Kingdom Monera focusing on their cell structure, shape, and mode of nutrition. How are they economically important to humans? (Give two beneficial roles).
[5 Marks]
Ans: General Characteristics of Monera: (3 Marks)
  • Cell Structure (Prokaryotic): Monerans lack a true, membrane-bound nucleus. The genetic material (DNA) lies naked in the cytoplasm forming a nucleoid. Membrane-bound organelles like mitochondria, chloroplasts, and ER are absent. The cell wall is usually made of peptidoglycan (murein).
  • Shape: Bacteria (the sole members) exist in four basic shapes: spherical (Coccus), rod-shaped (Bacillus), comma-shaped (Vibrio), and spiral (Spirillum).
  • Mode of Nutrition: Highly diverse. They can be autotrophic (photosynthetic or chemosynthetic) synthesizing their own food, or heterotrophic (parasitic, saprophytic, or symbiotic) relying on other organisms or dead organic matter.
Economic Importance (Beneficial Roles): (2 Marks - 1 each)
  1. In Food Industry: Certain bacteria are essential for making dairy products. E.g., Lactobacillus is used to convert milk into curd (fermentation of lactose).
  2. In Agriculture (Nitrogen Fixation): Symbiotic bacteria like Rhizobium found in the root nodules of leguminous plants, or free-living ones like Azotobacter, fix atmospheric nitrogen into nitrates, enriching soil fertility.
