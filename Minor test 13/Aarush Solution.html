SECTION B: Short Answer Type (6 Marks)

26. Define the Barter System. Name any two commodities that were used in ancient times for this exchange.
2 Marks
► Definition: The Barter System was the earliest form of exchange where people traded goods or services directly for other goods and services without the use of money.
► Commodities Used: (Any two) Cowrie shells, salt, tea, tobacco, cloth, cattle, or grains.
[1 Mark for correct definition + \( \frac{1}{2} \) Mark each for two valid commodities]
27. State the main difference between Weather and Climate.
2 Marks
► Weather: It is the condition of the atmosphere at a particular place and a specific, short period of time (e.g., a sunny day or a rainy afternoon).
► Climate: It is the average weather pattern of a region calculated over a long period of time (e.g., tropical, temperate).
[1 Mark for defining Weather accurately + 1 Mark for defining Climate accurately]
28. List any two advantages of using Digital Payments in the modern age.
2 Marks

Advantages of Digital Payments (Any two):

1. Speed & Convenience: Transactions can be done instantly at any time without visiting a bank.
2. High Security: Digital transactions require PINs/passwords and avoid the risk of carrying physical cash that can be stolen.
3. Automated Record-keeping: Every transaction is electronically logged, making tracking easy.
4. Hygiene: Contactless payments require no physical handling of cash.
[1 Mark for each valid advantage explained briefly]

SECTION C: Long Answer Type (9 Marks)

29. Explain any three major problems of the Barter System that led to the invention of money.
3 Marks

The major problems of the Barter System were (any three):

1. Double Coincidence of Wants: Both trading parties must exactly want what the other is offering. E.g., A farmer offering wheat must find someone who has shoes AND wants wheat.
2. No Common Standard Measure of Value: It is very difficult to determine a fair exchange rate between two completely different goods (like a cow and a bag of rice).
3. Problem of Divisibility: Some goods lose their value if divided. You cannot trade half an ox or half a table.
4. Problem of Durability/Storage: Perishable goods like fruits or vegetables spoil quickly and cannot be saved as wealth for the future.
5. Problem of Portability: Heavy goods like cattle or large sacks of grain are difficult to transport over long distances for trade.
[1 Mark for each point properly explained. Max 3 Marks.]
30. Name the five layers of the Earth's atmosphere in order from bottom to top. Which layer protects the Earth by absorbing harmful ultraviolet rays from the Sun?
3 Marks

The five layers of the Earth's atmosphere from bottom to top are:

1. Troposphere
2. Stratosphere
3. Mesosphere
4. Thermosphere
5. Exosphere

Protection from UV Rays:

► The Stratosphere protects the Earth because it contains the ozone layer, which absorbs harmful ultraviolet (UV) rays from the Sun.
[2 Marks for the correct sequence of the 5 layers (deduct \( \frac{1}{2} \) mark for each missing/misplaced layer) + 1 Mark for naming the Stratosphere / Ozone layer]
31. To overcome the limitations of the barter system, society developed money. Briefly explain any three of the four essential functions that money performs.
3 Marks

Money performs the following essential functions (any three):

1. Medium of Exchange: It removes the need for direct bartering. A person can sell goods to anyone for money and use that money to buy what they need from someone else.
2. Store of Value: Unlike perishable goods (fruits, vegetables), money preserves its purchasing power over time. This allows people to safely save their wealth for the future.
3. Standard of Deferred Payment: Money makes credit and loans possible. It allows people to buy today and promise to pay back a specific value in the future, including interest.
4. Measure of Value: Money acts as a common unit (a price tag). It provides a standard way to compare the value of vastly different goods and services easily.
[1 Mark for each correctly identified and explained function. Max 3 Marks.]
