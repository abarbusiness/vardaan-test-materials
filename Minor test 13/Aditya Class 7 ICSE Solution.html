Q26. State any three main characteristics of Kingdom Monera. Give one common example.
[2 + 1 = 3 Marks]
Ans:
Characteristics of Kingdom Monera (Any three - 2 marks):
  • They are primarily \( \text{unicellular} \) organisms.
  • They are \( \text{prokaryotic} \), meaning they lack a true, well-defined nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.
  • Some possess a cell wall (like bacteria), but it is not made of cellulose.
  • They reproduce predominantly asexually through binary fission.
Example (1 mark): Bacteria or Blue-green algae (Cyanobacteria).
Q27. Differentiate between autotrophic and heterotrophic nutrition. Provide one example for each type.
[2 + 1 = 3 Marks]
Ans:
Autotrophic Nutrition (1 Mark) Heterotrophic Nutrition (1 Mark)
It is the mode of nutrition in which organisms synthesize their own food from simple inorganic substances (like \( \text{CO}_2 \) and \( \text{H}_2\text{O} \)) using sunlight. It is the mode of nutrition in which organisms cannot synthesize their own food and depend on other organisms for nourishment.
Examples (1 mark):
\( \text{Autotroph:} \) Mango tree (or any green plant).
\( \text{Heterotroph:} \) Lion (or Amoeba, humans).
Q28. Write a short note on Kingdom Fungi. Ensure you mention its cell wall composition and its primary mode of nutrition.
[3 Marks]
Ans:
Kingdom Fungi includes eukaryotic organisms that can be unicellular (like yeast) or multicellular (like mushrooms). They lack chlorophyll and cannot perform photosynthesis.

Cell Wall (1.5 Marks): Unlike plants, the cell wall of fungi is not made of cellulose; instead, it is made of a tough, complex sugar called chitin.
Mode of Nutrition (1.5 Marks): Their primary mode of nutrition is heterotrophic, specifically saprophytic. They secrete digestive juices on dead and decaying organic matter and absorb the soluble nutrients.
Q29. Define classification. State any two reasons why it is important to classify living organisms.
[1 + 2 = 3 Marks]
Ans:
Definition (1 Mark): The systematic grouping of living organisms into categories based on their similarities, differences, and evolutionary relationships is known as classification.

Importance of Classification (Any two - 2 Marks):
  • It makes the study of a wide variety of organisms systematic, easier, and more organized.
  • It helps us understand the evolutionary paths and interrelationships among different groups of organisms.
  • By studying a few typical members of a group, we can deduce the characteristics of the entire group.
Q30. Classify the following organisms into their respective kingdoms: (a) Amoeba (b) Mushroom (c) Mango tree
[1 + 1 + 1 = 3 Marks]
Ans:
  • (a) Amoeba: Kingdom Protista (1 mark)
  • (b) Mushroom: Kingdom Fungi (1 mark)
  • (c) Mango tree: Kingdom Plantae (1 mark)
