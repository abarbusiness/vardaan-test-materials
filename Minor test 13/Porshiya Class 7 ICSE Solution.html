SECTION B (15 Marks)
26. Define the term 'Excretion'. Why is it strictly necessary for living organisms to excrete waste?
[1 + 2 = 3 Marks]
Answer:
Definition (1 Mark): Excretion is the biological process by which living organisms eliminate toxic, nitrogenous metabolic waste products (like urea, uric acid, and ammonia) produced inside their bodies.

Necessity of Excretion (2 Marks):
It is strictly necessary because:
  • Metabolic wastes are highly toxic. If they accumulate in the body, they act as poison and disrupt normal cellular activities, eventually leading to death. (1 Mark)
  • Excretion helps in maintaining salt and water balance (osmoregulation) inside the body, ensuring homeostasis. (1 Mark)
27. Name the four main organs of the human excretory system. State one primary function of the kidneys.
[2 + 1 = 3 Marks]
Answer:
Four main organs (2 Marks - ½ mark each):
  1. A pair of Kidneys
  2. A pair of Ureters
  3. Urinary bladder
  4. Urethra
Primary Function of Kidneys (1 Mark):
The primary function of the kidneys is to filter out nitrogenous wastes (like urea) and excess salts from the blood to form urine, thereby purifying the blood.
28. Unlike animals, plants do not have specific excretory organs. Mention any three ways by which plants get rid of their waste products.
[1 × 3 = 3 Marks]
Answer:
Plants eliminate their waste products in the following ways (Any three, 1 mark each):
  • Transpiration & Diffusion: Excess water is removed via transpiration through stomata. Gases like Oxygen (during day) and \(CO_2\) (during night) diffuse out through stomata and lenticels.
  • Shedding of Parts: Plants store solid waste in leaves, bark, or fruits, and get rid of these wastes when these parts mature and fall off (shedding of leaves/bark).
  • Secretion of By-products: Certain complex wastes are stored as resins, gums, and latex in older, non-functional xylem tissues or special structures.
  • Root Exudation: Some wastes are excreted directly into the surrounding soil by the roots.

30. Explain briefly the role of the skin and the liver as accessory excretory organs in the human body.
[1.5 + 1.5 = 3 Marks]
Answer:
Role of Skin (1.5 Marks):
The skin contains sweat glands. It helps in excretion by producing sweat, which contains excess water, small amounts of dissolved salts (mostly sodium chloride), and traces of urea. Sweating also provides a cooling effect to the body.

Role of Liver (1.5 Marks):
The liver acts as a critical detoxifying organ. It breaks down excess, toxic amino acids from the blood into a highly toxic gas (Ammonia), and then quickly converts this ammonia into less harmful urea. The blood then carries this urea to the kidneys for final removal. It also breaks down dead red blood cells to form bile pigments.
