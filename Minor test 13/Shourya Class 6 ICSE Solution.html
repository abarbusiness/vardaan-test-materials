Solution Key - Minor Test 13

Class 6 ICSE | Subject: Science (Biology) | Max Marks: 40

SECTION A: Multiple Choice Questions (25 Marks)

Q1. The process of taking food into the body is called: [1 Mark]
Answer: (b) Ingestion
Explanation: Ingestion is the primary step of nutrition where organisms take food into their mouth.
Q2. Which of the following teeth are used for biting and cutting food? [1 Mark]
Answer: (a) Incisors
Explanation: The flat, chisel-shaped front teeth used to slice or cut food are incisors.
Q3. The total number of milk teeth in a human child is: [1 Mark]
Answer: (a) 20
Explanation: Children develop a temporary set of 20 teeth called milk teeth (or deciduous teeth).
Q4. The hardest substance in the human body is: [1 Mark]
Answer: (c) Enamel
Explanation: Enamel forms the outer white covering of the crown of the tooth and is the hardest substance in the body.
Q5. Saliva contains an enzyme that breaks down: [1 Mark]
Answer: (c) Starch
Explanation: Salivary amylase (ptyalin) in saliva begins the digestion of complex carbohydrates (starch) into simpler sugars.
Q6. The tube that connects the mouth to the stomach is known as the: [1 Mark]
Answer: (b) Oesophagus
Explanation: The oesophagus, or food pipe, is a muscular tube leading from the mouth down to the stomach.
Q7. The wavelike movement that pushes food down the food pipe is called: [1 Mark]
Answer: (c) Peristalsis
Explanation: Rhythmic contraction and relaxation of the muscles in the alimentary canal is called peristalsis.
Q8. The stomach is a muscular bag shaped like the letter: [1 Mark]
Answer: (b) J
Explanation: The stomach is a J-shaped, thick-walled bag situated on the left side of the abdomen.
Q9. Which acid is secreted in the stomach to kill bacteria? [1 Mark]
Answer: (c) Hydrochloric acid
Explanation: Gastric glands secrete \(HCl\) which kills harmful germs and provides an acidic medium for digestive enzymes.
Q10. The largest gland in the human body is the: [1 Mark]
Answer: (b) Liver
Explanation: The liver is a reddish-brown gland situated in the upper part of the abdomen on the right side.
Q11. The liver secretes a digestive juice called: [1 Mark]
Answer: (c) Bile
Explanation: Bile is a greenish-yellow fluid that aids in the breakdown of fats.
Q12. Where is bile temporarily stored before it enters the small intestine? [1 Mark]
Answer: (c) Gall bladder
Explanation: The gall bladder is a small sac situated under the liver that stores bile.
Q13. The longest part of the human digestive system is the: [1 Mark]
Answer: (c) Small intestine
Explanation: It is highly coiled and is about 6 to 7.5 meters long in adult humans.
Q14. The tiny, finger-like projections in the small intestine that absorb digested food are called: [1 Mark]
Answer: (a) Villi
Explanation: Villi increase the surface area of the small intestine to maximize absorption of nutrients.
Q15. The complete digestion of food takes place in the: [1 Mark]
Answer: (c) Small intestine
Explanation: Intestinal juice, along with bile and pancreatic juice, completes the digestion of proteins, carbs, and fats here.
Q16. The main function of the large intestine is to absorb: [1 Mark]
Answer: (b) Water
Explanation: It absorbs water and some salts from the undigested food material.
Q17. Undigested semi-solid waste is stored in the: [1 Mark]
Answer: (b) Rectum
Explanation: The last part of the large intestine where faecal matter is temporarily held is the rectum.
Q18. The process of removing undigested solid waste from the body is called: [1 Mark]
Answer: (b) Egestion
Explanation: Egestion is the expulsion of undigested food remains (faeces) through the anus.
Q19. The total number of permanent teeth in an adult human is: [1 Mark]
Answer: (d) 32
Explanation: An adult has 16 teeth in the upper jaw and 16 in the lower jaw. \(\text{Total} = 32\).
Q20. Which teeth are well-developed in flesh-eating animals for tearing meat? [1 Mark]
Answer: (b) Canines
Explanation: Canines are sharp and pointed, designed specifically for piercing and tearing flesh.
Q21. The digestion of proteins begins in the: [1 Mark]
Answer: (c) Stomach
Explanation: Gastric juice contains the enzyme pepsin which starts the breakdown of proteins.
Q22. The sticky, yellowish film of bacteria that forms on uncleaned teeth is called: [1 Mark]
Answer: (c) Plaque
Explanation: Dental plaque is a sticky deposit on teeth in which bacteria proliferate, leading to cavities.
Q23. The part of the tooth embedded inside the gums is the: [1 Mark]
Answer: (b) Root
Explanation: A tooth consists of a crown (visible part above gum), neck, and root (part embedded in the jawbone).
Q24. Which of the following organs does NOT produce any digestive enzyme? [1 Mark]
Answer: (d) Liver
Explanation: The liver produces bile. Bile helps in emulsifying fats but contains NO digestive enzymes.
Q25. Which gland secretes pancreatic juice? [1 Mark]
Answer: (b) Pancreas
Explanation: The pancreas is a leaf-like gland below the stomach that secretes pancreatic juice into the small intestine.

SECTION B: Short Answer Type (6 Marks)

Q26. Define digestion. [2 Marks]
Answer:
Digestion is the biological process by which complex, large, and insoluble food molecules are broken down into simple, small, and soluble absorbable molecules with the help of digestive enzymes.
(1 mark for concept of breaking down food, 1 mark for complex to simple/soluble molecules).
Q27. Why is it important to chew our food properly before swallowing? [2 Marks]
Answer:
It is important to chew our food properly because:
1. Chewing breaks food into smaller pieces, increasing its surface area for enzymes to act upon efficiently.
2. It allows proper mixing of food with saliva, making it soft, slippery, and easy to swallow, while salivary amylase begins the digestion of starch.
(1 mark for breaking into smaller pieces/increasing surface area, 1 mark for mixing with saliva/easy swallowing).
Q28. What is the role of hydrochloric acid (HCl) present in the stomach? Mention any two points. [2 Marks]
Answer:
The roles of Hydrochloric Acid (HCl) in the stomach are:
1. It kills harmful bacteria and germs that may enter the stomach along with the food.
2. It provides an acidic medium which is strictly required for the activation of gastric enzymes (like pepsin) to digest proteins.
(1 mark for killing bacteria, 1 mark for providing acidic medium).

SECTION C: Long Answer Type (9 Marks)

Q29. Name the four different types of teeth found in an adult human. State the main function of any two types. [3 Marks]
Answer:
The four different types of teeth in an adult human are:
1. Incisors
2. Canines
3. Premolars
4. Molars

Functions (Any two):
* Incisors: Used for biting and cutting the food into smaller chunks.
* Canines: Pointed teeth used for piercing and tearing food (especially in a non-vegetarian diet).
* Premolars & Molars: Broad and flat teeth used for crushing and grinding the food.
(1 mark for naming all four correctly; 1 mark each for correctly stating the functions of any two types. Total: 3 marks).
Q30. Write one main function for each of the following parts of the digestive system: (a) Liver, (b) Villi, (c) Saliva. [3 Marks]
Answer:
(a) Liver: It secretes a greenish-yellow juice called bile, which helps in the emulsification (breaking down into tiny droplets) of fats.
(b) Villi: These are tiny finger-like projections in the inner wall of the small intestine that increase the surface area for the rapid absorption of digested food into the blood.
(c) Saliva: It moistens and lubricates the food for easy swallowing, and contains the enzyme amylase which begins the digestion of starch into sugars.
(1 mark for each correct function. Total: 3 marks).
Q31. Differentiate between Ingestion and Egestion (write at least two points of difference). [3 Marks]
Answer:
Feature Ingestion Egestion
Definition The process of taking in food into the body through the mouth is called ingestion. The process of expelling out the undigested and unabsorbed solid waste from the body is called egestion.
Organ Involved It occurs through the Mouth. It occurs through the Anus.
Stage of Digestion It is the very first step of the digestive process. It is the final (last) step of the digestive process.

(1.5 marks for each distinct, correctly matched difference up to 3 marks. Any two correct points acceptable).
