1.Which of the following is a scalar quantity?
[1 Mark]
Answer: C) Speed
Speed depends only on magnitude and path length, not on direction, making it a scalar. Displacement, velocity, and acceleration are all vectors.
2.The slope of a position-time graph gives:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) Velocity
The slope of an x-t graph is $v = \frac{dx}{dt}$, which is the definition of velocity.
3.The area under a velocity-time graph represents:
[1 Mark]
Answer: D) Displacement
The area is calculated as $\int v \, dt$, which yields the displacement of the particle in that time interval.
4.A body starts from rest and moves with a constant acceleration of 2 m/s². The distance covered by it in 3 seconds is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) 9 m
Using the 2nd equation of motion: $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$. Since $u = 0$, $s = \frac{1}{2}(2)(3)^2 = 9 \text{ m}$.
5.A car goes from A to B with speed v₁ and returns from B to A with speed v₂. Its average speed is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: C) 2v₁v₂ / (v₁ + v₂)
Let the distance be $x$. Total distance $= 2x$. Total time $= \frac{x}{v_1} + \frac{x}{v_2}$. Average speed $= \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}} = \frac{2x}{x(\frac{1}{v_1} + \frac{1}{v_2})} = \frac{2v_1v_2}{v_1 + v_2}$.
6.A body is thrown vertically upwards. At the highest point of its path:
[1 Mark]
Answer: C) Velocity is zero but acceleration is non-zero
At maximum height, the body stops momentarily ($v = 0$), but gravity is still pulling it down, so acceleration is $-g \approx -9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$.
7.The ratio of distances fallen by a freely falling body from rest in 1st, 2nd, and 3rd seconds is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) 1 : 3 : 5
Galileo's law of odd numbers: The distance covered during equal intervals of time for a body falling from rest are in the ratio of odd integers: 1 : 3 : 5 : 7...
8.The position x of a particle varies with time t as x = 3t² - 2t + 1. Its velocity at t = 2s is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: A) 10 m/s
Velocity $v = \frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt}(3t^2 - 2t + 1) = 6t - 2$.
At $t = 2\text{ s}$, $v = 6(2) - 2 = 12 - 2 = 10 \text{ m/s}$.
9.If the displacement of a particle is directly proportional to the square of time, its motion is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) Uniform acceleration
If $x \propto t^2$, then $x = kt^2$. Differentiating twice yields acceleration $a = \frac{d^2x}{dt^2} = 2k$, which is a constant.
10.The odometer of a car measures:
[1 Mark]
Answer: C) Distance
An odometer tracks the total path length traveled by a vehicle, regardless of direction, which defines distance.
11.Retardation means:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) Negative acceleration
Retardation implies slowing down, which occurs when acceleration acts in the opposite direction to velocity.
12.A ball is thrown vertically upwards with a velocity of 20 m/s. The maximum height attained is (take g = 10 m/s²):
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) 20 m
Using $v^2 = u^2 - 2gh$. At max height, $v=0$. Thus, $0 = (20)^2 - 2(10)h \Rightarrow 20h = 400 \Rightarrow h = 20 \text{ m}$.
13.The distance traveled in the n-th second of uniformly accelerated motion is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) u + a(2n - 1) / 2
The standard derivation for displacement in the $n^{th}$ second is $S_n = S_n - S_{n-1} = u + \frac{a}{2}(2n - 1)$.
14.The slope of a velocity-time graph gives:
[1 Mark]
Answer: C) Acceleration
Slope $= \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$, which defines the rate of change of velocity, i.e., acceleration.
15.The relative velocity of two bodies moving in opposite directions with speeds u and v is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) u + v
For bodies moving in opposite directions, relative velocity is the sum of their individual speeds: $v_{rel} = u - (-v) = u + v$.
16.A car moving at 72 km/h stops in 10 s after applying brakes. The magnitude of its retardation is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: A) 2 m/s²
First, convert km/h to m/s: $u = 72 \times \frac{5}{18} = 20 \text{ m/s}$. Final velocity $v=0$.
$a = \frac{v-u}{t} = \frac{0-20}{10} = -2 \text{ m/s}^2$. Retardation magnitude is $2 \text{ m/s}^2$.
17.The stopping distance of a vehicle is directly proportional to:
[1 Mark]
Answer: C) Square of initial velocity
Stopping distance $s = \frac{u^2}{2a}$. Thus, $s \propto u^2$.
18.Which of the following graphs cannot possibly represent one-dimensional motion in practice?
[1 Mark]
Answer: A) A circle in x-t graph
A circle in an x-t graph implies two different positions for a single time instance, and that time reverses, which is physically impossible.
19.The area under an acceleration-time graph represents:
[1 Mark]
Answer: A) Change in velocity
Since $a = \frac{dv}{dt}$, the integral $\int a \, dt$ (area) yields $\Delta v$ (change in velocity).
20.Motion under uniform gravity (free fall) is an example of:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) Uniform acceleration
During free fall, the object falls with a constant acceleration of $g$ (approx $9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$).
21.A boy goes to school at 3 km/h and returns at 2 km/h. If he takes 5 hours in all, the distance of the school is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) 6 km
Let distance be $d$. Time going $t_1 = d/3$. Time returning $t_2 = d/2$. Total time $t_1 + t_2 = 5$.
$\frac{d}{3} + \frac{d}{2} = 5 \Rightarrow \frac{5d}{6} = 5 \Rightarrow d = 6 \text{ km}$.
22.The velocity of a particle is v = (10 + 2t²) m/s. Its acceleration at t = 2s is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: C) 8 m/s²
Acceleration $a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt}(10 + 2t^2) = 4t$. At $t = 2\text{ s}$, $a = 4(2) = 8 \text{ m/s}^2$.
23.For a particle moving with constant acceleration, if its velocity changes from u to v over distance S, its velocity at the midpoint of S is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) √((u² + v²) / 2)
Let velocity at midpoint be $v'$. For first half: $v'^2 = u^2 + 2a(\frac{S}{2}) \dots(i)$
For whole journey: $v^2 = u^2 + 2aS \Rightarrow aS = \frac{v^2 - u^2}{2}$.
Substitute in $(i)$: $v'^2 = u^2 + \frac{v^2 - u^2}{2} = \frac{u^2 + v^2}{2}$. Thus, $v' = \sqrt{\frac{u^2 + v^2}{2}}$.
24.The numerical ratio of displacement to distance is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: D) ≤ 1
Distance is the total path length and displacement is the shortest straight line. Hence, $|\text{Displacement}| \le \text{Distance}$, meaning the ratio is at most 1.
25.If the velocity and acceleration of a body have opposite signs, the body is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) Slowing down
When acceleration opposes velocity, it means acceleration acts in the direction opposite to motion, causing the body to decelerate (slow down).
26.A train 100m long is moving at 72 km/h. The time it takes to completely cross a bridge 900m long is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: D) 50 s
Speed $v = 72 \text{ km/h} = 20 \text{ m/s}$. Total distance to cross $= 100 + 900 = 1000 \text{ m}$. Time $t = \frac{1000}{20} = 50 \text{ s}$.
27.The kinematic equation v² = u² + 2as is strictly valid only if:
[1 Mark]
Answer: B) Acceleration is constant
The equations of motion are derived under the assumption that acceleration $a$ does not change with time.
28.If a position-time graph is a straight line parallel to the time axis, the velocity of the body is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: A) Zero
A horizontal x-t graph means position is not changing with time. This implies the body is at rest, hence velocity is zero.
29.A body dropped from the top of a tower reaches the ground in 4 s. The height of the tower is (g = 10 m/s²):
[1 Mark]
Answer: C) 80 m
Since it is dropped, $u=0$. $s = \frac{1}{2}gt^2 = \frac{1}{2}(10)(4)^2 = 5 \times 16 = 80 \text{ m}$.
30.The position x of a particle is given by x = (t - 2)². The total distance covered from t = 0 to t = 4 seconds is:
[1 Mark]
Answer: C) 8 m
Velocity $v = \frac{dx}{dt} = 2(t-2)$. $v$ is $0$ at $t = 2\text{s}$. The particle reverses direction here.
At $t=0$, $x = (0-2)^2 = 4 \text{ m}$.
At $t=2$, $x = (2-2)^2 = 0 \text{ m}$. Distance $d_1 = |0-4| = 4 \text{ m}$.
At $t=4$, $x = (4-2)^2 = 4 \text{ m}$. Distance $d_2 = |4-0| = 4 \text{ m}$.
Total distance $= d_1 + d_2 = 4 + 4 = 8 \text{ m}$.
