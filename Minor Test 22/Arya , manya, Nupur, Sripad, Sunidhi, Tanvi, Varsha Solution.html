DETAILED SOLUTIONS: Our Environment
SECTION A
Q.1: Define the term "ecosystem". Name the two main constituents of an ecosystem.
[2 Marks]
Key Definition: Interacting organisms and non-living constituents.

Solution:
  • Ecosystem: All the interacting organisms in an area together with the non-living constituents of the environment form an ecosystem.
  • Constituents: An ecosystem consists of two main constituents:
    1. Biotic components: Comprising living organisms (plants, animals, microorganisms).
    2. Abiotic components: Comprising physical factors (temperature, rainfall, wind, soil, and minerals).
Q.2: What are trophic levels? Give one example of a food chain comprising four trophic levels.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
  • Trophic Levels: Each step or level of the food chain where transfer of energy occurs is called a trophic level.
  • Example of 4 Trophic Levels:
    Grass (Producer, 1st level) \(\rightarrow\) Insect (Primary Consumer, 2nd level) \(\rightarrow\) Frog (Secondary Consumer, 3rd level) \(\rightarrow\) Snake (Tertiary Consumer, 4th level).
Q.3: Why are green plants and certain bacteria called "producers"?
[2 Marks]
Solution:
Green plants and certain bacteria are called producers because they can make organic compounds like sugar and starch from inorganic substances using the radiant energy of the Sun in the presence of chlorophyll (a process known as photosynthesis). Since they produce food for themselves and directly/indirectly for all other organisms, they are termed producers.
Q.4: State the chemical equations to show what happens when higher energy ultraviolet (UV) radiations act on an oxygen \(\left(O_2\right)\) molecule at the higher levels of the atmosphere.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
The higher energy UV radiations split apart some molecular oxygen \(\left(O_2\right)\) into free oxygen \(\left(O\right)\) atoms. These atoms then combine with the molecular oxygen to form ozone \(\left(O_3\right)\).
$$ O_2 \xrightarrow{\text{UV}} O + O $$ $$ O + O_2 \longrightarrow O_3 \text{ (Ozone)} $$
Q.5: Differentiate between biodegradable and non-biodegradable substances. Give one example of each.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
  • Biodegradable substances: Substances that are easily broken down into simpler inorganic substances by biological processes (action of decomposers/bacteria). Example: Vegetable peels, paper, wood.
  • Non-biodegradable substances: Substances that are not broken down by biological processes. They remain inert and persist in the environment for a long time. Example: Plastics, DDT.
SECTION B
Q.6: What is biological magnification? Explain why the maximum concentration of harmful agricultural chemicals is found in human beings.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
  • Biological Magnification: The phenomenon wherein harmful chemicals (like pesticides) get progressively accumulated in increasing concentrations at each successive trophic level of a food chain is known as biological magnification.
  • Why maximum in humans? These chemicals are non-degradable. They are taken up by plants from the soil/water and enter the food chain. Since human beings occupy the top level in most food chains, the maximum concentration of these harmful chemicals gets accumulated in our bodies over time.
Q.7: "The flow of energy in an ecosystem is unidirectional." Justify this statement with valid reasons.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
The flow of energy is unidirectional for two main reasons:
  1. The energy that is captured by the autotrophs (producers) from the Sun does not revert back to the solar input.
  2. The energy which passes from the autotrophs to the herbivores (primary consumers) does not come back to autotrophs.
As it moves progressively through the various trophic levels, energy is lost as heat to the environment at each level (10% law), meaning it is no longer available to the previous level. It cannot flow in reverse.
Q.8: What is ozone? How does it affect any ecosystem? Mention the primary reason why the amount of ozone in the atmosphere began to drop sharply in the 1980s.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
  • What is it: Ozone \(\left(O_3\right)\) is a molecule formed by three atoms of oxygen. At ground level, it is a deadly poison.
  • Effect on ecosystem: At higher levels of the atmosphere (stratosphere), ozone performs an essential protective function. It shields the surface of the earth from harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation from the Sun, which is highly damaging to organisms (e.g., causing skin cancer).
  • Reason for drop: The sharp drop in the 1980s was linked to the use of synthetic chemicals like chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs), which were heavily used as refrigerants and in fire extinguishers. These chemicals reach the upper atmosphere and destroy ozone molecules.
Q.9: Food chains generally consist of only three or four steps. Give reasons to justify this statement based on the flow of energy.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
According to the 10% law of energy flow, when one organism is eaten by another, only an average of 10% of the food energy is turned into its own body and made available for the next level of consumers. The remaining 90% is lost as heat to the environment, goes into digestion, or is used in doing work.

Because the loss of energy at each step is so immense, very little usable energy remains after three or four trophic levels. Thus, food chains cannot sustain more than three or four steps, as the energy available at higher levels is insufficient to support living organisms.
Q.10: What is the role of decomposers in the ecosystem? What would happen if decomposers are completely removed from an ecosystem?
[3 Marks]
Solution:
  • Role: Decomposers (bacteria and fungi) break down the dead remains and waste products of organisms into simple inorganic substances that go back into the soil and are used up once more by the plants. They help in recycling nutrients.
  • If removed:
    1. Dead bodies of plants and animals, and organic garbage would pile up and never decay.
    2. The natural replenishment of soil nutrients would stop. Consequently, plants would eventually die due to nutrient deficiency, causing the entire ecosystem to collapse.
SECTION C
Q.11: (a) Draw a labeled diagram showing the flow of energy in an ecosystem. (b) Calculate the energy available to the secondary consumer if plants capture 10,000 J of solar energy.
[5 Marks]
Solution:
(a) Flow of Energy Diagram [2 Marks]
A block diagram flowing bottom to top:
\(\uparrow\) Top Carnivores
\(\uparrow\) Carnivores
\(\uparrow\) Herbivores
\(\uparrow\) Producers
\(\uparrow\) Sunlight
(Note: The flow is unidirectional, depicted by one-way arrows).

(b) Numerical Calculation [3 Marks]
Food chain: Sun \(\rightarrow\) Producer \(\rightarrow\) Primary Consumer \(\rightarrow\) Secondary Consumer
  • Solar Energy = \(10,000 \text{ J}\)
  • Producer level: Green plants capture about 1% of the energy of sunlight that falls on their leaves.
    Energy trapped by producer = \(1\% \text{ of } 10,000 \text{ J} = 100 \text{ J}\).
  • Primary Consumer level: According to the 10% law, only 10% is passed to the next level.
    Energy available = \(10\% \text{ of } 100 \text{ J} = 10 \text{ J}\).
  • Secondary Consumer level: 10% of primary consumer's energy.
    Energy available = \(10\% \text{ of } 10 \text{ J} = 1 \text{ J}\).
Final Answer: The energy available to the secondary consumer is 1 J.
Q.12: (a) List any three problems caused by the non-biodegradable wastes that we generate. (b) Suggest any two methods you can adopt in your daily life to help in reducing the problem of waste disposal.
[5 Marks]
Solution:
(a) Problems caused by non-biodegradable wastes: [3 Marks]
  1. They remain inert in the environment for hundreds of years, causing severe soil and water pollution.
  2. Harmful chemicals from wastes (like pesticides/plastics) enter the food chain, leading to biological magnification and severe health hazards in top consumers (like humans).
  3. Improper disposal methods (like burning plastics) release highly toxic gases into the atmosphere, causing air pollution and destroying the ozone layer (e.g., CFCs).
(b) Methods to reduce waste disposal problems: [2 Marks]
  1. Recycling: Collect and recycle items like paper, glass, and some plastics instead of throwing them in the general garbage.
  2. Using reusable items: Replacing disposable plastic bags/cups with reusable cloth bags and washable cups/utensils (e.g., adopting the practice of carrying a cloth bag while shopping).
  3. Segregation: Segregating biodegradable kitchen waste (to make compost) from non-biodegradable waste at home.
Q.13: (a) Will the impact of removing all the organisms in a trophic level be different for different trophic levels? Can the organisms of any trophic level be removed without causing any damage to the ecosystem? (b) Construct a simple food web.
[5 Marks]
Solution:
(a) Impact of removing organisms: [3 Marks]
  • Yes, the impact will be different for different trophic levels. For example, removing all producers (plants) will cause immediate collapse of the entire ecosystem as no energy will enter the system. Removing primary consumers (herbivores) will lead to starvation of carnivores and overgrowth of plants. Removing top carnivores may lead to an explosive population increase in herbivores, which will overgraze and eventually destroy the producers.
  • No, the organisms of any trophic level cannot be removed without causing damage. Every level is interdependent. Removing any single level breaks the food chain, creates imbalance, and negatively impacts both the lower and higher trophic levels.
(b) Food Web: [2 Marks]
A food web consists of many interconnected food chains. Example in a terrestrial/forest ecosystem:
  • Chain 1: Grass \(\rightarrow\) Grasshopper \(\rightarrow\) Frog \(\rightarrow\) Snake \(\rightarrow\) Hawk
  • Chain 2: Grass \(\rightarrow\) Rabbit \(\rightarrow\) Hawk
  • Chain 3: Grass \(\rightarrow\) Mouse \(\rightarrow\) Snake \(\rightarrow\) Hawk
  • Chain 4: Trees \(\rightarrow\) Deer \(\rightarrow\) Tiger
(Interconnections: The Hawk can feed on the Snake, Rabbit, or Mouse. The Snake can feed on the Mouse or Frog, thus linking multiple chains.)
