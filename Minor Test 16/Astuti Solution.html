SECTION A
Q1. Express the following as percentages: (a) \( \frac{3}{8} \) (b) \( 0.45 \)
[2 Marks]
Concept: To convert a fraction or decimal to a percentage, multiply by 100 and add the % sign.
(a) \( \frac{3}{8} \) as a percentage:
\[ = \frac{3}{8} \times 100\% \]
\[ = \frac{300}{8}\% = 37.5\% \] [1 Mark]
(b) \( 0.45 \) as a percentage:
\[ = 0.45 \times 100\% \]
\[ = 45\% \] [1 Mark]
Q2. Convert \( 12\frac{1}{2}\% \) into a fraction in its simplest form.
[2 Marks]
Concept: To convert percentage to a fraction, divide by 100 and simplify.
First, convert mixed fraction to an improper fraction:
\( 12\frac{1}{2}\% = \frac{25}{2}\% \) [0.5 Mark]
Now, divide by 100 (which means multiplying the denominator by 100):
\[ = \frac{25}{2 \times 100} \]
\[ = \frac{25}{200} \] [1 Mark]
Simplifying the fraction (dividing numerator and denominator by 25):
\[ = \frac{1}{8} \]
Ans: \( \frac{1}{8} \) [0.5 Mark]
Q3. Find the value of \( 15\% \) of \( \text{Rs. } 400 \).
[2 Marks]
Concept: "Percentage of" means multiplying the fraction representation of the percentage by the quantity.
Value = \( \frac{15}{100} \times 400 \) [1 Mark]
\[ = 15 \times 4 \]
\[ = 60 \]
Ans: Rs. 60 [1 Mark]
Q4. What percentage is \( 45 \) minutes of \( 2 \) hours?
[2 Marks]
Concept: Both quantities must be in the same unit before forming a fraction to convert to a percentage.
Convert 2 hours to minutes:
\( 2 \text{ hours} = 2 \times 60 = 120 \text{ minutes} \) [0.5 Mark]
Required Percentage = \( \frac{\text{First Quantity}}{\text{Second Quantity}} \times 100\% \)
\[ = \frac{45}{120} \times 100\% \] [0.5 Mark]
Simplifying: \( \frac{45}{120} = \frac{3}{8} \)
\[ = \frac{3}{8} \times 100\% = 37.5\% \]
Ans: 37.5% [1 Mark]
Q5. Find the value of \( x \) if \( 30\% \) of \( x \) is \( 120 \).
[2 Marks]
Concept: Finding the total given a percentage part. Form an equation.
According to the question:
\[ \frac{30}{100} \times x = 120 \] [1 Mark]
Solving for \( x \):
\[ x = \frac{120 \times 100}{30} \]
\[ x = 4 \times 100 = 400 \]
Ans: \( x = 400 \) [1 Mark]
SECTION B
Q6. Out of \( 800 \) students in a school, \( 45\% \) are girls. Find the total number of boys in the school.
[3 Marks]
Method 1: Find percentage of boys directly. Total is always 100%.
Total percentage of students = \( 100\% \)
Percentage of boys = \( 100\% - 45\% = 55\% \) [1 Mark]
Number of boys = \( 55\% \text{ of } 800 \)
\[ = \frac{55}{100} \times 800 \] [1 Mark]
\[ = 55 \times 8 = 440 \]
Ans: There are 440 boys in the school. [1 Mark]

(Alternate Method: Find number of girls = 45% of 800 = 360. Boys = 800 - 360 = 440. Full marks awarded if solved this way).
Q7. A man saves \( 18\% \) of his monthly income. If his monthly savings amount to \( \text{Rs. } 3600 \), calculate his total monthly income.
[3 Marks]
Concept: Establishing relation between the percentage given and its actual value.
Let the total monthly income be \( x \).
Savings = \( 18\% \) of income = \( \text{Rs. } 3600 \)
Therefore, \( \frac{18}{100} \times x = 3600 \) [1.5 Marks]
\[ x = \frac{3600 \times 100}{18} \]
\[ x = 200 \times 100 \]
\[ x = 20000 \] [1 Mark]
Ans: His total monthly income is Rs. 20,000. [0.5 Mark]
Q8. In an orchard, \( 20\% \) of the trees are mango trees, \( 35\% \) are apple trees, and the remaining are orange trees. Find the percentage of orange trees. If the total number of trees is \( 400 \), find the exact number of orange trees.
[3 Marks]
Part 1: Percentage of Orange Trees
Total percentage = \( 100\% \)
Percentage of mango + apple trees = \( 20\% + 35\% = 55\% \) [0.5 Mark]
Percentage of orange trees = \( 100\% - 55\% = 45\% \) [1 Mark]

Part 2: Number of Orange Trees
Total number of trees = 400
Number of orange trees = \( 45\% \text{ of } 400 \)
\[ = \frac{45}{100} \times 400 \] [0.5 Mark]
\[ = 45 \times 4 = 180 \]
Ans: There are 45% orange trees, which equals 180 trees. [1 Mark]
Q9. Rahul scored \( 36 \) marks out of \( 60 \) in his Mathematics test and \( 40 \) marks out of \( 50 \) in his Science test. In which subject did he perform better conceptually (in terms of percentage)?
[3 Marks]
Concept: Comparing performance with different max marks requires converting scores to percentages to form a standard base.
Mathematics:
Percentage scored = \( \left( \frac{36}{60} \right) \times 100\% \)
\[ = 6 \times 10\% = 60\% \] [1 Mark]
Science:
Percentage scored = \( \left( \frac{40}{50} \right) \times 100\% \)
\[ = 40 \times 2\% = 80\% \] [1 Mark]
Comparing the two: \( 80\% > 60\% \)
Ans: Rahul performed better in Science. [1 Mark]
Q10. The cost of a bicycle was \( \text{Rs. } 4500 \). Its price is increased by \( 12\% \). What is the new increased price of the bicycle?
[3 Marks]
Concept: New Price = Original Price + Increase amount
Original Price = \( \text{Rs. } 4500 \)
Increase amount = \( 12\% \text{ of } 4500 \)
\[ = \frac{12}{100} \times 4500 \] [1 Mark]
\[ = 12 \times 45 = \text{Rs. } 540 \] [1 Mark]
New Price = Original Price + Increase
\[ = 4500 + 540 \]
Ans: The new price of the bicycle is Rs. 5040. [1 Mark]
SECTION C
Q11. Mr. Sharma's monthly salary is \( \text{Rs. } 50,000 \). He spends \( 30\% \) of his salary on food, \( 20\% \) on house rent, \( 15\% \) on the education of his children, and saves the rest. Find: (a) The percentage of his salary that he saves. (b) His total monthly savings in Rupees. (c) The amount spent strictly on food.
[5 Marks]
Part (a): Percentage of Savings
Total Percentage = 100%
Total expenditure % = \( 30\% \text{ (Food)} + 20\% \text{ (Rent)} + 15\% \text{ (Edu)} = 65\% \) [1 Mark]
Savings percentage = \( 100\% - 65\% = 35\% \) [1 Mark]
Ans (a): He saves 35% of his salary.

Part (b): Monthly Savings (Rupees)
Total Salary = Rs. 50,000
Savings Amount = \( 35\% \text{ of } 50,000 \)
\[ = \frac{35}{100} \times 50000 \]
\[ = 35 \times 500 = \text{Rs. } 17,500 \] [1.5 Marks]
Ans (b): His total savings is Rs. 17,500.

Part (c): Amount spent on Food
Expenditure on food = \( 30\% \text{ of } 50,000 \)
\[ = \frac{30}{100} \times 50000 \]
\[ = 30 \times 500 = \text{Rs. } 15,000 \] [1.5 Marks]
Ans (c): Amount spent on food is Rs. 15,000.
Q12. In an examination, a candidate must secure \( 40\% \) marks to pass. A student gets \( 140 \) marks and fails by \( 20 \) marks. Find the maximum marks of the examination.
[5 Marks]
Concept: The passing marks are absolute. Find the absolute passing marks first, then equate it to the percentage.
Marks obtained by the student = 140
Failed by = 20 marks.
This means if the student got 20 more marks, they would have exactly reached the passing mark.
Passing Marks = Marks Obtained + Failed By
\[ \text{Passing Marks} = 140 + 20 = 160 \] [2 Marks]
Let the maximum marks of the exam be \( x \).
We are given that a candidate must secure 40% of the maximum marks to pass.
Therefore, \( 40\% \text{ of } x = 160 \) [1 Mark]
\[ \frac{40}{100} \times x = 160 \]
\[ x = \frac{160 \times 100}{40} \] [1 Mark]
\[ x = 4 \times 100 = 400 \]
Ans: The maximum marks of the examination is 400. [1 Mark]
Q13. An alloy consists of \( 40\% \) copper, \( 35\% \) zinc, and the rest is nickel. Find the weight of copper, zinc, and nickel separately in \( 2.4 \text{ kg} \) of the alloy. (Give your final answers in grams).
[5 Marks]
Concept: Convert the total quantity into the requested units (grams) first to make calculations simpler.
Step 1: Conversion and Percentage setup
Total weight of alloy = \( 2.4 \text{ kg} \)
Convert to grams: \( 2.4 \times 1000 = 2400 \text{ grams} \) [1 Mark]
Percentage of Nickel = \( 100\% - (40\% + 35\%) = 100\% - 75\% = 25\% \) [1 Mark]

Step 2: Find weights
Weight of Copper = \( 40\% \text{ of } 2400 \text{ g} \)
\[ = \frac{40}{100} \times 2400 = 40 \times 24 = 960 \text{ g} \] [1 Mark]
Weight of Zinc = \( 35\% \text{ of } 2400 \text{ g} \)
\[ = \frac{35}{100} \times 2400 = 35 \times 24 = 840 \text{ g} \] [1 Mark]
Weight of Nickel = \( 25\% \text{ of } 2400 \text{ g} \)
\[ = \frac{25}{100} \times 2400 = 25 \times 24 = 600 \text{ g} \] [1 Mark]
(Check: 960 + 840 + 600 = 2400g. Calculation is correct).
Ans: Copper = 960 g, Zinc = 840 g, Nickel = 600 g.
