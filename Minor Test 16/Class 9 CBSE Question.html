Minor Test 16 (Surprise Test)
Class: 9 CBSE Subject: Science Time: 1 Hour Max. Marks: 40
General Instructions:
  • All questions are compulsory.
  • The paper is divided into three sections: A, B, and C.
  • Section A consists of 5 questions of 2 marks each.
  • Section B consists of 5 questions of 3 marks each.
  • Section C consists of 3 questions of 5 marks each.
  • Write your answers neatly and legibly. Draw diagrams wherever necessary.
SECTION A (10 Marks)(Attempt all questions - 2 Marks Each)
1.
Define a mixture. Give one example each of a homogeneous and a heterogeneous mixture.
2.
A student shines a beam of light through a true solution of sugar and a colloidal solution of milk. What will be her observation in both cases? Give a reason for your answer.
3.
Identify the solute and the solvent in the following mixtures:
(a) Tincture of iodine
(b) Aerated drinks (like soda water)
4.
Name the physical separation technique you would use to separate:
(a) Butter from curd
(b) Iron pins mixed in sand
5.
What is an emulsion? Give one practical example of an emulsion from daily life.
SECTION B (15 Marks)(Short Answer Type - 3 Marks Each)
6.
Differentiate between a true solution, a suspension, and a colloid on the basis of:
(a) Particle size
(b) Stability (settling of particles)
(c) Appearance (transparency)
7.
A solution contains \( 40 \text{ g} \) of common salt dissolved in \( 320 \text{ g} \) of water. Calculate the concentration in terms of mass by mass percentage of the solution.
8.
State the principle used in the process of chromatography. Mention any two important applications of chromatography in real life.
9.
Air is a homogeneous mixture of gases. How can we obtain different gases (like Oxygen, Argon, Nitrogen) from liquid air? Draw a simple flow diagram to illustrate the sequential steps.
10.
Two miscible liquids 'A' and 'B' have boiling points that differ by more than \( 25 \text{ K} \).
(a) Name the specific technique used to separate them.
(b) State the underlying principle of this separation technique.
SECTION C (15 Marks)(Long Answer Type - 5 Marks Each)
11.
(a) What is the Tyndall effect? Which of the following will show the Tyndall effect: Salt solution, Milk, Copper sulphate solution, Starch solution? (2 Marks)
(b) Classify the following changes as chemical or physical changes with a brief reason for each:
(i) Rusting of an almirah
(ii) Melting of ice to form water
(iii) Burning of paper (3 Marks)
12.
A student is given a mixture containing common salt (sodium chloride) and ammonium chloride.
(a) Name the property of ammonium chloride that allows it to be separated from salt. (1 Mark)
(b) Describe the step-by-step method used to separate this mixture. (2 Marks)
(c) Draw a neat, labelled diagram of the experimental setup used for this separation. (2 Marks)
13.
(a) State any three major differences between a mixture and a chemical compound. (3 Marks)
(b) You are provided with a complex mixture consisting of sand, iron filings, and common salt. Describe a logical, step-by-step sequence of physical separation techniques you would employ to separate and recover all three components perfectly. (2 Marks)
