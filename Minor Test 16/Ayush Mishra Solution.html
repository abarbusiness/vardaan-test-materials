SECTION A — OBJECTIVE & VSA (10 MARKS)
Q1. Definition of \(\pi\) (Pi) Total: 1 Mark
The mathematical constant \(\pi\) (Pi) is defined as the ratio of which two properties of a circle?
Answer: (b) Circumference to Diameter [1 Mark for correct option]
Key Concept: \(\pi = \dfrac{C}{D} = \dfrac{C}{2r}\). It is a universal constant for all circles, approximately equal to \(\frac{22}{7}\) or \(3.14159...\)
Q2. Arc Length Formula Total: 1 Mark
The length \(l\) of an arc of a circle subtending angle \(\theta^\circ\) at the center...
Answer: (c) \(\dfrac{\theta}{360^\circ} \times 2\pi r\) [1 Mark for correct option]
Key Concept: A full circular turn is \(360^\circ\) with perimeter \(2\pi r\). A fraction of \(\theta^\circ\) covers arc length \(l = \dfrac{\theta}{360^\circ} \times 2\pi r\).
Q3. Finding radius from Circumference Total: 1 Mark
If circumference is \(44\text{ cm}\), what is the radius?
Answer: (b) \(7\text{ cm}\) [1 Mark for correct answer]
Calculation:
\(C = 2\pi r \implies 44 = 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times r \implies 44 = \dfrac{44}{7} r \implies r = 7\text{ cm}\).
Q4. Cyclic Quadrilateral Area Formula Total: 1 Mark
Which formula represents area of a cyclic quadrilateral with sides \(a, b, c, d\)?
Answer: (b) Brahmagupta's Formula [1 Mark for correct option]
Formula: \(\text{Area} = \sqrt{(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)(s-d)}\) where \(s = \dfrac{a+b+c+d}{2}\).
Q5. Perimeter of Semicircle Total: 1 Mark
Total perimeter of a semicircle including bounding diameter...
Answer: (c) \(\pi r + 2r\) [1 Mark for correct option]
Breakdown: Semicircular curved arc \(= \pi r\); Straight diameter boundary \(= 2r\). Total Perimeter \(= \pi r + 2r = r(\pi + 2)\).
Q6. Assertion-Reason: Triangles on same base and between same parallels Total: 1 Mark
Assertion: Triangles on same base & parallels are equal in area...
Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A. [1 Mark]
Q7. Assertion-Reason: Heron's formula as special case of Brahmagupta's Total: 1 Mark
Assertion: Heron's formula is special case of Brahmagupta's...
Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A. [1 Mark]
Q8. Sector Area Calculation Total: 1 Mark
Area of sector with \(r = 7\text{ cm}\) and \(\theta = 60^\circ\):
Answer: \(\dfrac{77}{3}\text{ cm}^2 \approx 25.67\text{ cm}^2\)
Steps:
\[\text{Area} = \dfrac{\theta}{360^\circ} \times \pi r^2 = \dfrac{60^\circ}{360^\circ} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 7^2 = \dfrac{1}{6} \times 22 \times 7 = \dfrac{154}{6} = \dfrac{77}{3}\text{ cm}^2\] [0.5 mark formula + 0.5 mark calculation = 1 Mark]
Q9. Angle subtended by Minute Hand Total: 1 Mark
Angle subtended by minute hand in \(10\text{ minutes}\):
Answer: \(60^\circ\)
Steps: In \(60\text{ minutes}\), minute hand completes \(360^\circ\).
In \(10\text{ minutes}\), angle subtended \(= \dfrac{10}{60} \times 360^\circ = 60^\circ\). [1 Mark]
Q10. Diagonal of Parallelogram Total: 1 Mark
Relationship between areas of two triangles formed by diagonal of a parallelogram:
Answer: They are equal in area (\(\text{Area}_1 = \text{Area}_2\)). A diagonal divides a parallelogram into two congruent, equal-area triangles. [1 Mark]
SECTION B — SHORT ANSWER TYPE I (10 MARKS)
Q11. Total Perimeter of Sector (\(r = 14\text{ cm}, \theta = 75^\circ\)) Total: 2 Marks
Find the total perimeter of a sector of radius \(14\text{ cm}\) with sector angle \(75^\circ\).
Answer:
1. Arc Length (\(l\)): \[l = \dfrac{\theta}{360^\circ} \times 2\pi r = \dfrac{75^\circ}{360^\circ} \times 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 14\] \[l = \dfrac{5}{24} \times 88 = \dfrac{55}{3}\text{ cm} \approx 18.33\text{ cm}\] 2. Total Sector Perimeter (\(P\)): \[P = l + 2r = \dfrac{55}{3} + 2(14) = \dfrac{55}{3} + 28 = \dfrac{55 + 84}{3} = \dfrac{139}{3}\text{ cm} \approx 46.33\text{ cm}\] [1 Mark arc length + 1 Mark total perimeter = 2 Marks]
Q12. Proof of Equal Areas of \(\triangle PCD\) and \(\triangle QCD\) Total: 2 Marks
Parallelogram \(ABCD\) with points \(P, Q\) on side \(AB\). Show \(\text{Area}(\triangle PCD) = \text{Area}(\triangle QCD)\).
Answer:
1. Both \(\triangle PCD\) and \(\triangle QCD\) lie on the same base \(CD\).
2. Since \(ABCD\) is a parallelogram, the opposite side \(AB\) is parallel to \(CD\) (\(AB \parallel CD\)).
3. Points \(P\) and \(Q\) lie on line \(AB\), so both triangles lie between the same parallel lines \(AB\) and \(CD\).
4. Therefore, the perpendicular height \(h\) from line \(AB\) to base \(CD\) is identical for both triangles.
\[\text{Area}(\triangle PCD) = \dfrac{1}{2} \times CD \times h = \text{Area}(\triangle QCD)\] [1 Mark stating same base & parallel height + 1 Mark conclusion = 2 Marks]
Q13. Area Swept by Wiper Blade Total: 2 Marks
Blade length \(r = 28\text{ cm}\), angle \(\theta = 120^\circ\). Find area cleaned.
Answer:
The area swept by one wiper blade is the area of a sector:
\[\text{Area} = \dfrac{\theta}{360^\circ} \times \pi r^2 = \dfrac{120^\circ}{360^\circ} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 28^2\] \[\text{Area} = \dfrac{1}{3} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 784 = \dfrac{1}{3} \times 22 \times 112 = \dfrac{2464}{3}\text{ cm}^2 \approx 821.33\text{ cm}^2\] [1 Mark formula substitution + 1 Mark final calculation = 2 Marks]
Q14. Area of a Rhombus Total: 2 Marks
Diagonals \(d_1 = 12\text{ cm}, d_2 = 16\text{ cm}\). Calculate area.
Answer:
1. Formula: \(\text{Area of Rhombus} = \dfrac{1}{2} \times d_1 \times d_2\)
2. Calculation: \[\text{Area} = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 12 \times 16 = 6 \times 16 = 96\text{ cm}^2\] [0.5 Mark formula + 1.5 Marks calculation & units = 2 Marks]
Q15. Area of Quadrant given Circumference \(44\text{ cm}\) Total: 2 Marks
Find area of a quadrant of a circle whose circumference is \(44\text{ cm}\).
Answer:
1. Find radius \(r\): \[2\pi r = 44 \implies 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times r = 44 \implies r = 7\text{ cm}\] 2. Area of Quadrant (\(\frac{1}{4}\) circle): \[\text{Area} = \dfrac{1}{4} \pi r^2 = \dfrac{1}{4} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 7^2 = \dfrac{154}{4} = \dfrac{77}{2}\text{ cm}^2 = 38.5\text{ cm}^2\] [1 Mark radius derivation + 1 Mark quadrant area = 2 Marks]
SECTION C — SHORT ANSWER TYPE II (15 MARKS)
Q16. Triangular Field Area & Height (Heron's Formula) Total: 3 Marks
Sides \(a = 13\text{ m}, b = 14\text{ m}, c = 15\text{ m}\). Find area and height to side \(15\text{ m}\).
Answer:
(a) Heron's Formula Area Calculation:
Semi-perimeter \(s = \dfrac{13 + 14 + 15}{2} = \dfrac{42}{2} = 21\text{ m}\).
\[\text{Area} = \sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)} = \sqrt{21 \times (21-13) \times (21-14) \times (21-15)}\] \[\text{Area} = \sqrt{21 \times 8 \times 7 \times 6} = \sqrt{(7 \times 3) \times (2^3) \times 7 \times (2 \times 3)}\] \[\text{Area} = \sqrt{7^2 \times 3^2 \times 2^4} = 7 \times 3 \times 4 = 84\text{ m}^2\]
(b) Height to side \(c = 15\text{ m}\):
\[\text{Area} = \dfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times h \implies 84 = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 15 \times h \implies h = \dfrac{168}{15} = 11.2\text{ m}\] [2 Marks Heron's area + 1 Mark height calculation = 3 Marks]
Q17. 400m Track Stagger Calculation Total: 3 Marks
Lane 1 radius \(r_1 = 36.8\text{ m}\), Lane width \(w = 1.22\text{ m}\). Find radius and stagger.
Answer:
(a) Effective Radius of Lane 2 (\(r_2\)):
\[r_2 = r_1 + \text{lane width} = 36.8 + 1.22 = 38.02\text{ m}\]
(b) Stagger Distance for one full circular loop (2 semicircles = 1 circle):
Difference in circumference between Lane 2 and Lane 1: \[\text{Stagger} = 2\pi r_2 - 2\pi r_1 = 2\pi (r_2 - r_1) = 2\pi w\] \[\text{Stagger} = 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 1.22 = \dfrac{53.68}{7} \approx 7.67\text{ m}\] [1 Mark radius + 2 Marks stagger formula & derivation = 3 Marks]
Q18. Sector and Minor Segment Area (\(r = 10\text{ cm}, \theta = 90^\circ\)) Total: 3 Marks
Find area of minor sector and minor segment with \(\pi = 3.14\).
Answer:
(a) Area of Minor Sector: \[\text{Area}_{\text{sector}} = \dfrac{90^\circ}{360^\circ} \times \pi r^2 = \dfrac{1}{4} \times 3.14 \times 10^2 = \dfrac{314}{4} = 78.5\text{ cm}^2\]
(b) Area of Triangle formed by radii (\(\angle = 90^\circ\)): \[\text{Area}_{\text{triangle}} = \dfrac{1}{2} \times r \times r \times \sin 90^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 10 = 50\text{ cm}^2\]
(c) Area of Minor Segment: \[\text{Area}_{\text{segment}} = \text{Area}_{\text{sector}} - \text{Area}_{\text{triangle}} = 78.5 - 50 = 28.5\text{ cm}^2\] [1 Mark sector area + 1 Mark triangle area + 1 Mark segment area = 3 Marks]
Q19. Brahmagupta's Formula for Cyclic Quadrilateral Total: 3 Marks
Sides \(a = 3, b = 4, c = 5, d = 6\text{ cm}\). Calculate area.
Answer:
1. Semi-perimeter \(s\): \[s = \dfrac{3 + 4 + 5 + 6}{2} = \dfrac{18}{2} = 9\text{ cm}\] 2. Brahmagupta's Formula: \[\text{Area} = \sqrt{(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)(s-d)}\] \[\text{Area} = \sqrt{(9-3)(9-4)(9-5)(9-6)} = \sqrt{6 \times 5 \times 4 \times 3}\] \[\text{Area} = \sqrt{360} = \sqrt{36 \times 10} = 6\sqrt{10}\text{ cm}^2 \approx 18.97\text{ cm}^2\] [1 Mark semi-perimeter + 1 Mark formula substitution + 1 Mark final radical/decimal value = 3 Marks]
Q20. Inscribed Equilateral Triangle Area Ratio Total: 3 Marks
Show that ratio of area of inscribed equilateral triangle to circle is \(\dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}\).
Answer:
(a) For an equilateral triangle inscribed in a circle of radius \(r\), the circumradius \(r = \dfrac{\text{side}}{\sqrt{3}} \implies \text{side } a = \sqrt{3}r\).
(b) Area of Equilateral Triangle: \[\text{Area}_{\text{triangle}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} a^2 = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} (\sqrt{3}r)^2 = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} (3r^2) = \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{4} r^2\] (c) Area of Circle: \(\text{Area}_{\text{circle}} = \pi r^2\).
(d) Ratio: \[\text{Ratio} = \dfrac{\text{Area}_{\text{triangle}}}{\text{Area}_{\text{circle}}} = \dfrac{\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4} r^2}{\pi r^2} = \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{4\pi} \approx 0.413\] [1 Mark side derivation + 1 Mark triangle area + 1 Mark ratio derivation = 3 Marks]
SECTION D — LONG ANSWER TYPE (15 MARKS)
Q21. Median Area Bisector Proof & Parallelogram Point on Diagonal Total: 5 Marks
(a) Prove median bisects triangle area. (b) Prove \(\text{Area}(\triangle PSO) = \text{Area}(\triangle PQO)\).
Answer:
Part (a): Proof that a median bisects triangle area [2.5 Marks]
Let \(\triangle ABC\) have median \(AD\) drawn to base \(BC\). So \(BD = DC\).
Draw perpendicular altitude \(AN \perp BC\) from vertex \(A\).
\[\text{Area}(\triangle ABD) = \dfrac{1}{2} \times BD \times AN\] \[\text{Area}(\triangle ADC) = \dfrac{1}{2} \times DC \times AN\] Since \(BD = DC\), \(\text{Area}(\triangle ABD) = \text{Area}(\triangle ADC)\). Hence proved.

Part (b): Proof for Parallelogram \(PQRS\) with point \(O\) on diagonal \(PR\) [2.5 Marks]
1. The diagonal \(PR\) bisects the parallelogram into two congruent triangles of equal area: \[\text{Area}(\triangle PSR) = \text{Area}(\triangle PQR)\] 2. Perpendicular heights \(h_1\) (from \(S\) to line \(PR\)) and \(h_2\) (from \(Q\) to line \(PR\)) are equal (\(h_1 = h_2\)) because \(PR\) passes through center of symmetry of parallelogram.
3. For \(\triangle PSO\) and \(\triangle PQO\), base is the common segment \(PO\) on diagonal \(PR\): \[\text{Area}(\triangle PSO) = \dfrac{1}{2} \times PO \times h_1\] \[\text{Area}(\triangle PQO) = \dfrac{1}{2} \times PO \times h_2\] Since \(h_1 = h_2\), \(\text{Area}(\triangle PSO) = \text{Area}(\triangle PQO)\). Hence proved. [2.5 Marks part (a) + 2.5 Marks part (b) = 5 Marks]
Q22. Garden Boundary Perimeter & Sector/Segment Calculations Total: 5 Marks
(a) Decorative garden boundary perimeter. (b) Sector, arc & segment area for \(r = 21\text{ cm}, \theta = 60^\circ\).
Answer:
Part (a): Garden Boundary Outer Perimeter [2 Marks]
Each side of square \(= 14\text{ cm}\). Four semicircles are built on the 4 sides as diameters.
Diameter \(d = 14\text{ cm} \implies \text{radius } r = 7\text{ cm}\).
Outer perimeter consists of 4 semicircular arcs:
\[\text{Perimeter} = 4 \times (\pi r) = 4 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 7 = 88\text{ cm}\]
Part (b): Circle calculations for \(r = 21\text{ cm}, \theta = 60^\circ\) [3 Marks]
(i) Arc Length (\(l\)): \[l = \dfrac{60^\circ}{360^\circ} \times 2\pi r = \dfrac{1}{6} \times 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 21 = 22\text{ cm}\] (ii) Area of Sector: \[\text{Area}_{\text{sector}} = \dfrac{60^\circ}{360^\circ} \times \pi r^2 = \dfrac{1}{6} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 21^2 = \dfrac{1}{6} \times 22 \times 63 = 231\text{ cm}^2\] (iii) Area of Minor Segment:
The triangle formed by two radii of length \(21\text{ cm}\) with included angle \(60^\circ\) is an equilateral triangle with side \(21\text{ cm}\). \[\text{Area}_{\text{triangle}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \times 21^2 = \dfrac{1.732}{4} \times 441 \approx 0.433 \times 441 \approx 190.95\text{ cm}^2\] \[\text{Area}_{\text{segment}} = \text{Area}_{\text{sector}} - \text{Area}_{\text{triangle}} = 231 - 190.95 = 40.05\text{ cm}^2\] [2 Marks part (a) + 3 Marks part (b) = 5 Marks]
Q23. Squaring a Rectangle Concept & Quadrilateral Park Area Total: 5 Marks
(a) Geometric squaring of rectangle. (b) Quadrilateral \(ABCD\) area with \(\angle C = 90^\circ\).
Answer:
Part (a): Squaring a Rectangle Concept [2 Marks]
1. "Squaring a rectangle" is a classical geometric construction to convert a rectangle of sides \(a\) and \(b\) into a square of identical surface area.
2. Since \(\text{Area of Rectangle} = a \times b\) and \(\text{Area of Square} = x^2\), setting areas equal gives: \[x^2 = a \cdot b \implies x = \sqrt{a \cdot b}\] The side \(x\) of the square is the geometric mean of the dimensions \(a\) and \(b\).

Part (b): Quadrilateral Park Area Calculation [3 Marks]
In quadrilateral \(ABCD\), \(\angle C = 90^\circ\), \(BC = 12\text{ m}, CD = 5\text{ m}, AB = 9\text{ m}, AD = 8\text{ m}\).
Join diagonal \(BD\) to divide quadrilateral into two triangles: \(\triangle BCD\) and \(\triangle ABD\).

1. Area and hypotenuse of right triangle \(\triangle BCD\): \[\text{Area}(\triangle BCD) = \dfrac{1}{2} \times BC \times CD = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 12 \times 5 = 30\text{ m}^2\] By Pythagoras theorem in \(\triangle BCD\): \[BD = \sqrt{BC^2 + CD^2} = \sqrt{12^2 + 5^2} = \sqrt{144 + 25} = \sqrt{169} = 13\text{ m}\]
2. Area of \(\triangle ABD\) using Heron's Formula (sides \(9\text{ m}, 8\text{ m}, 13\text{ m}\)): \[s = \dfrac{9 + 8 + 13}{2} = 15\text{ m}\] \[\text{Area}(\triangle ABD) = \sqrt{15(15-9)(15-8)(15-13)} = \sqrt{15 \times 6 \times 7 \times 2}\] \[\text{Area}(\triangle ABD) = \sqrt{1260} = 6\sqrt{35} \approx 6 \times 5.916 = 35.5\text{ m}^2\]
3. Total Area of Park \(ABCD\): \[\text{Total Area} = \text{Area}(\triangle BCD) + \text{Area}(\triangle ABD) = 30 + 35.5 = 65.5\text{ m}^2\] [2 Marks part (a) + 3 Marks part (b) = 5 Marks]
