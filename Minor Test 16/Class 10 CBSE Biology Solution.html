Q13. Sensory Neurons vs Motor Neurons Total: 2 Marks
Distinguish between the functional roles of sensory neurons and motor neurons.
Answer:
1. Sensory Neurons: Transmit nerve impulses from sense organs/receptors toward the Central Nervous System (Spinal Cord / Brain).
2. Motor Neurons: Transmit nerve signals/commands from the Central Nervous System away to the effector organs (muscles or glands) to produce a response. [1 Mark for Sensory + 1 Mark for Motor = 2 Marks]
Q14. Insulin: Gland, Function, and Deficiency Total: 2 Marks
(a) State the gland that produces Insulin. (b) What is its physiological function and deficiency disease?
Answer:
(a) Gland: Pancreas (Islets of Langerhans).
(b) Function: Regulates blood glucose level by promoting glucose uptake into cells and conversion into glycogen.
• Deficiency Disease: Diabetes Mellitus (leads to elevated sugar levels in blood and urine). [0.5 Mark gland + 1 Mark function + 0.5 Mark disease = 2 Marks]
Q15. Mechanism of Phototropism by Auxin Total: 2 Marks
Explain briefly how auxin hormone causes a plant shoot tip to bend towards light.
Answer:
1. Auxin hormone synthesized at the shoot tip is light-sensitive and diffuses toward the shaded side of the shoot when unidirectional light strikes.
2. Higher concentration of auxin on the shaded side stimulates faster cell elongation there compared to the illuminated side. This differential growth causes the shoot stem to bend towards light (positive phototropism). [1 Mark for shade side diffusion + 1 Mark for cell elongation mechanism = 2 Marks]

Q18. Feedback Mechanism of Blood Sugar Regulation Total: 3 Marks
Explain "Feedback Mechanism" of hormone regulation using insulin as an example.
Answer:
1. Definition: Feedback mechanism is a regulatory mechanism in which the timing and amount of hormone secreted by a gland is directly controlled by the level of its target substance in the blood.
2. Example (Pancreatic Insulin Regulation):
• High Sugar Signal: When blood sugar level rises after meals, it is detected by the \(\beta\)-cells of the Pancreas.
• Response: Pancreas responds by secreting more Insulin into the bloodstream, lowering sugar levels.
• Feedback Stop: As blood sugar falls back to normal range, the signal to secrete insulin is turned off, preventing hypoglycemia. [1 Mark definition + 2 Marks detailed step-by-step example = 3 Marks]

SECTION D — LONG ANSWER TYPE (15 MARKS)
Q21. Protection of CNS, Comparison of Systems & Ductless Glands Total: 5 Marks
(a) Protection of Brain & Spinal Cord (b) Nervous vs Hormonal comparison (c) Ductless glands concept
Answer:
(a) Protection of Central Nervous System:
• Human Brain: Enclosed inside a bony protective box called the Cranium (Skull). Inside the cranium, it is suspended in a fluid called Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF) contained within meninges membranes, which acts as a liquid shock absorber.
• Spinal Cord: Encased inside a bony, flexible column called the Vertebral Column (Backbone).

(b) Nervous Control vs Chemical Control Comparison:
1. Speed: Electrical impulses travel almost instantaneously (milliseconds); chemical hormones diffuse through blood relatively slowly.
2. Target Specificity: Nerve connections target specific individual cells/muscles; hormones spread widely in body fluids to affect all cells having compatible receptors.
3. Duration: Nervous response is short-lived and stops immediately; hormonal response persists over longer periods.

(c) Ductless Glands:
Endocrine glands lack excretory ducts to carry secretions. They release their synthesized hormones directly into surrounding capillary blood capillaries for transport to target organs. Hence termed "ductless glands". [2 Marks for CNS protection + 2 Marks comparison + 1 Mark ductless definition = 5 Marks]

When a nerve impulse reaches the axonal terminal attached to a muscle fiber:
1. Axon ends release acetylcholine chemical into the junction gap.
2. Binding of acetylcholine to muscle cell membrane causes shift of cellular ions, altering muscle protein configurations.
3. Muscle cells contract, creating physical movement.

(c) Evolutionary Significance of Reflex Arcs:
In early animals, the complex neural network of the brain was relatively slow to process incoming sensory data. Reflex arcs evolved in the spinal cord as quick, efficient emergency shortcuts to enable survival responses against life-threatening stimuli (like extreme heat/sharp pain) without waiting for cerebral conscious processing.
