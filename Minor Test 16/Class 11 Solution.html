DETAILED SOLUTIONS: Minor Test 16 (Chemical Bonding)
SECTION A (1 Mark Each)
Q1. Draw the Lewis dot structure of the carbonate ion, \( \text{CO}_3^{2-} \).
[1 Mark]
Central C double bonded to one O, single bonded to two O's.

Structure: The Carbon (C) atom is at the center, sharing 4 electrons (double bond) with one Oxygen atom, and 2 electrons each (single bonds) with the other two Oxygen atoms. The single-bonded oxygens carry a negative charge each to complete their octet. (Total valence electrons = 4 + 18 + 2 = 24e⁻).
Q2. Identify the molecule that has a zero dipole moment: \( \text{NH}_3 \), \( \text{NF}_3 \), \( \text{BF}_3 \), or \( \text{H}_2\text{O} \).
[1 Mark]
\( \text{BF}_3 \) (Boron Trifluoride)

Explanation: \( \text{BF}_3 \) has a highly symmetrical trigonal planar geometry. The three individual B-F bond dipoles are at 120° to each other and cancel out perfectly, resulting in a net zero dipole moment.
Q3. State the essential condition for the formation of a hydrogen bond.
[1 Mark]
Presence of a highly electronegative atom (F, O, or N) covalently bonded to a Hydrogen atom.
Q4. Comparing \( \text{O}_2 \) and \( \text{O}_2^+ \), which species has a shorter bond length?
[1 Mark]
\( \text{O}_2^+ \)

Explanation: The bond order of \( \text{O}_2 \) is 2.0. In \( \text{O}_2^+ \), an electron is removed from the antibonding \( \pi^* \) orbital, which increases its bond order to 2.5. Higher bond order implies a stronger and shorter bond.
Q5. How many sigma and pi bonds are present in ethyne (\( \text{C}_2\text{H}_2 \))?
[1 Mark]
3 Sigma (\( \sigma \)) bonds and 2 Pi (\( \pi \)) bonds.

Ethyne structure is \( \text{H-C}\equiv\text{C-H} \). The triple bond consists of 1 \( \sigma \) and 2 \( \pi \) bonds. The two C-H bonds are \( \sigma \) bonds.

Q7. What is the shape of the \( \text{ClF}_3 \) molecule according to VSEPR theory?
[1 Mark]
T-shaped

It has 3 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs on the central Chlorine atom (sp³d hybridization). The lone pairs occupy equatorial positions to minimize repulsion, resulting in a bent T-shape.
Q8. Which out of o-nitrophenol and p-nitrophenol has a higher boiling point and why?
[1 Mark]
p-nitrophenol

It forms intermolecular hydrogen bonds leading to molecular association. o-nitrophenol forms intramolecular hydrogen bonds (chelation), reducing its ability to interact with other molecules, hence a lower boiling point.
Q9. Write the state of hybridization of carbon in methane and ethene.
[1 Mark]
Methane (\( \text{CH}_4 \)): \( \text{sp}^3 \) | Ethene (\( \text{C}_2\text{H}_4 \)): \( \text{sp}^2 \)
Q10. Define the term 'Bond Order' in MOT.
[1 Mark]
Bond Order = \( \frac{1}{2} (N_b - N_a) \)

It is defined as half of the difference between the number of electrons in bonding molecular orbitals (\( N_b \)) and antibonding molecular orbitals (\( N_a \)).
SECTION B (2 Marks Each)
Q11. Calculate the formal charge on the central oxygen atom in an ozone (\( \text{O}_3 \)) molecule.
[2 Marks]
Formula: \( \text{Formal Charge} = [\text{Total valence } e^-] - [\text{Total non-bonding } e^-] - \frac{1}{2}[\text{Total bonding } e^-] \)
For the central Oxygen atom (bonded to one O with a double bond and the other with a single dative bond):
Valence \( e^- \) of Oxygen = 6
Non-bonding \( e^- \) (lone pairs) = 2
Bonding \( e^- \) = 6 (3 bonds total: 1 double + 1 single)
\( \text{Formal Charge} = 6 - 2 - \frac{1}{2}(6) = 6 - 2 - 3 = +1 \)
Formal Charge = +1
Q12. Explain why \( \text{H}_2\text{O} \) is a liquid but \( \text{H}_2\text{S} \) is a gas.
[2 Marks]
Oxygen is highly electronegative and small in size, which allows \( \text{H}_2\text{O} \) molecules to form strong, extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonds. This molecular association requires high energy to break, making water a liquid.
Sulfur is larger and less electronegative; thus, \( \text{H}_2\text{S} \) molecules cannot form hydrogen bonds. They are held together only by weak Van der Waals forces, making it a gas at room temperature.
Q13. Predict the shape and hybridization of \( \text{XeF}_4 \).
[2 Marks]
Xenon has 8 valence electrons. It forms 4 single bonds with 4 Fluorine atoms, using 4 electrons. The remaining 4 electrons form 2 lone pairs.
Total electron pairs = 4 Bond Pairs + 2 Lone Pairs = 6.
Hybridization: \( \text{sp}^3\text{d}^2 \)
Shape: To minimize repulsion, the 2 lone pairs occupy axial positions (180° apart). The 4 Fluorine atoms occupy the equatorial positions, resulting in a Square Planar shape.
Q14. Although \( \text{NH}_3 \) and \( \text{H}_2\text{O} \) have sp³ hybridization, their bond angles differ. Explain.
[2 Marks]
Both have an sp³ tetrahedral electron geometry (ideal angle 109.5°). However:
- \( \text{NH}_3 \) has 1 lone pair. Repulsion between lone pair and bond pair (lp-bp) is greater than bp-bp, compressing the angle to 107°.
- \( \text{H}_2\text{O} \) has 2 lone pairs. The lp-lp repulsion is much stronger than lp-bp and bp-bp repulsions, compressing the angle even further to 104.5°.
Q15. Give two main differences between Sigma and Pi bonds.
[2 Marks]
1. Formation: A Sigma (\( \sigma \)) bond is formed by the head-on (axial) overlapping of atomic orbitals. A Pi (\( \pi \)) bond is formed by the lateral (sideways) overlapping of parallel orbitals.
2. Strength/Rotation: A Sigma bond is stronger due to a larger extent of overlapping, and free rotation is possible around it. A Pi bond is weaker, and it restricts free rotation around the bond axis.
SECTION C (3 Marks Each)
Q16. Why does \( \text{NH}_3 \) have a higher dipole moment than \( \text{NF}_3 \)?
[3 Marks]
Dipole moment is the product of the magnitude of the charge and the distance of separation between the charges (\( \mu = Q \times r \)). It is a vector quantity.
Both molecules have a pyramidal shape with one lone pair.
In \( \text{NH}_3 \), Nitrogen is more electronegative than Hydrogen. The N-H bond dipoles point towards Nitrogen. The orbital dipole of the lone pair is also in the same upward direction. Thus, all dipoles add up, giving a high net dipole moment.
In \( \text{NF}_3 \), Fluorine is more electronegative than Nitrogen. The N-F bond dipoles point downward (away from N), while the lone pair orbital dipole points upward. The dipoles oppose and cancel each other out partially, resulting in a much lower net dipole moment.
Q17. Hybridization in \( \text{PCl}_5 \) and length of axial bonds.
[3 Marks]
Hybridization: The process of intermixing of atomic orbitals of slightly different energies to produce a new set of equivalent orbitals (hybrid orbitals) is called hybridization.
In \( \text{PCl}_5 \), Phosphorus undergoes \( \text{sp}^3\text{d} \) hybridization, yielding a trigonal bipyramidal geometry.
Axial vs Equatorial: There are 3 equatorial P-Cl bonds (at 120° to each other) and 2 axial P-Cl bonds (at 90° to the equatorial plane). The axial bond pairs suffer greater repulsive interaction from the three equatorial bond pairs. To minimize this repulsion, the axial bonds elongate, becoming longer and weaker than the equatorial bonds.
Q18. MOT configuration of \( \text{O}_2 \), Bond Order, and Paramagnetism.
[3 Marks]
Oxygen atom has 8 electrons; \( \text{O}_2 \) molecule has 16 electrons.
MO Configuration:
\( \sigma 1s^2, \sigma^* 1s^2, \sigma 2s^2, \sigma^* 2s^2, \sigma 2p_z^2, (\pi 2p_x^2 = \pi 2p_y^2), (\pi^* 2p_x^1 = \pi^* 2p_y^1) \)
Bond Order:
\( N_b \) (bonding electrons) = 10
\( N_a \) (antibonding electrons) = 6
\( \text{Bond Order} = \frac{10 - 6}{2} = 2 \)
Magnetic property: The molecule is paramagnetic because the highest occupied molecular orbitals (\( \pi^* 2p_x \) and \( \pi^* 2p_y \)) contain two unpaired electrons.
Q19. Postulates of VSEPR theory and shape of \( \text{BrF}_5 \).
[3 Marks]
Postulates of VSEPR (Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion) Theory:
  • The shape of a molecule depends upon the number of valence shell electron pairs (bonded or non-bonded) around the central atom.
  • Pairs of electrons in the valence shell repel one another.
  • These pairs tend to occupy positions in space that minimize repulsion and maximize distance between them.
  • Order of repulsion: Lone Pair - Lone Pair (lp-lp) > Lone Pair - Bond Pair (lp-bp) > Bond Pair - Bond Pair (bp-bp).
Shape of \( \text{BrF}_5 \): Central Bromine atom has 7 valence electrons. It forms 5 single bonds with Fluorine and has 1 lone pair. Total pairs = 6 (sp³d² hybridization).
Geometry is octahedral, but due to one lone pair at the bottom, the molecular shape is Square Pyramidal.
Q20. Resonance in Carbonate ion (\( \text{CO}_3^{2-} \)).
[3 Marks]
Resonance: It is the phenomenon where a single Lewis structure cannot completely describe a molecule's properties. The molecule is instead represented by multiple contributing structures (resonance structures), and the actual structure is a hybrid of all of them.
In \( \text{CO}_3^{2-} \): The ion can be represented by three equivalent Lewis structures where the double bond shifts between the central carbon and the three oxygen atoms.
Because the double bond character is distributed equally among all three C-O bonds via delocalization of pi electrons, all three C-O bonds are experimentally found to be perfectly identical in length, lying between a standard single and double bond length.
SECTION D (5 Marks Each)
Q21. (a) Inter/Intra H-bonding. (b) Shape of \( \text{SF}_4 \), \( \text{PH}_3 \). (c) Zero dipole of \( \text{BeCl}_2 \).
[5 Marks]
(a) Hydrogen Bonding:
- Intermolecular H-bonding: Formed between two different molecules of the same or different compounds. Example: \( \text{H}_2\text{O} \) molecules, HF molecules.
- Intramolecular H-bonding: Formed when hydrogen atom is between two highly electronegative atoms present within the same molecule. Example: o-nitrophenol.

(b) Shapes via VSEPR:
- \( \text{SF}_4 \): Sulfur has 6 valence electrons. 4 bond pairs + 1 lone pair = 5 pairs (sp³d). Shape is See-Saw (lone pair occupies equatorial position).
- \( \text{PH}_3 \): Phosphorus has 5 valence electrons. 3 bond pairs + 1 lone pair = 4 pairs (sp³). Shape is Trigonal Pyramidal.

(c) Dipole of \( \text{BeCl}_2 \):
\( \text{BeCl}_2 \) undergoes sp hybridization, leading to a perfectly linear geometry (Cl-Be-Cl angle is 180°). The two Be-Cl bond dipoles are equal in magnitude but perfectly opposite in direction, canceling each other out to give a net zero dipole moment.
Q22. MOT, bonding vs antibonding, \( \text{N}_2 \), \( \text{N}_2^+ \), \( \text{N}_2^- \) stability.
[5 Marks]
(a) MOT & Orbitals:
- Bonding MOs: Formed by the addition (constructive interference) of atomic orbitals. They have lower energy and higher stability than the combining atomic orbitals. Electron density is concentrated between nuclei.
- Antibonding MOs: Formed by subtraction (destructive interference) of atomic orbitals. They have higher energy and lower stability. They possess a nodal plane between the nuclei.

(b) Configurations (for Nitrogen up to 14 e⁻, \( \pi 2p \) is below \( \sigma 2p_z \)):
- \( \text{N}_2 \) (14 e⁻): \( \sigma 1s^2, \sigma^* 1s^2, \sigma 2s^2, \sigma^* 2s^2, (\pi 2p_x^2 = \pi 2p_y^2), \sigma 2p_z^2 \)
- \( \text{N}_2^+ \) (13 e⁻): Electron removed from bonding \( \sigma 2p_z \).
- \( \text{N}_2^- \) (15 e⁻): Electron added to antibonding \( \pi^* 2p \).

(c) Bond Orders and Stability:
- BO of \( \text{N}_2 \) = \( \frac{10 - 4}{2} = 3.0 \)
- BO of \( \text{N}_2^+ \) = \( \frac{9 - 4}{2} = 2.5 \)
- BO of \( \text{N}_2^- \) = \( \frac{10 - 5}{2} = 2.5 \)
Stability order: \( \text{N}_2 \) is the most stable. Between \( \text{N}_2^+ \) and \( \text{N}_2^- \), \( \text{N}_2^+ \) is slightly more stable because \( \text{N}_2^- \) has more electrons in antibonding orbitals causing greater destabilization.
Order: \( \text{N}_2 > \text{N}_2^+ > \text{N}_2^- \).
Q23. Lattice enthalpy, Fajan's rules, Octet rule & exceptions.
[5 Marks]
(a) Lattice Enthalpy: The energy required to completely separate one mole of a solid ionic compound into its gaseous constituent ions. Higher lattice enthalpy means stronger electrostatic forces between ions, leading to a more highly stable ionic compound.

(b) Fajan's Rules: They predict the covalent character in an ionic bond. High covalent character is favored by:
1. Small size of cation, 2. Large size of anion, 3. High charge on either ion.
Why AgCl is covalent: The \( \text{Ag}^+ \) ion has a pseudo-noble gas configuration (\( \text{d}^{10} \)), giving it a much higher polarizing power compared to the \( \text{Na}^+ \) ion which has a noble gas configuration (\( \text{s}^2\text{p}^6 \)). Thus, \( \text{Ag}^+ \) heavily polarizes the \( \text{Cl}^- \) electron cloud, causing significant electron sharing (covalent character).

(c) Octet Rule: Atoms tend to combine either by transfer of electrons or by sharing of electrons in such a way that they complete an octet (8 electrons) in their valence shells to attain stability.
Expanded Octet Exceptions (more than 8 e⁻):
1. \( \text{PF}_5 \) (Phosphorus has 10 valence electrons).
2. \( \text{SF}_6 \) (Sulfur has 12 valence electrons).
