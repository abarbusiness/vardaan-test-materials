SECTION A
Q1. If the cost of 15 identical articles is ₹ 450, find the cost of 24 such articles.
[2 Marks]
Concept: Direct Variation (More articles = More cost)
Cost of 15 articles = ₹ 450
Cost of 1 article = \( \frac{450}{15} = \text{₹ } 30 \) [1 Mark]
Cost of 24 articles = \( 30 \times 24 \)
\[ = 720 \]
Ans: The cost of 24 articles is ₹ 720. [1 Mark]
Q2. If 12 men can complete a piece of work in 8 days, in how many days will 6 men complete the same work?
[2 Marks]
Concept: Inverse Variation (Less men = More days)
Days taken by 12 men = 8 days
Days taken by 1 man = \( 12 \times 8 = 96 \text{ days} \) [1 Mark]
Days taken by 6 men = \( \frac{96}{6} \)
\[ = 16 \]
Ans: 6 men will complete the work in 16 days. [1 Mark]
Q3. A car takes 4 hours to reach a destination travelling at a speed of 60 km/hr. How long will it take to reach the same destination if it travels at 80 km/hr?
[2 Marks]
Concept: Inverse Variation (Higher speed = Less time for same distance)
Total Distance = Speed × Time
Distance = \( 60 \times 4 = 240 \text{ km} \) [1 Mark]
New Time = \( \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{New Speed}} \)
\[ = \frac{240}{80} = 3 \text{ hours} \]
Ans: The car will take 3 hours. [1 Mark]
Q4. A printing machine prints 1500 pages in 5 minutes. How many pages will it print in 12 minutes?
[2 Marks]
Concept: Direct Variation
Pages printed in 5 minutes = 1500
Pages printed in 1 minute = \( \frac{1500}{5} = 300 \text{ pages} \) [1 Mark]
Pages printed in 12 minutes = \( 300 \times 12 \)
\[ = 3600 \]
Ans: The machine will print 3600 pages. [1 Mark]
Q5. A military garrison of 120 men has provisions for 30 days. How long will the provisions last if the garrison size increases to 150 men?
[2 Marks]
Concept: Inverse Variation
Total provision (man-days) = \( 120 \times 30 = 3600 \text{ man-days} \) [1 Mark]
Duration for 150 men = \( \frac{\text{Total Provision}}{\text{New number of men}} \)
\[ = \frac{3600}{150} = 24 \]
Ans: The provisions will last for 24 days. [1 Mark]
SECTION B
Q6. A can do a piece of work in 10 days and B can do the same work in 15 days. How long will they take to finish the work if they work together?
[3 Marks]
Concept: Time and Work (Unitary approach per day)
Work done by A in 1 day = \( \frac{1}{10} \) [0.5 Mark]
Work done by B in 1 day = \( \frac{1}{15} \) [0.5 Mark]
Work done by (A + B) in 1 day = \( \frac{1}{10} + \frac{1}{15} \)
\[ = \frac{3 + 2}{30} = \frac{5}{30} \] [1 Mark]
\[ = \frac{1}{6} \]
Since they do \( \frac{1}{6} \text{th} \) of the work in 1 day, total time taken = \( 6 \) days.
Ans: Working together, they will finish the work in 6 days. [1 Mark]
Q7. If 8 women can weave 120 meters of cloth in a day, how many meters of cloth can 12 women weave in a day working at the same rate?
[3 Marks]
Concept: Direct Variation
Cloth woven by 8 women = 120 meters
Cloth woven by 1 woman = \( \frac{120}{8} \) [1 Mark]
\[ = 15 \text{ meters} \] [1 Mark]
Cloth woven by 12 women = \( 15 \times 12 \)
\[ = 180 \text{ meters} \]
Ans: 12 women can weave 180 meters of cloth. [1 Mark]
Q8. A scout camp has enough food for 200 students for 20 days. After 5 days, 50 students leave the camp. How long will the remaining food last for the remaining students?
[3 Marks]
Concept: Inverse Variation (Adjusting base after a time lapse)
Total initial provision is for 200 students for 20 days.
After 5 days, remaining food is sufficient for 200 students for \( (20 - 5) = 15 \text{ days} \). [1 Mark]
Total students remaining = \( 200 - 50 = 150 \text{ students} \).
Let the remaining food last for \( x \) days.
By inverse variation: \( 200 \times 15 = 150 \times x \) [1 Mark]
\[ x = \frac{200 \times 15}{150} \]
\[ x = 20 \]
Ans: The remaining food will last for 20 days. [1 Mark]
Q9. Tap A can fill an empty tank in 4 hours, while Tap B can empty the full tank in 6 hours. If both taps are opened simultaneously, in how many hours will the empty tank be filled?
[3 Marks]
Concept: Pipes and Cisterns (Filling vs Emptying)
Part of tank filled by Tap A in 1 hour = \( \frac{1}{4} \) [0.5 Mark]
Part of tank emptied by Tap B in 1 hour = \( \frac{1}{6} \) [0.5 Mark]
Net part of tank filled in 1 hour = \( \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{6} \) [1 Mark]
\[ = \frac{3 - 2}{12} = \frac{1}{12} \]
Since \( \frac{1}{12} \text{th} \) of the tank is filled in 1 hour, total time to fill = 12 hours.
Ans: The tank will be filled in 12 hours. [1 Mark]
Q10. A wheel makes 360 revolutions in 1 minute. How many revolutions does it make in 45 seconds?
[3 Marks]
Concept: Direct Variation (Unit conversion necessary)
1 minute = 60 seconds [0.5 Mark]
Revolutions in 60 seconds = 360
Revolutions in 1 second = \( \frac{360}{60} = 6 \) [1.5 Marks]
Revolutions in 45 seconds = \( 6 \times 45 \)
\[ = 270 \]
Ans: The wheel makes 270 revolutions in 45 seconds. [1 Mark]
SECTION C
Q11. A and B can do a piece of work in 12 days and 18 days respectively. They begin working together, but A leaves after 4 days.
(a) Find the fraction of work completed in the first 4 days.
(b) How many days will B take to finish the remaining work alone?
[5 Marks]
Part (a): Work done in first 4 days
A's 1 day work = \( \frac{1}{12} \); B's 1 day work = \( \frac{1}{18} \)
(A + B)'s 1 day work = \( \frac{1}{12} + \frac{1}{18} \)
\[ = \frac{3 + 2}{36} = \frac{5}{36} \] [1 Mark]
Work done by (A + B) in 4 days = \( 4 \times \frac{5}{36} = \frac{20}{36} = \frac{5}{9} \) [1.5 Marks]
Ans (a): \( \frac{5}{9} \) of the work is completed.

Part (b): Remaining Work by B
Remaining work = \( 1 - \frac{5}{9} = \frac{4}{9} \) [1 Mark]
Time taken by B to finish remaining work = \( \frac{\text{Remaining Work}}{\text{B's 1 day work}} \)
\[ = \frac{\frac{4}{9}}{\frac{1}{18}} \]
\[ = \frac{4}{9} \times \frac{18}{1} = 4 \times 2 = 8 \text{ days} \] [1.5 Marks]
Ans (b): B will take 8 days to finish the remaining work.
Q12. Working 8 hours a day, 45 men can build a wall in 16 days.
(a) Express the total work required in terms of "man-hours".
(b) How many men are required to build the same wall in 20 days if they work 6 hours a day?
[5 Marks]
Part (a): Total man-hours
Total work (man-hours) = Men × Days × Hours/Day
\[ = 45 \times 16 \times 8 \] [1 Mark]
\[ = 5760 \text{ man-hours} \] [1.5 Marks]
Ans (a): Total work is 5760 man-hours.

Part (b): Men required for new conditions
Let the number of men required be \( M \).
According to the chain rule, the total man-hours must remain constant for the same wall.
New man-hours = \( M \times 20 \times 6 \)
\[ M \times 120 = 5760 \] [1.5 Marks]
\[ M = \frac{5760}{120} = 48 \]
Ans (b): 48 men are required. [1 Mark]
Q13. A train travelling at a uniform speed of 72 km/hr takes 15 seconds to cross a stationary telegraph pole.
(i) Calculate the speed of the train in m/s.
(ii) Find the length of the train.
(iii) How much time will the same train take to cross a platform which is 200 meters long?
[5 Marks]
Part (i): Convert Speed
Speed = 72 km/hr
To convert to m/s, multiply by \( \frac{5}{18} \).
\[ = 72 \times \frac{5}{18} = 20 \text{ m/s} \] [1 Mark]
Ans (i): 20 m/s

Part (ii): Length of the train
When crossing a pole, Distance travelled = Length of train.
Distance = Speed × Time
\[ = 20 \text{ m/s} \times 15 \text{ s} \] [1 Mark]
\[ = 300 \text{ meters} \] [1 Mark]
Ans (ii): 300 meters

Part (iii): Time to cross platform
When crossing a platform, Total Distance = Length of Train + Length of Platform
Total Distance = \( 300 + 200 = 500 \text{ meters} \) [1 Mark]
Time = \( \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Speed}} \)
\[ = \frac{500}{20} \]
\[ = 25 \text{ seconds} \] [1 Mark]
Ans (iii): 25 seconds
