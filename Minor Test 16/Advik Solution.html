Detailed Marking Scheme & Solutions
SECTION A (10 Marks)
1.
Define an 'atom'. Name the central, dense part of an atom.
[2]
Solution: Atom: An atom is the smallest invisible particle of an element that can take part in a chemical reaction and may or may not exist independently. (1 Mark)
The central, dense part of an atom is called the Nucleus. (1 Mark)
2.
Name the three fundamental subatomic particles present in an atom and state their respective electrical charges.
[2]
Solution: The three fundamental subatomic particles are:
  • Protons: Positively charged (+1)
  • Electrons: Negatively charged (-1)
  • Neutrons: Neutral (No charge / 0)
(1 Mark for naming all three, 1 Mark for correct charges)
3.
What is meant by the 'Atomic Number' of an element? Write the symbol used to denote it.
[2]
Solution: Atomic Number: It is defined as the total number of protons present in the nucleus of an atom of that element. (1 Mark)
It is denoted by the symbol Z. (1 Mark)
4.
If an atom contains 6 protons and 6 neutrons, what is its mass number?
[2]
Solution:
\[ \text{Mass Number (A)} = \text{Number of Protons} + \text{Number of Neutrons} \] \[ \text{A} = 6 + 6 \] \[ \text{A} = 12 \]
The mass number of the atom is 12. (1 Mark for formula, 1 Mark for correct answer)
5.
What are orbits (or electron shells) in an atom?
[2]
Solution: Orbits (or Shells): These are the fixed, circular paths or imaginary tracks around the nucleus of an atom in which electrons continuously revolve. They are denoted by K, L, M, N, etc. (2 Marks for a clear definition)
SECTION B (15 Marks)
6.
State any three key differences between protons and electrons.
[3]
Solution:
Property Proton Electron
Charge Positively charged (+1) Negatively charged (-1)
Location Present inside the nucleus. Revolve around the nucleus in orbits.
Mass Relatively heavy (approx. 1 amu). Negligible mass (approx. 1/1837 amu).
(1 Mark for each correct difference point)
7.
An element 'X' has an atomic number of 11. Write its electronic configuration and state its valency.
[3]
Solution: Atomic Number (Z) = 11. This means it has 11 electrons.
Electronic Configuration:
\[ K=2, L=8, M=1 \quad \text{or simply} \quad 2, 8, 1 \]
(1.5 Marks for correct configuration)

Valency: The outermost shell (M shell) has 1 electron. It will lose 1 electron to achieve stability (octet). Therefore, its valency is 1 (or +1).
(1.5 Marks for correct valency)
8.
Define the term 'Isotope'. Give one example of isotopes of hydrogen.
[3]
Solution: Isotopes: Atoms of the same element that have the same atomic number but different mass numbers are called isotopes. This means they have the same number of protons but a different number of neutrons. (2 Marks)

Example (Isotopes of Hydrogen):
Protium (\( ^{1}_{1}H \)), Deuterium (\( ^{2}_{1}H \)), and Tritium (\( ^{3}_{1}H \)).
(1 Mark for mentioning at least one valid example or naming them)
9.
Calculate the number of neutrons in a Sodium atom (Given: Mass Number = 23, Atomic Number = 11). Show your working.
[3]
Solution: Given:
Mass Number (A) = 23
Atomic Number (Z) = 11
\[ \text{Mass Number (A)} = \text{Atomic Number (Z)} + \text{Number of Neutrons (n)} \] \[ n = A - Z \] \[ n = 23 - 11 \] \[ n = 12 \]
The number of neutrons in the Sodium atom is 12.
(1 Mark for identifying given values, 1 Mark for formula, 1 Mark for correct calculation)
10.
What is 'Valency'? Why do noble gases (like Neon or Argon) have a zero valency?
[3]
Solution: Valency: The combining capacity of an element with other elements to form molecules or compounds is known as its valency. It is determined by the number of electrons in its outermost shell. (1.5 Marks)

Why Noble Gases have Zero Valency: Noble gases possess a completely filled outermost shell (octet configuration, e.g., 8 electrons in the outermost shell). Because their valence shell is completely full, they are highly stable and do not need to lose, gain, or share electrons. Hence, their combining capacity or valency is zero. (1.5 Marks)
SECTION C (15 Marks)
11.
(a) State any three postulates of Dalton's Atomic Theory.
(b) What are molecules? How is a molecule different from an atom?
[3+2=5]
Solution: (a) Dalton's Atomic Theory Postulates (Any three):
  1. All matter is made up of very tiny, indivisible particles called atoms.
  2. Atoms can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction.
  3. Atoms of a given element are identical in mass and chemical properties.
  4. Atoms of different elements have different masses and chemical properties.
  5. Atoms combine in the ratio of small whole numbers to form compounds.
(1 Mark for each correct postulate = 3 Marks)

(b) Molecules and Difference:
A molecule is a group of two or more atoms chemically bonded together. It is the smallest particle of a substance (element or compound) that can exist independently and shows all the properties of that substance. (1 Mark)
Difference: An atom is the basic building block and usually cannot exist independently (except noble gases), whereas a molecule is formed by the combination of atoms and is capable of independent existence. (1 Mark)

13.
(a) Define Mass Number.
(b) Write the mathematical relationship between Mass Number (A), Atomic Number (Z), and the number of neutrons (n).
(c) An atom 'Y' has 8 protons, 8 electrons, and 8 neutrons. Find its Atomic Number and Mass Number. Name the element 'Y'.
[1+2+2=5]
Solution: (a) Mass Number:
The total sum of the number of protons and neutrons present in the nucleus of an atom is called its Mass Number. (1 Mark)

(b) Mathematical Relationship:
\[ \text{Mass Number (A)} = \text{Atomic Number (Z)} + \text{Number of Neutrons (n)} \] \[ \text{or} \quad A = Z + n \]
(2 Marks)

(c) Calculations for Atom 'Y':
Given: Protons = 8, Neutrons = 8, Electrons = 8.
  • Atomic Number (Z): Equal to the number of protons. Hence, Z = 8. (0.5 Mark)
  • Mass Number (A): Protons + Neutrons = 8 + 8 = 16. (0.5 Mark)
  • Name of Element: The element with atomic number 8 is Oxygen (O). (1 Mark)
