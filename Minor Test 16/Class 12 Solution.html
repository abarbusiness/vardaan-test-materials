Q1. Find \( \frac{dy}{dx} \) if \( y = x^{\sin x} \), where \( x > 0 \).
[2 Marks]
Answer: \( x^{\sin x} \left( \cos x \ln x + \frac{\sin x}{x} \right) \)
Step-by-step Solution:
Given, \( y = x^{\sin x} \)
Taking natural logarithm on both sides:
\( \ln y = \ln(x^{\sin x}) \) \( \ln y = \sin x \cdot \ln x \)
Differentiating both sides with respect to \( x \) using the product rule:
\( \frac{1}{y} \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{d}{dx}(\sin x) \cdot \ln x + \sin x \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(\ln x) \) \( \frac{1}{y} \frac{dy}{dx} = \cos x \cdot \ln x + \sin x \cdot \left(\frac{1}{x}\right) \)
Multiplying by \( y \) on both sides:
\( \frac{dy}{dx} = y \left( \cos x \ln x + \frac{\sin x}{x} \right) \)
Substitute back \( y = x^{\sin x} \):
\( \frac{dy}{dx} = x^{\sin x} \left( \cos x \ln x + \frac{\sin x}{x} \right) \)
Q2. Find the Cartesian equation of the line passing through the point \( (1, 2, -4) \) and parallel to the given line \( \frac{x-3}{2} = \frac{y+1}{7} = \frac{z-2}{-3} \).
[2 Marks]
Answer: \( \frac{x-1}{2} = \frac{y-2}{7} = \frac{z+4}{-3} \)
Step-by-step Solution:
The required line is parallel to the line \( \frac{x-3}{2} = \frac{y+1}{7} = \frac{z-2}{-3} \).
Therefore, the direction ratios (DRs) of the required line are proportional to \( (2, 7, -3) \).
The required line passes through the point \( (x_1, y_1, z_1) = (1, 2, -4) \).
The Cartesian equation of a line passing through a point and having direction ratios \( (a, b, c) \) is given by:
\( \frac{x - x_1}{a} = \frac{y - y_1}{b} = \frac{z - z_1}{c} \)
Substituting the values:
\( \frac{x - 1}{2} = \frac{y - 2}{7} = \frac{z - (-4)}{-3} \)
\( \frac{x - 1}{2} = \frac{y - 2}{7} = \frac{z + 4}{-3} \)
Q3. If \( x = a(\theta - \sin \theta) \) and \( y = a(1 + \cos \theta) \), find \( \frac{dy}{dx} \) in its simplest form.
[2 Marks]
Answer: \( -\cot\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) \)
Step-by-step Solution:
Given parametric equations:
\( x = a(\theta - \sin \theta) \) and \( y = a(1 + \cos \theta) \)
Differentiate with respect to \( \theta \):
\( \frac{dx}{d\theta} = a(1 - \cos \theta) \) \( \frac{dy}{d\theta} = a(0 - \sin \theta) = -a \sin \theta \)
Using the chain rule for parametric functions:
\( \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{\left(\frac{dy}{d\theta}\right)}{\left(\frac{dx}{d\theta}\right)} = \frac{-a \sin \theta}{a(1 - \cos \theta)} = \frac{-\sin \theta}{1 - \cos \theta} \)
Applying half-angle trigonometric identities (\( \sin \theta = 2\sin(\theta/2)\cos(\theta/2) \) and \( 1 - \cos \theta = 2\sin^2(\theta/2) \)):
\( \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{-2 \sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) \cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}{2 \sin^2\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)} \)
\( \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)} = -\cot\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) \)
Q4. Find the direction cosines of the straight line whose vector equation is \( \vec{r} = (2\hat{i} - \hat{j} + 3\hat{k}) + \lambda(3\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 6\hat{k}) \).
[2 Marks]
Answer: \( \frac{3}{7}, \frac{-2}{7}, \frac{6}{7} \) or \( \frac{-3}{7}, \frac{2}{7}, \frac{-6}{7} \)
Step-by-step Solution:
The vector equation of a line is \( \vec{r} = \vec{a} + \lambda\vec{b} \), where \( \vec{b} \) is the direction vector.
Here, the direction vector is \( \vec{b} = 3\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 6\hat{k} \).
Thus, the Direction Ratios (DRs) of the line are proportional to \( a = 3, b = -2, c = 6 \).
To find the Direction Cosines (DCs), we first find the magnitude of the direction vector:
\( |\vec{b}| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2} = \sqrt{3^2 + (-2)^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{9 + 4 + 36} = \sqrt{49} = 7 \)
The Direction Cosines \( (l, m, n) \) are given by \( \left( \pm\frac{a}{|\vec{b}|}, \pm\frac{b}{|\vec{b}|}, \pm\frac{c}{|\vec{b}|} \right) \):
\( l = \frac{3}{7}, \quad m = \frac{-2}{7}, \quad n = \frac{6}{7} \)
(Taking the positive set is standard unless specified otherwise).
Q5. Differentiate \( \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{2x}{1+x^2}\right) \) with respect to \( x \), assuming \( -1 < x < 1 \).
[2 Marks]
Answer: \( \frac{2}{1+x^2} \)
Step-by-step Solution:
Let \( y = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{2x}{1+x^2}\right) \).
Use substitution method. Let \( x = \tan \theta \). Since \( -1 < x < 1 \), \( -\frac{\pi}{4} < \theta < \frac{\pi}{4} \).
Substitute \( x \) in the expression:
\( y = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{2 \tan \theta}{1 + \tan^2 \theta}\right) \)
We know the trigonometric identity: \( \sin 2\theta = \frac{2 \tan \theta}{1 + \tan^2 \theta} \).
\( y = \sin^{-1}(\sin 2\theta) \)
Since \( \theta \in (-\pi/4, \pi/4) \), \( 2\theta \in (-\pi/2, \pi/2) \), which is the principal value branch of \( \sin^{-1} \).
Therefore, \( y = 2\theta \).
Substituting back \( \theta = \tan^{-1} x \):
\( y = 2 \tan^{-1} x \)
Differentiate with respect to \( x \):
\( \frac{dy}{dx} = 2 \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(\tan^{-1} x) = 2 \left( \frac{1}{1+x^2} \right) = \frac{2}{1+x^2} \)
Q6. Find the shortest distance between the following pair of skew lines:
\( \vec{r} = (\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 3\hat{k}) + \lambda(\hat{i} - 3\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}) \)
\( \vec{r} = (4\hat{i} + 5\hat{j} + 6\hat{k}) + \mu(2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} + \hat{k}) \)
[3 Marks]
Answer: \( \frac{3}{\sqrt{19}} \) units
Step-by-step Solution:
Comparing given equations with \( \vec{r} = \vec{a}_1 + \lambda \vec{b}_1 \) and \( \vec{r} = \vec{a}_2 + \mu \vec{b}_2 \):
\( \vec{a}_1 = \hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 3\hat{k}, \quad \vec{b}_1 = \hat{i} - 3\hat{j} + 2\hat{k} \)
\( \vec{a}_2 = 4\hat{i} + 5\hat{j} + 6\hat{k}, \quad \vec{b}_2 = 2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} + \hat{k} \)

1) Find \( \vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1 \):
\( \vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1 = (4-1)\hat{i} + (5-2)\hat{j} + (6-3)\hat{k} = 3\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} + 3\hat{k} \)
2) Find \( \vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2 \) (Cross Product):
\( \vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2 = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 1 & -3 & 2 \\ 2 & 3 & 1 \end{vmatrix} \) \( = \hat{i}(-3 - 6) - \hat{j}(1 - 4) + \hat{k}(3 - (-6)) \) \( = -9\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} + 9\hat{k} \)
3) Find magnitude \( |\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2| \):
\( |\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2| = \sqrt{(-9)^2 + 3^2 + 9^2} = \sqrt{81 + 9 + 81} = \sqrt{171} = 3\sqrt{19} \)
4) Apply Shortest Distance Formula:
\( d = \left| \frac{(\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2) \cdot (\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1)}{|\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2|} \right| \) \( (\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2) \cdot (\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1) = (-9)(3) + (3)(3) + (9)(3) = -27 + 9 + 27 = 9 \)
\( d = \left| \frac{9}{3\sqrt{19}} \right| = \frac{3}{\sqrt{19}} \) units.
Q7. If \( x^y + y^x = a^b \), find \( \frac{dy}{dx} \).
[3 Marks]
Answer: \( -\frac{y \cdot x^{y-1} + y^x \cdot \ln y}{x^y \cdot \ln x + x \cdot y^{x-1}} \)
Step-by-step Solution:
Let \( u = x^y \) and \( v = y^x \). Given equation becomes \( u + v = a^b \).
Differentiating wrt \( x \): \( \frac{du}{dx} + \frac{dv}{dx} = 0 \)   --- (1)

For \( u = x^y \):
Take log on both sides: \( \ln u = y \ln x \)
Differentiating: \( \frac{1}{u}\frac{du}{dx} = y \cdot \frac{1}{x} + \ln x \cdot \frac{dy}{dx} \) \( \frac{du}{dx} = u \left( \frac{y}{x} + \ln x \cdot \frac{dy}{dx} \right) = x^y \left( \frac{y}{x} + \ln x \cdot \frac{dy}{dx} \right) = y x^{y-1} + x^y \ln x \cdot \frac{dy}{dx} \)

For \( v = y^x \):
Take log: \( \ln v = x \ln y \)
Differentiating: \( \frac{1}{v}\frac{dv}{dx} = x \cdot \frac{1}{y}\frac{dy}{dx} + \ln y \cdot 1 \) \( \frac{dv}{dx} = v \left( \frac{x}{y}\frac{dy}{dx} + \ln y \right) = y^x \left( \frac{x}{y}\frac{dy}{dx} + \ln y \right) = x y^{x-1} \cdot \frac{dy}{dx} + y^x \ln y \)

Substitute into (1):
\( y x^{y-1} + x^y \ln x \frac{dy}{dx} + x y^{x-1} \frac{dy}{dx} + y^x \ln y = 0 \)
Collect \( \frac{dy}{dx} \) terms:
\( \frac{dy}{dx} \left( x^y \ln x + x y^{x-1} \right) = -\left( y x^{y-1} + y^x \ln y \right) \)
\( \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{y x^{y-1} + y^x \ln y}{x^y \ln x + x y^{x-1}} \)
Q8. Find the coordinates of the foot of the perpendicular drawn from the point \( P(2, -1, 5) \) to the line \( \frac{x-11}{10} = \frac{y+2}{-4} = \frac{z+8}{-11} \).
[3 Marks]
Answer: \( (1, 2, 3) \)
Step-by-step Solution:
Let the line be \( L: \frac{x-11}{10} = \frac{y+2}{-4} = \frac{z+8}{-11} = \lambda \).
Any general point on this line can be written as \( Q(10\lambda + 11, -4\lambda - 2, -11\lambda - 8) \).
If \( Q \) is the foot of the perpendicular from \( P(2, -1, 5) \) to the line \( L \), then the line segment \( PQ \) will be perpendicular to the line \( L \).

Direction ratios (DRs) of the line \( L \): \( (10, -4, -11) \).
Direction ratios of \( PQ \):
\( ((10\lambda + 11) - 2, (-4\lambda - 2) - (-1), (-11\lambda - 8) - 5) \) \( = (10\lambda + 9, -4\lambda - 1, -11\lambda - 13) \)

Since \( PQ \) is perpendicular to \( L \), the dot product of their DRs is zero:
\( 10(10\lambda + 9) + (-4)(-4\lambda - 1) + (-11)(-11\lambda - 13) = 0 \) \( 100\lambda + 90 + 16\lambda + 4 + 121\lambda + 143 = 0 \) \( 237\lambda + 237 = 0 \) \( \lambda = -1 \)

Substitute \( \lambda = -1 \) in coordinates of \( Q \):
\( x = 10(-1) + 11 = 1 \)
\( y = -4(-1) - 2 = 2 \)
\( z = -11(-1) - 8 = 3 \)
The foot of the perpendicular is \( (1, 2, 3) \).
Q9. If \( y = (\tan^{-1} x)^2 \), show that \( (x^2+1)^2 \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + 2x(x^2+1) \frac{dy}{dx} = 2 \).
[3 Marks]
Answer: Proof (See steps)
Step-by-step Solution:
Given, \( y = (\tan^{-1} x)^2 \).
Differentiate with respect to \( x \):
\( \frac{dy}{dx} = y_1 = 2(\tan^{-1} x) \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(\tan^{-1} x) \) \( y_1 = 2(\tan^{-1} x) \cdot \frac{1}{1+x^2} \)
Cross-multiply to remove the denominator (this simplifies finding the second derivative):
\( (1+x^2)y_1 = 2\tan^{-1} x \)

Differentiate again with respect to \( x \) using the product rule on LHS:
\( (1+x^2) \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(y_1) + y_1 \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(1+x^2) = 2 \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(\tan^{-1} x) \) \( (1+x^2)y_2 + y_1(2x) = 2 \left( \frac{1}{1+x^2} \right) \)

Multiply the entire equation by \( (1+x^2) \):
\( (1+x^2)^2 y_2 + 2x(1+x^2)y_1 = 2 \)
Writing \( y_2 \) as \( \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} \) and \( y_1 \) as \( \frac{dy}{dx} \):
\( (x^2+1)^2 \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + 2x(x^2+1) \frac{dy}{dx} = 2 \)
Hence Proved.
Q10. If \( x = a \cos t + a t \sin t \) and \( y = a \sin t - a t \cos t \), evaluate \( \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} \) at \( t = \frac{\pi}{4} \).
[3 Marks]
Answer: \( \frac{8\sqrt{2}}{a\pi} \)
Step-by-step Solution:
Find \( \frac{dx}{dt} \):
\( \frac{dx}{dt} = -a \sin t + a (1 \cdot \sin t + t \cdot \cos t) = -a \sin t + a \sin t + a t \cos t = a t \cos t \)
Find \( \frac{dy}{dt} \):
\( \frac{dy}{dt} = a \cos t - a (1 \cdot \cos t + t(-\sin t)) = a \cos t - a \cos t + a t \sin t = a t \sin t \)
Find \( \frac{dy}{dx} \):
\( \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{\left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)}{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)} = \frac{a t \sin t}{a t \cos t} = \tan t \)
Find \( \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} \):
Caution: differentiate with respect to \( x \), not \( t \).
\( \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{d}{dx}(\tan t) = \frac{d}{dt}(\tan t) \cdot \frac{dt}{dx} \) \( \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \sec^2 t \cdot \frac{1}{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)} = \sec^2 t \cdot \frac{1}{a t \cos t} = \frac{\sec^3 t}{a t} \)
Evaluate at \( t = \pi/4 \):
\( \left. \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} \right|_{t=\pi/4} = \frac{(\sec(\pi/4))^3}{a (\pi/4)} = \frac{(\sqrt{2})^3}{a\pi/4} \) \( = \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{a\pi/4} = \frac{8\sqrt{2}}{a\pi} \)
Q11. If \( y = e^{a \cos^{-1} x} \) where \( -1 \le x \le 1 \), prove that: \( (1-x^2)\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - x\frac{dy}{dx} - a^2y = 0 \).
[5 Marks]
Answer: Proof (See steps)
Step-by-step Solution:
Given: \( y = e^{a \cos^{-1} x} \)
Differentiate both sides with respect to \( x \):
\( \frac{dy}{dx} = e^{a \cos^{-1} x} \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(a \cos^{-1} x) \) \( y_1 = y \cdot \left( \frac{-a}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} \right) \)
Squaring both sides to simplify the expression before second derivative:
\( y_1^2 = \frac{a^2 y^2}{1-x^2} \)
\( (1-x^2) y_1^2 = a^2 y^2 \)

Differentiate implicitly with respect to \( x \) again:
\( \frac{d}{dx}[(1-x^2) y_1^2] = \frac{d}{dx}[a^2 y^2] \) \( (1-x^2) \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(y_1^2) + y_1^2 \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(1-x^2) = a^2 \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(y^2) \) \( (1-x^2) (2 y_1 y_2) + y_1^2 (-2x) = a^2 (2 y y_1) \)

Divide the entire equation by \( 2y_1 \) (assuming \( y_1 \neq 0 \)):
\( (1-x^2) y_2 - x y_1 = a^2 y \)
Rearranging the terms:
\( (1-x^2) y_2 - x y_1 - a^2 y = 0 \)
Substituting back the standard derivative notations:
\( (1-x^2)\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - x\frac{dy}{dx} - a^2y = 0 \)
Hence Proved.
Q12. Find the image of the point \( (1, 6, 3) \) in the line \( \frac{x}{1} = \frac{y-1}{2} = \frac{z-2}{3} \). Also, write the equation of the line joining the given point and its image, and find the perpendicular distance from the point to the given line.
[5 Marks]
Answer: Image: \( (1, 0, 7) \), Eq: \( \frac{x-1}{0} = \frac{y-6}{-6} = \frac{z-3}{4} \), Distance: \( \sqrt{13} \) units.
Step-by-step Solution:
Let \( P(1, 6, 3) \) be the given point and the line be \( L: \frac{x}{1} = \frac{y-1}{2} = \frac{z-2}{3} = \lambda \).
Let \( Q \) be the foot of perpendicular from \( P \) to \( L \).
Any point on \( L \) is \( Q(\lambda, 2\lambda+1, 3\lambda+2) \).

1) Find Foot of Perpendicular (Q):
Direction ratios (DRs) of \( PQ \): \( (\lambda - 1, 2\lambda + 1 - 6, 3\lambda + 2 - 3) = (\lambda - 1, 2\lambda - 5, 3\lambda - 1) \).
DRs of line \( L \) are \( (1, 2, 3) \).
Since \( PQ \perp L \), dot product of DRs is 0:
\( 1(\lambda - 1) + 2(2\lambda - 5) + 3(3\lambda - 1) = 0 \) \( \lambda - 1 + 4\lambda - 10 + 9\lambda - 3 = 0 \) \( 14\lambda - 14 = 0 \implies \lambda = 1 \)
Coordinates of foot \( Q \) (put \( \lambda=1 \)): \( Q(1, 2(1)+1, 3(1)+2) = (1, 3, 5) \).

2) Find Image (P'):
Let the image of \( P \) be \( P'(x', y', z') \). \( Q \) is the midpoint of \( PP' \).
\( \frac{x' + 1}{2} = 1 \implies x' = 1 \) \( \frac{y' + 6}{2} = 3 \implies y' = 0 \) \( \frac{z' + 3}{2} = 5 \implies z' = 7 \)
Image is \( P'(1, 0, 7) \).

3) Equation of line joining \( P \) and \( P' \):
Passes through \( P(1, 6, 3) \) and \( P'(1, 0, 7) \).
DRs of \( PP' \): \( (1-1, 0-6, 7-3) = (0, -6, 4) \).
Equation: \( \frac{x-1}{0} = \frac{y-6}{-6} = \frac{z-3}{4} \).

4) Perpendicular Distance (PQ):
Distance between \( P(1, 6, 3) \) and \( Q(1, 3, 5) \):
\( PQ = \sqrt{(1-1)^2 + (3-6)^2 + (5-3)^2} = \sqrt{0 + (-3)^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{9 + 4} = \) \( \sqrt{13} \) units.
Q13. Show that the lines \( \frac{x+1}{3} = \frac{y+3}{5} = \frac{z+5}{7} \) and \( \frac{x-2}{1} = \frac{y-4}{3} = \frac{z-6}{5} \) intersect each other. Find their point of intersection.
[5 Marks]
Answer: Point of Intersection: \( (1/2, -1/2, -3/2) \)
Step-by-step Solution:
Let \( L_1 : \frac{x+1}{3} = \frac{y+3}{5} = \frac{z+5}{7} = \lambda \)
Let \( L_2 : \frac{x-2}{1} = \frac{y-4}{3} = \frac{z-6}{5} = \mu \)

Any general point on \( L_1 \) is \( P(3\lambda - 1, 5\lambda - 3, 7\lambda - 5) \).
Any general point on \( L_2 \) is \( Q(\mu + 2, 3\mu + 4, 5\mu + 6) \).

If the lines intersect, they must have a common point. Thus, for some values of \( \lambda \) and \( \mu \), \( P \) and \( Q \) must be identical.
Equating corresponding coordinates:
1) \( 3\lambda - 1 = \mu + 2 \implies 3\lambda - \mu = 3 \)   --- (Eq. i)
2) \( 5\lambda - 3 = 3\mu + 4 \implies 5\lambda - 3\mu = 7 \)   --- (Eq. ii)
3) \( 7\lambda - 5 = 5\mu + 6 \implies 7\lambda - 5\mu = 11 \)   --- (Eq. iii)

Solve (Eq. i) and (Eq. ii):
From (i), \( \mu = 3\lambda - 3 \).
Substitute in (ii):
\( 5\lambda - 3(3\lambda - 3) = 7 \) \( 5\lambda - 9\lambda + 9 = 7 \) \( -4\lambda = -2 \implies \lambda = \frac{1}{2} \)
Find \( \mu \): \( \mu = 3(1/2) - 3 = \frac{3}{2} - 3 = -\frac{3}{2} \).

Check if these values satisfy (Eq. iii):
LHS: \( 7(1/2) - 5(-3/2) = \frac{7}{2} + \frac{15}{2} = \frac{22}{2} = 11 \).
RHS: \( 11 \).
LHS = RHS. Since the third equation is satisfied, the lines intersect.

Point of Intersection:
Substitute \( \lambda = 1/2 \) into the coordinates of \( P \) (or \( \mu = -3/2 \) into \( Q \)):
\( x = 3(1/2) - 1 = 1/2 \) \( y = 5(1/2) - 3 = -1/2 \) \( z = 7(1/2) - 5 = -3/2 \)
The point of intersection is \( (1/2, -1/2, -3/2) \).
