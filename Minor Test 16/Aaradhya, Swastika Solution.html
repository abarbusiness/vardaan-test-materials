[2 Marks]

(i) \( 3^{-2} \)
Using the law \( a^{-m} = \frac{1}{a^m} \) [1/2 Mark]
\( = \frac{1}{3^2} = \frac{1}{9} \) [1/2 Mark]

(ii) \( \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-3} \)
Using the law \( \left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{-m} = \left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^m \) [1/2 Mark]
\( = \left(\frac{2}{1}\right)^3 = 2^3 = 8 \) [1/2 Mark]

SECTION B (15 Marks)
Q6.
Simplify the following expression: \( \frac{25 \times t^{-4}}{5^{-3} \times 10 \times t^{-8}} \) (Assume \( t \neq 0 \))
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Given: \( \frac{25 \times t^{-4}}{5^{-3} \times 10 \times t^{-8}} \)
Step 1: Express 25 and 10 in terms of prime factors. [1 Mark]
\( = \frac{5^2 \times t^{-4}}{5^{-3} \times (2 \times 5) \times t^{-8}} \)
Step 2: Group the like bases (5, 2, and t).
\( = \frac{5^2 \times t^{-4}}{5^{-3+1} \times 2 \times t^{-8}} \) \( = \frac{5^2 \times t^{-4}}{5^{-2} \times 2 \times t^{-8}} \) [1 Mark]
Step 3: Apply the quotient law \( \frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n} \).
\( = \frac{1}{2} \times 5^{2 - (-2)} \times t^{-4 - (-8)} \)
\( = \frac{1}{2} \times 5^{2 + 2} \times t^{-4 + 8} \)
\( = \frac{1}{2} \times 5^4 \times t^4 \)
Final Answer = \( \frac{625 t^4}{2} \) [1 Mark]
Q7.
Find the value of \( m \) if: \( \left(\frac{5}{3}\right)^{-4} \times \left(\frac{5}{3}\right)^{-5} = \left(\frac{5}{3}\right)^{3m} \)
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Given equation: \( \left(\frac{5}{3}\right)^{-4} \times \left(\frac{5}{3}\right)^{-5} = \left(\frac{5}{3}\right)^{3m} \)
Step 1: Apply the product law \( a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n} \) on the Left Hand Side (LHS). [1 Mark]
\( \left(\frac{5}{3}\right)^{-4 + (-5)} = \left(\frac{5}{3}\right)^{3m} \)
\( \left(\frac{5}{3}\right)^{-9} = \left(\frac{5}{3}\right)^{3m} \) [1 Mark]
Step 2: Since the bases on both sides are equal and non-zero, their exponents must be equal. [1/2 Mark]
\( -9 = 3m \)
\( m = \frac{-9}{3} \)
Final Answer: \( m = -3 \) [1/2 Mark]

Q9.
Describe a simple activity to show that a magnetic force can act from a distance and is a non-contact force.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Activity: [3 Marks Total]
  1. Take a bar magnet and place it flat on a wooden table. [1 Mark]
  2. Take a few iron nails and slowly bring them near one of the poles of the magnet without actually letting them touch the magnet. [1 Mark]
  3. Observation/Conclusion: The iron nails will instantly get pulled and jump towards the magnet. This shows that the magnet exerts a pulling force on the nails from a distance, proving that magnetic force is a non-contact force. [1 Mark]
Q10.
In a game of tug-of-war, Team X pulls the rope with a force of \( 350 \) N towards the East, and Team Y pulls the rope with a force of \( 400 \) N towards the West. Calculate the net force and determine which team will win the game.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Force applied by Team X = \( 350 \) N (towards East) [0.5 Mark]
Force applied by Team Y = \( 400 \) N (towards West) [0.5 Mark]

Since the two forces are acting in opposite directions, the net force is the difference between them.
Net Force = \( 400 \text{ N} - 350 \text{ N} = 50 \text{ N} \) [1 Mark]

Conclusion: Team Y will win the game because the net force of \( 50 \) N is directed towards the West (Team Y's direction). [1 Mark]

Q11.
Simplify the given expression completely by applying the laws of exponents (Show all prime factorization steps): \[ \frac{3^{-5} \times 10^{-5} \times 125}{5^{-7} \times 6^{-5}} \]
[5 Marks]
Solution:
Given: \( \frac{3^{-5} \times 10^{-5} \times 125}{5^{-7} \times 6^{-5}} \)

Step 1: Write all composite numbers as products of their prime factors.
\( 10 = 2 \times 5 \)
\( 125 = 5^3 \)
\( 6 = 2 \times 3 \)
Substituting these back: [1.5 Marks]
\( = \frac{3^{-5} \times (2 \times 5)^{-5} \times 5^3}{5^{-7} \times (2 \times 3)^{-5}} \)
Step 2: Apply the power of a product law \( (ab)^m = a^m \times b^m \). [1 Mark]
\( = \frac{3^{-5} \times 2^{-5} \times 5^{-5} \times 5^3}{5^{-7} \times 2^{-5} \times 3^{-5}} \)
Step 3: Group terms with the same base together (Bases: 2, 3, 5).
Numerator powers of 5: \( 5^{-5} \times 5^3 = 5^{-5+3} = 5^{-2} \) [1 Mark]
\( = \frac{3^{-5} \times 2^{-5} \times 5^{-2}}{5^{-7} \times 2^{-5} \times 3^{-5}} \)
Step 4: Use quotient law \( \frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n} \). [1 Mark]
For base 2: \( 2^{-5 - (-5)} = 2^0 \)
For base 3: \( 3^{-5 - (-5)} = 3^0 \)
For base 5: \( 5^{-2 - (-7)} = 5^{-2 + 7} = 5^5 \)
Step 5: Simplify.
\( = 2^0 \times 3^0 \times 5^5 \) \( = 1 \times 1 \times 3125 \) (since \(a^0 = 1\))
Final Answer = 3125 [0.5 Mark]

Q13.
Answer the following questions:
(a) Differentiate between Contact and Non-Contact forces giving one example of each. (3 Marks)
(b) A boy pushes a toy car with a force of \( 15 \) N. His friend joins him and pushes the car in the same direction with a force of \( 10 \) N. Calculate the net force applied on the car. What would happen to the net force if his friend pushed from the opposite direction with the same \( 10 \) N force? (2 Marks)
[5 Marks]
Solution:
Part (a): Differences [3 Marks]
Contact Forces Non-Contact Forces
Forces that act only when two interacting objects are in physical contact with each other. [1 Mark] Forces that can act between two objects even when they are not in physical contact. [1 Mark]
Example: Frictional force, Muscular force. [0.5 Mark] Example: Magnetic force, Gravitational force. [0.5 Mark]

Part (b): Net Force [2 Marks]
Case 1 (Pushing in the same direction):
When forces act in the same direction, they add up.
Net Force = \( 15 \text{ N} + 10 \text{ N} = 25 \text{ N} \) [1 Mark]

Case 2 (Pushing in opposite directions):
When forces act in opposite directions, the net force is the difference between them.
Net Force = \( 15 \text{ N} - 10 \text{ N} = 5 \text{ N} \) (in the direction of the boy's push). [1 Mark]
