SECTION A

Q1. Find the mean of the first five prime numbers.
[2 Marks]
Step 1: Identify the first five prime numbers.
The first five prime numbers are: 2, 3, 5, 7, and 11.
Step 2: Apply the mean formula.
\[ \text{Mean} = \frac{\text{Sum of observations}}{\text{Total number of observations}} \] \[ \text{Mean} = \frac{2 + 3 + 5 + 7 + 11}{5} = \frac{28}{5} \] \[ \text{Mean} = 5.6 \]
Answer: 5.6
Q2. The mean of 8 observations is 15. If each observation is multiplied by 2, what will be the new mean? Justify your answer.
[2 Marks]
Property of Mean: If each observation of a data set is multiplied by a constant \(k\), the new mean is also multiplied by \(k\).
Old Mean = 15
Multiplier (\(k\)) = 2
\[ \text{New Mean} = \text{Old Mean} \times 2 \] \[ \text{New Mean} = 15 \times 2 = 30 \]
Answer: 30
Q3. Find the median of the following ungrouped data: 25, 34, 31, 23, 22, 26, 35, 28, 20, 32.
[2 Marks]
Step 1: Arrange data in ascending order.
20, 22, 23, 25, 26, 28, 31, 32, 34, 35
Step 2: Find the number of observations (\(n\)).
Here, \(n = 10\) (which is an even number).
Step 3: Apply median formula for even \(n\).
\[ \text{Median} = \frac{\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{\text{th}} \text{term} + \left(\frac{n}{2} + 1\right)^{\text{th}} \text{term}}{2} \] \[ \text{Median} = \frac{5^{\text{th}} \text{term} + 6^{\text{th}} \text{term}}{2} \] \[ \text{Median} = \frac{26 + 28}{2} = \frac{54}{2} = 27 \]
Answer: 27
Q4. Convert the following inclusive class intervals into exclusive class intervals: 10-19, 20-29, 30-39. Also, write the true class limits of the second interval.
[2 Marks]
Step 1: Find the adjustment factor.
Difference between lower limit of second class and upper limit of first class = \(20 - 19 = 1\).
Adjustment factor = \(1 / 2 = 0.5\).
Step 2: Subtract 0.5 from lower limits and add 0.5 to upper limits.
  • 10-19 becomes 9.5 - 19.5
  • 20-29 becomes 19.5 - 29.5
  • 30-39 becomes 29.5 - 39.5
Step 3: True class limits of the second interval.
Exclusive Intervals: 9.5-19.5, 19.5-29.5, 29.5-39.5 | True Limits of 2nd: 19.5 to 29.5
Q5. For the data set: 14, 17, 13, 14, 14, 3, 15, 1, 17, 14. Find the mode of the given data.
[2 Marks]
Definition: Mode is the observation that occurs most frequently in the data set.
Let's count the frequencies:
  • 14 occurs 4 times.
  • 17 occurs 2 times.
  • 13, 3, 15, 1 occur 1 time each.
Since 14 has the highest frequency (4), it is the mode.
Answer: 14

Q6. The mean of 5 numbers is 18. If one number is excluded, the mean of the remaining numbers becomes 16. Find the excluded number.
[3 Marks]
\[ \text{Sum of 5 numbers} = \text{Mean} \times \text{Number of observations} \] \[ \text{Sum of 5 numbers} = 18 \times 5 = 90 \]
If one number is excluded, remaining numbers = 4.
\[ \text{New Sum of 4 numbers} = 16 \times 4 = 64 \]
\[ \text{Excluded Number} = (\text{Sum of 5 numbers}) - (\text{Sum of 4 numbers}) \] \[ \text{Excluded Number} = 90 - 64 = 26 \]
Answer: 26

Q13. (i) Mean of x, x+2, x+4, x+6, x+8 is 11. Find x and mean of first 3 obs. (ii) Differences between Bar Graph & Histogram.
[5 Marks]
Part (i): [3 Marks]
\[ \text{Mean} = \frac{x + (x+2) + (x+4) + (x+6) + (x+8)}{5} = 11 \] \[ \frac{5x + 20}{5} = 11 \] \[ 5x + 20 = 55 \] \[ 5x = 35 \implies x = 7 \]
First 3 observations are: \(x, x+2, x+4\) \(\rightarrow 7, 9, 11\).
\[ \text{Mean of first 3} = \frac{7 + 9 + 11}{3} = \frac{27}{3} = 9 \]
Part (ii): [2 Marks]
Differences between Bar Graph and Histogram:
