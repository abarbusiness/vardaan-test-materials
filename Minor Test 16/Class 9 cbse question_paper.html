Minor Test 16
Class: 9 CBSE Subject: Science (Physics) Time: 1 Hour Max. Marks: 40
General Instructions:
  • All questions are compulsory.
  • The paper is divided into three sections: A, B, and C.
  • Section A has 5 questions of 2 marks each.
  • Section B has 5 questions of 3 marks each.
  • Section C has 3 questions of 5 marks each.
  • Write your answers neatly, use standard SI units, and show calculations where necessary.
SECTION A (10 Marks)(Attempt all questions - 2 Marks Each)
1.
A person carrying a heavy load on his head walks on a horizontal road. Explain scientifically why the work done by him against gravity is considered to be zero.
2.
Differentiate between Class II and Class III levers based on the positions of the fulcrum, load, and effort. Which of these levers always acts as a speed multiplier?
3.
Define 1 Watt of power. Express the commercial unit of energy, 1 kilowatt-hour (kWh), in Joules.
4.
If the velocity of an object is doubled while its mass remains constant, what will be the effect on its kinetic energy? Justify your answer using the relevant formula.
5.
Does the gravitational potential energy of an object depend on the path taken to raise it to a certain height? State the formula for gravitational potential energy.
SECTION B (15 Marks)(Short Answer Type - 3 Marks Each)
6.
Define positive and negative work. Give one real-life example of negative work. What is the nature of the work done by frictional force when a block slides on a table?
7.
Derive the mathematical expression for the kinetic energy (\(E_k\)) of an object of mass \(m\) moving with a uniform velocity \(v\), assuming it started from rest.
8.
An electric bulb of 60 W is used for 6 hours per day. Calculate the 'units' of electrical energy consumed in a span of 30 days by this bulb.
9.
What is a block and tackle system? If a block and tackle system has 4 pulleys in total, state its ideal velocity ratio (VR) and ideal mechanical advantage (MA). Why is the efficiency of a practical pulley system always less than 100%?
10.
Calculate the amount of work to be done to increase the velocity of a car from 30 km/h to 60 km/h, given that the mass of the car is 1500 kg.
SECTION C (15 Marks)(Long Answer Type - 5 Marks Each)
11.
(a) State the Law of Conservation of Energy. (1 Mark)
(b) Using the example of an object of mass \(m\) falling freely from a height \(h\), theoretically trace the energy transformations and prove that the total mechanical energy remains constant throughout its path. (4 Marks)
12.
A practical machine requires an effort of 200 N to lift a load of 800 N. The effort moves through a distance of 10 m while the load moves through a distance of 2 m in the same time interval. Calculate:
(a) The Mechanical Advantage (MA) of the machine. (1.5 Marks)
(b) The Velocity Ratio (VR) of the machine. (1.5 Marks)
(c) The Efficiency (\(\eta\)) of the machine as a percentage. (1 Mark)
(d) Explain why the efficiency of this machine is not 100%. (1 Mark)
13.
(a) An object is pushed up an inclined plane of length \(l\) and vertical height \(h\). State the formula for the ideal mechanical advantage of this inclined plane. (1 Mark)
(b) A porter lifts a luggage of 15 kg from the ground and puts it on his head 1.5 m above the ground. Calculate the work done by him on the luggage. (Take \(g = 10 \text{ m/s}^2\)) (2 Marks)
(c) If the porter takes exactly 5 seconds to lift the luggage from the ground to his head, calculate his average power. (2 Marks)
