DETAILED SOLUTIONS - Minor Test 16

Class 7 | Mathematics (Exponents) | 40 Marks

SECTION A

Q1. Express the following in exponential form: \(5 \times 5 \times 5 \times 7 \times 7 \times 7 \times 7\)
[2 Marks]
Group the identical bases together:
\[ = (5 \times 5 \times 5) \times (7 \times 7 \times 7 \times 7) \]
Count the occurrences. 5 appears 3 times, and 7 appears 4 times.
Final Answer: \(5^3 \times 7^4\)
Q2. Find the value of: \((-3)^3 \times (-5)^2\)
[2 Marks]
Evaluate the powers individually:
\((-3)^3 = (-3) \times (-3) \times (-3) = -27\) (Negative base with odd power is negative)
\((-5)^2 = (-5) \times (-5) = 25\) (Negative base with even power is positive)
Multiply the results:
\[ = -27 \times 25 \]
Final Answer: \(-675\)
Q3. Simplify and write in exponential form using laws of exponents: \(2^3 \times 2^4 \times 2^5\)
[2 Marks]
Using the law of exponents for multiplication with the same base: \(a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}\)
\[ = 2^{3 + 4 + 5} \]
\[ = 2^{12} \]
Final Answer: \(2^{12}\)
Q4. Express \(729\) as a power of \(3\).
[2 Marks]
Find the prime factorization of 729 by successively dividing by 3:
\(729 \div 3 = 243\)
\(243 \div 3 = 81\)
\(81 \div 3 = 27\)
\(27 \div 3 = 9\)
\(9 \div 3 = 3\)
\(3 \div 3 = 1\)
Therefore, \(729 = 3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3\)
Final Answer: \(3^6\)
Q5. Write the number \(34,560,000\) in standard form.
[2 Marks]
To express in standard form (\(k \times 10^n\) where \(1 \leq k < 10\)), move the decimal point 7 places to the left.
\[ 34,560,000 = 3.456 \times 10,000,000 \]
Final Answer: \(3.456 \times 10^7\)

SECTION B

Q6. Simplify and express the result in exponential form: \((5^2)^3 \div 5^4\)
[3 Marks]
First, simplify the power of a power using the law \((a^m)^n = a^{mn}\):
\[ (5^2)^3 = 5^{2 \times 3} = 5^6 \]
Now substitute this back into the expression:
\[ = 5^6 \div 5^4 \]
Use the division law of exponents \(a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n}\):
\[ = 5^{6 - 4} \]
Final Answer: \(5^2\)
Q7. By what number should \((-4)^3\) be divided so that the quotient may be \(8^2\)?
[3 Marks]
Let the required number be \(x\).
According to the question:
\[ \frac{(-4)^3}{x} = 8^2 \]
Evaluate the powers:
\((-4)^3 = (-4) \times (-4) \times (-4) = -64\)
\(8^2 = 8 \times 8 = 64\)
Substitute the values into the equation:
\[ \frac{-64}{x} = 64 \]
Solve for \(x\):
\[ -64 = 64 \times x \]
\[ x = \frac{-64}{64} \]
Final Answer: \(x = -1\)
Q8. Simplify: \( \frac{2^5 \times 3^4 \times 4}{3^2 \times 32} \)
[3 Marks]
First, express all composite numbers (\(4\) and \(32\)) as powers of prime bases:
\(4 = 2^2\)
\(32 = 2^5\)
Substitute these into the expression:
\[ = \frac{2^5 \times 3^4 \times 2^2}{3^2 \times 2^5} \]
Group bases in the numerator using \(a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}\):
\[ = \frac{2^{5+2} \times 3^4}{2^5 \times 3^2} = \frac{2^7 \times 3^4}{2^5 \times 3^2} \]
Apply the division law \(a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n}\) for bases 2 and 3:
\[ = 2^{7-5} \times 3^{4-2} \]
\[ = 2^2 \times 3^2 \]
\[ = 4 \times 9 \]
Final Answer: \(36\)
Q9. Compare \(2.7 \times 10^{12}\) and \(1.5 \times 10^8\). State which is larger and why.
[3 Marks]
To compare numbers written in standard form, first look at the powers of 10.
Number 1: \(2.7 \times 10^{12}\) (Exponent is 12)
Number 2: \(1.5 \times 10^8\) (Exponent is 8)
Since \(10^{12}\) is much greater than \(10^8\) (\(10^{12} = 10^4 \times 10^8\)), the number with the larger power of 10 is always greater regardless of the decimal part (as long as it's standard form between 1 and 10).
Final Answer: \(2.7 \times 10^{12} > 1.5 \times 10^8\)
Q10. Evaluate: \((3^0 + 4^0 + 5^0) \times (2^0 \times 3^0)\)
[3 Marks]
Recall the law of exponents: Any non-zero number raised to the power 0 is equal to 1 (\(a^0 = 1\)).
Substitute \(1\) for all terms with a power of \(0\):
\[ = (1 + 1 + 1) \times (1 \times 1) \]
Perform addition and multiplication in the brackets:
\[ = (3) \times (1) \]
\[ = 3 \]
Final Answer: \(3\)

SECTION C

Q11. (a) Simplify: \(\left[ \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^3 \right]^2 \times \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^5 \div \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^7\)
(b) Express \(432\) as a product of powers of prime factors.
[5 Marks]
Part (a) [3 Marks]
Apply power of a power law \((a^m)^n = a^{mn}\):
\[ \left[ \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^3 \right]^2 = \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^{3 \times 2} = \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^6 \]
Expression becomes:
\[ \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^6 \times \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^5 \div \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^7 \]
Apply multiplication law \(a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}\):
\[ = \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^{6+5} \div \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^7 = \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^{11} \div \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^7 \]
Apply division law \(a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n}\):
\[ = \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^{11-7} = \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^4 \]
Ans (a): \(\left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^4\)
Part (b) [2 Marks]
Prime factorization of 432:
\(432 \div 2 = 216\)
\(216 \div 2 = 108\)
\(108 \div 2 = 54\)
\(54 \div 2 = 27\)
\(27 \div 3 = 9\)
\(9 \div 3 = 3\)
\(3 \div 3 = 1\)
Thus, \(432 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3\)
Ans (b): \(2^4 \times 3^3\)
Q12. (a) Find \(x\) if \(2^x \times 2^3 = 2^8\).
(b) Simplify: \( \frac{25 \times 5^2 \times t^8}{10^3 \times t^4} \)
[5 Marks]
Part (a) [2 Marks]
Using law \(a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}\) on the LHS:
\[ 2^{x+3} = 2^8 \]
Since the bases are same on both sides, their exponents must be equal:
\[ x + 3 = 8 \]
\[ x = 8 - 3 = 5 \]
Ans (a): \(x = 5\)
Part (b) [3 Marks]
Convert all numbers into prime bases:
\(25 = 5^2\)
\(10^3 = (2 \times 5)^3 = 2^3 \times 5^3\)
Substitute in the expression:
\[ = \frac{5^2 \times 5^2 \times t^8}{2^3 \times 5^3 \times t^4} \]
Combine powers of 5 in the numerator:
\[ = \frac{5^{2+2} \times t^8}{2^3 \times 5^3 \times t^4} = \frac{5^4 \times t^8}{2^3 \times 5^3 \times t^4} \]
Apply division law for base 5 and base \(t\):
\[ = \frac{5^{4-3} \times t^{8-4}}{2^3} \]
\[ = \frac{5^1 \times t^4}{8} \]
Ans (b): \(\frac{5t^4}{8}\)
Q13. (a) Earth to Moon distance: \(384,000,000 \text{ m}\) in standard form.
(b) If \(a=2, b=3\), find (i) \((a+b)^2\) (ii) \((a^b + b^a)\).
[5 Marks]
Part (a) [2 Marks]
The decimal point in \(384,000,000\) is at the end. We need to move it 8 places to the left to get a number between 1 and 10.
\[ = 3.84 \times 100,000,000 \]
Ans (a): \(3.84 \times 10^8 \text{ m}\)
Part (b) [3 Marks]
Given \(a = 2\) and \(b = 3\).
(i) Find \((a+b)^2\):
Substitute the values: \((2 + 3)^2\)
First evaluate the brackets: \((5)^2\)
\[ 5^2 = 5 \times 5 = 25 \]
Ans (b)(i): \(25\)
(ii) Find \((a^b + b^a)\):
Substitute the values: \((2^3 + 3^2)\)
Evaluate individual powers:
\(2^3 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8\)
\(3^2 = 3 \times 3 = 9\)
Add the results: \(8 + 9 = 17\)
Ans (b)(ii): \(17\)
