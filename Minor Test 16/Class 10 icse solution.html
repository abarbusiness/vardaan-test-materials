DETAILED SOLUTIONS: Minor Test 16 (10 ICSE Chemistry)
SECTION A (10 Marks)
Q1. Name the drying agent used in the laboratory preparation of hydrogen chloride gas. Why is concentrated sulphuric acid chosen for this purpose?
[2 Marks]

Drying Agent: Concentrated Sulphuric Acid (\(H_2SO_4\)). (1 Mark)

Reason: It is chosen because it is a strong dehydrating agent and it does not react chemically with hydrogen chloride gas (both being acidic in nature). (1 Mark)

Q2. Write the balanced chemical equation for the action of warm water on Magnesium nitride (\(Mg_3N_2\)). Name the pungent-smelling gas evolved.
[2 Marks]
\[ Mg_3N_2 + 6H_2O \xrightarrow{\text{warm}} 3Mg(OH)_2 + 2NH_3 \uparrow \]
(1 Mark for correct balanced equation)

Gas Evolved: Ammonia (\(NH_3\)). (1 Mark)

Q3. How is "back suction" avoided during the preparation of hydrochloric acid by dissolving hydrogen chloride gas in water? Name the special arrangement used.
[2 Marks]

Method: Back suction is avoided by using an inverted funnel arrangement. (1 Mark)

Special Arrangement Name: Inverted Funnel Arrangement. It provides a large surface area for absorption of HCl gas. When the rate of absorption exceeds the rate of production, water rises in the funnel, the rim loses contact with water, and air enters, equalizing pressure and pushing the water back down, preventing back suction into the flask. (1 Mark for explanation/name)

Q4. State your observation when ammonium hydroxide is added dropwise and then in excess to an aqueous solution of copper (II) sulphate.
[2 Marks]

Dropwise addition: A pale blue precipitate of copper (II) hydroxide is formed. (1 Mark)

Excess addition: The pale blue precipitate dissolves to form a deep blue / inky blue solution of tetrammine copper (II) sulphate. (1 Mark)

Q5. What is aqua regia? State its unique chemical property regarding noble metals.
[2 Marks]

Aqua Regia: It is a mixture of concentrated hydrochloric acid and concentrated nitric acid in the ratio of 3:1 by volume. (1 Mark)

Unique Property: It can dissolve noble metals like gold and platinum (by liberating nascent chlorine), which are generally unreactive with other individual acids. (1 Mark)

SECTION B (15 Marks)
Q6. Differentiate between the burning of ammonia in oxygen and the catalytic oxidation of ammonia by stating the conditions and writing the balanced chemical equations.
[3 Marks]
1. Burning of Ammonia in Oxygen:

Condition: No catalyst is required; ammonia simply burns in an atmosphere of pure oxygen with a yellowish-green flame.

\[ 4NH_3 + 3O_2 \longrightarrow 2N_2 \uparrow + 6H_2O \]
2. Catalytic Oxidation of Ammonia:

Condition: Platinum (Pt) catalyst, heated to approximately 800°C.

\[ 4NH_3 + 5O_2 \xrightarrow{Pt, \ 800^\circ C} 4NO \uparrow + 6H_2O + \text{Heat} \]

(1 Mark for each correct equation, 1 Mark for mentioning conditions clearly)

Q7. Write balanced chemical equations for the reaction of dilute hydrochloric acid with: (a) Sodium sulphite (b) Lead nitrate solution (c) Sodium hydrogen carbonate.
[3 Marks]

(a) \[ Na_2SO_3 + 2HCl \longrightarrow 2NaCl + H_2O + SO_2 \uparrow \] (1 Mark)

(b) \[ Pb(NO_3)_2 + 2HCl \longrightarrow PbCl_2 \downarrow + 2HNO_3 \] (1 Mark)

(c) \[ NaHCO_3 + HCl \longrightarrow NaCl + H_2O + CO_2 \uparrow \] (1 Mark)

Q8. Dry ammonia gas is passed over heated Copper (II) oxide. (a) State what you observe. (b) Write the balanced chemical equation.
[3 Marks]

(a) Observation: The black Copper (II) oxide is reduced to a reddish-brown metal (Copper). Colourless droplets of water condense on cooler parts of the tube. (1.5 Marks)

(b) Equation:

\[ 3CuO + 2NH_3 \longrightarrow 3Cu + 3H_2O + N_2 \uparrow \]

(1.5 Marks for balanced equation)

Q9. Explain the Fountain Experiment for Hydrogen Chloride gas. State the two specific properties demonstrated.
[3 Marks]

Explanation: A dry round-bottomed flask filled with dry HCl gas is fitted with a stopper containing a dropper with water and a jet tube dipped in blue litmus solution. When a few drops of water are squeezed into the flask, HCl dissolves instantly, creating a partial vacuum. Atmospheric pressure pushes the blue litmus solution up the jet tube, which emerges as a red fountain (since HCl is acidic). (2 Marks)

Properties Demonstrated:
1. High solubility of HCl gas in water.
2. The acidic nature of the gas/solution. (1 Mark)

Q10. Write balanced equations for the reaction of chlorine gas with: (a) Excess of ammonia. (b) Ammonia, where chlorine is in excess. Name the highly explosive product.
[3 Marks]

(a) Excess Ammonia: \[ 8NH_3 + 3Cl_2 \longrightarrow 6NH_4Cl + N_2 \uparrow \] (1 Mark)

(b) Excess Chlorine: \[ NH_3 + 3Cl_2 \longrightarrow NCl_3 + 3HCl \] (1 Mark)

Explosive Product: Nitrogen trichloride (\(NCl_3\)). (1 Mark)

SECTION C (15 Marks)
Q11. With reference to the lab preparation of ammonia from ammonium chloride: (a) Equation (b) Why is Calcium hydroxide preferred? (c) Drying agent and why conc. H2SO4 is not used (d) Collection method.
[5 Marks]
(a) Equation:
\[ 2NH_4Cl + Ca(OH)_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} CaCl_2 + 2H_2O + 2NH_3 \uparrow \]
(1 Mark)
(b) Calcium Hydroxide preference: It is preferred because it is cheap, non-deliquescent (unlike NaOH or KOH which absorb moisture and stick to the flask). (1 Mark)
(c) Drying Agent: Quicklime (\(CaO\)). (1 Mark)
Why Conc. \(H_2SO_4\) is not used: Ammonia is a basic gas, and it reacts with concentrated sulphuric acid to form a salt (Ammonium sulphate), hence it cannot be used to dry the gas.
(\(2NH_3 + H_2SO_4 \longrightarrow (NH_4)_2SO_4\)). (1 Mark)
(d) Collection: It is collected by downward displacement of air because it is lighter than air (vapor density = 8.5) and highly soluble in water. (1 Mark)
Q12. Haber's Process: (a) Reactants & ratio (b) Temp, pressure, catalyst (c) Equation (d) Two uses of ammonia.
[5 Marks]
(a) Reactants & Ratio: Nitrogen and Hydrogen in the volume ratio of 1 : 3. (1 Mark)
(b) Optimum Conditions:
Temperature: 450°C - 500°C
Pressure: 200 - 900 atm
Catalyst: Finely divided iron (with molybdenum as a promoter). (2 Marks)
(c) Equation:
\[ N_2 + 3H_2 \rightleftharpoons 2NH_3 + \text{Heat} \]
(1 Mark)
(d) Uses: 1. Manufacture of nitrogenous fertilizers (e.g., urea, ammonium nitrate). 2. Used as a refrigerant gas. (1 Mark)
Q13. Chemical equations and observations: (a) Dilute HCl + FeS (b) AgNO3 + dil HCl, followed by excess NH4OH (c) Aqueous NH3 + FeCl3 solution.
[5 Marks]
(a) Reaction of HCl and FeS:

Gas Evolved: Hydrogen Sulphide (\(H_2S\)) - a gas with the smell of rotten eggs.

\[ FeS + 2HCl \longrightarrow FeCl_2 + H_2S \uparrow \]
(1.5 Marks)
(b) Reaction of \(AgNO_3\), HCl, and excess \(NH_4OH\):

Observation & Equation 1: A curdy white precipitate of Silver chloride is formed.

\[ AgNO_3 + HCl \longrightarrow AgCl \downarrow + HNO_3 \]

Observation & Equation 2: The white precipitate dissolves in excess ammonium hydroxide to form a clear, colourless solution of Diammine silver (I) chloride.

\[ AgCl + 2NH_4OH \longrightarrow [Ag(NH_3)_2]Cl + 2H_2O \]
(2 Marks)
(c) Reaction of Aqueous Ammonia and \(FeCl_3\):

Observation: A reddish-brown precipitate of Iron (III) hydroxide is formed.

\[ FeCl_3 + 3NH_4OH \longrightarrow Fe(OH)_3 \downarrow + 3NH_4Cl \]
(1.5 Marks)
