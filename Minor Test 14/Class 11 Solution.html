Q1. Write the official IUPAC name and the symbol of the element with atomic number 114.
[1 Mark]
Flerovium (Fl)

Q2. Which element out of \( \text{F} \) and \( \text{Cl} \) has a more negative electron gain enthalpy?
[1 Mark]
Chlorine (\( \text{Cl} \))

Explanation: Due to the very small size of the Fluorine (\( \text{F} \)) atom, there are strong inter-electronic repulsions in the relatively compact \( 2\text{p} \) subshell, making the incoming electron feel less attraction from the nucleus compared to the larger \( 3\text{p} \) subshell of Chlorine.
Q3. Identify the element which has the general outer electronic configuration of \( \text{ns}^2 \text{np}^5 \) and belongs to the 3rd period.
[1 Mark]
Chlorine (\( \text{Cl} \))

Explanation: Period = 3 means \( \text{n}=3 \). Configuration is \( 3\text{s}^2 3\text{p}^5 \). Total electrons = \( 1\text{s}^2 2\text{s}^2 2\text{p}^6 3\text{s}^2 3\text{p}^5 = 17 \). Atomic number 17 is Chlorine.
Q4. Arrange the following in decreasing order of their ionic radius: \( \text{Mg}^{2+}, \text{O}^{2-}, \text{Na}^+, \text{F}^-, \text{N}^{3-} \).
[1 Mark]
\( \text{N}^{3-} > \text{O}^{2-} > \text{F}^- > \text{Na}^+ > \text{Mg}^{2+} \)

Explanation: These are isoelectronic species (all have 10 electrons). For isoelectronic ions, the ionic radius decreases as the nuclear charge (atomic number, Z) increases because the electrons are pulled closer to the nucleus. (Z: N=7, O=8, F=9, Na=11, Mg=12).
Q5. Why is the first ionization enthalpy of Nitrogen higher than that of Oxygen?
[1 Mark]
Reason: Nitrogen has an exactly half-filled \( 2\text{p} \) orbital (\( 1\text{s}^2 2\text{s}^2 2\text{p}^3 \)). Half-filled subshells are extra stable due to exchange energy and symmetry. Therefore, more energy is required to remove an electron from \( \text{N} \) compared to \( \text{O} \) (\( 2\text{p}^4 \)).
Q6. What is the basic difference between the approach of Mendeleev's Periodic Law and the Modern Periodic Law?
[1 Mark]
Mendeleev's law is based on Atomic Mass (properties are periodic functions of atomic weights), whereas the Modern Periodic Law is based on Atomic Number (properties are periodic functions of atomic numbers).

Q9. Assertion (A): The radius of a cation is always smaller than its parent atom. Reason (R): A cation is formed by the loss of electrons, resulting in an increase in effective nuclear charge per electron.
[1 Mark]

Q10. How does the basic character of oxides vary down a group in the periodic table?
[1 Mark]
The basic character of oxides increases down a group. This is because atomic size increases and ionization enthalpy decreases down a group, causing the metallic character (and thus the basic nature of their oxides) to increase.
SECTION B (2 Marks Each)
Q11. Explain why the electron gain enthalpy of noble gases is large and positive.
[2 Marks]
Noble gases have a completely filled stable octet/duplet configuration (\( \text{ns}^2\text{np}^6 \)).
Because they are highly stable, they have no tendency to accept an additional electron. To add an electron, it must enter the next higher principal quantum level (\( \text{n}+1 \)), which requires a substantial input of energy (to overcome repulsions and force the electron into a higher state). Hence, their electron gain enthalpies are large and positive.
Q12. The first IE of Mg is higher than Al, but its second IE is lower than that of Al. Explain.
[2 Marks]
First IE (\( \Delta_iH_1 \)): \( \text{Mg} (1\text{s}^2 2\text{s}^2 2\text{p}^6 3\text{s}^2) \) vs \( \text{Al} (1\text{s}^2 2\text{s}^2 2\text{p}^6 3\text{s}^2 3\text{p}^1) \). It is harder to remove an electron from the fully filled and more penetrating \( 3\text{s} \) orbital of \( \text{Mg} \) than the single \( 3\text{p} \) electron of \( \text{Al} \). Hence \( \text{IE}_1 (\text{Mg}) > \text{IE}_1 (\text{Al}) \).

Second IE (\( \Delta_iH_2 \)): After losing 1 electron, \( \text{Mg}^+ \) is \( 3\text{s}^1 \) and \( \text{Al}^+ \) is \( 3\text{s}^2 \). Now, \( \text{Al}^+ \) has a stable fully filled \( 3\text{s} \) orbital, whereas \( \text{Mg}^+ \) has a half-filled \( 3\text{s}^1 \). Removing the second electron from the stable \( 3\text{s}^2 \) configuration of \( \text{Al}^+ \) requires much more energy. Hence \( \text{IE}_2 (\text{Al}) > \text{IE}_2 (\text{Mg}) \).
Q13. Predict the period, group number, and block of the element with atomic number 35. State whether it is a metal or a non-metal.
[2 Marks]
Atomic Number \( Z=35 \).
Electronic Configuration: \( 1\text{s}^2 2\text{s}^2 2\text{p}^6 3\text{s}^2 3\text{p}^6 4\text{s}^2 3\text{d}^{10} 4\text{p}^5 \) or \( [\text{Ar}] 3\text{d}^{10} 4\text{s}^2 4\text{p}^5 \).
  • Period: 4 (Highest principal quantum number n=4)
  • Block: p-block (Last electron enters p orbital)
  • Group: \( 10 + 2 (\text{s}) + 5 (\text{p}) = 17 \)
  • Nature: Non-metal (It is a halogen, Bromine).
Q14. Define electronegativity. How is it different from electron gain enthalpy?
[2 Marks]
Electronegativity is a qualitative measure of the tendency of an atom in a chemical compound to attract the shared pair of electrons towards itself.

Difference: Electron gain enthalpy is the energy change when a completely isolated gaseous atom accepts an electron (a measurable, quantitative property of an isolated atom). Electronegativity is a relative tendency of an atom in a bonded molecule (not an isolated property and has no specific units).
Q15. Consider the elements B, C, N, F, Si. (a) Which is a metalloid? (b) Arrange in increasing non-metallic character.
[2 Marks]
(a) Metalloid: Silicon (\( \text{Si} \)) [Boron is also sometimes considered, but Si is the classic textbook metalloid. B is acceptable].
(b) Non-metallic character increases across a period and decreases down a group. Si is in period 3 (least non-metallic among these), the rest are period 2.
Increasing order: \( \text{Si} < \text{B} < \text{C} < \text{N} < \text{F} \).
SECTION C (3 Marks Each)
Q16. What are isoelectronic species? Give the formula of a species isoelectronic with (i) F⁻ (ii) Ar (iii) Mg²⁺.
[3 Marks]
Isoelectronic Species: Atoms, molecules, or ions containing the exact same number of electrons are called isoelectronic species.
Examples:
(i) \( \text{F}^- \) has 10 electrons (\( 9+1 \)). Isoelectronic: \( \text{O}^{2-}, \text{Ne}, \text{Na}^+ \) (Any one is correct).
(ii) \( \text{Ar} \) has 18 electrons. Isoelectronic: \( \text{Cl}^-, \text{K}^+, \text{Ca}^{2+}, \text{S}^{2-} \).
(iii) \( \text{Mg}^{2+} \) has 10 electrons (\( 12-2 \)). Isoelectronic: \( \text{Ne}, \text{F}^-, \text{Na}^+, \text{Al}^{3+} \).
Q17. Based on the given data table, identify (a) reactive non-metal, (b) active alkali metal, (c) noble gas.
[3 Marks]
(a) Reactive Non-metal: II. Element II has a highly negative electron gain enthalpy (-328 kJ/mol) and high ionization enthalpies, which is characteristic of halogens (like Fluorine or Chlorine).
(b) Active Alkali Metal: I. Element I has a very low first IE (520) but an extremely high second IE (7300), indicating it has one valence electron which it loses easily, but removing the second disrupts a stable noble gas core (characteristic of Group 1 metals like Li).

Q18. Consider N, P, O, S. Arrange in increasing (a) first ionization enthalpy (b) non-metallic character.
[3 Marks]
(a) First Ionization Enthalpy: \( \text{S} < \text{P} < \text{O} < \text{N} \)
Reason: IE increases across a period and decreases down a group. Hence period 2 elements (N,O) > period 3 elements (P,S). Between P and S, P has a stable half-filled \( 3\text{p}^3 \) configuration, so \( \text{P} > \text{S} \). Between N and O, N has a stable half-filled \( 2\text{p}^3 \) configuration, so \( \text{N} > \text{O} \).
(b) Non-metallic character: \( \text{P} < \text{S} < \text{N} < \text{O} \)
Reason: Non-metallic character increases across a period (left to right) and decreases down a group. Oxygen is top right (most non-metallic here), Phosphorus is bottom left (least).
Q19. Explain: (a) Halogens act as strong oxidizing agents. (b) Atomic radius of Ga < Al.
[3 Marks]
(a) Halogens have the general configuration \( \text{ns}^2\text{np}^5 \). They are one electron short of a noble gas configuration. They have very high electronegativities and highly negative electron gain enthalpies, meaning they readily accept electrons from other substances, thereby oxidizing them.
(b) Gallium (Ga) is below Aluminum (Al) in Group 13. Before Ga, the 3d-orbitals are filled (\( 3\text{d}^{10} \)). The d-electrons offer poor shielding/screening effect for the outer 4p electrons from the increased nuclear charge (+10). Due to this poor shielding, the effective nuclear charge increases significantly, pulling the valence electrons closer and making Ga smaller than Al.
Q20. Chemical equations to show (a) Na₂O is basic (b) Cl₂O₇ is acidic (c) Al₂O₃ is amphoteric.
[3 Marks]
(a) Basic nature of \( \text{Na}_2\text{O} \): It reacts with water to form a strong base (Alkali).
$$ \text{Na}_2\text{O}(s) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \rightarrow 2\text{NaOH}(aq) $$
(b) Acidic nature of \( \text{Cl}_2\text{O}_7 \): It reacts with water to form a strong acid (Perchloric acid).
$$ \text{Cl}_2\text{O}_7(l) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \rightarrow 2\text{HClO}_4(aq) $$
(c) Amphoteric nature of \( \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 \): (Showing basic behavior by reacting with an acid)
$$ \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3(s) + 6\text{HCl}(aq) \rightarrow 2\text{AlCl}_3(aq) + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) $$
(Alternatively, reacting with NaOH to form sodium aluminate shows its acidic behavior).

Q22. (a) Modern Periodic Law, (b) Shielding effect and IE down a group, (c) Why Alkali / Halogens names?
[5 Marks]
(a) Modern Periodic Law: "The physical and chemical properties of the elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers." It is based on the fundamental property of atomic number (number of protons/electrons), which governs the electronic configuration.

(b) Shielding Effect: In multi-electron atoms, the electrons in the inner shells repel the electrons in the outermost valence shell, pushing them outwards and partially shielding them from the attractive pull of the nucleus. This is called the shielding or screening effect.
Effect on IE: As we move down a group, the number of inner electron shells increases, which greatly increases the shielding effect. This outweighs the increase in nuclear charge, resulting in a decrease in the effective nuclear charge felt by valence electrons. Thus, Ionization Enthalpy decreases down a group.

(c) Group 1 elements are called alkali metals because they react with water to form highly alkaline (basic) hydroxides (e.g., NaOH, KOH).
Group 17 elements are called halogens (from Greek: halo = salt, gens = producing) because they react with metals to form various salts (e.g., NaCl, CaF2).
Q23. (a) Radius of Na/Na⁺ vs Cl/Cl⁻, (b) Anomalous Li, (c) Covalency vs Ox State of Al.
[5 Marks]
(a)
- \( \text{Na}^+ \) is formed by losing an electron. The entire 3rd shell is lost, and the remaining 10 electrons are pulled strongly by 11 protons, increasing effective nuclear charge. Thus, cation < parent atom (\( 95 < 186 \)).
- \( \text{Cl}^- \) is formed by gaining an electron. The electron cloud expands due to increased inter-electronic repulsions among the 18 electrons, while the nuclear charge remains 17. Thus, anion > parent atom (\( 181 > 99 \)).

(b) Anomalous properties of Lithium (Li): Li shows anomalous behavior because:
1. Exceptionally small size of its atom and ion.
2. Extremely high polarizing power (charge/radius ratio) leading to covalent character in its compounds.
3. Absence of vacant d-orbitals in its valence shell.

(c) In the complex ion \( [\text{Al}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{3+} \):
- Covalency is the number of bonds formed by the atom. Aluminum is surrounded by and bonded to 6 water molecules via coordinate covalent bonds, so its covalency is 6.
- Oxidation state is the formal charge. Since water is a neutral ligand, the entire +3 charge of the complex belongs to the Aluminum ion. Thus, oxidation state is +3.
Q24. (a) Factors affecting electron gain enthalpy, (b) Second EGE of Oxygen, (c) Why 3rd period has 8 elements.
[5 Marks]
(a) Factors influencing Electron Gain Enthalpy:
1. Atomic Size: As size increases, the distance of the added electron from the nucleus increases, making EGE less negative.
2. Nuclear Charge: Greater nuclear charge exerts stronger attraction, making EGE more negative.
3. Electronic Configuration: Elements with exactly half-filled or fully filled orbitals are very stable, leading to positive or less negative EGE.

(b) Second EGE of Oxygen:
First EGE: \( \text{O}(g) + e^- \rightarrow \text{O}^-(g) \) (Energy is released, so EGE is negative).
Second EGE: \( \text{O}^-(g) + e^- \rightarrow \text{O}^{2-}(g) \). Here, an electron is being forced onto an already negatively charged \( \text{O}^- \) ion. The severe electrostatic repulsion between the anion and the incoming electron requires energy to be supplied to overcome it. Hence, the second EGE is highly positive.

(c) Third period elements:
The third period corresponds to the filling of the 3rd principal shell (n=3). The available subshells are 3s, 3p, and 3d. According to the Aufbau principle (based on n+l rule), the energies of subshells follow the order 3s < 3p < 4s < 3d.
Thus, only the 3s (1 orbital = 2 e-) and 3p (3 orbitals = 6 e-) subshells are filled before the 4th period begins. Total electrons = \( 2 + 6 = 8 \). Therefore, the 3rd period contains exactly 8 elements.
Q25. General trends of (a) Atomic radius, (b) Metallic character, (c) Valency.
[5 Marks]
(a) Atomic Radius:
- Across a period: Decreases. Reason: Electrons are added to the same principal shell, while nuclear charge increases. This increases the effective nuclear charge, pulling electrons closer.
- Down a group: Increases. Reason: New principal electron shells are added. The shielding effect increases, outweighing the increased nuclear charge, causing size to increase.

(b) Metallic Character (tendency to lose electrons):
- Across a period: Decreases. Reason: As atomic size decreases and effective nuclear charge increases, ionization enthalpy increases, making it harder to lose electrons.
- Down a group: Increases. Reason: Atomic size increases and ionization enthalpy decreases, making it easier for the atom to lose valence electrons.

(c) Valency (combining capacity):
- Across a period (w.r.t Hydrogen or Oxygen): Valency first increases from 1 to 4, and then decreases to 0 (for H) or increases up to 7 (for O). For example, with respect to Hydrogen in Period 3: NaH(1), MgH2(2), AlH3(3), SiH4(4), PH3(3), H2S(2), HCl(1). This happens because it depends on the number of valence electrons (Valency = valence e⁻ if ≤ 4, or 8 - valence e⁻ if > 4).
- Down a group: Remains constant. Reason: All elements in a group have the same number of valence electrons and similar outer electronic configurations.
