SECTION A
Q1. Drift velocity variation with (i) temperature, (ii) potential difference.
(i) Temperature: When temperature increases, the thermal speed of electrons increases, leading to more frequent collisions. Thus, relaxation time (\(\tau\)) decreases. Since \(v_d \propto \tau\), the drift velocity decreases. [1 Mark]
(ii) Potential Difference (V): We know \(v_d = \frac{eE}{m}\tau = \frac{eV}{mL}\tau\). If V increases (and length L is constant), the electric field E increases, hence drift velocity increases linearly. [1 Mark]
Q2. Wire of \(10 \, \Omega\) stretched to increase length by 10%. New resistance?
Original length = \(l\). New length \(l' = l + 0.10l = 1.1l\).
Since volume remains constant (\(A \cdot l = A' \cdot l'\)), new area \(A' = \frac{A}{1.1}\). [1 Mark]
\(R' = \rho \frac{l'}{A'} = \rho \frac{1.1l}{A / 1.1} = 1.21 \left(\rho \frac{l}{A}\right) = 1.21 R\).
\(R' = 1.21 \times 10 = \mathbf{12.1 \, \Omega}\). [1 Mark]
Q3. Heating coil (100W, 220V) cut in half, connected in parallel. Total power?
Original resistance of the full coil \(R = \frac{V^2}{P} = \frac{(220)^2}{100} = \frac{48400}{100} = 484 \, \Omega\).
When cut into two identical halves, resistance of each half \(R' = \frac{484}{2} = 242 \, \Omega\). [1 Mark]
When both halves are in parallel, equivalent resistance \(R_{eq} = \frac{R'}{2} = \frac{242}{2} = 121 \, \Omega\).
Total power consumed \(P_{total} = \frac{V^2}{R_{eq}} = \frac{(220)^2}{121} = \frac{48400}{121} = \mathbf{400 \text{ W}}\). [1 Mark]
Q4. Potentiometer balance shift from 35.0 cm (1.25V) to 63.0 cm. New emf?
Principle of potentiometer: \(E \propto l \implies E = kl\). [1 Mark]
Therefore, \(\frac{E_1}{E_2} = \frac{l_1}{l_2} \implies \frac{1.25}{E_2} = \frac{35.0}{63.0}\).
\(E_2 = 1.25 \times \frac{63}{35} = 1.25 \times 1.8 = \mathbf{2.25 \text{ V}}\). [1 Mark]
Q5. Temp coeff. \(R_0=5.00 \Omega, R_{100}=5.23 \Omega, R_t=5.795 \Omega\). Find \(t\).
Formula for temperature: \(t = \frac{R_t - R_0}{R_{100} - R_0} \times 100\). [1 Mark]
\(t = \frac{5.795 - 5.00}{5.23 - 5.00} \times 100 = \frac{0.795}{0.23} \times 100 = 3.4565 \times 100 \approx \mathbf{345.65 {}^{\circ}\text{C}}\). [1 Mark]
SECTION B
Q6. Cell \(E, r\). Current \(0.5\text{A}\) via \(12\Omega\), \(0.25\text{A}\) via \(25\Omega\). Find \(E, r\).
Using equation \(E = I(R + r)\):
Case 1: \(E = 0.5(12 + r) \implies E = 6 + 0.5r\)   --- (Eq 1) [1 Mark]
Case 2: \(E = 0.25(25 + r) \implies E = 6.25 + 0.25r\)   --- (Eq 2) [1 Mark]
Equating Eq 1 and Eq 2:
\(6 + 0.5r = 6.25 + 0.25r \implies 0.25r = 0.25 \implies \mathbf{r = 1 \, \Omega}\).
Substitute \(r\) in Eq 1: \(E = 6 + 0.5(1) = \mathbf{6.5 \text{ V}}\). [1 Mark]
Q7. Meter bridge: null at \(39.5\text{cm}\), \(Y=12.5\Omega\). Find \(X\). Interchange effect?
(i) Balanced condition: \(\frac{X}{Y} = \frac{l}{100-l}\). [1 Mark]
\(\frac{X}{12.5} = \frac{39.5}{100 - 39.5} = \frac{39.5}{60.5}\)
\(X = 12.5 \times \frac{39.5}{60.5} \approx \mathbf{8.16 \, \Omega}\). [1 Mark]
(ii) If the galvanometer and cell are interchanged at the balance point, the balance condition (\(P/Q = R/S\)) remains mathematically intact. Therefore, the galvanometer will not show any current (null point remains unchanged). [1 Mark]
Q8. Principle of potentiometer. How to increase sensitivity?
Principle: When a steady current flows through a wire of uniform cross-section and composition, the potential drop across any length of the wire is directly proportional to that length. \((V \propto l \implies V = kl)\), where \(k\) is potential gradient. [1.5 Marks]
Sensitivity: Sensitivity increases when the potential gradient (\(k = V/L\)) decreases, allowing smaller potential differences to be measured accurately.
This is achieved by:
1. Increasing the length (\(L\)) of the potentiometer wire.
2. Decreasing the current in the main circuit using a rheostat. [1.5 Marks]
Q9. 24 cells mixed grouping for max current. Find \(m, n\) and \(I_{max}\).
Total cells \(mn = 24\).
Condition for maximum current: External resistance must equal total equivalent internal resistance of the battery array.
\(R = \frac{nr}{m} \implies 3 = \frac{n(0.5)}{m} \implies 3m = 0.5n \implies n = 6m\). [1 Mark]
Substitute \(n = 6m\) into \(mn = 24\):
\(m(6m) = 24 \implies 6m^2 = 24 \implies m^2 = 4 \implies \mathbf{m = 2}\) (rows).
Therefore, \(n = 24 / 2 = \mathbf{12}\) (cells per row). [1 Mark]
Maximum current \(I_{max} = \frac{mnE}{mR + nr} = \frac{24 \times 1.5}{2(3) + 12(0.5)} = \frac{36}{6 + 6} = \frac{36}{12} = \mathbf{3 \text{ A}}\). [1 Mark]
Q10. V-I graph intercepts: \(V=1.5\text{V}\), \(I=3.0\text{A}\). Find \(E, r, P_{max}\).
(i) The voltage intercept (when \(I=0\)) gives the EMF of the cell. \(\mathbf{E = 1.5 \text{ V}}\). [1 Mark]
(ii) The current intercept (when \(V=0\)) gives the short-circuit current \(I_{sc}\).
\(I_{sc} = \frac{E}{r} \implies 3.0 = \frac{1.5}{r} \implies \mathbf{r = 0.5 \, \Omega}\). [1 Mark]
(iii) Maximum power is delivered when \(R = r = 0.5 \, \Omega\).
\(P_{max} = \frac{E^2}{4r} = \frac{(1.5)^2}{4(0.5)} = \frac{2.25}{2} = \mathbf{1.125 \text{ W}}\). [1 Mark]
SECTION C
Q11. Equivalent resistance of infinite ladder network (\(1\Omega\) series, \(2\Omega\) shunt).
Let the equivalent resistance of the entire infinite ladder between points A and B be \(R_{eq}\).
Since the network is infinite, adding or removing one repeating unit (one \(1 \, \Omega\) and one \(2 \, \Omega\) resistor) from the front does not alter the total equivalent resistance. [1 Mark]
Therefore, the circuit can be modeled as the first \(1 \, \Omega\) resistor in series with a parallel combination of the first \(2 \, \Omega\) resistor and the rest of the infinite network (\(R_{eq}\)). [1 Mark]
Mathematically: \(R_{eq} = 1 + \left( \frac{2 \times R_{eq}}{2 + R_{eq}} \right)\) [1 Mark]
Multiplying by \((2 + R_{eq})\) to solve:
\(R_{eq}(2 + R_{eq}) = 1(2 + R_{eq}) + 2R_{eq}\)
\(2R_{eq} + R_{eq}^2 = 2 + 3R_{eq}\)
\(R_{eq}^2 - R_{eq} - 2 = 0\) [1 Mark]
Factoring the quadratic equation:
\((R_{eq} - 2)(R_{eq} + 1) = 0\)
Since resistance cannot be negative, \(R_{eq} = \mathbf{2 \, \Omega}\). [1 Mark]
Q12. Kirchhoff's laws on two-loop circuit. Current through \(5\Omega\) middle branch.
Let current \(I_1\) flow from the 10V battery (clockwise in left loop) and \(I_2\) flow from the 4V battery (anticlockwise in right loop). Both currents meet at the top node and flow downwards through the middle \(5 \, \Omega\) resistor. Thus, current through middle = \((I_1 + I_2)\). [1 Mark]
Loop 1 (Left Loop, Clockwise KVL):
\(+10 - 2I_1 - 5(I_1 + I_2) = 0\)
\(10 = 7I_1 + 5I_2\)    --- (Eq 1) [1 Mark]
Loop 2 (Right Loop, Anticlockwise KVL):
\(+4 - 3I_2 - 5(I_1 + I_2) = 0\)
\(4 = 5I_1 + 8I_2\)    --- (Eq 2) [1 Mark]
Solving simultaneously:
Multiply Eq 1 by 8: \(56I_1 + 40I_2 = 80\)
Multiply Eq 2 by 5: \(25I_1 + 40I_2 = 20\)
Subtracting: \(31I_1 = 60 \implies I_1 = \frac{60}{31} \text{ A}\).
Substitute \(I_1\) in Eq 2: \(5(\frac{60}{31}) + 8I_2 = 4 \implies \frac{300}{31} + 8I_2 = \frac{124}{31} \implies 8I_2 = -\frac{176}{31} \implies I_2 = -\frac{22}{31} \text{ A}\). (Negative sign implies actual flow is opposite to assumed direction). [1 Mark]
Current through \(5 \, \Omega\) resistor = \(I_1 + I_2 = \frac{60}{31} + \left(-\frac{22}{31}\right) = \mathbf{\frac{38}{31} \text{ A} \approx 1.23 \text{ A}}\).
Direction: Downwards (since the net result is positive). [1 Mark]
Q13. Wheatstone condition and unknown R calculation.
(a) Condition: A Wheatstone bridge is balanced when no current flows through the galvanometer. This happens when the ratio of resistances in adjacent arms is equal: \(\frac{P}{Q} = \frac{R}{S}\). [1 Mark]
(b) Calculation: For zero deflection, the bridge must be balanced.
Comparing with standard bridge arms: \(R_{AB} = 10 \, \Omega, R_{BC} = 20 \, \Omega\).
\(R_{AD} = 15 \, \Omega\), and \(R_{DC}\) is the parallel combination of \(R\) and \(60 \, \Omega\). Let it be \(R_p\). [1 Mark]
Balance condition: \(\frac{R_{AB}}{R_{BC}} = \frac{R_{AD}}{R_p}\)
\(\frac{10}{20} = \frac{15}{R_p} \implies \frac{1}{2} = \frac{15}{R_p}\) [1 Mark]
Solving for \(R_p\): \(R_p = 30 \, \Omega\).
Now, \(R_p\) is formed by \(R\) and \(60 \, \Omega\) in parallel: \(\frac{1}{R_p} = \frac{1}{R} + \frac{1}{60}\). [1 Mark]
\(\frac{1}{30} = \frac{1}{R} + \frac{1}{60} \implies \frac{1}{R} = \frac{1}{30} - \frac{1}{60} = \frac{2 - 1}{60} = \frac{1}{60}\).
\(\mathbf{R = 60 \, \Omega}\). [1 Mark]
Q14. Heating coils: time for series and parallel combinations.
Let the heat required to boil the water be \(H\).
When connected individually: \(H = \frac{V^2}{R_1} t_1 \implies R_1 = \frac{V^2 t_1}{H}\) and \(R_2 = \frac{V^2 t_2}{H}\). [1 Mark]
(a) Series: Equivalent resistance \(R_s = R_1 + R_2 = \frac{V^2}{H}(t_1 + t_2)\).
Time taken \(t_s\): \(H = \frac{V^2}{R_s} t_s \implies t_s = \frac{H R_s}{V^2} = \frac{H}{V^2} \left( \frac{V^2}{H}(t_1 + t_2) \right)\).
\(t_s = t_1 + t_2 = 15 + 30 = \mathbf{45 \text{ min}}\). [1.5 Marks]
(b) Parallel: Equivalent resistance \(R_p = \frac{R_1 R_2}{R_1 + R_2}\).
Time taken \(t_p\): \(t_p = \frac{H R_p}{V^2} = \frac{H}{V^2} \left( \frac{\frac{V^2 t_1}{H} \cdot \frac{V^2 t_2}{H}}{\frac{V^2 t_1}{H} + \frac{V^2 t_2}{H}} \right) = \frac{H}{V^2} \left( \frac{V^2}{H} \cdot \frac{t_1 t_2}{t_1 + t_2} \right)\).
\(t_p = \frac{t_1 t_2}{t_1 + t_2} = \frac{15 \times 30}{15 + 30} = \frac{450}{45} = \mathbf{10 \text{ min}}\). [2.5 Marks]
Q15. (a) Derivation for cells in series. (b) Find \(V_X - V_Y\) in given branch.
(a) Derivation: Consider two cells \(E_1, r_1\) and \(E_2, r_2\) connected in series. Current \(I\) flows through them. Let potentials at nodes be \(V_A\), \(V_B\), \(V_C\).
\(V_A - V_B = E_1 - I r_1\)
\(V_B - V_C = E_2 - I r_2\) [1 Mark]
Total potential difference \(V = V_A - V_C = (V_A - V_B) + (V_B - V_C) = (E_1 - I r_1) + (E_2 - I r_2)\).
\(V = (E_1 + E_2) - I(r_1 + r_2)\). [1 Mark]
Comparing with standard equation \(V = E_{eq} - I r_{eq}\), we get:
\(\mathbf{E_{eq} = E_1 + E_2}\) and \(\mathbf{r_{eq} = r_1 + r_2}\). [1 Mark] [3 Marks]
(b) Potential Difference \(V_X - V_Y\):
Apply KVL along the branch from X to Y. Current \(I = 2 \text{ A}\) flows in direction of tracing.
\(V_X - (\text{Drop across 4V battery, going + to -}) - I(\text{Internal res 1\Omega}) - I(\text{Res 2\Omega}) + (\text{Rise across 6V battery, going - to +}) - I(\text{Internal res 2\Omega}) = V_Y\). [1 Mark]
Mathematically:
\(V_X - 4 - 2(1) - 2(2) + 6 - 2(2) = V_Y\)
\(V_X - 4 - 2 - 4 + 6 - 4 = V_Y\)
\(V_X - 10 + 2 = V_Y \implies V_X - 8 = V_Y \implies \mathbf{V_X - V_Y = 8 \text{ V}}\). [1 Mark]
