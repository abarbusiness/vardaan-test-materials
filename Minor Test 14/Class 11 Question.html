MINOR TEST 14: CLASSIFICATION OF ELEMENTS & PERIODICITY
Class: 11 Subject: Chemistry Time: 1.5 Hours Max. Marks: 60
General Instructions:
  • All questions are compulsory. Follow strictly the rationalised NCERT syllabus.
  • Section A: Q1 to Q10 are very short answer/MCQ/Assertion-Reason type questions carrying 1 mark each.
  • Section B: Q11 to Q15 are short answer questions carrying 2 marks each.
  • Section C: Q16 to Q20 are short answer questions carrying 3 marks each.
  • Section D: Q21 to Q25 are long answer questions carrying 5 marks each.
  • Write precise answers. Use appropriate chemical symbols and electronic configurations where required.
SECTION A (10 Marks)(Very Short Answer / Objective Type - 1 Mark Each)
1.
Write the official IUPAC name and the symbol of the element with atomic number 114.
2.
Which element out of \( \text{F} \) and \( \text{Cl} \) has a more negative electron gain enthalpy?
3.
Identify the element which has the general outer electronic configuration of \( \text{ns}^2 \text{np}^5 \) and belongs to the 3rd period.
4.
Arrange the following in decreasing order of their ionic radius: \( \text{Mg}^{2+}, \text{O}^{2-}, \text{Na}^+, \text{F}^-, \text{N}^{3-} \).
5.
Why is the first ionization enthalpy of Nitrogen higher than that of Oxygen?
6.
What is the basic difference between the approach of Mendeleev's Periodic Law and the Modern Periodic Law?
7.
Write the general outer electronic configuration of the d-block elements.
8.
Name the most electronegative element and the element with the highest electron gain enthalpy in the periodic table.
9.
Assertion (A): The radius of a cation is always smaller than its parent atom.
Reason (R): A cation is formed by the loss of one or more electrons, resulting in an increase in effective nuclear charge per electron.
(a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true but R is false.
(d) A is false but R is true.
10.
How does the basic character of oxides vary down a group in the periodic table?
SECTION B (10 Marks)(Short Answer Type - 2 Marks Each)
11.
Explain why the electron gain enthalpy of noble gases is large and positive.
12.
The first ionization enthalpy (\( \Delta_iH_1 \)) of Magnesium is higher than that of Aluminum, but its second ionization enthalpy (\( \Delta_iH_2 \)) is lower than that of Aluminum. Explain why.
13.
Predict the period, group number, and block of the element with atomic number 35. State whether it is a metal or a non-metal.
14.
Define electronegativity. How is it different from electron gain enthalpy? State one key difference.
15.
Consider the elements \( \text{B}, \text{C}, \text{N}, \text{F}, \text{Si} \).
(a) Which of these is a metalloid?
(b) Arrange them in increasing order of their non-metallic character.
SECTION C (15 Marks)(Short Answer Type - 3 Marks Each)
16.
What are isoelectronic species? Give the formula of a species that will be isoelectronic with each of the following atoms or ions:
(i) \( \text{F}^- \)
(ii) \( \text{Ar} \)
(iii) \( \text{Mg}^{2+} \)

Identify the element which is likely to be:

18.
Consider the elements \( \text{N}, \text{P}, \text{O} \) and \( \text{S} \).
(a) Arrange them in the increasing order of their first ionization enthalpy. Give a valid reason.
(b) Arrange them in increasing order of their non-metallic character.
19.
Explain the following phenomena based on periodic properties:
(a) Halogens act as strong oxidizing agents.
(b) The atomic radius of Gallium (135 pm) is less than that of Aluminum (143 pm).
20.
Write the chemical equations to show that:
(a) \( \text{Na}_2\text{O} \) is a basic oxide.
(b) \( \text{Cl}_2\text{O}_7 \) is an acidic oxide.
(c) \( \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 \) is amphoteric in nature (write only one reaction showing its acidic or basic behavior).
SECTION D (25 Marks)(Long Answer Type - 5 Marks Each)
21.
An element 'X' has an atomic number of 34.
(a) Write the complete electronic configuration of element 'X'.
(b) Predict its group number, period number, and block in the periodic table.
(c) Is it a metal or a non-metal? Justify.
(d) Predict the formula of the compound it forms with Sodium (\( \text{Na} \)).
(e) Compare its first ionization enthalpy with that of the element having atomic number 33, and state the reason.
22.
(a) State the Modern Periodic Law. What is the fundamental property on which it is based?
(b) What is meant by the 'shielding effect' (or screening effect)? How does it affect the magnitude of ionization enthalpy of an element down a group?
(c) Why are elements of group 1 called alkali metals and elements of group 17 called halogens?
23.
Account for the following observations thoroughly:
(a) The radius of \( \text{Na}^+ \) (95 pm) is significantly less than that of \( \text{Na} \) atom (186 pm), but the radius of \( \text{Cl}^- \) (181 pm) is more than that of \( \text{Cl} \) atom (99 pm).
(b) Among alkali metals, Lithium (\( \text{Li} \)) shows anomalous properties compared to the rest of the group members. Give two reasons for this behavior.
(c) Covalency of Aluminum in \( [\text{Al}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{3+} \) is 6, while its oxidation state is +3. Explain.
24.
(a) Discuss the factors that influence the magnitude of electron gain enthalpy of elements.
(b) Why is the second electron gain enthalpy of oxygen always positive, even though its first electron gain enthalpy is negative?
(c) Justify why the third period of the periodic table contains 8 elements and not 18 elements.
25.
Describe the general trends in the following properties of elements across a period (from left to right) and down a group (from top to bottom). Support your answers with the underlying atomic reasons:
(a) Atomic radius
(b) Metallic character
(c) Valency (with respect to Hydrogen or Oxygen)
