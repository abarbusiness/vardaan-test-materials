1. Who discovered North America and after which explorer is it named?
[2 Marks]
Answer: North America was discovered by Christopher Columbus in 1492 CE. It is named after the Italian explorer Amerigo Vespucci.
2. Define the term 'mineral' in your own words.
[2 Marks]
Answer: Minerals are naturally-occurring, pure inorganic substances found in the Earth's crust that have definite chemical properties.

[2 Marks]
Answer: Any two of the following:
  • The Western Cordilleras

4. What are 'ores'? Give one example of a metallic ore.
[2 Marks]
Answer: Ores are minerals from which metals can be profitably extracted.
Example: Iron ore (Magnetite/Hematite) or Bauxite (ore of aluminium).
5. State the two main types of mining methods used to extract minerals.
[2 Marks]
Answer: The two main types of mining methods are:
  • Surface mining
  • Underground mining (or shaft mining)
SECTION B
6. Why is the lumbering industry considered important in Canada?
[3 Marks]
Answer: The lumbering industry is vital in Canada because:
  • More than 40% of Canada is covered with Taiga or coniferous forests, providing an abundance of softwood.
  • Softwood is ideal for paper and pulp industries, making Canada a leading global exporter of newsprint.
  • It boosts the economy significantly through the export of lumber products like plywood, furniture, cellulose, and cardboard.

8. Why is it essential to conserve mineral resources? State any two reasons.
[3 Marks]
Answer: It is essential to conserve minerals because:
  • Non-renewable Nature: They take millions of years to form and are non-renewable. Once consumed, they cannot be replenished quickly.
  • Environmental Protection: Mining methods destroy the environment, pollute water and air, and harm natural habitats. Conservation reduces the need for aggressive mining.
9. Write a short note on the Canadian Shield highlighting its economic importance.
[3 Marks]
Answer: The Canadian Shield (or Laurentian Shield) forms the heart of North America, stretching from Hudson Bay to the Great Lakes.
Economic Importance: It is formed of old, hard rocks that contain extremely rich deposits of valuable minerals like gold, silver, copper, nickel, and lead. The region is also rich in water resources due to glacial lakes, which are crucial for industrial and economic development.
10. What are 'fossil fuels'? Name any two widely used fossil fuels.
[3 Marks]
Answer: Fossil fuels are energy resources formed from the dead remains of plants and animals (fossils) that were trapped and buried between layers of sedimentary rocks millions of years ago.
Examples: Coal, Petroleum, and Natural Gas. (Any two)
SECTION C
11. Describe the location and extent of North America. Mention the oceans that border it on its northern, eastern, and western sides.
[5 Marks]
Answer:
Location & Extent: North America is the third-largest continent and lies entirely in the Northern Hemisphere. Its latitudinal extent is from \(7^\circ \text{N}\) to \(85^\circ \text{N}\), and its longitudinal extent is from \(20^\circ \text{W}\) to \(179^\circ \text{W}\). Both the Tropic of Cancer and the Arctic Circle pass through it. It is separated from Asia by the Bering Strait and joined to South America by the Isthmus of Panama.

Bordering Oceans:
  • North: The Arctic Ocean
  • East: The North Atlantic Ocean
  • West: The North Pacific Ocean
12. Explain the different categories of lumberjacks (high riggers, buckers, skidders, haulers) and the specific work they perform in the lumbering industry.
[5 Marks]
Answer: Lumberjacks are divided into specialized categories based on their tasks:
  • High Riggers: They do the daring job of felling the tree. They climb to the top, chop off branches (limbing), tie cables to guide the fall, and cut the main stem using power saws.
  • Sawers and Buckers: Their work begins once the tree is on the ground. They cut the felled tree into standard-sized logs (usually 4.5 to 5m long) using power saws.
  • Skidders: They collect the sized logs and drag them to the nearest road, railway track, or frozen river bank using tractors over skid roads.
  • Haulers: They transport the logs to sawmills. They often float logs down melting rivers in spring and ensure they do not pile up to create a 'logjam', clearing tangles with long poles if necessary.
13. (a) What is the difference between surface mining and underground mining? (b) State any two uses of the mineral Bauxite.
[5 Marks]
Answer:

(a) Surface Mining vs. Underground Mining [3 Marks]:
  • Surface Mining: This method is used when minerals are located near the surface. It involves the removal of surface vegetation, dirt, and top layers to dig out minerals (e.g., Coal, Salt).
  • Underground Mining: This method is used when valuable minerals lie deep below the earth's surface. It involves digging deep holes or shafts into the ground to extract minerals safely (e.g., Gold, Silver, deep Iron reserves).

(b) Uses of Bauxite [2 Marks]:
Bauxite is the primary raw material for making aluminium. Because aluminium is light, durable, and malleable, it is used for:
  • Making soft drink cans, foils, and kitchen utensils.
  • Manufacturing aeroplane parts and window frames.
