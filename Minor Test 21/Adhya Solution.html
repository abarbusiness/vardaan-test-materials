DETAILED SOLUTIONS: Class 6 Physics - Simple Machines
SECTION A
1. Define a simple machine. Give one example.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
A simple machine is a tool or device that makes our work easier, faster, or more convenient by changing the direction or magnitude of the force applied.
Example: A lever, a pulley, or an inclined plane. (Any one valid example)
2. Name the three important parts of a lever.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
The three important parts of a lever are:
1. Fulcrum (F): The fixed point around which the lever moves.
2. Load (L): The object or weight being moved or lifted.
3. Effort (E): The force applied to the lever to move the load.
3. What is an inclined plane?
[2 Marks]
Solution:
An inclined plane is a flat, sloping surface that connects a lower level to a higher level. It helps in moving heavy objects upwards with less effort compared to lifting them straight up.
4. State the difference between a wedge and a screw.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
Wedge: It is made up of two inclined planes placed back-to-back to form a sharp edge. It is used to split or cut objects (e.g., an axe).
Screw: It is an inclined plane wrapped around a cylinder or a rod. It is used to hold objects together firmly (e.g., a nut and bolt).
5. Which class of lever always has the fulcrum in the middle? Give an example.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
A Class I lever always has the fulcrum in the middle, between the Load and the Effort.
Example: A pair of scissors, a see-saw, or a crowbar.
SECTION B
6. Differentiate between Class II and Class III levers with one example each.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
  • Class II Lever: The Load is located between the Fulcrum and the Effort. This setup always acts as a force multiplier.
    Example: A wheelbarrow or a nutcracker.
  • Class III Lever: The Effort is located between the Fulcrum and the Load. This setup is used to gain speed or distance.
    Example: A pair of tongs, tweezers, or a fishing rod.
7. How does a single fixed pulley help us in drawing water from a well?
[3 Marks]
Solution:
A single fixed pulley does not multiply the force, meaning the effort applied is equal to the load. However, it helps us by changing the direction of the force applied to a more convenient direction. Instead of pulling the rope upward against gravity, we can pull it downwards, using our own body weight to assist in lifting the bucket of water easily.
8. Define 'Load' and 'Effort' in the context of a simple machine.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
Load: The resisting force to be overcome by a machine is called the load. It is the actual weight of the object that needs to be moved or lifted.
Effort: The external force applied to the machine to overcome the load is called the effort.
9. A wheel and axle is a simple machine. Explain briefly with a common example from daily life.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
A wheel and axle consists of a large wheel rigidly attached to a smaller cylinder called the axle. When the wheel turns, the axle also turns. A small force applied to the large wheel produces a larger force on the axle, making work easier.
Example: A steering wheel of a car or a doorknob. Turning the outer rim of the doorknob (wheel) makes it easy to turn the inner mechanism (axle).
10. Why is a pair of scissors considered a Class I lever? Explain.
[3 Marks]
Solution:
A pair of scissors is considered a Class I lever because of the arrangement of its parts. The fixed screw in the center around which the blades move acts as the Fulcrum. The paper or cloth being cut by the blades represents the Load. The handles where we apply force with our fingers represent the Effort. Since the Fulcrum is exactly in the middle between the Load and Effort, it is a Class I lever.
SECTION C
11. (a) State the principle of a lever.
(b) Identify the class of lever for: (i) Nutcracker, (ii) Sugar tongs, (iii) See-saw.
[5 Marks]
Solution:
(a) Principle of a Lever: In an ideal lever at equilibrium, the product of the effort and the effort arm is equal to the product of the load and the load arm. Mathematically, it is expressed as:
\( \text{Load} \times \text{Load Arm} = \text{Effort} \times \text{Effort Arm} \)
(b) Class of Levers:
(i) Nutcracker: Class II Lever (Load is in the middle).
(ii) Sugar tongs: Class III Lever (Effort is in the middle).
(iii) See-saw: Class I Lever (Fulcrum is in the middle).
12. List the six different types of simple machines. Provide one daily life example for each type.
[5 Marks]
Solution:
The six different types of simple machines and their examples are:
1. Lever: e.g., A crowbar, scissors, or a seesaw.
2. Inclined Plane: e.g., A ramp used for wheelchairs or loading trucks.
3. Pulley: e.g., The mechanism used to draw water from a well or raise a flag.
4. Wheel and Axle: e.g., A steering wheel, a screwdriver, or a doorknob.
5. Wedge: e.g., A knife, an axe, or a chisel.
6. Screw: e.g., A bottle cap, a nut and bolt, or a screw-jack.
13. (a) Describe a Class II lever and state exactly where the load is positioned relative to the other parts.
(b) Give two examples where an inclined plane is used in everyday life to make work easier.
[5 Marks]
Solution:
(a) Description of Class II Lever:
In a Class II lever, the Load is positioned exactly between the Fulcrum (on one end) and the Effort (on the other end). Because the effort arm is always longer than the load arm in this setup, less effort is required to lift a heavier load, acting as a force multiplier.
(b) Examples of an Inclined Plane:
1. Ramps provided at hospitals for moving stretchers or wheelchairs easily.
2. Wooden planks used to load heavy drums or furniture into the back of a truck.
