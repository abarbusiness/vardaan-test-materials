DETAILED SOLUTIONS: Minor Test 21 (Light Energy & Heat)
Class 7 ICSE | Max Marks: 40
SECTION A (10 Marks)
1. Define temperature and state its S.I. unit.
[2 Marks]

Temperature: It is the reliable measure of the degree of hotness or coldness of a body. (1 Mark)

S.I. Unit: The S.I. unit of temperature is Kelvin (K). (1 Mark)

2. What is meant by the reflection of light? Name the two types of reflection.
[2 Marks]

Reflection: The bouncing back of light rays into the same medium after striking a surface is called reflection of light. (1 Mark)

Types: (i) Regular Reflection (ii) Irregular or Diffused Reflection. (1 Mark)

3. Convert $45^\circ \text{C}$ to the Kelvin scale.
[2 Marks]

Formula: $$ K = C + 273 $$ (For class 7 standard, 273 is accepted in place of 273.15) (1 Mark)

$$ K = 45 + 273 = 318 \text{ K} $$ (1 Mark)

Answer: $318 \text{ K}$

4. State two properties of an image formed by a plane mirror.
[2 Marks]

Any two of the following properties (1 Mark each):

  • The image is virtual and erect.
  • The size of the image is equal to the size of the object.
  • The image is laterally inverted.
  • Distance of the image behind the mirror equals distance of the object in front of it.
5. Define a real image. Can it be obtained on a screen?
[2 Marks]

Real Image: An image formed when reflected or refracted light rays actually intersect at a point is called a real image. (1 Mark)

Screen: Yes, a real image can be obtained on a screen. (1 Mark)

SECTION B (15 Marks)
6. State the two laws of reflection of light.
[3 Marks]

The two laws are:

  1. The angle of incidence is always equal to the angle of reflection ($ \angle i = \angle r $). (1.5 Marks)
  2. The incident ray, the reflected ray, and the normal at the point of incidence, all lie in the same plane. (1.5 Marks)
7. The normal body temperature of a human is $98.6^\circ \text{F}$. Calculate this temperature on the Celsius scale.
[3 Marks]

Using the conversion formula: $$ \frac{C}{5} = \frac{F - 32}{9} $$ (1 Mark)

Substitute $F = 98.6^\circ \text{F}$:

$$ \frac{C}{5} = \frac{98.6 - 32}{9} $$

$$ \frac{C}{5} = \frac{66.6}{9} $$

$$ \frac{C}{5} = 7.4 $$ (1 Mark)

$$ C = 7.4 \times 5 = 37^\circ \text{C} $$ (1 Mark)

Answer: $37^\circ \text{C}$

8. Distinguish between regular reflection and irregular (diffused) reflection by giving two points of difference.
[3 Marks]
Regular Reflection Irregular (Diffused) Reflection
Takes place from smooth and highly polished surfaces (e.g., plane mirror). Takes place from rough or uneven surfaces (e.g., wall, paper).
A parallel beam of incident light remains parallel after reflection. A parallel beam of incident light scatters in different directions after reflection.

(1.5 Marks for each valid difference point)

9. Why does a clinical thermometer have a slight bend (kink) in its capillary tube? Explain briefly.
[3 Marks]

Reason: The kink (or constriction) is located just above the bulb. (1 Mark)

Explanation: It prevents the mercury level from falling back into the bulb on its own when the thermometer is removed from the patient's mouth. (1.5 Marks) This gives the user ample time to read the temperature accurately. (0.5 Mark)

10. What is meant by the rectilinear propagation of light? Give one everyday example to support your answer.
[3 Marks]

Definition: The property of light to travel in a straight line in a uniform or homogeneous medium is known as the rectilinear propagation of light. (2 Marks)

Example: Formation of shadows when an opaque object comes in the path of light, OR light beams from a car headlamp appearing straight in mist/smoke. (1 Mark)

SECTION C (15 Marks)
11. (a) Formula for $C$ and $F$. (b) Convert $20^\circ \text{C}$ to Fahrenheit. (c) Define heat energy.
[5 Marks]

(a) The relationship formula is: $$ \frac{C}{5} = \frac{F - 32}{9} $$ (2 Marks)

(b) Given $C = 20$. Using the formula:
$$ \frac{20}{5} = \frac{F - 32}{9} $$
$$ 4 = \frac{F - 32}{9} $$
$$ F - 32 = 4 \times 9 = 36 $$
$$ F = 36 + 32 = 68^\circ \text{F} $$ (2 Marks)

(c) Heat Energy: It is a form of energy that causes the sensation of hotness and coldness, and it flows from a body at a higher temperature to a body at a lower temperature. (1 Mark)

12. (a) Diagram of reflection of a light ray. (b) Angle of incidence is $30^\circ$, calculate angle of reflection and total angle.
[5 Marks]

(a) Diagram (3 Marks):
(Students must draw a diagram containing:)
- A straight line with hatch marks representing the plane mirror. (0.5 Mark)
- An incident ray with an arrow pointing towards the mirror. (0.5 Mark)
- A reflected ray with an arrow pointing away. (0.5 Mark)
- A normal (dotted line) perpendicular to the mirror surface at the point of incidence. (0.5 Mark)
- Clear labeling of Angle of Incidence ($\angle i$) and Angle of Reflection ($\angle r$). (1 Mark)

(b) Calculation (2 Marks):

Given Angle of incidence ($\angle i$) = $30^\circ$.

By the first law of reflection, Angle of reflection ($\angle r$) = $\angle i = 30^\circ$. (1 Mark)

Total angle between incident ray and reflected ray = $\angle i + \angle r = 30^\circ + 30^\circ = 60^\circ$. (1 Mark)

13. (a) Differentiate between Conduction, Convection, and Radiation. (b) Give one example of a good conductor and one of an insulator.
[5 Marks]

(a) Modes of Heat Transfer (3 Marks):

  • Conduction: Heat transfer in solids where energy is passed from molecule to molecule without any actual movement of the molecules themselves. (1 Mark)
  • Convection: Heat transfer in liquids and gases caused by the actual movement of the heated molecules from the hotter region to the colder region. (1 Mark)
  • Radiation: The process of heat transfer that does not require any material medium. Heat travels in the form of electromagnetic waves (like sunlight). (1 Mark)

(b) Examples (2 Marks):

Good Conductor: Copper, Iron, Aluminum, Silver (Any one, 1 Mark)

Insulator (Bad Conductor): Wood, Plastic, Rubber, Glass, Air (Any one, 1 Mark)
