DETAILED SOLUTIONS: Minor Test 21 (Pressure)

SECTION A

Q1. Define pressure. What is the SI unit of pressure?
[2 Marks]
Final Answer: Pressure is Force per unit area. Unit: Pascal (Pa).
Definition: The force acting perpendicularly on a unit area of a surface is called pressure. Formula: $\text{Pressure} = \frac{\text{Force}}{\text{Area}}$.
SI Unit: The SI unit of pressure is Newton per square metre ($\text{N/m}^2$), which is also called Pascal (Pa).
Q2. Why do school bags have wide and broad straps?
[2 Marks]
Final Answer: To reduce pressure on the shoulders by increasing area.
We know that $\text{Pressure} = \frac{\text{Force}}{\text{Area}}$. This means pressure is inversely proportional to the area of contact.
School bags have wide straps to increase the area over which the weight (force) of the bag is distributed. An increased area results in a decreased pressure on the child's shoulders, making it more comfortable to carry.
Q3. What happens to the pressure exerted by a liquid as the depth increases?
[2 Marks]
Final Answer: Liquid pressure increases with depth.
The pressure exerted by a liquid increases uniformly with the increase in depth.
This is because as depth increases, the weight of the water column pushing down from above also increases, resulting in greater pressure at the bottom.
Q4. Define atmospheric pressure. Why are our bodies not crushed by it?
[2 Marks]
Final Answer: Pressure by air envelope. Balanced by internal blood pressure.
Definition: The pressure exerted by the weight of the air (atmosphere) column over a unit area on the Earth's surface is known as atmospheric pressure.
Reason: Our bodies are not crushed under this massive pressure because the pressure of the fluids (like blood) inside our bodies is equal to the atmospheric pressure outside. Thus, it cancels out the external pressure.
Q5. A force of $100\text{ N}$ is applied to an area of $2\text{ m}^2$. Calculate the pressure.
[2 Marks]
Final Answer: Pressure = $50\text{ Pa}$
Given: Force ($F$) $= 100\text{ N}$
Area ($A$) $= 2\text{ m}^2$
$$ \text{Pressure } (P) = \frac{\text{Force } (F)}{\text{Area } (A)} $$
$$ P = \frac{100\text{ N}}{2\text{ m}^2} = 50\text{ N/m}^2 \text{ or } 50\text{ Pa} $$

SECTION B

Q6. Explain scientifically why it is much easier to cut an apple with a sharp knife rather than a blunt knife.
[3 Marks]
Final Answer: Sharp knife has less area, generating more pressure for cutting.
1. A sharp knife has a very thin edge, meaning the area of contact with the apple is extremely small.
2. A blunt knife has a thicker edge, which means a relatively larger area of contact.
3. According to the formula $\text{Pressure} = \frac{\text{Force}}{\text{Area}}$, for the same amount of force applied by our hand, the smaller area of a sharp knife produces a much larger pressure on the apple. This high pressure easily cuts through the apple.
Q7. Describe a simple experiment to show that liquids exert pressure on the walls of the container.
[3 Marks]
Experiment: Bulging rubber balloon on a side tube.
Experiment Setup: Take a glass tube open at both ends. Attach a short glass tube near the bottom on its side. Tie a thin rubber balloon tightly to the mouth of this side tube.
Procedure: Start pouring water from the top of the main glass tube.
Observation: As the water level rises in the tube, the rubber balloon attached to the side tube starts bulging outwards.
Conclusion: The balloon bulges out because the water column exerts a sideways force. This proves that liquids exert pressure on the walls of their container.
Q8. (a) Porters place a round piece of cloth on their heads. (b) Tractors have broad tyres.
[3 Marks]
Final Answer: Both scenarios increase area to decrease pressure.
(a) Porters and round cloth: A round piece of cloth increases the area of contact between the heavy load and the porter's head. Since $\text{Pressure} \propto \frac{1}{\text{Area}}$, the larger area reduces the pressure on the head, making it less painful to carry the heavy load.
(b) Tractors and broad tyres: Tractors are heavy machines used in muddy fields. Broad tyres drastically increase the surface area of the vehicle in contact with the ground. This reduces the pressure exerted on the soil, preventing the heavy tractor from sinking into the soft earth.
Q9. Calculate pressure from $800\text{ N}$ force on $2.0\text{ m}^2$. What if the area is halved?
[3 Marks]
Initial Pressure = $400\text{ Pa}$. New Pressure = $800\text{ Pa}$.
Part 1: Initial Pressure
$$ P_1 = \frac{F}{A_1} = \frac{800\text{ N}}{2.0\text{ m}^2} = 400\text{ Pa} $$
Part 2: Area is halved
New Area ($A_2$) $= \frac{2.0}{2} = 1.0\text{ m}^2$
Force ($F$) remains $800\text{ N}$.
$$ P_2 = \frac{F}{A_2} = \frac{800\text{ N}}{1.0\text{ m}^2} = 800\text{ Pa} $$
Conclusion: Halving the area doubles the pressure.
Q10. What is a barometer? Liquid used? Why astronauts wear spacesuits?
[3 Marks]
Device to measure atmospheric pressure; Mercury; To counter internal body pressure.
Barometer: It is a scientific instrument used to measure atmospheric pressure.
Liquid Used: Mercury is conventionally used in a standard barometer.
Astronaut Spacesuits: In space, there is a vacuum, meaning there is no atmospheric pressure outside. However, the blood and fluids inside an astronaut's body still exert high outward pressure. Without atmospheric pressure to balance it, blood vessels would burst. Special pressurized suits provide artificial external pressure to keep astronauts safe.

SECTION C

Q11. (a) Properties of liquid pressure. (b) Pressure of $250\text{ N}$ block on $0.5\text{ m}^2$.
[5 Marks]
(a) Acts in all directions, increases with depth, equal at same depth. (b) $500\text{ Pa}$.
Part (a): Properties of Liquid Pressure (3 Marks)
1. Liquid exerts pressure on the walls and the base of the container holding it.
2. The pressure exerted by a liquid increases with an increase in the depth of the liquid.
3. At any given depth, a liquid exerts equal pressure in all directions.
Part (b): Numerical (2 Marks)
Weight of block (Force $F$) $= 250\text{ N}$. Area ($A$) $= 0.5\text{ m}^2$.
$$ \text{Pressure } (P) = \frac{F}{A} $$
$$ P = \frac{250}{0.5} = \frac{250 \times 10}{5} = 500\text{ Pa} $$
Q12. (a) Activity for equal pressure at same depth. (b) Why deep-sea divers wear special suits?
[5 Marks]
(a) Bottle with holes at same level. (b) To withstand high pressure at ocean depths.
Part (a): Activity (3 Marks)
1. Take an empty plastic bottle. Drill 3-4 small holes all around the bottle at exactly the same height from the bottom.
2. Fill the bottle with water.
3. Observation: You will notice that water streams coming out of all the holes fall at the exact same distance from the base of the bottle.
4. Conclusion: Since the water is pushed out with equal force in all directions at that specific height, it proves that liquids exert equal pressure at the same depth.
Part (b): Deep-Sea Divers (2 Marks)
As depth increases, the pressure exerted by the water increases tremendously. Deep down in the ocean, the water pressure is high enough to crush a human body. Divers wear specially designed rigid suits to protect themselves from this immense crushing liquid pressure.
Q13. (a) Atmospheric pressure using rubber sucker. (b) Nose bleeding at high altitudes.
[5 Marks]
(a) Vacuum created inside, external air pressure holds it. (b) Low external pressure causes vessels to burst.
Part (a): Rubber Sucker (3 Marks)
A rubber sucker looks like a small concave rubber cup.
When we press a rubber sucker firmly against a smooth, flat surface (like a wall), most of the air trapped between its cup and the surface is forced out.
This creates a partial vacuum (low pressure) inside the cup. The atmospheric pressure acting on the outside of the sucker is much higher.
This immense external atmospheric pressure pushes the sucker firmly against the wall, causing it to stick tightly. It takes a significant pulling force to overcome this atmospheric pressure to detach it.
Part (b): Nose Bleeding (2 Marks)
Atmospheric pressure decreases as altitude increases. At very high altitudes (like high mountains), the atmospheric pressure becomes much lower than normal. However, the blood pressure inside our bodies remains high. Due to this pressure difference, the delicate blood vessels in the nose may burst outwards, causing nosebleeds.
