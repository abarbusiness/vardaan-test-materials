SECTION A

1. Define the terms 'sonorous' and 'lustre' in the context of metals.
[2 Marks]
  • Sonorous: The property of metals to produce a deep, ringing sound when struck with a hard object. (1 Mark)
  • Lustre: The characteristic shiny appearance of the freshly cut surface of a metal. (1 Mark)
2. What is meant by a 'joint sector industry'? Give one prominent example in India.
[2 Marks]
  • Joint Sector Industry: These are industries that are jointly owned and managed by the government (state) and private individuals or a group of individuals. (1 Mark)
  • Example: Maruti Udyog Limited (joint venture between Indian Govt and Suzuki). (1 Mark)
3. Name a metal and a non-metal that exist in the liquid state at room temperature.
[2 Marks]
  • Liquid Metal: Mercury (Hg). (1 Mark)
  • Liquid Non-metal: Bromine (Br). (1 Mark)
4. State two reasons why aluminium is widely preferred for manufacturing cooking utensils.
[2 Marks]

Aluminium is preferred because: (Any two for 1 mark each)

  • It is a very good conductor of heat.
  • It is light in weight and highly malleable.
  • It does not corrode or rust easily due to the formation of a protective oxide layer.
5. Briefly explain the term 'cooperative sector industries' with an example.
[2 Marks]
  • Cooperative Sector: These industries are owned and operated by the producers or suppliers of raw materials, workers, or both. They pool their resources and share profits or losses proportionally. (1 Mark)
  • Example: Anand Milk Union Limited (AMUL) or Sudha Dairy. (1 Mark)

SECTION B

6. Differentiate between 'mineral' and 'ore'. Are all minerals considered ores? Justify.
[3 Marks]
  • Mineral: Naturally occurring inorganic solid substances found in the Earth's crust with a definite chemical composition. (1 Mark)
  • Ore: Those specific minerals from which a metal can be extracted profitably and conveniently. (1 Mark)
  • Justification: No, all minerals are not ores. While an ore is a mineral, a mineral is only considered an ore if it has a high concentration of a specific metal that makes commercial extraction viable. (1 Mark)
7. Classify industries on the basis of raw materials used. Provide at least three categories with one example for each.
[3 Marks]

On the basis of raw materials, industries are classified into: (1 Mark for each category with example)

  • Agro-based Industries: Use plant/animal products as raw material. Example: Cotton textiles, sugar industry.
  • Mineral-based Industries: Use mineral ores as raw material. Example: Iron & Steel, Cement.
  • Marine-based Industries: Use products from the sea/oceans. Example: Fish oil processing, sea food packing.
  • Forest-based Industries: Use forest produce. Example: Paper industry, furniture. (Any 3)
8. Write three specific physical properties of non-metals that distinguish them from metals.
[3 Marks]

Three distinguishing physical properties of non-metals: (1 Mark each)

  • Brittleness: Solid non-metals are brittle, meaning they break into pieces when hammered, unlike metals which are malleable.
  • Non-lustrous: They have a dull appearance and lack the shiny metallic lustre (except Iodine and Diamond).
  • Poor Conductors: They are generally poor conductors of heat and electricity (insulators), with the exception of graphite.
9. What is rust chemically? Write down the two essential conditions required for the rusting of iron.
[3 Marks]
  • Chemical nature of rust: Chemically, rust is hydrated iron (III) oxide, represented by the formula $$ Fe_2O_3 \cdot xH_2O $$. It forms a reddish-brown flaky crust. (1 Mark)
  • Essential Conditions: The two necessary conditions for rusting are the presence of Oxygen (air) and Moisture (water). (2 Marks, 1 for each condition)
10. Explain how government policies and the availability of cheap labor play a crucial role in deciding the location of a new industry.
[3 Marks]
  • Government Policies: The government often sets up industrial zones and offers subsidies, tax holidays, and cheaper electricity to encourage industries to locate in backward areas for balanced regional development. Industries prefer these favorable policies. (1.5 Marks)
  • Cheap Labor: Industries (especially labor-intensive ones like textiles) require a large workforce. Locating near densely populated regions ensures a steady supply of cheap and skilled/unskilled labor, thereby reducing production costs. (1.5 Marks)

13. (a) Compare metals and non-metals on malleability, ductility, and electrical conductivity. (b) Why is phosphorus stored underwater?
[5 Marks]

(a) Comparison (3 Marks):

  • Malleability: Metals are malleable (can be beaten into thin sheets). Non-metals are non-malleable and break into pieces (brittle). (1 Mark)
  • Ductility: Metals are ductile (can be drawn into wires). Non-metals are non-ductile. (1 Mark)
  • Electrical Conductivity: Metals are good conductors of electricity. Non-metals are poor conductors (insulators), except graphite. (1 Mark)

(b) Storage of Phosphorus (2 Marks):

Phosphorus is a highly reactive non-metal. It reacts vigorously with atmospheric oxygen and can spontaneously catch fire at room temperature. However, it does not react with water. Therefore, to prevent it from coming into contact with air and catching fire, it is safely stored completely submerged underwater. (2 Marks for complete reasoning)
