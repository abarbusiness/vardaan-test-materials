SECTION A SOLUTIONS (10 MARKS)

Q1. From a point $Q$, the length of the tangent to a circle is $24\text{ cm}$ and the distance of $Q$ from the centre is $25\text{ cm}$. Find the radius of the circle.
[2 Marks]
Correct Option: (A) $7\text{ cm}$
Step 1: Conceptual Understanding
Let $O$ be the centre of the circle and $P$ be the point of contact of the tangent drawn from point $Q$.
According to NCERT Theorem 10.1, the tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact. Thus, $\angle OPQ = 90^\circ$.
Step 2: Applying Pythagoras Theorem
In right-angled triangle $\Delta OPQ$:
$$OQ^2 = OP^2 + PQ^2$$ $$(25)^2 = OP^2 + (24)^2$$ $$625 = OP^2 + 576$$ $$OP^2 = 625 - 576 = 49$$ $$OP = \sqrt{49} = 7\text{ cm}$$
Hence, the radius of the circle is $7\text{ cm}$.
Q2. In Fig. 10.11, if $TP$ and $TQ$ are the two tangents to a circle with centre $O$ so that $\angle POQ = 110^\circ$, then find $\angle PTQ$.
[2 Marks]
Correct Option: (B) $70^\circ$
Step 1: Identify Given Values & Angle Properties
$TP$ and $TQ$ are tangents at points $P$ and $Q$ respectively.
By Theorem 10.1: $OP \perp TP \implies \angle OPT = 90^\circ$ and $OQ \perp TQ \implies \angle OQT = 90^\circ$.
Step 2: Angle Sum Property of Quadrilateral
In quadrilateral $OPTQ$, the sum of all interior angles is $360^\circ$:
$$\angle OPT + \angle POQ + \angle OQT + \angle PTQ = 360^\circ$$ $$90^\circ + 110^\circ + 90^\circ + \angle PTQ = 360^\circ$$ $$290^\circ + \angle PTQ = 360^\circ$$ $$\angle PTQ = 360^\circ - 290^\circ = 70^\circ$$
Q3. If tangents $PA$ and $PB$ from a point $P$ to a circle with centre $O$ are inclined to each other at an angle of $80^\circ$, then find $\angle POA$.
[2 Marks]
Correct Option: (A) $50^\circ$
Step 1: Congruence of Tangent Triangles
In $\Delta POA$ and $\Delta POB$:
  • $OA = OB$ (Radii of the same circle)
  • $PA = PB$ (Lengths of tangents from external point $P$, Theorem 10.2)
  • $OP = OP$ (Common side)
Therefore, $\Delta POA \cong \Delta POB$ by SSS Congruence Criterion.
By CPCT, $\angle APO = \angle BPO = \frac{1}{2} \angle APB = \frac{1}{2} (80^\circ) = 40^\circ$.
Step 2: Calculating $\angle POA$
In right-angled triangle $\Delta POA$ ($\angle OAP = 90^\circ$):
$$\angle POA + \angle OAP + \angle APO = 180^\circ$$ $$\angle POA + 90^\circ + 40^\circ = 180^\circ$$ $$\angle POA = 180^\circ - 130^\circ = 50^\circ$$
Q4. Two concentric circles are of radii $5\text{ cm}$ and $3\text{ cm}$. Find the length of the chord of the larger circle which touches the smaller circle.
[2 Marks]
Length of Chord $= 8\text{ cm}$
Step 1: Geometry Setup
Let $C_1$ and $C_2$ be two concentric circles with centre $O$. Let $AB$ be a chord of larger circle $C_1$ touching smaller circle $C_2$ at point $P$.
Join $OP$ and $OA$. Here, $OA = 5\text{ cm}$ (radius of $C_1$) and $OP = 3\text{ cm}$ (radius of $C_2$).
Step 2: Applying Tangent-Radius Property
Since $AB$ is tangent to $C_2$ at $P$, $OP \perp AB$.
In right $\Delta OPA$:
$$OA^2 = OP^2 + AP^2 \implies 5^2 = 3^2 + AP^2$$ $$AP^2 = 25 - 9 = 16 \implies AP = 4\text{ cm}$$
Since the perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord: $$AB = 2 \times AP = 2 \times 4\text{ cm} = 8\text{ cm}$$
Q5. The length of a tangent from a point $A$ at a distance $5\text{ cm}$ from the centre of the circle is $4\text{ cm}$. Find the radius of the circle.
[2 Marks]
Radius $= 3\text{ cm}$
Detailed Calculation
Let $O$ be the centre of the circle and $P$ be the point of contact of the tangent from point $A$.
Given: $OA = 5\text{ cm}$ and $AP = 4\text{ cm}$.
Since radius is perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact, $\angle OPA = 90^\circ$.
In right $\Delta OPA$:
$$OA^2 = OP^2 + AP^2$$ $$5^2 = OP^2 + 4^2$$ $$25 = OP^2 + 16 \implies OP^2 = 9 \implies OP = 3\text{ cm}$$
Thus, the radius of the circle is $3\text{ cm}$.

SECTION B SOLUTIONS (15 MARKS)

Q6. Prove that the tangents drawn at the ends of a diameter of a circle are parallel.
[3 Marks]
Proof:
Given: A circle with centre $O$ and diameter $AB$. Let $l_1$ be the tangent at end $A$ and $l_2$ be the tangent at end $B$.
To Prove: $l_1 \parallel l_2$.

Proof Steps:
  1. By NCERT Theorem 10.1, tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.
  2. Since $OA$ is radius to tangent $l_1$: $\angle OAP = 90^\circ$ (where $P$ is a point on $l_1$).
  3. Similarly, $OB$ is radius to tangent $l_2$: $\angle OBQ = 90^\circ$ (where $Q$ is a point on $l_2$).
  4. Now consider line $AB$ as a transversal intersecting two lines $l_1$ and $l_2$.
  5. Notice that $\angle OAP$ and $\angle OBQ$ form a pair of alternate interior angles, and: $$\angle OAP = \angle OBQ = 90^\circ$$
  6. Since alternate interior angles are equal, the lines $l_1$ and $l_2$ must be parallel ($l_1 \parallel l_2$). $\blacksquare$
Q7. A quadrilateral $ABCD$ is drawn to circumscribe a circle. Prove that $AB + CD = AD + BC$.
[3 Marks]
Proof (NCERT Ex 10.2 Q10):
Given: Tangents $PA$ and $PB$ from external point $P$ touching circle at $A$ and $B$. Centre is $O$.
To Prove: $\angle APB + \angle AOB = 180^\circ$.

Proof:
  1. Radius $OA \perp PA \implies \angle OAP = 90^\circ$.
  2. Radius $OB \perp PB \implies \angle OBP = 90^\circ$.
  3. In quadrilateral $OAPB$, sum of all four interior angles $= 360^\circ$:
    $$\angle OAP + \angle APB + \angle OBP + \angle AOB = 360^\circ$$ $$90^\circ + \angle APB + 90^\circ + \angle AOB = 360^\circ$$ $$180^\circ + \angle APB + \angle AOB = 360^\circ$$ $$\angle APB + \angle AOB = 360^\circ - 180^\circ = 180^\circ$$
Hence, the angle between the two tangents is supplementary to the angle subtended by the points of contact at the centre. $\blacksquare$
Q9. Prove that the parallelogram circumscribing a circle is a rhombus.
[3 Marks]
Proof (NCERT Ex 10.2 Q11):
Let $ABCD$ be a parallelogram circumscribing a circle.
Since $ABCD$ is a parallelogram, opposite sides are equal: $$AB = CD \quad \text{and} \quad AD = BC \quad \text{--- (1)}$$ From Question 7, we know that for any quadrilateral circumscribing a circle: $$AB + CD = AD + BC \quad \text{--- (2)}$$ Substitute equation (1) into (2):
$$AB + AB = AD + AD$$ $$2AB = 2AD \implies AB = AD$$
Since adjacent sides $AB$ and $AD$ are equal, and opposite sides are equal: $$AB = BC = CD = DA$$ A parallelogram with all four sides equal is a rhombus. Hence Proved. $\blacksquare$
Q10. Two tangents $TP$ and $TQ$ are drawn to a circle with centre $O$ from an external point $T$. Prove that $\angle PTQ = 2\angle OPQ$.
[3 Marks]
Proof (NCERT Example 2):
Let $\angle PTQ = \theta$.
By Theorem 10.2, $TP = TQ$. Therefore, $\Delta TPQ$ is an isosceles triangle.
Thus, $\angle TPQ = \angle TQP = \frac{1}{2}(180^\circ - \theta) = 90^\circ - \frac{1}{2}\theta$.

By Theorem 10.1, $OP \perp TP \implies \angle OPT = 90^\circ$.
Now:
$$\angle OPQ = \angle OPT - \angle TPQ$$ $$\angle OPQ = 90^\circ - \left(90^\circ - \frac{1}{2}\theta\right) = \frac{1}{2}\theta$$ $$\angle OPQ = \frac{1}{2}\angle PTQ \implies \angle PTQ = 2\angle OPQ$$
Hence Proved. $\blacksquare$

SECTION C SOLUTIONS (15 MARKS)

Q11. In Fig. 10.13, $XY$ and $X'Y'$ are two parallel tangents to a circle with centre $O$ and another tangent $AB$ with point of contact $C$ intersecting $XY$ at $A$ and $X'Y'$ at $B$. Prove that $\angle AOB = 90^\circ$.
[5 Marks]
Detailed Proof (NCERT Ex 10.2 Q12):
Let circle touch sides $BC, CA, AB$ at $D, E, F$ respectively.
Given: $BD = 8\text{ cm}, CD = 6\text{ cm}$, radius $r = 4\text{ cm}$.
By Theorem 10.2:
  • $BF = BD = 8\text{ cm}$
  • $CE = CD = 6\text{ cm}$
  • Let $AF = AE = x\text{ cm}$
Sides of $\Delta ABC$ are: $$a = BC = 6 + 8 = 14\text{ cm}$$ $$b = AC = x + 6\text{ cm}$$ $$c = AB = x + 8\text{ cm}$$
Step 1: Semi-perimeter $s$ and Area via Heron's Formula
$$s = \frac{a + b + c}{2} = \frac{14 + (x + 6) + (x + 8)}{2} = \frac{2x + 28}{2} = x + 14$$ $$\text{Area}(\Delta ABC) = \sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}$$ $$\text{Area} = \sqrt{(x+14)(x+14-14)(x+14-(x+6))(x+14-(x+8))}$$ $$\text{Area} = \sqrt{(x+14)(x)(8)(6)} = \sqrt{48x(x+14)} \quad \text{--- (1)}$$
Step 2: Area via Sum of Sub-Triangles
$$\text{Area}(\Delta ABC) = \text{Area}(\Delta OBC) + \text{Area}(\Delta OCA) + \text{Area}(\Delta OAB)$$ $$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}(14)(4) + \frac{1}{2}(x+6)(4) + \frac{1}{2}(x+8)(4)$$ $$\text{Area} = 28 + 2x + 12 + 2x + 16 = 4x + 56 = 4(x + 14) \quad \text{--- (2)}$$
Step 3: Equating and Solving for $x$
$$\sqrt{48x(x+14)} = 4(x+14)$$ $$\text{Squaring both sides: } 48x(x+14) = 16(x+14)^2$$ $$\text{Divide by } 16(x+14): \quad 3x = x + 14 \implies 2x = 14 \implies x = 7\text{ cm}$$
Final Answer:
$$AB = x + 8 = 7 + 8 = \mathbf{15\text{ cm}}$$ $$AC = x + 6 = 7 + 6 = \mathbf{13\text{ cm}}$$
Q13. [HOTS NON-NCERT] Two circles $C_1$ ($r_1 = 9\text{ cm}$) and $C_2$ ($r_2 = 4\text{ cm}$) touch externally at $P$. $AB$ is a direct common tangent. A third circle $C_3$ of radius $r_3$ touches $C_1, C_2$ externally and line $AB$ tangentially.
(a) Find $AB$. (b) Find $r_3$. (c) Find curvilinear area bounded by $C_1, C_2,$ and $AB$.
[5 Marks]
Part (a): Length of Direct Common Tangent $AB$ [1.5 Marks]
Let $O_1, O_2$ be centres of $C_1, C_2$. Distance between centres $O_1O_2 = r_1 + r_2 = 9 + 4 = 13\text{ cm}$.
Draw $O_2K \perp O_1A$. In right $\Delta O_1 K O_2$: $$O_1K = r_1 - r_2 = 9 - 4 = 5\text{ cm}$$
$$AB = KO_2 = \sqrt{O_1O_2^2 - O_1K^2} = \sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{169 - 25} = \sqrt{144} = \mathbf{12\text{ cm}}$$
Part (b): Radius $r_3$ of Smallest Circle $C_3$ [2 Marks]
The segment $AB$ is equal to the sum of tangent segments from $C_1$ to $C_3$ and $C_3$ to $C_2$: $$AB = 2\sqrt{r_1 r_3} + 2\sqrt{r_2 r_3} = 2\sqrt{r_1 r_2}$$ $$\sqrt{r_3}\left(\sqrt{r_1} + \sqrt{r_2}\right) = \sqrt{r_1 r_2} \implies \frac{1}{\sqrt{r_3}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{r_1}} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{r_2}}$$ Substituting $r_1 = 9$ and $r_2 = 4$:
$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{r_3}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{9}} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{4}} = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{5}{6}$$ $$\sqrt{r_3} = \frac{6}{5} \implies r_3 = \left(\frac{6}{5}\right)^2 = \mathbf{1.44\text{ cm}} \quad \text{or } \left(\frac{36}{25}\text{ cm}\right)$$
Part (c): Area of Curvilinear Region [1.5 Marks]
Area of right-trapezium $O_1 A B O_2$: $$\text{Area}_{\text{trap}} = \frac{1}{2}(r_1 + r_2) \times AB = \frac{1}{2}(9 + 4) \times 12 = 78\text{ cm}^2$$ Angles at centres: $\cos \theta_1 = \frac{O_1K}{O_1O_2} = \frac{5}{13} \implies \theta_1 \approx 67.38^\circ$.
Angle $\theta_2 = 180^\circ - \theta_1 = 112.62^\circ$.
Area of Sector 1 ($C_1$): $\frac{67.38}{360} \times \pi \times 9^2 \approx 47.63\text{ cm}^2$.
Area of Sector 2 ($C_2$): $\frac{112.62}{360} \times \pi \times 4^2 \approx 15.72\text{ cm}^2$.
$$\text{Enclosed Area} = 78 - (47.63 + 15.72) = 78 - 63.35 = \mathbf{14.65\text{ cm}^2}$$
