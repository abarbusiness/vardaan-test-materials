PART I: SCIENCE - MATTER
Q1. Define: (a) Matter (b) Intermolecular space [1 + 1 = 2 Marks]
Solution:
(a) Matter: Anything that has mass, occupies space (volume), and can be perceived by our senses is called matter. [1 Mark]
(b) Intermolecular space: The empty space existing between the molecules of matter is called intermolecular space. [1 Mark]
Q2. State any two fundamental characteristics of the gaseous state of matter. [2 Marks]
Solution:
Any two of the following characteristics:
  • Gases have neither a definite shape nor a definite volume. [1 Mark]
  • They are highly compressible due to large intermolecular spaces. [1 Mark]
  • They can flow freely and fill the entire container they are kept in.
Q3. Differentiate between solids, liquids, and gases on the basis of their shape and volume. [3 Marks]
Solution:
Property Solids Liquids Gases
Shape Have a definite shape. No definite shape (takes shape of container). No definite shape.
Volume Have a definite volume. Have a definite volume. No definite volume.
[1 Mark for correctly distinguishing each state]
Q4. What is sublimation? Give one practical example. [2 + 1 = 3 Marks]
Solution:
Sublimation: The process by which a solid directly changes into a gaseous state on heating, without passing through the intermediate liquid state, is called sublimation. [2 Marks]
Example: Camphor, Naphthalene balls, or Dry ice. [1 Mark]
Q5. Explain molecular arrangement in solids, liquids, and gases. How does it affect compressibility? [5 Marks]
Solution:
1. Solids: Molecules are very closely packed. Intermolecular space is negligible, and intermolecular force of attraction is very strong. [1.5 Marks]
2. Liquids: Molecules are loosely packed compared to solids. Intermolecular space is larger, and the force of attraction is weaker than in solids. [1.5 Marks]
3. Gases: Molecules are very far apart. Intermolecular space is maximum, and the force of attraction is negligible. [1 Mark]
Effect on Compressibility: Because gases have large intermolecular spaces, they can be compressed easily. Solids cannot be compressed because there is practically no space between molecules. [1 Mark]
Q6. Describe changes when ice at 0°C is heated to steam at 100°C. Name the processes. [5 Marks]
Solution:
When a block of ice at \(0^\circ\text{C}\) is continuously heated, the following changes occur step-by-step:
  1. Melting: The ice (solid) absorbs heat and changes into water (liquid) at a constant temperature of \(0^\circ\text{C}\). This process of solid turning to liquid is called Melting. [2 Marks]
  2. Heating Water: Once all ice has melted, further heating raises the temperature of the water from \(0^\circ\text{C}\) to \(100^\circ\text{C}\). [1 Mark]
  3. Boiling (Vaporization): At \(100^\circ\text{C}\), the water starts changing into steam (gas). The temperature remains constant during this phase change. This process of liquid turning to gas is called Boiling or Vaporization. [2 Marks]

Q7. Express \(45 \frac{7}{100}\) as a decimal. Find the place value of 7. [1 + 1 = 2 Marks]
Solution:
The mixed fraction is \(45 \frac{7}{100}\).
This can be written as: \( 45 + \frac{7}{100} \)
Decimal conversion: \( 45 + 0.07 = \mathbf{45.07} \) [1 Mark]
Place value of 7 in 45.07: It is in the second place after the decimal point.
Therefore, the place value is Hundredths (or \(\frac{7}{100}\) or \(0.07\)). [1 Mark]

Q10. A ribbon is 25.5 m long. Cut into 5 equal pieces. Length of each piece? [3 Marks]
Solution:
Total length of the ribbon = \( 25.5 \text{ m} \)
Number of pieces = \( 5 \)
Length of one piece = Total Length \( \div \) Number of pieces
\( = 25.5 \div 5 \) [1 Mark]
Performing the division:
\( 25 \div 5 = 5 \) and \( 0.5 \div 5 = 0.1 \)
So, \( 25.5 \div 5 = \mathbf{5.1 \text{ m}} \) [2 Marks]
Q11. Rahul bought 5.5 kg apples at ₹ 120.00/kg & 3.5 kg oranges at ₹ 80.50/kg. Total spent and change from ₹ 1000? [5 Marks]

  • Cost of 1 kg apples = ₹ \( 120.00 \)

  • Cost of 1 kg oranges = ₹ \( 80.50 \)

(b) Calculating Change Received:
Amount given to shopkeeper = ₹ \( 1000.00 \)

Q12. Simplify: \( 4.5 \times (3.2 + 1.8) - 12.6 \div 3 + 2.15 \) [5 Marks]
Solution:
Applying BODMAS rule (Brackets, Of, Division, Multiplication, Addition, Subtraction):
Step 1: Brackets
\( = 4.5 \times (\mathbf{3.2 + 1.8}) - 12.6 \div 3 + 2.15 \)
\( = 4.5 \times (\mathbf{5.0}) - 12.6 \div 3 + 2.15 \) [1 Mark]
Step 2: Division
\( = 4.5 \times 5.0 - (\mathbf{12.6 \div 3}) + 2.15 \)
\( = 4.5 \times 5.0 - \mathbf{4.2} + 2.15 \) [1.5 Marks]
Step 3: Multiplication
\( = (\mathbf{4.5 \times 5.0}) - 4.2 + 2.15 \)
\( = \mathbf{22.5} - 4.2 + 2.15 \) [1 Mark]
Step 4: Addition & Subtraction (Left to Right or Addition first)
Let's perform Addition first to strictly follow BODMAS convention:
Rearrange: \( = 22.5 + 2.15 - 4.2 \)
\( = 24.65 - 4.2 \) [0.5 Mark]
\( = \mathbf{20.45} \) [1 Mark]
Final Answer: 20.45
