SECTION A (10 Marks)
Q1. Find the ratio of \( 5 \text{ km} \) to \( 500 \text{ m} \) in its simplest form.
[2 Marks]
Solution:

To find the ratio, both quantities must be in the same unit.

We know that \( 1 \text{ km} = 1000 \text{ m} \).

\[ \text{Therefore, } 5 \text{ km} = 5 \times 1000 \text{ m} = 5000 \text{ m} \]

Now, find the ratio:

\[ \text{Ratio} = \frac{5000 \text{ m}}{500 \text{ m}} = \frac{50}{5} = \frac{10}{1} \]
Required Ratio = \( 10 : 1 \)
Q2. Are the ratios \( 1:2 \) and \( 2:3 \) equivalent? Justify your answer.
[2 Marks]
Solution:

To compare the ratios, we write them as fractions: \( \frac{1}{2} \) and \( \frac{2}{3} \).

To check for equivalence, we can cross-multiply or make the denominators equal.

Method (Equal Denominators): LCM of \( 2 \) and \( 3 \) is \( 6 \).

\[ \frac{1 \times 3}{2 \times 3} = \frac{3}{6} \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{2 \times 2}{3 \times 2} = \frac{4}{6} \]

Since \( \frac{3}{6} \neq \frac{4}{6} \), the fractions are not equal.

No, the ratios \( 1:2 \) and \( 2:3 \) are NOT equivalent.
Q3. If the cost of \( 7 \) meters of a certain cloth is \( \text{₹ } 294 \), find the cost of \( 5 \) meters of the same cloth.
[2 Marks]
Solution:

This is a case of Direct Proportion. Let the cost of \( 5 \text{ m} \) of cloth be \( x \).

Length of cloth (m)\( 7 \)\( 5 \)
Cost (₹)\( 294 \)\( x \)
\begin{align*} \frac{7}{294} &= \frac{5}{x} \\ 7x &= 294 \times 5 \\ x &= \frac{294 \times 5}{7} \\ x &= 42 \times 5 \\ x &= 210 \end{align*}
The cost of 5 meters of cloth is ₹ 210.
Q4. \( 6 \) pipes are required to fill a tank in \( 1 \text{ hour and } 20 \text{ minutes} \). How long will it take if only \( 5 \) pipes of the same type are used?
[2 Marks]
Solution:

First, convert the time to minutes: \( 1 \text{ hour } 20 \text{ mins} = 60 + 20 = 80 \text{ minutes} \).

Fewer pipes will take more time to fill the tank. Hence, it is a case of Inverse Proportion.

Let the time taken by \( 5 \) pipes be \( y \) minutes.

\begin{align*} x_1 y_1 &= x_2 y_2 \\ 6 \times 80 &= 5 \times y \\ 480 &= 5y \\ y &= \frac{480}{5} = 96 \text{ minutes} \end{align*}

Converting back: \( 96 \text{ minutes} = 1 \text{ hour } 36 \text{ minutes} \).

It will take 1 hour and 36 minutes.
Q5. The scale of a map is given as \( 1 : 30,000,000 \). Two cities are \( 4 \text{ cm} \) apart on the map. Find the actual distance between them in kilometers.
[2 Marks]
Solution:

Map scale means \( 1 \text{ cm} \) on the map represents \( 30,000,000 \text{ cm} \) in reality.

Actual distance for \( 4 \text{ cm} \) on the map = \( 4 \times 30,000,000 \text{ cm} = 120,000,000 \text{ cm} \).

Now, convert cm to km. (\( 1 \text{ km} = 100,000 \text{ cm} \))

\[ \text{Distance in km} = \frac{120,000,000}{100,000} \text{ km} = 1200 \text{ km} \]
The actual distance is 1200 km.
SECTION B (15 Marks)
Q6. A loaded truck travels \( 14 \text{ km} \) in \( 25 \text{ minutes} \). If the speed remains the same, how far can it travel in \( 5 \text{ hours} \)?
[3 Marks]
Solution:

Since the speed is constant, distance and time are in Direct Proportion.

Time given = \( 5 \text{ hours} \). Let's convert it to minutes: \( 5 \times 60 = 300 \text{ minutes} \).

Let the distance traveled in \( 300 \text{ minutes} \) be \( d \text{ km} \).

Distance (km)\( 14 \)\( d \)
Time (minutes)\( 25 \)\( 300 \)
\begin{align*} \frac{14}{25} &= \frac{d}{300} \\ d &= \frac{14 \times 300}{25} \\ d &= 14 \times 12 \\ d &= 168 \end{align*}
The truck can travel 168 km in 5 hours.
Q7. If \( 15 \) workers can build a wall in \( 48 \text{ hours} \), how many workers will be required to do the same work in \( 30 \text{ hours} \)?
[3 Marks]
Solution:

Less time requires more workers to finish the same job. Therefore, it is a case of Inverse Proportion.

Let the required number of workers be \( w \).

Number of workers\( 15 \)\( w \)
Time (hours)\( 48 \)\( 30 \)
\begin{align*} x_1 y_1 &= x_2 y_2 \\ 15 \times 48 &= w \times 30 \\ w &= \frac{15 \times 48}{30} \\ w &= \frac{48}{2} = 24 \end{align*}
24 workers will be required.
Q8. In a school, there are \( 8 \) periods a day, each of \( 45 \text{ minutes} \) duration. How long would each period be, if the school has \( 9 \) periods a day?
[3 Marks]
Solution:

Total school time is fixed. If the number of periods increases, the duration of each period must decrease. Hence, they are in Inverse Proportion.

Let the new duration of each period be \( p \text{ minutes} \).

\begin{align*} \text{Number of periods} \times \text{Duration} &= \text{Constant} \\ 8 \times 45 &= 9 \times p \\ 360 &= 9p \\ p &= \frac{360}{9} = 40 \end{align*}
Each period would be 40 minutes long.
Q9. A photograph of a bacteria enlarged 50,000 times attains a length of 5 cm. (i) Actual length? (ii) Enlarged length if enlarged 20,000 times?
[3 Marks]
Solution:

(i) Actual length of the bacteria:

Actual length = \( \frac{\text{Enlarged Length}}{\text{Magnification Scale}} \)

\[ \text{Actual length} = \frac{5}{50,000} \text{ cm} = \frac{1}{10,000} \text{ cm} = 10^{-4} \text{ cm} \]

(ii) If enlarged \( 20,000 \) times:

This is a Direct Proportion between enlargement scale and length.

Let the new length be \( l \).

\begin{align*} \frac{5}{50000} &= \frac{l}{20000} \\ l &= \frac{5 \times 20000}{50000} \\ l &= \frac{100000}{50000} = 2 \text{ cm} \end{align*}
(i) Actual length = \( 10^{-4} \) cm. (ii) New length = 2 cm.
Q10. \( 2 \text{ kg} \) of sugar contains \( 9 \times 10^6 \) crystals. How many in (a) \( 5 \text{ kg} \)? (b) \( 1.2 \text{ kg} \)?
[3 Marks]
Solution:

The quantity of sugar and the number of crystals are in Direct Proportion.

(a) In \( 5 \text{ kg} \) of sugar: Let the crystals be \( x \).

\begin{align*} \frac{2}{9 \times 10^6} &= \frac{5}{x} \\ 2x &= 45 \times 10^6 \\ x &= 22.5 \times 10^6 = 2.25 \times 10^7 \text{ crystals} \end{align*}

(b) In \( 1.2 \text{ kg} \) of sugar: Let the crystals be \( y \).

\begin{align*} \frac{2}{9 \times 10^6} &= \frac{1.2}{y} \\ 2y &= 1.2 \times 9 \times 10^6 \\ 2y &= 10.8 \times 10^6 \\ y &= 5.4 \times 10^6 \text{ crystals} \end{align*}
SECTION C (15 Marks)
Q11. A garrison of \( 1200 \) men has provisions for \( 60 \text{ days} \). After \( 15 \text{ days} \), \( 300 \) men leave. How long will the remaining food last?
[5 Marks]
Solution:

Step 1: Understand the state of provisions after \( 15 \text{ days} \).

Total initial days = \( 60 \). Days passed = \( 15 \).

If no one left, the food would have lasted for \( 1200 \) men for \( (60 - 15) = 45 \text{ days} \).

Step 2: Identify the new variables.

Original men (\( M_1 \)) = \( 1200 \)

Original remaining days (\( D_1 \)) = \( 45 \)

Number of men leaving = \( 300 \)

Remaining men (\( M_2 \)) = \( 1200 - 300 = 900 \)

Let the food last for \( D_2 \) days for these \( 900 \) men.

Step 3: Apply Inverse Proportion.

More men \(\implies\) food lasts for fewer days. Hence, Inverse Proportion.

\begin{align*} M_1 \times D_1 &= M_2 \times D_2 \\ 1200 \times 45 &= 900 \times D_2 \\ D_2 &= \frac{1200 \times 45}{900} \\ D_2 &= \frac{12 \times 45}{9} \\ D_2 &= 12 \times 5 = 60 \end{align*}
The remaining food will last for exactly 60 days for the remaining men.
Q12. A train is moving at a uniform speed of \( 75 \text{ km/hr} \). (a) Distance in \( 20 \text{ mins} \)? (b) Time for \( 250 \text{ km} \)?
[5 Marks]
Solution:

Speed is constant, so Distance and Time are in Direct Proportion.

Speed = \( 75 \text{ km/hr} \) implies distance = \( 75 \text{ km} \) in \( 60 \text{ minutes} \).

(a) Distance traveled in \( 20 \text{ minutes} \): Let distance be \( d_1 \).

\begin{align*} \frac{75}{60} &= \frac{d_1}{20} \\ d_1 &= \frac{75 \times 20}{60} \\ d_1 &= \frac{75}{3} = 25 \text{ km} \end{align*}

(b) Time required to cover \( 250 \text{ km} \): Let time in hours be \( t_2 \).

Using km and hours directly:

\begin{align*} \frac{75}{1} &= \frac{250}{t_2} \\ 75 \times t_2 &= 250 \\ t_2 &= \frac{250}{75} = \frac{10}{3} \text{ hours} \end{align*}

Convert to hours and minutes: \( \frac{10}{3} \text{ hours} = 3 \text{ hours} \) and \( \frac{1}{3} \text{ hour} \).

\( \frac{1}{3} \times 60 \text{ mins} = 20 \text{ mins} \).

(a) The train travels 25 km in 20 minutes.
(b) It takes 3 hours and 20 minutes to cover 250 km.
Q13. Two persons fit windows in \( 3 \text{ days} \). (a) One person falls ill, time taken? (b) Persons needed for \( 1 \text{ day} \)? (c) Justify proportion type.
[5 Marks]
Solution:

(c) Justification of Proportion:

The amount of work (fitting windows) is constant. If the number of persons working decreases, the time taken to complete the job will increase. Similarly, if we want to finish the job in fewer days, we need more persons. Hence, the number of persons and days are in Inverse Proportion.

(a) One person falls ill:

Initial persons (\( P_1 \)) = \( 2 \), Initial days (\( D_1 \)) = \( 3 \).

New persons (\( P_2 \)) = \( 2 - 1 = 1 \). Let new days be \( D_2 \).

\begin{align*} P_1 \times D_1 &= P_2 \times D_2 \\ 2 \times 3 &= 1 \times D_2 \\ D_2 &= 6 \end{align*}

The job will now take \( 6 \text{ days} \).

(b) Persons needed to finish in exactly one day:

New target days (\( D_3 \)) = \( 1 \). Let required persons be \( P_3 \).

\begin{align*} P_1 \times D_1 &= P_3 \times D_3 \\ 2 \times 3 &= P_3 \times 1 \\ P_3 &= 6 \end{align*}
(a) 6 days are required now.
(b) 6 persons are needed to complete the work in 1 day.
