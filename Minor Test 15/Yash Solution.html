SECTION A: SCIENCE SOLUTIONS (20 Marks)
Q1. Define reflection of light. State the two laws of reflection.
[2 Marks]
Solution:

Reflection of Light: The bouncing back of light rays into the same medium after striking a smooth and polished surface is called reflection of light. (1 Mark)

Laws of Reflection: (1 Mark)

  • First Law: The incident ray, the reflected ray, and the normal at the point of incidence, all lie in the same plane.
  • Second Law: The angle of incidence ($\angle i$) is always equal to the angle of reflection ($\angle r$).

Q3. Define the term 'intermolecular space'. How does it logically differ in the arrangement of solids, liquids, and gases?
[3 Marks]
Solution:

Intermolecular Space: The empty space existing between the molecules of matter is known as intermolecular space. (1 Mark)

Difference in states: (2 Marks)

  • Solids: Molecules are tightly packed; hence, intermolecular space is minimum (negligible).
  • Liquids: Molecules are loosely packed compared to solids; hence, intermolecular space is moderate (more than solids, less than gases).
  • Gases: Molecules are very far apart; hence, intermolecular space is maximum.
Q4. What is a shadow? State the three essential conditions required for a shadow to be formed.
[3 Marks]
Solution:

Shadow: A dark patch formed on a screen when an opaque object blocks the path of light is called a shadow. (1.5 Marks)

Essential Conditions: (1.5 Marks - 0.5 each)

  1. A source of light.
  2. An opaque object to obstruct the light.
  3. A screen or surface behind the object on which the shadow can be cast.
Q5. Explain the Kinetic Theory of Matter. Postulate any three main points regarding the movement, attraction, and arrangement of particles in matter.
[5 Marks]
Solution:

Kinetic Theory of Matter: This theory states that all matter is composed of tiny, invisible particles (atoms or molecules) which are in a continuous state of random motion. Because of this motion, they possess kinetic energy. (2 Marks)

Main Postulates (Any three): (3 Marks - 1 each)

  • Movement/Kinetic Energy: The particles of matter are always in continuous random motion. The kinetic energy of particles increases with an increase in temperature.
  • Intermolecular Force of Attraction: There exists a force of attraction between the particles of matter. This force is maximum in solids and minimum in gases.
  • Intermolecular Space: There are spaces between the particles of matter. This space varies across the three states (least in solids, most in gases).
  • Arrangement: The state of matter (solid, liquid, gas) depends entirely on how the particles are arranged due to the balance between intermolecular forces and kinetic energy.
Q6. (a) What do you understand by 'lateral inversion'? Give one practical example.
(b) State three primary characteristics of the image formed by a plane mirror.
[5 Marks]
Solution:

(a) Lateral Inversion: (2 Marks)
The phenomenon where the right side of an object appears as the left side of the image, and vice-versa, when reflected in a plane mirror is called lateral inversion.
Example: The word 'AMBULANCE' is written laterally inverted on vehicles so drivers ahead can read it correctly in their rear-view mirrors.

(b) Characteristics of Plane Mirror Image: (3 Marks - 1 each)

  • The image formed is virtual (cannot be obtained on a screen) and erect (upright).
  • The image is of the same size as the object.
  • The image is formed at the same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front of it.
SECTION B: MATHEMATICS SOLUTIONS (20 Marks)
Q7. An unbiased coin is tossed once. What is the probability of getting (i) a head? (ii) a tail?
[2 Marks]
Solution:

Total possible outcomes when a coin is tossed = {Head (H), Tail (T)} = 2

$$ \text{(i) } P(\text{Head}) = \frac{\text{Number of favorable outcomes}}{\text{Total number of outcomes}} = \frac{1}{2} $$
$$ \text{(ii) } P(\text{Tail}) = \frac{\text{Number of favorable outcomes}}{\text{Total number of outcomes}} = \frac{1}{2} $$

(1 Mark for each correct part)

Q8. A standard die is thrown once. Find the probability of getting a prime number.
[2 Marks]
Solution:

Total possible outcomes when a die is thrown = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}. Therefore, Total outcomes = 6.

Favorable outcomes (Prime numbers) = {2, 3, 5}. Number of favorable outcomes = 3.

$$ P(\text{Prime Number}) = \frac{\text{Number of favorable outcomes}}{\text{Total outcomes}} $$
$$ P(\text{Prime Number}) = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2} $$

(1 Mark for identifying primes, 1 Mark for final probability)

Q9. A bag contains 4 red balls, 5 blue balls, and 3 green balls. Find the probability that the ball drawn is: (i) a blue ball. (ii) not a red ball.
[3 Marks]
Solution:

Total number of balls in the bag = $4 \text{ (red)} + 5 \text{ (blue)} + 3 \text{ (green)} = 12$ balls.

Total possible outcomes = 12.

(i) Probability of getting a blue ball:

Number of blue balls (Favorable outcomes) = 5.

$$ P(\text{Blue Ball}) = \frac{5}{12} $$

(ii) Probability of getting 'not a red ball':

Number of balls that are not red = Total balls - Red balls = $12 - 4 = 8$.
(Alternatively, Non-red balls = Blue + Green = $5 + 3 = 8$)

$$ P(\text{Not Red}) = \frac{8}{12} = \frac{2}{3} $$

(1 Mark for total balls, 1 Mark for part (i), 1 Mark for part (ii))

Q10. The letters of the word 'MATHEMATICS' are written on cards. What is the probability of drawing a vowel?
[3 Marks]
Solution:

Total number of letters in the word 'MATHEMATICS' = 11.
So, Total possible outcomes = 11. (1 Mark)

The vowels in the word 'MATHEMATICS' are: A, E, A, I.

Number of vowels (Favorable outcomes) = 4. (1 Mark)

$$ P(\text{Drawing a Vowel}) = \frac{\text{Number of Vowels}}{\text{Total Letters}} = \frac{4}{11} $$

(1 Mark for final probability)

Q11. In a class of 40 students, 15 like mathematics, 12 like science, and the rest like languages. Find the probability that a student chosen at random: (i) likes mathematics. (ii) likes languages. (iii) does not like science.
[5 Marks]
Solution:

Total number of students (Total outcomes) = 40. (0.5 Mark)

Students who like Mathematics = 15.
Students who like Science = 12.
Students who like Languages = $40 - (15 + 12) = 40 - 27 = 13$. (1.5 Marks)

(i) Probability that student likes Mathematics:

$$ P(\text{Maths}) = \frac{15}{40} = \frac{3}{8} $$

(ii) Probability that student likes Languages:

$$ P(\text{Languages}) = \frac{13}{40} $$

(iii) Probability that student does not like Science:

Students who do not like Science = Total students - Students who like Science = $40 - 12 = 28$.
(Or, Maths + Languages = $15 + 13 = 28$).

$$ P(\text{Not Science}) = \frac{28}{40} = \frac{7}{10} $$

(1 Mark for each probability part)

Q12. Two coins are tossed simultaneously. (i) Write the sample space. (ii) Probability of getting exactly one head. (iii) Probability of getting at least one tail.
[5 Marks]
Solution:

(i) Sample Space:

When two coins are tossed, the possible outcomes are:
Sample Space (S) = { (H, H), (H, T), (T, H), (T, T) }
Total number of possible outcomes = 4. (2 Marks)

(ii) Probability of getting exactly one head:

Outcomes with exactly one head = { (H, T), (T, H) }
Number of favorable outcomes = 2.

$$ P(\text{Exactly one head}) = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2} $$

(1.5 Marks)

(iii) Probability of getting at least one tail:

"At least one tail" means 1 tail or 2 tails.
Favorable outcomes = { (H, T), (T, H), (T, T) }
Number of favorable outcomes = 3.

$$ P(\text{At least one tail}) = \frac{3}{4} $$

(1.5 Marks)
