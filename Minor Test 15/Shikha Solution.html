PART I : SCIENCE (20 Marks)
Q1. State any two fundamental differences between an Element and a Compound.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
Element Compound
1. It is a pure substance made up of only one kind of atom. 1. It is a pure substance made up of two or more different elements combined chemically.
2. It cannot be broken down into simpler substances by physical or chemical means. 2. It can be broken down into its constituent elements by chemical methods.

(Award 1 mark for each correct difference)

Q2. Define contact and non-contact forces. Give one example of each.
[2 Marks]
Solution:
  • Contact Force: The force that acts on a body only when it is in physical contact with the object exerting the force.
    Example: Frictional force, Muscular force. (1 Mark)
  • Non-Contact Force: The force that acts on a body from a distance without any physical contact.
    Example: Gravitational force, Magnetic force. (1 Mark)
Q3. Identify the most appropriate separation technique used for separating:
(a) Iron filings from sand
(b) Pure water from a salt solution
(c) Mud from muddy water
[3 Marks]
Solution:
  1. Magnetic Separation (Since iron is magnetic and sand is non-magnetic).
  2. Distillation (Evaporation leaves salt behind, but distillation allows us to collect pure water by condensation).
  3. Filtration or Decantation (Filtration is the most appropriate as it completely separates suspended mud particles).

(1 Mark for each correct technique)

Q4. A heavy wooden box is resting on the floor. State any three effects that a force can produce on a body, illustrating them with examples from everyday life.
[3 Marks]
Solution:

A force can produce the following effects (any three):

  1. Force can initiate motion in a stationary object: Pushing the heavy wooden box makes it move.
  2. Force can change the speed or direction of a moving object: Applying brakes to a moving bicycle.
  3. Force can change the shape and size of an object: Squeezing a rubber ball or stretching a rubber band.
  4. Force can stop a moving object: Catching a moving cricket ball.

(1 Mark for each correct effect with an example)

Q5. (a) Define a mixture. (2 Marks)
(b) State three distinct properties that differentiate a mixture from a chemical compound. (3 Marks)
[5 Marks]
Solution:

(a) Mixture: A mixture is a substance consisting of two or more elements or compounds mechanically mixed together in any proportion, where they do not combine chemically. (2 Marks)

(b) Differences: (1 Mark each, any three)

  • Composition: In a mixture, constituents can be present in any ratio. In a compound, constituents are present in a fixed mass ratio.
  • Properties: A mixture shows the properties of its constituent substances. A compound has entirely new properties different from its constituents.
  • Separation: Constituents of a mixture can be separated by simple physical methods. Constituents of a compound can only be separated by chemical methods.
  • Energy change: No energy is generally released or absorbed when a mixture is formed. Energy in the form of heat or light is usually released or absorbed during compound formation.
Q6. (a) What is friction? Which type of force is it? (2 Marks)
(b) "Friction is a necessary evil." Justify by stating two advantages and two disadvantages. (3 Marks)
[5 Marks]
Solution:

(a) Friction: The force that opposes the relative motion between two surfaces in contact is called friction. It is a Contact Force. (1 + 1 = 2 Marks)

(b) Advantages (Any two for 1.5 Marks):

  • It allows us to walk on the ground without slipping.
  • It enables vehicles to stop when brakes are applied.
  • It helps us to write on paper using a pen or pencil.

Disadvantages (Any two for 1.5 Marks):

  • It causes wear and tear of moving parts in machinery and soles of shoes.
  • It produces unnecessary heat, wasting energy (e.g., in car engines).
  • It reduces the efficiency of machines.

Q8. Arrange in ascending order: \( 3.14, \quad 3.41, \quad 3.04, \quad 3.104, \quad 3.401 \)
[2 Marks]
Solution:

Step 1: Convert all decimals to like decimals (same number of decimal places, i.e., 3).

\( 3.140, \quad 3.410, \quad 3.040, \quad 3.104, \quad 3.401 \)

Step 2: Compare the whole numbers (all are 3), then compare the decimal parts:

\( 040 < 104 < 140 < 401 < 410 \)

Step 3: Arrange in ascending order:

\( 3.04 < 3.104 < 3.14 < 3.401 < 3.41 \)

(2 Marks for correct final sequence. 1 Mark if one misplaced).

Q10. A tailor requires \( 2.45 \) meters of cloth to stitch one shirt. Calculate cloth required for 15 shirts.
[3 Marks]
Solution:

Cloth required for 1 shirt = \( 2.45 \text{ m} \)

Number of shirts to be stitched = 15

Total cloth required = Cloth for one shirt \( \times \) Total number of shirts

\( \text{Total Cloth} = 2.45 \times 15 \)
\( \phantom{\times} 245 \) (ignoring decimal for calculation)
\( \underline{\times \phantom{0}15} \)
\( 1225 \)
\( \underline{2450} \)
\( 3675 \)

Since there are two decimal places in \( 2.45 \), the final product will have two decimal places.

Total cloth required = \( 36.75 \text{ meters} \).

(Formula/setup: 1 Mark. Calculation: 1 Mark. Final Answer with Units: 1 Mark).

Q11. Evaluate: (a) \( 4.56 \times 1.2 \) (b) \( 15.12 \div 3.6 \)
[5 Marks]
Solution:
(a) Multiplication (2 Marks):
Ignore decimals and multiply: \( 456 \times 12 = 5472 \)
Total decimal places = \( 2 \text{ (from 4.56)} + 1 \text{ (from 1.2)} = 3 \) decimal places.
Place decimal 3 digits from the right:
\( 4.56 \times 1.2 = 5.472 \)

(b) Division (3 Marks):
\( 15.12 \div 3.6 = \frac{15.12}{3.6} \)
Multiply numerator and denominator by 10 to remove decimal from divisor:
\( = \frac{151.2}{36} \)
Now divide 151.2 by 36:
\( 36 \times 4 = 144 \)
\( 151 - 144 = 7 \). Bring down 2, making it 72 (place decimal point in quotient).
\( 36 \times 2 = 72 \)
Quotient = \( 4.2 \)
Q12. Rahul bought \( 5.5 \text{ kg} \) of apples at Rs. 120.50/kg and \( 2.75 \text{ kg} \) of oranges at Rs. 80.25/kg. Calculate total amount paid.
[5 Marks]
Solution:

Step 1: Cost of Apples (2 Marks)

Quantity of apples = \( 5.5 \text{ kg} \)
Rate per kg = Rs. 120.50
Total cost of apples = \( 5.5 \times 120.50 \)
Calculation: \( 12050 \times 55 = 662750 \). Placing decimal 3 places from right (2 from 120.50 + 1 from 5.5):
Cost of apples = Rs. 662.75

Step 2: Cost of Oranges (2 Marks)

Quantity of oranges = \( 2.75 \text{ kg} \)
Rate per kg = Rs. 80.25
Total cost of oranges = \( 2.75 \times 80.25 \)
Calculation: \( 8025 \times 275 = 2206875 \). Placing decimal 4 places from right:
Cost of oranges = Rs. 220.6875 (which can be rounded to Rs. 220.69 in real life, but exact calculation is kept for maths).

Step 3: Total Amount Paid (1 Mark)

Total Amount = Cost of Apples + Cost of Oranges
Total Amount = \( 662.75 + 220.6875 \)
Make like decimals:
\( \phantom{+} 662.7500 \)
\( \underline{+ 220.6875} \)
\( \phantom{+} 883.4375 \)

Final Answer: The total amount paid by Rahul is Rs. 883.4375 (or Rs. 883.44 approx).
