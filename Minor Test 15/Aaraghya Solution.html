SECTION A (10 Marks)
1.
Express the ratio \( 75 \text{ cm} \) to \( 1.5 \text{ m} \) in its simplest form.
[2]
Solution:
To find the ratio, both quantities must be in the same unit.
Convert \( 1.5 \text{ m} \) into cm:
\[ 1.5 \text{ m} = 1.5 \times 100 \text{ cm} = 150 \text{ cm} \]
Now, the ratio is \( 75 \text{ cm} : 150 \text{ cm} \)
\[ = \frac{75}{150} = \frac{1}{2} \]
Ratio = 1 : 2
2.
If \( A : B = 3 : 4 \) and \( B : C = 8 : 9 \), find the ratio \( A : C \).
[2]
Solution:
Given: \( \frac{A}{B} = \frac{3}{4} \) and \( \frac{B}{C} = \frac{8}{9} \)
To find \( A:C \), multiply the two ratios:
\[ \frac{A}{B} \times \frac{B}{C} = \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{8}{9} \]
\[ \frac{A}{C} = \frac{3 \times 8}{4 \times 9} = \frac{24}{36} \]
Simplifying \( \frac{24}{36} \) by dividing numerator and denominator by 12:
\[ \frac{A}{C} = \frac{2}{3} \]
A : C = 2 : 3
3.
Determine whether the numbers \( 4, 6, 8, \) and \( 12 \) are in proportion.
[2]
Solution:
Four numbers \(a, b, c, d\) are in proportion if \( \text{Product of Extremes} = \text{Product of Means} \).
Here, Extremes are \( 4 \) and \( 12 \). Means are \( 6 \) and \( 8 \).
\[ \text{Product of Extremes} = 4 \times 12 = 48 \]
\[ \text{Product of Means} = 6 \times 8 = 48 \]
Since \( 48 = 48 \), the condition is satisfied.
Yes, they are in proportion.
4.
A manufacturing machine produces \( 150 \) widgets in \( 3 \text{ hours} \). How many widgets will it produce in \( 5 \text{ hours} \)?
[2]
Solution:
Using the unitary method (Direct Variation):
Widgets produced in \( 3 \text{ hours} = 150 \)
\[ \text{Widgets produced in } 1 \text{ hour} = \frac{150}{3} = 50 \]
\[ \text{Widgets produced in } 5 \text{ hours} = 50 \times 5 = 250 \]
The machine will produce 250 widgets.
5.
Find the mean proportional between \( 9 \) and \( 25 \).
[2]
Solution:
Let the mean proportional be \( x \).
Then, \( 9, x, \) and \( 25 \) are in continued proportion, meaning \( 9 : x :: x : 25 \).
\[ x \times x = 9 \times 25 \] \[ x^2 = 225 \] \[ x = \sqrt{225} = 15 \]
The mean proportional is 15.
SECTION B (15 Marks)
6.
Divide ₹\( 1400 \) among Ram, Shyam, and Mohan in the ratio \( 2 : 3 : 5 \). Find the final share of each.
[3]
Solution:
Given Ratio = \( 2 : 3 : 5 \)
Sum of ratio parts = \( 2 + 3 + 5 = 10 \) parts.
Total Amount = ₹\( 1400 \)
Value of 1 part:
\[ \frac{1400}{10} = \] ₹\( 140 \)
Calculating individual shares:
\[ \text{Ram's share} = 2 \text{ parts} = 2 \times 140 = \] ₹\( 280 \)
\[ \text{Shyam's share} = 3 \text{ parts} = 3 \times 140 = \] ₹\( 420 \)
\[ \text{Mohan's share} = 5 \text{ parts} = 5 \times 140 = \] ₹\( 700 \)
Shares: Ram = ₹280, Shyam = ₹420, Mohan = ₹700
7.
If \( 15 \) men can complete a piece of work in \( 42 \) days, in how many days will \( 35 \) men complete the same work?
[3]
Solution:
This is a case of Inverse Variation (more men = less days).
Number of days taken by 15 men = 42 days
Number of days taken by 1 man (requires more time):
\[ = 15 \times 42 = 630 \text{ days} \]
Number of days taken by 35 men:
\[ = \frac{630}{35} \]
Solving the division: \( 630 \div 35 = 18 \)
35 men will complete the work in 18 days.
8.
Two numbers are in the ratio \( 5 : 7 \). If \( 3 \) is subtracted from each number, the new ratio becomes \( 2 : 3 \). Find the original numbers.
[3]
Solution:
Let the original numbers be \( 5x \) and \( 7x \).
According to the problem, if 3 is subtracted from both:
\[ \frac{5x - 3}{7x - 3} = \frac{2}{3} \]
Cross multiplying to solve for \( x \):
\[ \begin{aligned} 3(5x - 3) &= 2(7x - 3) \\ 15x - 9 &= 14x - 6 \\ 15x - 14x &= -6 + 9 \\ x &= 3 \end{aligned} \]
Now substitute \( x = 3 \) back into the original expressions:
\[ \text{First number} = 5 \times 3 = 15 \]
\[ \text{Second number} = 7 \times 3 = 21 \]
The original numbers are 15 and 21.
9.
The cost of \( 8 \text{ kg} \) of premium rice is ₹\( 360 \).
(a)
What will be the cost of \( 15 \text{ kg} \) of this rice?
(b)
What quantity of rice can be purchased for ₹\( 675 \)?
[3]
Solution:
First, find the cost of \( 1 \text{ kg} \) of rice (Unitary Method):
\[ \text{Cost of 1 kg} = \frac{360}{8} = \] ₹\( 45 \)
(a) Cost of \( 15 \text{ kg} \) of rice:
\[ = 15 \times 45 = \] ₹\( 675 \)
(b) Quantity of rice for ₹\( 675 \):
From part (a), we already see the answer is 15 kg. Mathematically:
\[ \text{Quantity} = \frac{\text{Total Amount}}{\text{Cost per kg}} = \frac{675}{45} = 15 \text{ kg} \]
(a) Cost is ₹675. (b) Quantity is 15 kg.
10.
What specific number must be added to each of the numbers \( 5, 9, 7, \) and \( 12 \) so that the resulting numbers are in continued proportion?
[3]
Solution:
Let the required number to be added be \( x \).
The new numbers will be \( (5+x), (9+x), (7+x), \) and \( (12+x) \).
For these to be in proportion:
\[ \frac{5+x}{9+x} = \frac{7+x}{12+x} \]
Cross multiplying:
\[ \begin{aligned} (5+x)(12+x) &= (9+x)(7+x) \\ 60 + 5x + 12x + x^2 &= 63 + 9x + 7x + x^2 \end{aligned} \]
Subtracting \( x^2 \) from both sides and simplifying:
\[ \begin{aligned} 60 + 17x &= 63 + 16x \\ 17x - 16x &= 63 - 60 \\ x &= 3 \end{aligned} \]
The number to be added is 3.
SECTION C (15 Marks)
11.
A military garrison of \( 1200 \) men has provisions for \( 60 \text{ days} \). After \( 15 \text{ days} \), \( 300 \) more men join the garrison. How long will the remaining food last for the newly formed group? Support your answer with a detailed unitary calculation.
[5]
Solution:
Initial state: Provisions are sufficient for 1200 men for 60 days.
After 15 days, food has been consumed.
Remaining food is sufficient for the original 1200 men for:
\[ 60 - 15 = 45 \text{ days} \]
Now, 300 more men join. New total number of men:
\[ 1200 + 300 = 1500 \text{ men} \]
Let the remaining food last for \( D \) days for the 1500 men.
This is an Inverse Variation problem (more men = fewer days).
\[ M_1 \times D_1 = M_2 \times D_2 \] \[ 1200 \times 45 = 1500 \times D \]
Solving for \( D \):
\[ D = \frac{1200 \times 45}{1500} \] \[ D = \frac{12 \times 45}{15} \] \[ D = 12 \times 3 = 36 \]
The remaining food will last for 36 days.
12.
Solve the following proportional relationships:
(a)
If \( \frac{3x - 5y}{3x + 5y} = \frac{1}{5} \), find the value of the ratio \( x : y \).
(b)
Find the fourth proportional to the decimal numbers \( 2.5, 1.5, \) and \( 3.0 \).
[5]
Solution:
Part (a) [3 Marks]:
Given:
\[ \frac{3x - 5y}{3x + 5y} = \frac{1}{5} \]
Cross multiply:
\[ \begin{aligned} 5(3x - 5y) &= 1(3x + 5y) \\ 15x - 25y &= 3x + 5y \end{aligned} \]
Group like terms together:
\[ \begin{aligned} 15x - 3x &= 5y + 25y \\ 12x &= 30y \\ \frac{x}{y} &= \frac{30}{12} \end{aligned} \]
Simplify the fraction (divide by 6):
\[ \frac{x}{y} = \frac{5}{2} \]
x : y = 5 : 2
Part (b) [2 Marks]:
Let the fourth proportional be \( p \). Then, \( 2.5, 1.5, 3.0, p \) are in proportion.
\[ \frac{2.5}{1.5} = \frac{3.0}{p} \]
Cross multiply:
\[ \begin{aligned} 2.5 \times p &= 1.5 \times 3.0 \\ 2.5p &= 4.5 \\ p &= \frac{4.5}{2.5} = \frac{45}{25} = 1.8 \end{aligned} \]
Fourth proportional is 1.8.
13.
A transport bus travels \( 432 \text{ km} \) consuming \( 48 \text{ litres} \) of diesel.
(a)
How far would the bus travel if it is supplied with \( 20 \text{ litres} \) of diesel?
(b)
How many litres of diesel are required for the bus to travel a total distance of \( 630 \text{ km} \)?
[5]
Solution:
First, establish the base rate (Unitary Method).
Distance traveled on 48 litres = 432 km.
\[ \text{Distance on 1 litre} = \frac{432}{48} = 9 \text{ km} \]
Part (a) [2 Marks]:
We need to find the distance for 20 litres.
\[ \text{Distance on 20 litres} = 20 \times 9 = 180 \text{ km} \]
The bus travels 180 km on 20 litres.
Part (b) [3 Marks]:
We need to find the diesel required for 630 km.
We know that 9 km requires 1 litre of diesel.
\[ \text{Diesel for 1 km} = \frac{1}{9} \text{ litres} \]
\[ \text{Diesel for 630 km} = 630 \times \frac{1}{9} = \frac{630}{9} = 70 \text{ litres} \]
70 litres of diesel are required.
