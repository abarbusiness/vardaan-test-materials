SECTION A (10 Marks)
1.
Define an exterior angle of a triangle. State the theorem related to the exterior angle of a triangle.
[2]
Solution:

Definition: An exterior angle of a triangle is formed when any one side of the triangle is extended outwards. [1 Mark]

Theorem: The Exterior Angle Theorem states that the measure of an exterior angle of a triangle is equal to the sum of the measures of its two interior opposite angles. [1 Mark]

2.
Identify the constants and variables in the following algebraic expression:
\( 3x^2y - 7xy + \frac{5}{2} - z \)
[2]
Solution:

In the given expression \( 3x^2y - 7xy + \frac{5}{2} - z \):

Variables: The literal numbers whose values can change. They are \(x, y, \) and \(z\). [1 Mark]

Constants: The fixed numerical values that do not change. The constant term is \( \frac{5}{2} \). (Note: 3 and -7 are coefficients, but \(\frac{5}{2}\) is a pure constant term). [1 Mark]

3.
In a triangle \(ABC\), if \(\angle A = 45^\circ\) and \(\angle B = 65^\circ\), find the measure of \(\angle C\). What type of triangle is \(ABC\) based on its angles?
[2]
Solution:

According to the angle sum property of a triangle:

\(\angle A + \angle B + \angle C = 180^\circ\)

\(45^\circ + 65^\circ + \angle C = 180^\circ\)

\(110^\circ + \angle C = 180^\circ\)

\(\angle C = 180^\circ - 110^\circ = 70^\circ\)
[1 Mark]

Since all three angles (\(45^\circ, 65^\circ, 70^\circ\)) are less than \(90^\circ\), triangle \(ABC\) is an acute-angled triangle. [1 Mark]

4.
Write down the algebraic expression for the following statement:
"Five times a number \(x\) is subtracted from the sum of \(y\) and \(z\)."
[2]
Solution:

Step 1: "Sum of \(y\) and \(z\)" is written as \((y + z)\).

Step 2: "Five times a number \(x\)" is written as \(5x\).

Step 3: Subtracting \(5x\) from \((y + z)\) gives:

\((y + z) - 5x\)
[2 Marks (1 for each correct part)]

5.
Is it possible to construct a triangle with side lengths \(3\text{ cm}\), \(4\text{ cm}\), and \(8\text{ cm}\)? Give a valid mathematical reason for your answer.
[2]
Solution:

No, it is not possible. [0.5 Marks]

Reason: According to the Triangle Inequality Theorem, the sum of the lengths of any two sides of a triangle must be strictly greater than the length of the third side. [0.5 Marks]

Let's check the given sides: \(3\text{ cm}, 4\text{ cm}, 8\text{ cm}\).

Sum of two smaller sides = \(3 + 4 = 7\text{ cm}\).

Since \(7\text{ cm}\) is not greater than the third side (\(8\text{ cm}\)), a triangle cannot be formed. (\(3 + 4 \ngtr 8\)). [1 Mark]

6.
Evaluate the algebraic expression \( 2a^2 - 3ab + b^2 \) when \( a = -2 \) and \( b = 3 \).
[3]
Solution:

Given expression: \( 2a^2 - 3ab + b^2 \)

Substitute \( a = -2 \) and \( b = 3 \) into the expression: [1 Mark for correct substitution]

\( = 2(-2)^2 - 3(-2)(3) + (3)^2 \)

Calculate the terms:

\( = 2(4) - (-18) + 9 \) [1 Mark for correct squaring and multiplication]
\( = 8 + 18 + 9 \)
\( = 35 \) [1 Mark for final correct answer]
7.
In an isosceles triangle \(PQR\), \(PQ = PR\). If the vertical angle \(\angle P\) is \(50^\circ\), calculate the base angles \(\angle Q\) and \(\angle R\). State the property used.
[3]
Solution:

Since triangle \(PQR\) is isosceles with \(PQ = PR\), the angles opposite to these equal sides are also equal. Therefore, \(\angle Q = \angle R\). Let \(\angle Q = \angle R = x\). [1 Mark for stating \(\angle Q = \angle R\)]

Using the Angle Sum Property of a triangle (\(\angle P + \angle Q + \angle R = 180^\circ\)):

\(50^\circ + x + x = 180^\circ\)
[1 Mark for equation]

\(2x = 180^\circ - 50^\circ\)

\(2x = 130^\circ\)

\(x = \frac{130^\circ}{2} = 65^\circ\)

Therefore, \(\angle Q = 65^\circ\) and \(\angle R = 65^\circ\). [1 Mark for final answer]

Property used: Angles opposite to equal sides of an isosceles triangle are equal, and angle sum property.

8.
Classify the following algebraic expressions as monomials, binomials, or trinomials. Also, state the degree of expression (b):
(a) \( 7pqr \)
(b) \( 4x^3 - 2xy^2 + 5 \)
(c) \( m^2 - n^2 \)
[3]
Solution:

(a) \( 7pqr \) has only one term. It is a Monomial. [0.5 Mark]

(b) \( 4x^3 - 2xy^2 + 5 \) has three terms. It is a Trinomial. [0.5 Mark]

    Degree of (b): The terms are \(4x^3\) (degree 3), \(-2xy^2\) (degree \(1+2=3\)), and \(5\) (degree 0). The highest degree is 3. So, Degree = 3. [1 Mark]

(c) \( m^2 - n^2 \) has two terms. It is a Binomial. [1 Mark]

9.
The exterior angle of a triangle is \(110^\circ\) and one of its interior opposite angles is \(40^\circ\). Find the other two angles of the triangle.
[3]
Solution:

Let the interior angles of the triangle be \(\angle 1, \angle 2, \) and \(\angle 3\).

Let Exterior Angle = \(110^\circ\). Let interior opposite angles be \(\angle 1\) and \(\angle 2\). Let \(\angle 1 = 40^\circ\).

To find \(\angle 2\): Using the Exterior Angle Theorem (Exterior Angle = Sum of two interior opposite angles):

\(110^\circ = \angle 1 + \angle 2\)
[1 Mark for using theorem]
\(110^\circ = 40^\circ + \angle 2\)

\(\angle 2 = 110^\circ - 40^\circ = \textbf{70^\circ}\)
[1 Mark]

To find \(\angle 3\): \(\angle 3\) and the exterior angle form a linear pair on a straight line.

\(\angle 3 + 110^\circ = 180^\circ\)

\(\angle 3 = 180^\circ - 110^\circ = \textbf{70^\circ}\)
[1 Mark]
(Alternatively, use angle sum property: \(\angle 1 + \angle 2 + \angle 3 = 180 \Rightarrow 40 + 70 + \angle 3 = 180 \Rightarrow \angle 3 = 70^\circ\))

The other two angles are \(70^\circ\) and \(70^\circ\).

10.
Add the following algebraic expressions:
\( (3x^2 - 5x + 2) \) and \( (-x^2 + 4x - 6) \)
[3]
Solution:

Write the expressions with an addition sign between them:

\( (3x^2 - 5x + 2) + (-x^2 + 4x - 6) \)

Remove the brackets:

\( 3x^2 - 5x + 2 - x^2 + 4x - 6 \) [1 Mark]

Group the like terms together:

\( = (3x^2 - x^2) + (-5x + 4x) + (2 - 6) \) [1 Mark]

Combine the coefficients of like terms:

\( = 2x^2 + (-1x) + (-4) \)
\( = 2x^2 - x - 4 \) [1 Mark for final answer]
SECTION C (15 Marks)
11.
In a triangle \(ABC\), the angles are in the ratio \( 2 : 3 : 4 \).
(a) Find the measure of each angle of the triangle.
(b) Classify the triangle based on its angles.
(c) State the property of triangles used to solve this problem.
[5]
Solution:

(a) Let the angles of the triangle be \(2x, 3x,\) and \(4x\). [0.5 Marks]

Using the angle sum property of a triangle:

\(2x + 3x + 4x = 180^\circ\)
[0.5 Marks]
\(9x = 180^\circ\)

\(x = \frac{180^\circ}{9} = 20^\circ\)
[1 Mark]

Now, calculate each angle:
First angle = \(2x = 2 \times 20^\circ = \textbf{40^\circ}\)
Second angle = \(3x = 3 \times 20^\circ = \textbf{60^\circ}\)
Third angle = \(4x = 4 \times 20^\circ = \textbf{80^\circ}\) [1 Mark for all three correct]

(b) Since all three angles (\(40^\circ, 60^\circ, 80^\circ\)) are less than \(90^\circ\), it is an acute-angled triangle. [1 Mark]

(c) Property used: Angle Sum Property of a Triangle (which states that the sum of the interior angles of a triangle is \(180^\circ\)). [1 Mark]

12.
Simplify the following algebraic expression by opening the brackets and combining like terms:
\( 5x - [3y - \{2x - (y - x)\}] \)
Show each step of your working clearly.
[5]
Solution:

Expression: \( 5x - [3y - \{2x - (y - x)\}] \)

Follow the BODMAS/PEMDAS rule, removing innermost brackets first.

Step 1: Remove the innermost parentheses \((\ )\). Keep in mind the negative sign outside changes the signs inside.

\( = 5x - [3y - \{2x - y + x\}] \)
[1.5 Marks]

Step 2: Simplify the terms inside the curly braces \(\{\ \}\). Combine like terms \(2x\) and \(x\).

\( = 5x - [3y - \{3x - y\}] \)
[1 Mark]

Step 3: Remove the curly braces \(\{\ \}\). Again, the negative sign outside changes the signs inside.

\( = 5x - [3y - 3x + y] \)
[1 Mark]

Step 4: Simplify the terms inside the square brackets \([\ ]\). Combine \(3y\) and \(y\).

\( = 5x - [-3x + 4y] \)
[0.5 Marks]

Step 5: Remove the square brackets \([\ ]\). The negative sign distributes.

\( = 5x + 3x - 4y \)
[0.5 Marks]

Step 6: Final simplification by combining \(5x\) and \(3x\).

\( = 8x - 4y \)
[0.5 Marks]

13.
Consider the algebraic expression: \( 4x^3y - 5x^2y^2 + 7xy^3 - 2 \)
(a) How many terms are there in this expression?
(b) Write the coefficient of \(x^2\) in the term \( -5x^2y^2 \).
(c) What is the numerical coefficient of the term \( 4x^3y \)?
(d) Find the degree of the entire algebraic expression. Explain how you arrived at it.
[5]
Solution:

Given expression: \( 4x^3y - 5x^2y^2 + 7xy^3 - 2 \)

(a) Terms are separated by '+' or '-' signs. The terms are \(4x^3y\), \(-5x^2y^2\), \(7xy^3\), and \(-2\). Therefore, there are 4 terms. [1 Mark]

(b) The coefficient of \(x^2\) in the term \(-5x^2y^2\) is the remaining part of the term when \(x^2\) is removed. Therefore, the coefficient is \(-5y^2\). [1 Mark]

(c) The numerical coefficient is the constant number multiplier of the term. For \(4x^3y\), the numerical coefficient is 4. [1 Mark]

(d) Degree of the expression: [2 Marks]
To find the degree, calculate the sum of the powers of the variables in each term:
- Term 1: \(4x^3y^1 \rightarrow\) sum of powers = \(3 + 1 = 4\)
- Term 2: \(-5x^2y^2 \rightarrow\) sum of powers = \(2 + 2 = 4\)
- Term 3: \(7x^1y^3 \rightarrow\) sum of powers = \(1 + 3 = 4\)
- Term 4: \(-2\) (constant) \(\rightarrow\) degree = \(0\)
The highest sum of powers among all the terms is 4. Therefore, the degree of the algebraic expression is 4.
