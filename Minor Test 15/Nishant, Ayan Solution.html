SECTION A (10 Marks)
1.
Define a 'simple machine'. Give one everyday example of a simple machine.
[2]
Ans.
A simple machine is a mechanical device that changes the direction or magnitude of a force. It helps us to do work more easily by applying less effort over a greater distance. (1 Mark)
Example: A crowbar, a pair of scissors, or a knife. (1 Mark)
2.
What is a lever? Name the three main points of a lever.
[2]
Ans.
A lever is a rigid rod or bar that rests and turns on a fixed point. (0.5 Marks)
The three main points of a lever are:
1. Fulcrum (F): The fixed point about which the lever turns.
2. Load (L): The object to be lifted or moved.
3. Effort (E): The force applied to the lever to move the load. (1.5 Marks)
3.
State the fundamental difference between an inclined plane and a wedge.
[2]
Ans.
An inclined plane is a flat, stationary sloping surface used to push or pull loads up or down.
A wedge is fundamentally two inclined planes placed back-to-back. Unlike a standard inclined plane which is stationary, a wedge moves to do its work (e.g., splitting an object). (2 Marks)

SECTION B (15 Marks)
6.
Classify the following into classes of levers with a brief reason: (a) See-saw (b) Wheelbarrow (c) Sugar tongs
[3]
Ans.
(a) See-saw: Class I Lever. Reason: The Fulcrum is exactly between the Load and Effort. (1 Mark)
(b) Wheelbarrow: Class II Lever. Reason: The Load is between the Fulcrum (wheel) and the Effort (handles). (1 Mark)
(c) Sugar tongs: Class III Lever. Reason: The Effort is applied in the middle, between the Fulcrum (hinge) and the Load (sugar cube). (1 Mark)
7.
Explain how a pulley makes our work easier. Differentiate between a fixed pulley and a movable pulley.
[3]
Ans.
A pulley makes work easier by changing the direction of the applied force, allowing us to use our body weight (gravity) to pull downwards to lift a load upwards. (1.5 Marks)

Difference:
A fixed pulley remains attached to a rigid support and only changes the direction of force (Mechanical Advantage = 1). A movable pulley moves with the load, essentially multiplying the effort force (Mechanical Advantage = 2), meaning it requires half the effort to lift the load. (1.5 Marks)
8.
What is mechanical advantage? If a machine lifts a load of \( 100 \text{ N} \) using an effort of \( 25 \text{ N} \), calculate its mechanical advantage.
[3]
Ans.
Mechanical Advantage (M.A.) is the ratio of the Load lifted by the machine to the Effort applied to it. (1 Mark)
\[ M.A. = \frac{\text{Load (L)}}{\text{Effort (E)}} \] \[ M.A. = \frac{100 \text{ N}}{25 \text{ N}} \] \[ M.A. = 4 \] (2 Marks)

10.
A piece of cloth is \( 24.5 \) meters long. If it is cut into \( 5 \) equal pieces, what is the length of each piece?
[3]
Ans.
Total length of cloth = \( 24.5 \text{ m} \)
Number of pieces = \( 5 \)
Length of each piece = \( 24.5 \div 5 \) (1 Mark)
\( = \frac{24.5}{5} \)
\( = 4.9 \text{ m} \) (2 Marks)
Answer: The length of each piece is \( 4.9 \text{ meters} \).
SECTION C (15 Marks)
11.
(a) Draw a neat, labeled block diagram of a Class II lever. (b) Explain the wheel and axle with an example.
[5]
Ans.
(a) Class II Lever Diagram:
(Student must draw a linear bar showing:)
- Fulcrum (F) at one extreme end.
- Effort (E) at the other extreme end, acting upwards.
- Load (L) in the middle, acting downwards.
[ F ] ------------------ [ L (down) ] ------------------ [ E (up) ] (3 Marks: 1 mark each for correct placement of F, L, E)
(b) Wheel and Axle:
A wheel and axle is a simple machine consisting of a large wheel rigidly secured to a smaller shaft called an axle. When the wheel turns, the axle also turns. A small force applied to the large wheel produces a larger force on the smaller axle, making turning tasks easier. (1 Mark)
Example: A steering wheel of a car or a doorknob. (1 Mark)
12.
Perform: (a) Multiply \( 4.56 \) by \( 1.2 \) (b) Divide \( 12.96 \) by \( 0.4 \)
[5]
Ans.
(a) \( 4.56 \times 1.2 \)
First, ignore the decimals and multiply: \( 456 \times 12 \)
\( 456 \times 10 = 4560 \)
\( 456 \times 2 = 912 \)
Total = \( 5472 \) (1.5 Marks)
Count the total decimal places: 2 (in 4.56) + 1 (in 1.2) = 3 decimal places.
Final Answer = \( 5.472 \) (1 Mark)
(b) \( 12.96 \div 0.4 \)
Multiply both the dividend and divisor by 10 to make the divisor a whole number:
\( = \frac{12.96 \times 10}{0.4 \times 10} = \frac{129.6}{4} \) (1 Mark)
Now divide 129.6 by 4:
\( 12 \div 4 = 3 \)
\( 9 \div 4 = 2 \) (remainder 1)
\( 16 \div 4 = 4 \)
Final Answer = \( 32.4 \) (1.5 Marks)
13.
Rahul bought 3.5 kg of apples at ₹ 120.50/kg and 2.75 kg of oranges at ₹ 80.00/kg. He gave a ₹ 1000 note. Calculate (a) total cost (b) change received.
[5]
Ans.
(a) Calculating Total Cost:
Cost of apples = \( 3.5 \text{ kg} \times ₹ 120.50 \)
\( = ₹ 421.75 \) (1 Mark)

Cost of oranges = \( 2.75 \text{ kg} \times ₹ 80.00 \)
\( = ₹ 220.00 \) (1 Mark)

Total Cost = \( ₹ 421.75 + ₹ 220.00 \)
Total Cost = \( ₹ 641.75 \) (1 Mark)
(b) Calculating Change Received:
Amount given to shopkeeper = \( ₹ 1000.00 \)
Total Cost of fruits = \( ₹ 641.75 \)
Change = \( 1000.00 - 641.75 \) (1 Mark)
Change Received = \( ₹ 358.25 \) (1 Mark)
