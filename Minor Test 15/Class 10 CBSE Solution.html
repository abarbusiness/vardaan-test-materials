SECTION A
Q1. If \( \tan A = \frac{3}{4} \), find the value of \( \sin A \cos A \).
Solution:
Given, \( \tan A = \frac{3}{4} = \frac{\text{Perpendicular}}{\text{Base}} \).
Let Perpendicular \( P = 3k \) and Base \( B = 4k \).
By Pythagoras theorem, Hypotenuse \( H = \sqrt{P^2 + B^2} = \sqrt{(3k)^2 + (4k)^2} = \sqrt{9k^2 + 16k^2} = \sqrt{25k^2} = 5k \).
Now, \( \sin A = \frac{P}{H} = \frac{3k}{5k} = \frac{3}{5} \)
\( \cos A = \frac{B}{H} = \frac{4k}{5k} = \frac{4}{5} \)
Therefore, \( \sin A \cos A = \left(\frac{3}{5}\right) \times \left(\frac{4}{5}\right) = \frac{12}{25} \).
\( \frac{12}{25} \)

Q2. Evaluate: \( 2 \tan^2 45^\circ + \cos^2 30^\circ - \sin^2 60^\circ \).
Solution:
We know the trigonometric values:
\( \tan 45^\circ = 1 \)
\( \cos 30^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \)
\( \sin 60^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \)
Substituting these values in the expression:
\( = 2(1)^2 + \left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^2 - \left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^2 \)
Notice that \( \cos^2 30^\circ \) and \( \sin^2 60^\circ \) cancel each other out.
\( = 2(1) + \frac{3}{4} - \frac{3}{4} \)
\( = 2 + 0 = 2 \).
2

Q3. Prove the identity: \( (1 - \sin^2 \theta) \sec^2 \theta = 1 \).
Solution:
LHS = \( (1 - \sin^2 \theta) \sec^2 \theta \)
Using the fundamental trigonometric identity \( \sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta = 1 \), we have \( 1 - \sin^2 \theta = \cos^2 \theta \).
Substitute this in LHS:
LHS = \( (\cos^2 \theta) \sec^2 \theta \)
We know that \( \sec \theta = \frac{1}{\cos \theta} \), so \( \sec^2 \theta = \frac{1}{\cos^2 \theta} \).
LHS = \( \cos^2 \theta \times \frac{1}{\cos^2 \theta} \)
LHS = 1 = RHS.
Hence Proved.

Q4. A tower is \( 10\sqrt{3} \) m high. Find the angle of elevation of its top from a point 30 m away from its foot on the ground.
Solution:
Let AB be the tower where height \( AB = 10\sqrt{3} \) m.
Let C be the point on the ground 30 m away from the foot B. So, \( BC = 30 \) m.
Let the angle of elevation \( \angle ACB = \theta \).
In right-angled \( \Delta ABC \):
\( \tan \theta = \frac{\text{Perpendicular}}{\text{Base}} = \frac{AB}{BC} \)
\( \tan \theta = \frac{10\sqrt{3}}{30} \)
\( \tan \theta = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \)
We know that \( \tan 30^\circ = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \).
Therefore, \( \theta = 30^\circ \).
Angle of elevation = \( 30^\circ \)

Q5. If \( \sin(A - B) = \frac{1}{2} \) and \( \cos(A + B) = \frac{1}{2} \), where \( 0^\circ < A + B \le 90^\circ \) and \( A > B \), find \( A \) and \( B \).
Solution:
Given: \( \sin(A - B) = \frac{1}{2} \).
We know \( \sin 30^\circ = \frac{1}{2} \).
Therefore, \( A - B = 30^\circ \)     ...(Equation 1)

Also given: \( \cos(A + B) = \frac{1}{2} \).
We know \( \cos 60^\circ = \frac{1}{2} \).
Therefore, \( A + B = 60^\circ \)     ...(Equation 2)

Adding Eq (1) and Eq (2):
\( (A - B) + (A + B) = 30^\circ + 60^\circ \)
\( 2A = 90^\circ \implies A = 45^\circ \).

Substitute \( A = 45^\circ \) in Eq (2):
\( 45^\circ + B = 60^\circ \implies B = 15^\circ \).
\( A = 45^\circ, B = 15^\circ \)

SECTION B
Q6. Prove that: \( \frac{\cos A}{1 + \sin A} + \frac{1 + \sin A}{\cos A} = 2 \sec A \).
Solution:
LHS = \( \frac{\cos A}{1 + \sin A} + \frac{1 + \sin A}{\cos A} \)
Taking the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of the denominators:
\( = \frac{\cos^2 A + (1 + \sin A)^2}{(1 + \sin A)\cos A} \)
Expanding \( (1 + \sin A)^2 \) using \( (a+b)^2 = a^2 + b^2 + 2ab \):
\( = \frac{\cos^2 A + 1 + \sin^2 A + 2\sin A}{(1 + \sin A)\cos A} \)
Rearranging terms and grouping \( (\sin^2 A + \cos^2 A) \):
\( = \frac{(\sin^2 A + \cos^2 A) + 1 + 2\sin A}{(1 + \sin A)\cos A} \)
Since \( \sin^2 A + \cos^2 A = 1 \):
\( = \frac{1 + 1 + 2\sin A}{(1 + \sin A)\cos A} \)
\( = \frac{2 + 2\sin A}{(1 + \sin A)\cos A} \)
Taking 2 common in the numerator:
\( = \frac{2(1 + \sin A)}{(1 + \sin A)\cos A} \)
Cancelling \( (1 + \sin A) \) from numerator and denominator:
\( = \frac{2}{\cos A} \)
\( = 2 \sec A \) = RHS.
Hence Proved.

Q7. A circus artist is climbing a 20 m long rope, which is tightly stretched and tied from the top of a vertical pole to the ground. Find the height of the pole, if the angle made by the rope with the ground level is \( 30^\circ \).
Solution:
Let \( AB \) be the vertical pole of height '\( h \)' meters.
Let \( AC \) be the rope tightly stretched from top \( A \) to ground point \( C \).
Given, length of rope \( AC = 20 \) m.
Angle made by rope with ground, \( \angle ACB = 30^\circ \).

In right-angled triangle \( ABC \) (right angled at \( B \)):
To relate the perpendicular (\( AB \)) and hypotenuse (\( AC \)), we use the sine ratio.
\( \sin(\angle ACB) = \frac{\text{Perpendicular}}{\text{Hypotenuse}} = \frac{AB}{AC} \)
\( \sin 30^\circ = \frac{h}{20} \)
We know \( \sin 30^\circ = \frac{1}{2} \).
\( \frac{1}{2} = \frac{h}{20} \)
\( 2h = 20 \implies h = 10 \) m.
Therefore, the height of the vertical pole is 10 meters.
Height of pole = 10 m

Q8. If \( x = a \sin \theta \) and \( y = b \tan \theta \), prove that \( \frac{a^2}{x^2} - \frac{b^2}{y^2} = 1 \).
Solution:
Given: \( x = a \sin \theta \implies \frac{x}{a} = \sin \theta \).
Therefore, taking reciprocals on both sides:
\( \frac{a}{x} = \frac{1}{\sin \theta} = \csc \theta \)     ...(i)

Given: \( y = b \tan \theta \implies \frac{y}{b} = \tan \theta \).
Therefore, taking reciprocals on both sides:
\( \frac{b}{y} = \frac{1}{\tan \theta} = \cot \theta \)     ...(ii)

LHS = \( \frac{a^2}{x^2} - \frac{b^2}{y^2} \)
This can be written as: \( \left(\frac{a}{x}\right)^2 - \left(\frac{b}{y}\right)^2 \).
Substituting values from (i) and (ii):
LHS = \( (\csc \theta)^2 - (\cot \theta)^2 \)
LHS = \( \csc^2 \theta - \cot^2 \theta \).
From fundamental trigonometric identities, we know that \( 1 + \cot^2 \theta = \csc^2 \theta \), which means \( \csc^2 \theta - \cot^2 \theta = 1 \).
Therefore, LHS = 1 = RHS.
Hence Proved.
[3 Marks]
Q9. The shadow of a tower standing on a level ground is found to be 40 m longer when the Sun's altitude is \( 30^\circ \) than when it is \( 60^\circ \). Find the height of the tower.
Solution:
Let the height of the tower \( AB \) be '\( h \)' meters.
Let \( BC \) be the length of the shadow when the Sun's altitude is \( 60^\circ \). Let \( BC = x \) meters.
When Sun's altitude is \( 30^\circ \), the shadow is \( BD \). Given \( BD \) is 40 m longer than \( BC \).
So, \( BD = x + 40 \) meters.

In right \( \Delta ABC \):
\( \tan 60^\circ = \frac{AB}{BC} \implies \sqrt{3} = \frac{h}{x} \implies h = x\sqrt{3} \)     ...(i)

In right \( \Delta ABD \):
\( \tan 30^\circ = \frac{AB}{BD} \implies \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{h}{x + 40} \)
\( \sqrt{3}h = x + 40 \)     ...(ii)

Substitute the value of \( h \) from (i) into (ii):
\( \sqrt{3}(x\sqrt{3}) = x + 40 \)
\( 3x = x + 40 \)
\( 2x = 40 \implies x = 20 \) m.

Now put \( x = 20 \) in eq (i) to find \( h \):
\( h = 20\sqrt{3} \) m.
(Optional: If \( \sqrt{3} \approx 1.732 \), \( h \approx 34.64 \) m).
Height of tower = \( 20\sqrt{3} \) m

Q10. Prove that: \( \sqrt{\frac{1+\sin A}{1-\sin A}} = \sec A + \tan A \).
Solution:
LHS = \( \sqrt{\frac{1+\sin A}{1-\sin A}} \)
Rationalize the denominator inside the square root by multiplying numerator and denominator by \( (1 + \sin A) \):
\( = \sqrt{\frac{(1+\sin A)(1+\sin A)}{(1-\sin A)(1+\sin A)}} \)
Using \( (a-b)(a+b) = a^2 - b^2 \) in the denominator:
\( = \sqrt{\frac{(1+\sin A)^2}{1 - \sin^2 A}} \)
Using the identity \( 1 - \sin^2 A = \cos^2 A \):
\( = \sqrt{\frac{(1+\sin A)^2}{\cos^2 A}} \)
Taking the square root on both numerator and denominator:
\( = \frac{1+\sin A}{\cos A} \)
Splitting the fraction:
\( = \frac{1}{\cos A} + \frac{\sin A}{\cos A} \)
Using reciprocal and quotient relations (\( \sec A = \frac{1}{\cos A}, \tan A = \frac{\sin A}{\cos A} \)):
\( = \sec A + \tan A \) = RHS.
Hence Proved.

SECTION C
Q11. From the top of a 7 m high building, the angle of elevation of the top of a cable tower is \( 60^\circ \) and the angle of depression of its foot is \( 45^\circ \). Determine the height of the tower.
Solution:
Let \( AB \) be the building of height 7 m, so \( AB = 7 \) m.
Let \( CD \) be the cable tower of height '\( h \)' m.
From top of building \( A \), draw a horizontal line \( AE \) parallel to ground \( BD \). So \( E \) lies on \( CD \).
Then \( ED = AB = 7 \) m and \( AE = BD \).
Let \( CE = y \) m. Therefore, height of tower \( h = y + 7 \).

Given: Angle of elevation of top \( C \) from \( A \) is \( \angle CAE = 60^\circ \).
Angle of depression of foot \( D \) from \( A \) is \( \angle EAD = 45^\circ \).
By alternate interior angles, \( \angle ADB = \angle EAD = 45^\circ \).

In right \( \Delta ABD \):
\( \tan 45^\circ = \frac{AB}{BD} \)
\( 1 = \frac{7}{BD} \implies BD = 7 \) m.
Since \( AE = BD \), then \( AE = 7 \) m.

In right \( \Delta AEC \):
\( \tan 60^\circ = \frac{CE}{AE} \)
\( \sqrt{3} = \frac{y}{7} \implies y = 7\sqrt{3} \) m.

Height of the tower \( h = CE + ED = y + 7 \).
\( h = 7\sqrt{3} + 7 = 7(\sqrt{3} + 1) \) m.
Height = \( 7(\sqrt{3} + 1) \) m

Q12. Prove that: \( \frac{\tan \theta}{1 - \cot \theta} + \frac{\cot \theta}{1 - \tan \theta} = 1 + \sec \theta \csc \theta \).
Solution:
LHS = \( \frac{\tan \theta}{1 - \cot \theta} + \frac{\cot \theta}{1 - \tan \theta} \)
Convert everything into sine and cosine:
\( \tan \theta = \frac{\sin \theta}{\cos \theta}, \cot \theta = \frac{\cos \theta}{\sin \theta} \)

\( \text{LHS} = \frac{\frac{\sin \theta}{\cos \theta}}{1 - \frac{\cos \theta}{\sin \theta}} + \frac{\frac{\cos \theta}{\sin \theta}}{1 - \frac{\sin \theta}{\cos \theta}} \)
\( = \frac{\frac{\sin \theta}{\cos \theta}}{\frac{\sin \theta - \cos \theta}{\sin \theta}} + \frac{\frac{\cos \theta}{\sin \theta}}{\frac{\cos \theta - \sin \theta}{\cos \theta}} \)
\( = \frac{\sin^2 \theta}{\cos \theta(\sin \theta - \cos \theta)} + \frac{\cos^2 \theta}{\sin \theta(\cos \theta - \sin \theta)} \)

Note that \( (\cos \theta - \sin \theta) = -(\sin \theta - \cos \theta) \). Adjusting the sign of the second term:
\( = \frac{\sin^2 \theta}{\cos \theta(\sin \theta - \cos \theta)} - \frac{\cos^2 \theta}{\sin \theta(\sin \theta - \cos \theta)} \)
Taking \( \frac{1}{\sin \theta - \cos \theta} \) common:
\( = \frac{1}{\sin \theta - \cos \theta} \left[ \frac{\sin^2 \theta}{\cos \theta} - \frac{\cos^2 \theta}{\sin \theta} \right] \)
\( = \frac{1}{\sin \theta - \cos \theta} \left[ \frac{\sin^3 \theta - \cos^3 \theta}{\sin \theta \cos \theta} \right] \)

Using algebraic identity \( a^3 - b^3 = (a-b)(a^2+ab+b^2) \):
\( = \frac{(\sin \theta - \cos \theta)(\sin^2 \theta + \sin \theta \cos \theta + \cos^2 \theta)}{(\sin \theta - \cos \theta)(\sin \theta \cos \theta)} \)
Cancelling \( (\sin \theta - \cos \theta) \) and using \( \sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta = 1 \):
\( = \frac{1 + \sin \theta \cos \theta}{\sin \theta \cos \theta} \)
\( = \frac{1}{\sin \theta \cos \theta} + \frac{\sin \theta \cos \theta}{\sin \theta \cos \theta} \)
\( = (\csc \theta \cdot \sec \theta) + 1 \)
\( = 1 + \sec \theta \csc \theta \) = RHS.
Hence Proved.
[5 Marks]
Q13. A straight highway leads to the foot of a tower. A man standing at the top of the tower observes a car at an angle of depression of \( 30^\circ \), which is approaching the foot of the tower with a uniform speed. Six seconds later, the angle of depression of the car is found to be \( 60^\circ \). Find the time taken by the car to reach the foot of the tower from this point.
Solution:
Let \( AB \) be the tower of height '\( h \)' m. Let man be at top \( A \).
Let \( C \) be the initial position of the car. Angle of depression is \( 30^\circ \), so alternate interior \( \angle ACB = 30^\circ \).
Let \( D \) be the position of the car after 6 seconds. Angle of depression is \( 60^\circ \), so alternate interior \( \angle ADB = 60^\circ \).
Let the uniform speed of the car be '\( v \)' m/s.
Distance covered in 6 seconds = \( CD = 6v \) meters.
Let the time taken to reach foot \( B \) from \( D \) be '\( t \)' seconds.
Distance \( DB = vt \) meters.

In right \( \Delta ABD \):
\( \tan 60^\circ = \frac{AB}{DB} \implies \sqrt{3} = \frac{h}{vt} \implies h = \sqrt{3}vt \)     ...(i)

In right \( \Delta ABC \):
\( \tan 30^\circ = \frac{AB}{BC} = \frac{AB}{CD + DB} \)
\( \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{h}{6v + vt} \)
\( \sqrt{3}h = v(6 + t) \)     ...(ii)

Substitute the value of \( h \) from (i) into (ii):
\( \sqrt{3}(\sqrt{3}vt) = v(6 + t) \)
\( 3vt = v(6 + t) \)
Since \( v \neq 0 \) (speed of car), we can cancel \( v \):
\( 3t = 6 + t \)
\( 2t = 6 \implies t = 3 \) seconds.

Therefore, the car will take 3 seconds to reach the foot of the tower from point \( D \).
Time taken = 3 seconds
